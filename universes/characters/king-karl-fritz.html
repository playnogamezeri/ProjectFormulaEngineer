King Karl Fritz

The 145th King of Eldia who retreats to Paradis, creates the Walls and imposes a vow renouncing war on the Founding Titan's inheritors. His ideology shapes the false peace inside the Walls.

01 / IDENTITY

Identity

EXPAND IDENTITY DETAILS

Karl Fritz is the 145th king of Eldia and the founder of the regime established on Paradis Island. His decision to withdraw from the mainland and renounce war becomes one of the defining political forces in the history of the Walls. He is not the same person as the later Reiss monarchs who inherited his ideology, and he is distinct from the First King Fritz associated with Ymir’s enslavement in the ancient era. His legacy is a system built around collective memory control and a promise of peace enforced through threat.

02 / APPEARANCE

Appearance

EXPAND APPEARANCE DETAILS

Karl Fritz is presented primarily through historical accounts, inherited memories and the political structures created in his name. His image is that of a royal ruler whose authority rests on the Founding Titan and the power to reshape the memories of Subjects of Ymir. The anime provides limited direct characterization compared with the central cast, so visual details should not be expanded beyond what the scenes and official material establish.

03 / PERSONALITY

Personality

EXPAND PERSONALITY DETAILS

Karl Fritz is described as deeply ashamed of Eldia’s imperial history and determined to end its cycle of conquest. His pacifism is absolute rather than pragmatic: he is willing to accept the destruction of his own people rather than risk a return to the old empire. This conviction gives his plan moral seriousness but also exposes its coercive nature. He chooses for generations who follow him, denying them knowledge and the freedom to decide their own future.

04 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography

EXPAND BIOGRAPHY DETAILS

After the Great Titan War, Karl Fritz works with the Tybur family to bring about the fall of Eldian imperial dominance. He relocates a large population of Eldians to Paradis, constructs the Walls using the Founding Titan and erases the memories of the people within them. He establishes the vow renouncing war, binding future inheritors of the Founding Titan to his ideology. The Reiss family preserves the throne and the vow, while the public is taught a fabricated account of humanity’s survival behind the Walls.

05 / ABILITIES

Abilities

EXPAND ABILITIES DETAILS

Karl Fritz possesses the Founding Titan, whose power allows control over Titans and the bodies and memories of Subjects of Ymir. He uses this power on a massive political scale: altering collective memory, constructing the Walls and creating a vow that constrains future royal inheritors. His influence persists long after his death because the Founding Titan’s power and the royal succession preserve the ideological structure he established.

06 / COMBAT & SKILLS

Combat & Skills

EXPAND COMBAT & SKILLS DETAILS

Karl Fritz’s defining actions are political and strategic rather than battlefield combat. He uses the Founding Titan to create a defensive enclosure and deterrent, threatening to unleash the Wall Titans if the outside world attacks. Yet the vow renouncing war prevents the royal inheritors from using the threat as an ordinary military strategy. His plan relies on deterrence, isolation and secrecy, not on a conventional campaign of conquest.

07 / RELATIONSHIPS

Relationships

EXPAND RELATIONSHIPS DETAILS

Karl’s relationship with the Tybur family is central to the transition from Eldian imperial rule to the new balance of power. The Reiss family becomes the keeper of his throne, bloodline and vow. The people of Paradis are the intended beneficiaries of his withdrawal, but they are also the subjects of his memory alteration. His relationship with future generations is therefore paternalistic: he seeks to protect them by withholding the truth and limiting their choices.

08 / DEVELOPMENT

Development

EXPAND DEVELOPMENT DETAILS

Karl Fritz is not given a conventional on-screen character arc. The audience’s understanding of him changes as the story reveals the gap between the public myth of the Walls and the political plan behind it. At first, the Walls appear to be humanity’s last refuge; later, they are understood as the product of a ruler’s guilt, fear and unilateral decision. His legacy becomes a central question about whether peace imposed through deception can be morally sustainable.

09 / FINAL FATE

Final Fate

EXPAND FINAL FATE DETAILS

Karl Fritz dies long before the main events, but his vow and political design continue through the Reiss royal line. His ideology is eventually confronted when Eren and Zeke enter the Paths and the conflict over the Founding Titan reaches its climax. The vow’s influence is overcome, exposing how one ruler’s decision had constrained the choices of generations.

10 / ANALYSIS

Analysis

EXPAND ANALYSIS DETAILS

Karl Fritz represents the danger of confusing pacifism with the right to decide other people’s fate. His guilt over historical atrocities is understandable, and his desire to prevent renewed conquest is significant. However, his method uses mass deception and inherited coercion. The story invites a distinction between refusing violence oneself and depriving an entire population of the knowledge needed to choose. His plan creates temporary isolation, not a durable reconciliation with the world.

11 / CHRONOLOGY

Chronology

EXPAND CHRONOLOGY DETAILS

Karl Fritz rules during the decline of the Eldian Empire and the Great Titan War. He cooperates with the Tybur family, withdraws to Paradis, constructs the Walls and alters the memories of the population. He establishes the vow renouncing war and leaves the Founding Titan to the royal line. His death precedes the main narrative, but his ideology governs the Reiss monarchy until the events of the series.

13 / PURPOSES & REASONING

Purposes & Reasoning

EXPAND PURPOSES & REASONING

Karl Fritz’s purpose is to embody the contradiction between pacifist intention and coercive control. He wants to end Eldia’s imperial violence and prevent a return to conquest, but he imposes his solution on an entire population through memory alteration, isolation and a vow that binds future inheritors. His reasoning is shaped by guilt over historical atrocities and the belief that his people should accept eventual destruction rather than repeat them. The story asks whether one ruler has the right to decide the moral future of generations who cannot know the truth or consent to the plan. Karl is not the ancient First King Fritz; this profile concerns the 145th king who founded the Walls regime. His legacy shows that peace built on secrecy and the removal of choice may suppress conflict without resolving its causes.
