Willy Tybur

A Marleyan noble who publicly reveals the truth of the island threat and declares war on Paradis. Eren kills him during the Liberio attack; his sister Lara is revealed as the War Hammer Titan.

01 / IDENTITY

Identity

EXPAND IDENTITY DETAILS

Willy Tybur is the head of the influential Tybur family, an Eldian noble house whose political power in Marley rests on its role in ending the Great Titan War. Unlike most Eldians, the Tyburs enjoy privileged status and influence over Marley’s government. Willy becomes a central political actor when he stages a public declaration in Liberio, using history, performance and calculated sacrifice to unite the world against Paradis.

02 / APPEARANCE

Appearance

EXPAND APPEARANCE DETAILS

Willy is a tall, refined man with light hair, formal clothing and a composed public presence. He is usually presented in the carefully arranged setting of the Tybur family’s estate or the Liberio festival. His elegant appearance supports the image of a statesman and public figure. During his speech, his gestures and expressions are theatrical by design, as he understands that the audience must not only hear his message but believe in the sincerity of the performance.

03 / PERSONALITY

Personality

EXPAND PERSONALITY DETAILS

Willy is articulate, politically intelligent and willing to accept personal danger for a cause he considers necessary. He understands that the Tybur family’s power has been sustained by a selective version of history. His decision to reveal the truth is courageous in one sense, but it is also strategic: he wants the world to recognize a common threat and support a unified military response. His willingness to die does not make his plan morally uncomplicated, because he knowingly places civilians in danger by choosing Liberio as the stage.

04 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography

EXPAND BIOGRAPHY DETAILS

The Tybur family helped bring about the fall of the Eldian Empire and became a privileged force within Marley. Willy inherits leadership of the family and works with military figures, including Theo Magath, as the threat posed by Paradis grows. He organizes a festival in Liberio and invites foreign dignitaries, intending to reveal the history of the Great Titan War and identify Eren Yeager as the immediate danger. During the speech, Eren transforms and attacks, killing Willy and many of the gathered civilians.

05 / ABILITIES

Abilities

EXPAND ABILITIES DETAILS

Willy does not inherit one of the Nine Titans and is not a frontline combatant. His abilities are political: he can command attention, organize a large international event, shape a public narrative and use the Tybur family’s prestige to influence governments. He understands the importance of timing and symbolism. His public performance is designed to transform a complicated historical conflict into a clear political call for action.

06 / COMBAT & SKILLS

Combat & Skills

EXPAND COMBAT & SKILLS DETAILS

Willy has no significant personal combat role. His plan places him in a location where he expects to be killed, and his death is part of the political strategy. He relies on the protection and military coordination surrounding the festival, especially Magath’s preparations. His confrontation with Eren is rhetorical and political rather than physical: he attempts to make the world choose a common enemy before the attack begins.

07 / RELATIONSHIPS

Relationships

EXPAND RELATIONSHIPS DETAILS

The Tybur family is the source of Willy’s authority and the responsibility he inherits. His relationship with Theo Magath is a pragmatic partnership built around the future of Marley and the threat from Paradis. His wife and children represent the personal cost of his decision, as he knows his public role may end in death. His relationship with the Eldian population is complicated by his family’s privilege: he is an Eldian who has benefited from a system that oppresses most Eldians.

08 / DEVELOPMENT

Development

EXPAND DEVELOPMENT DETAILS

Willy is introduced as a powerful figure who appears to control the public stage. As his speech unfolds, the audience learns that he is willing to expose the Tybur family’s role in history and accept death to achieve a political outcome. His development is concentrated in the Liberio sequence, where performance becomes action. The scene reveals both his courage and the limits of his moral vision: he seeks unity through fear of a new enemy.

09 / FINAL FATE

Final Fate

EXPAND FINAL FATE DETAILS

Willy is killed when Eren transforms during the Liberio festival. His death is anticipated and incorporated into the strategy he helped design, but the attack also kills civilians and begins a wider escalation. His speech succeeds in helping shape international opinion against Paradis, yet the immediate result is further violence rather than a stable peace.

10 / ANALYSIS

Analysis

EXPAND ANALYSIS DETAILS

Willy illustrates the power of political storytelling. He knows that history is not only a record of events but a force that can organize public emotion and state action. His decision to tell a hidden truth is significant, but he frames it to create a coalition against Paradis, not to dismantle the structures that enabled oppression. The story asks whether a leader can condemn an old injustice while still relying on fear and collective punishment to secure a future.

11 / CHRONOLOGY

Chronology

EXPAND CHRONOLOGY DETAILS

Willy inherits leadership of the Tybur family and becomes involved in Marley’s response to the changing balance of power. He coordinates with Theo Magath, arranges the Liberio festival and prepares a public account of the Great Titan War. During his speech, Eren attacks Liberio and kills him. His death becomes a catalyst for the international conflict that follows.

13 / PURPOSES & REASONING

Purposes & Reasoning

EXPAND PURPOSES & REASONING

Willy’s purpose is to show how political leaders use history, public performance and fear to shape international action. He knows that the Tybur family’s prestige rests on a carefully managed account of the past, and he chooses to reveal a version of the truth at the Liberio festival. His reasoning is strategic: he believes the world must unite against Paradis and is willing to make himself a target to create that unity. The story complicates this apparent sacrifice because the event places civilians in danger and frames collective fear as the route to political solidarity. Willy’s role asks whether exposing historical truth is enough when the proposed response still relies on militarization and the designation of a new enemy. He is a political actor, not a Titan fighter, and his power lies in narrative and influence.
