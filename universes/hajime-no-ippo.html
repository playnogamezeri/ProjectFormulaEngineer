Synopsis & Production Context

Rescued from bullies by middleweight prodigy Mamoru Takamura, shy fishing-boat helper Ippo Makunouchi begins training under veteran coach Genji Kamogawa. Through grueling fundamentals and signature techniques like the Dempsey Roll, Ippo conquers the Rookie King Tournament and JBC Featherweight belt—while the manga continues into his retirement and world-stage rivalries.

02World & SettingEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Grounded in real-world Tokyo boxing—Kamogawa Boxing Gym, Korakuen Hall, and Osaka's Naniwa Gym—governed by authentic JBC, OPBF, WBA, WBC, and IBF rankings, weight cuts, and medical rules.

Kamogawa Boxing Gym (Tokyo)

Modest old-school gym run by Coach Genji Kamogawa.

Korakuen Hall (Bunkyō, Tokyo)

Mecca of Japanese professional boxing hosting JBC title bouts.

Osaka & Nekota's Mountain

Takeshi Sendō's Naniwa turf and Kamogawa/Nekota's rugged training cabin.

Mexico & U.S. World Arenas

Stage for Ricardo Martínez, Volg's IBF title, and Takamura's world belts.

03Story & ChronologyEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Chronological Progression

Season 1 charts Ippo's debut through his Lallapallooza title win over Sendō; Champion Road and Mashiba vs. Kimura cover early defenses; New Challenger and Rising cover Takamura's world belts and Ippo vs. Sawamura. The manga continues through Ch. 1515+ with Ippo's retirement (Ch. 1208) and Sendō vs. Ricardo Martínez.

ANIME VS. SOURCE CONTINUITY DIFFERENCES: The anime swaps the chronological order of Champion Road and Mashiba vs. Kimura, moves the post-WWII Kamogawa/Nekota flashback to the end of Rising (Ep. 124–127), and omits minor chapters and spars.

06Battle & Combat SystemEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Strictly grounded in real-world boxing weight classes, biomechanics, stamina, severe weight cuts, and cumulative brain trauma—with zero supernatural powers.

Signature Boxing Techniques

Dempsey Roll weaving hooks, Gazelle Punch, Flicker Jab, Sendō's Smash, and Miyata's Jolt Counter.

Weight Cuts & Punch-Drunk Risk

Realistic stamina drains, Miyata's 126-lb weight-cut toll, and chronic traumatic encephalopathy fears.

12PurposesEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Moral of the Story: True Strength Is Found in Effort, Humility, and Respect for the Ring

Hajime no Ippo elevates boxing from brute brawling into a lifelong study of discipline, courage, and care for those who step through the ropes.

Even away from active competition, Ippo's dedication proves that boxing's heart lies in how you carry yourself inside and outside the ring.
