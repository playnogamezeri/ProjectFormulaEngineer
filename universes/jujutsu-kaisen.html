EXPANDED RESEARCH / SERIES DOSSIER

World, mechanics and critical analysis

Gege Akutami’s dark action series follows Yuji Itadori after he swallows a cursed object containing Ryomen Sukuna’s finger and becomes the vessel of a dangerous curse. He enters Tokyo Jujutsu High and learns to confront curses born from negative human emotion. The narrative moves from exorcism missions and school rivalries into institutional crisis, ideological conflict and questions about the value of individual lives.

World structure and institutions

Modern Japan contains a hidden jujutsu society whose sorcerers protect ordinary people from curses. Jujutsu schools, clans, mission authorities and conservative leadership structures shape who is trained and who is sacrificed. The balance between secrecy and public danger becomes unstable as major incidents expose the limits of the existing system.

Systems, rules and limitations

Cursed energy is generated from negative emotion and can be controlled by sorcerers. Innate techniques, barriers, binding vows, reverse cursed technique, domain expansion and cursed tools are distinct mechanisms. A domain's sure-hit effect, barrier conditions and refinement can determine a contest, but domains have costs and counters. Heavenly Restrictions and cursed objects introduce other exceptional rules.

Factions and organizational dynamics

Tokyo and Kyoto Jujutsu High, the major sorcerer clans, the higher-ups, curse-user groups and organized cursed spirits each have separate motives. The Zenin, Gojo and Kamo clans represent inherited power structures. The protagonists' school affiliation does not mean the institution is morally unified; internal authority and generational conflict are central.

Themes and adaptation continuity

The series explores death, responsibility, institutional conservatism, exploitation, friendship, power and the limits of utilitarian sacrifice. Its combat is highly rule-driven, so the conditions and costs of techniques matter more than vague power labels. The manga, television seasons and theatrical prequel adaptation should be indexed separately, including anime-original presentation choices and coverage boundaries.
