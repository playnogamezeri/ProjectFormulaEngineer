Synopsis & Production Context

Sharing a dream with his late friend Piao to become Great Generals of the Heavens, war orphan Li Xin helps exiled Qin King Ying Zheng reclaim his throne from Cheng Jiao's coup. Rising through meritocratic campaigns at Dakan Plains, Bayou, Sanyou, and the Six-State Coalition War, Xin's Hi Shin Unit becomes a cornerstone of Qin's unification drive.

02World & SettingEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Set during China's Warring States period (475–221 BC) across seven rival kingdoms—Qin, Zhao, Wei, Chu, Qi, Yan, and Han—governed by aristocratic courts, Legalist statecraft, and meritocratic warfare.

State of Qin (Xianyang & Hangu Pass)

Western powerhouse uniting under Ying Zheng and Changping Jun.

State of Zhao (Handan, Bayou & Gyou)

Qin's fiercest northern rival defended by Grand Strategist Li Mu.

Sanyou & Kokuyou Hills

Key strategic gateways contested between Qin, Wei, and Zhao.

States of Chu, Wei, Han & Qi

Rival powers caught in Coalition and unification wars.

03Story & ChronologyEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Chronological Progression

Spans Cheng Jiao's Rebellion, Bayou (Wang Qi's fall), Sanyou, the Coalition Invasion at Hangu Pass and Sai, Ying Zheng's Crowning Ceremony vs. Lü Buwei, Kokuyou Hills, Western Zhao Invasion (Gyou), and the manga's Eikyuu, Gian, Hango, and Han conquests.

ANIME VS. SOURCE CONTINUITY DIFFERENCES: Season 1 used heavy early 3DCG, toned down gore, and skipped the Assassination Plot Arc (Ch. 82–100). From Season 3 onward, Pierrot and Studio Signpost shifted to crisp 2D animation and faithful pacing (~3.5–4 chapters per episode).

06Battle & Combat SystemEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Strictly grounded in Warring States military strategy, logistics, formations, and magic-free martial duels.

Instinctual vs. Strategical Command

Strategical generals (Li Mu, Wang Jian) orchestrate logic and supply lines; Instinctual generals (Xin, Duke Hyou) read battlefield 'sparks.'

Sieges, Logistics & Duels

Campaigns hinge on siege towers, starvation tactics (Gyou), and commander duels.

12PurposesEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Moral of the Story: Uniting a Fractured World Demands Both Vision and Bearing Its Cost

Kingdom refuses to sanitize war while asking how leaders and soldiers justify fighting for a lasting peace.

From a slave boy's promise to a king's legalist vision, every battle tests whether ambition serves humanity or consumes it.
