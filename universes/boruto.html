01 / SERIES PROFILE

Overview

Boruto: Naruto Next Generations continues the Naruto setting after the Fourth Shinobi World War. Boruto, son of Seventh Hokage Naruto Uzumaki, enters the Ninja Academy in a period of peace and rapid modernization. His generation inherits a world with fewer conventional wars but faces new threats tied to scientific experimentation, secret organizations and the Ōtsutsuki.

Original concept & supervision

Masashi Kishimoto created the Naruto world and supervises the Boruto manga. Mikio Ikemoto illustrates the manga; Ukyo Kodachi wrote its early portion before Kishimoto resumed writing duties.

Television production

Studio Pierrot produced the television anime. The official anime site lists Masayuki Kōda as director, Noriyuki Abe as animation supervisor, and Yasuharu Takanashi with -yaiba- for music.

Publication

The manga began in 2016. Its first part concluded in 2023; the continuation is titled Boruto: Two Blue Vortex.

Anime format

The television series began in 2017 and its first part ended in 2023. The anime includes manga adaptations and extensive anime-original story arcs.

Premise

The new generation grows up in a world where the Five Great Shinobi Villages cooperate more closely and technology changes daily life. Boruto initially resists the expectations attached to his father's position. His team encounters threats that reveal the peace-era world is not free from exploitation, hidden power struggles or existential danger.

Genres and narrative modes

Action-adventure, martial arts fantasy, science fantasy, coming-of-age drama, family drama and ensemble storytelling. Its narrative alternates between academy life, missions, political crises and large-scale supernatural conflict.

02 / WORLD & SETTING

World & Setting

The series inherits the geography and political history of Naruto but presents them in a later period. The end of the Fourth Shinobi World War created a foundation for cooperation; it did not erase national borders, village identities or the need for security.

The Five Great Shinobi Countries

  • Land of Fire — Konohagakure: Boruto's home, now a modernized city with high-rise buildings, public screens and rail transport.
  • Land of Wind — Sunagakure: the Hidden Sand, led by the Kazekage and allied with Konoha.
  • Land of Water — Kirigakure: the Hidden Mist, whose history includes a violent internal era and later reform.
  • Land of Lightning — Kumogakure: the Hidden Cloud, a major military and political power.
  • Land of Earth — Iwagakure: the Hidden Stone, another major village within the Five Kage system.

Institutions and everyday life

The Ninja Academy, mission administration, Kage offices, medical services, intelligence networks and research divisions remain important. Civilian life is more visible in Konoha than in Naruto's childhood era, and shinobi increasingly operate alongside modern infrastructure.

Beyond the villages

Kara's hidden facilities, Ōtsutsuki dimensions and the Divine Tree system expand the story beyond ordinary national geography. These locations should be understood as distinct from the conventional map of the shinobi countries.

03 / STORY & CHRONOLOGY

Story & Chronology

Peace-era childhood and Academy entry

Boruto grows up in Konoha under Naruto's Hokage administration and begins formal shinobi education.

Team formation and early missions

Boruto, Sarada and Mitsuki form a new Team 7 under Konohamaru. Their missions expose them to threats beyond routine village assignments.

Chūnin Exams and Momoshiki

The exams become the setting for an Ōtsutsuki attack. Boruto's encounter with Momoshiki leaves him marked by Karma.

Mujina Bandits and Kara

Investigations reveal a clandestine organization and its interest in vessels, scientific experimentation and Ōtsutsuki power.

Vessel and Kawaki

Kawaki enters Konoha's orbit. His past and Karma connect the village directly to Kara's central project.

Isshiki and Code's aftermath

The conflict escalates into a struggle over the Ōtsutsuki legacy, with consequences for Naruto, Sasuke, Boruto and Kawaki.

Omnipotence and Two Blue Vortex

A reality-altering ability reshapes how people understand Boruto and Kawaki. The second manga part follows the resulting fractured relationships and a new threat.

Exact chapter and episode numbers are maintained in the official episode guide and manga publication record; arc placement differs between anime and manga.

04 / MAJOR STORY ARCS

Major Story Arcs

Academy Entrance

Introduces Boruto's classmates and the academy's new social environment. The anime labels this opening arc anime-original.

Sarada Uchiha

Focuses on Sarada's family identity and her relationship with Sasuke, expanding the Uchiha family's postwar story.

School Trip / Hidden Mist

Moves the class beyond the Land of Fire and introduces a younger generation in Kirigakure against the village's violent historical legacy.

Graduation & Genin

Concludes the academy stage and establishes the new genin teams.

Chūnin Exams / Versus Momoshiki

Connects the new generation's examination to an Ōtsutsuki invasion and a major turning point in Boruto's life.

Mujina Bandits

Introduces a criminal network and sets up the transition toward the Kara storyline.

Kara Actuation & Vessel

Explores Kara's experiments, the vessel project and Kawaki's history.

Kawaki

Centers the formation of a new household bond and the tension between belonging, trauma and Ōtsutsuki danger.

Code's Assault

Follows the conflict after Isshiki and the danger posed by Code and the remnants of Kara.

Two Blue Vortex

The manga's second part follows the changed world after Omnipotence, Boruto's altered status and the emergence of the Shinju threat.

The anime also contains numerous anime-original arcs, including Byakuya Gang, Mitsuki's Disappearance, Parent and Child Day, Time Slip, Mujina-related expansions, Kara Actuation and the Kawaki/Himawari Academy material. These are identified as anime continuity rather than silently merged into manga chapter chronology.

06 / BATTLE & COMBAT SYSTEM

Battle & Combat System

Chakra

Chakra is the energy used to perform most shinobi techniques. It is produced through the combination of physical and spiritual energies, shaped through training and control. Large reserves do not automatically guarantee skill: control, timing, knowledge and tactical judgment matter.

Core disciplines

  • Ninjutsu: techniques that manipulate chakra to produce specialized effects, including elemental transformations.
  • Taijutsu: direct physical combat, conditioning, movement and hand-to-hand technique.
  • Genjutsu: techniques that manipulate perception and sensory experience.
  • Dōjutsu: ocular abilities such as the Sharingan and Byakugan, with distinct inherited rules and limitations.
  • Senjutsu: natural-energy integration, including Sage Mode and related transformations.

Scientific ninja tools

Technology can store, reproduce or deploy techniques. These tools change access to power and raise questions about skill, fairness, reliability and the security risks of weaponized research. Their function should not be conflated with natural chakra mastery.

Karma and Ōtsutsuki abilities

Karma is an Ōtsutsuki-related seal that can function as a compressed data imprint and vessel mechanism. It can grant power while threatening the host's bodily autonomy and identity. Its extraction, activation and compatibility rules are story-specific and not interchangeable with ordinary curse marks.

Limits and counters

Chakra exhaustion, injury, technique compatibility, sensory limits, preparation, sealing, absorption and the opponent's knowledge can all affect outcomes. Power comparisons should specify the story period and conditions rather than treating every character's strongest form as continuously available.

08 / RELATIONSHIPS

Relationships

  • Boruto and Naruto: a father-son relationship strained by the demands of the Hokage office, then reshaped by crisis and sacrifice.
  • Boruto and Hinata: a family bond grounded in care, with Hinata often mediating the emotional effects of Naruto's absence.
  • Boruto and Himawari: sibling affection and mutual concern; their family connection becomes more consequential as the story progresses.
  • Boruto and Sarada: teammates whose ambitions and temperaments differ but whose trust grows through shared danger.
  • Boruto and Mitsuki: a close bond through which Mitsuki explores self-determination.
  • Boruto and Kawaki: a brother-like bond transformed by trauma, fear and conflicting ideas about protecting Naruto.
  • Sarada and Sasuke: a father-daughter relationship complicated by Sasuke's long absences and investigative duties.
  • Kawaki and Naruto: a parental bond that gives Kawaki a sense of belonging but also becomes the focus of his extreme protective decisions.

Relationship entries distinguish established canon from unresolved or interpretive readings. Romantic pairings are not inferred from friendship or fan preference.

09 / ANIME & MANGA GUIDE

Anime/Manga Guide

Manga

Boruto: Naruto Next Generations began in 2016 and follows the sequel story in manga form. The first part concludes before the time-skip continuation, Boruto: Two Blue Vortex. The manga is the primary source for the main serialized continuity.

Television anime

The television anime premiered in 2017. Part I concluded in 2023. Its adaptation includes anime-original arcs that expand the class, village life and supporting cast. These arcs are part of the television continuity and should be identified separately from manga-adapted material.

Viewing and reading order

  1. Complete the Naruto and Naruto: Shippuden story for the full predecessor context.
  2. Watch or read Boruto from its opening Academy period through the Chūnin Exams and Momoshiki conflict.
  3. Continue through the Kara, Vessel, Kawaki and Code developments.
  4. For the manga continuation, proceed to Two Blue Vortex after the first part.

10 / THEMES & ANALYSIS

Themes & Analysis

Inheritance versus self-definition

Boruto resists being reduced to Naruto's son. The series asks how a younger generation can honor inherited achievements without being confined by them.

Peace and institutional adaptation

The absence of large-scale war changes the role of shinobi. Security, education, diplomacy and civilian life become more visible, while hidden threats reveal that peace requires continued work.

Technology and the meaning of skill

Scientific ninja tools challenge older ideas about merit and mastery. The narrative does not treat technology as automatically evil; it examines how tools are designed, controlled and used.

Identity, autonomy and constructed bodies

Mitsuki, Kawaki and the Karma system raise different questions about bodily autonomy, artificial creation, coercion and self-determination.

Family and the cost of protection

Parental absence, chosen family and protective obsession recur throughout the story. Good intentions can become harmful when they deny another person's agency.

11 / ENDING EXPLAINED

Ending Explained

The overall story is ongoing in the manga continuation. The television anime's first part ended in 2023 and does not adapt the complete manga narrative. Therefore, there is no final series ending to explain at this time.

The major turning point at the end of the first manga part is the reality-altering effect known as Omnipotence, which changes how people perceive Boruto and Kawaki and sets the conditions for Two Blue Vortex. This is a major plot mechanism, not the final resolution of the story.

12 / PURPOSE & MORAL QUESTIONS

Purposes — Moral of the Story

  • A peaceful world still requires responsibility, vigilance and institutions capable of adapting.
  • Children should not be forced to live as copies of their parents; inherited history can inform identity without dictating it.
  • Technology is shaped by the ethics and intentions of those who create and deploy it.
  • Protecting someone does not justify taking away their agency or controlling their entire life.
  • Belonging can be built through trust and care, but trauma may make trust difficult and protection destructive.

These are interpretive themes supported by the narrative, not a single official moral statement.
