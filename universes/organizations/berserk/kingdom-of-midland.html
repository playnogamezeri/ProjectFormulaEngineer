01 / ORGANIZATION PROFILE

Profile & Purpose

Midland is a feudal kingdom in the early narrative. Its royal court, nobility and military command structure shape the Hundred-Year War and the political environment in which Griffith's mercenary company gains influence.

Purpose

A major human kingdom whose court politics and wars provide the setting for the Band of the Hawk's rise.

Classification

FEUDAL KINGDOM · HUMAN POLITY. This archive distinguishes political institutions, mercenary forces and later supernatural military formations.

02 / HISTORICAL FOUNDATION

Origin

The kingdom's exact founding history is not the focus of the early narrative. It is established as an existing feudal power engaged in a prolonged conflict with the Tudor Empire.

03 / ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE

Structure & Members

Midland is ruled by a king and supported by a royal court, noble houses, provincial authorities and military forces. The Band of the Hawk is a mercenary force serving Midland, not a permanent branch of its royal army.

Notable members and associated figures

Key figures include the King of Midland, Princess Charlotte, Minister Foss, various nobles and military commanders, and Griffith during his period of royal service. These people belong to different court and military roles.

04 / NARRATIVE RECORD

Story History

Midland's war against Tudor provides the setting for the Hawks' victories. Griffith's growing prestige and relationship with Charlotte draw him into court politics. The kingdom's stability collapses amid the Eclipse and the supernatural events that follow; later political conditions are transformed by the emergence of Falconia.

05 / CONTEXT & INTERPRETATION

Story Significance

Midland illustrates the fragility of feudal authority and the way military success can become political currency. Its institutions also provide a contrast to the later supernatural reshaping of the world.

Continuity note

This dossier follows the manga's broader narrative. The 1997, Golden Age film/Memorial Edition and 2016–2017 anime adaptations cover different portions and do not provide a single complete screen continuity.
