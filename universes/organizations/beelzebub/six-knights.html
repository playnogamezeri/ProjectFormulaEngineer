The Six Knights

Saint Ishiyama Academy's elite student force: six captains who enforce the school's internal rules and initially move against the transferred Ishiyama students.

01 / PROFILE & PURPOSE

Identity and mission

The Six Knights (Rokkisei) are six exceptionally strong students at Saint Ishiyama Academy. They are associated with leadership roles in sports teams and clubs and function as the school's elite enforcement group.

Stated function

Uphold the academy's rules of conduct and exercise the school's special judgment authority over students.

Operational mission

Maintain the existing school order and initially remove or discipline the incoming Ishiyama delinquents.

Authority concern

Their judgment power is portrayed as unfairly broad, making their authority a central source of conflict.

Membership

Six captains and elite fighters; their individual club roles differ.

02 / ORIGIN

Formation and background

The manga introduces the Six Knights as an existing elite within Saint Ishiyama. The series does not narrate their original formation or provide a formal charter. Their authority comes from their recognized positions in the academy's clubs and sports teams, combined with exceptional fighting skill.

They become the principal institutional obstacle for Oga's group after the Ishiyama students transfer.

03 / STRUCTURE & MEMBERS

Six members

Kaname Izuma

Leader of the Six Knights; kendo captain, master of the Izuma dojo and a highly skilled swordsman.

Hisaya Miki

Karate captain and the only first-year member. He has a personal history with Oga from middle school.

Ichirō Shinjō

One of the six elite student fighters.

Shizuka Nanami

Archery captain and former student-council vice-president; the only female member.

Mitsuteru Sakaki

One of the six Knights; associated with the academy's elite group and its conflicts with Kunieda.

Hiromichi Gō

Muscular third-year and radio-club captain; an aggressive fighter.

The six names above are the roster identified in the character references. The manga gives more personal detail for some members than others.

04 / STORY HISTORY

History through the story

  1. Institutional opposition: The Knights enforce the academy's rules against Oga and his group, backed by a disciplinary system that allows them to issue judgments.
  2. Rooftop confrontation: Conflict escalates into a direct contest between the Knights and the transferred Ishiyama students.
  3. Volleyball match: The decisive school contest gives Oga's group a route to remain at the academy and changes the student body's attitude.
  4. Teimou threat: External danger pushes former opponents toward cooperation.
  5. Later alliances: Individual Knights continue to intersect with the main cast and later supernatural conflicts, particularly through Saotome and the Pillar Squad storyline.
05 / NARRATIVE ROLE

Significance to Beelzebub

The Six Knights embody institutional authority in a series otherwise dominated by informal delinquent hierarchies. Their storyline asks whether rules are legitimate simply because powerful people enforce them. Their defeat does not erase the academy; it changes the social relationship between its established students and the newcomers.

Their members also expand the cast beyond Ishiyama and help connect school life to the wider network of martial arts, clubs and supernatural combat.

Core significance: The Six Knights are a test of institutional fairness: authority must be judged by how it is used, not merely by the title attached to it.
