01 / PROFILE & PURPOSE

Identity and purpose

The Demon Royal Household is the family and court surrounding the Great Demon Lord. It is not a single fighting squad: it includes the ruler, royal children, attendants, nurses, servants and other figures who serve or influence the royal family.

Institutional purpose

Maintain the royal household and the Demon Lord's family, court and succession structure.

Political question

The standing and future of the royal heirs, especially Beelzebub IV and Prince En.

Authority

The Great Demon Lord is the sovereign; the manga portrays him as notably detached from choosing which son should prevail.

Mission status

No single formal mission statement is given. Family continuity, royal service and succession are the central functions visible in the story.

02 / ORIGIN

Royal background

The royal household predates the main story and belongs to the Demon World's ruling structure. The Great Demon Lord's reign and Beelzebub III's earlier unification of the Demon World form part of the political history surrounding the family.

The manga does not provide a complete genealogy or court chronicle. This page therefore focuses on named family members and attendants who are relevant to the narrative.

04 / STORY HISTORY

History through the story

  1. Beel's placement: Beel is sent to the human world and becomes Oga's charge, with Hilda serving as his attendant.
  2. En's position: Prince En's ambitions and the forces supporting him create tension around the future of the royal family.
  3. Beel's condition: Beel's illness draws Oga and his allies into the Demon World and reveals more of the royal household's reach.
  4. Succession pressure: Behemoth's military operations and other demon factions make the rivalry between the heirs a matter of political and military consequence.
  5. Wider royal history: Later manga revelations connect the royal family to the Seven Deadly Sins and the Solomon Company's designs.
05 / NARRATIVE ROLE

Significance to Beelzebub

The royal household explains why an infant's guardianship becomes a matter of world-scale consequence. Beel is not simply an unusual child; he is a royal heir whose growth and relationships affect demon-world politics.

The household also creates a contrast between inherited status and personal relationships. Oga's connection to Beel develops through daily care, not through royal rank. Hilda's devotion and the presence of attendants show the difference between serving a royal child and personally raising him.

Core significance: The royal household supplies the political stakes, while Oga and Beel's bond supplies the emotional centre of the story.
