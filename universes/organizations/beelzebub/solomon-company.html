Solomon Company

A covert organization with connections in both the human and demon worlds. Its operations influence several major conflicts, manipulate key relationships and shape events around Beel's growth.

01 / PROFILE & PURPOSE

Identity and objectives

The Solomon Company is a secretive organization with networks in the human world and Demon World. Its name derives from Solomon, identified in the story as the Demon Lord's first Spellmaster. Its methods include covert observation, supply of demons, manipulation and intervention in events involving powerful individuals.

Known activities

Acquiring and moving significant objects, supplying demons to human groups, observing individuals and interfering in relationships and political conflicts.

Possible strategic goal

Exploit conflict involving Beel and Satan and profit from the resulting demand for protection and power.

Command

Johan, the Company's Reaper, reports to three senior elders.

Transparency

Its complete purpose, internal charter and all leadership identities remain partly unexplained.

The Company’s motives are not fully resolved by the text. Confirmed operations and strong implications are separated below from speculation.

02 / ORIGIN

Name and background

The organization takes its name from Solomon, the first Spellmaster of the Demon Lord. The manga does not provide a full founding date or a comprehensive account of how the Company developed into a transworld network.

Its senior figures are shown to recognize the Great Demon Lord, indicating a long-standing connection to demon-world power. Its human-world reach allows it to influence events far from the Demon Lord's court.

04 / STORY HISTORY

Operations and remaining history

  1. Stolen painting: The Company is connected to the theft of the Great Demon Lord's painting and its delivery to Himekawa and Kugayama, damaging their friendship.
  2. Supply network: It supplies demons to the Six Omens of Slaughter and is implicated in the disappearance of members of Behemoth's 34th Pillar Division.
  3. Observation: Udagawa monitors the Fallen Angels, especially Takamiya.
  4. Staged development: Johan's actions suggest that Ishiyama and Saint Ishiyama events were treated as stages through which Oga and Beel could grow.
  5. Succession exploitation: The Company appears prepared to profit if Beel and Satan awaken their powers and enter conflict, including by selling protection.
  6. Confrontation: Its role becomes clearer during the Solomon Company arc, but several motives and relationships remain unresolved even after its defeat.

Interpretive boundary: The idea that the Company orchestrated nearly every major event is presented in reference summaries as a strong implication. The manga leaves parts of its planning and motives ambiguous, so this dossier does not treat every suspected manipulation as proven.

05 / NARRATIVE ROLE

Significance to Beelzebub

The Solomon Company is the hidden connective tissue of the late manga. It links otherwise separate events—Himekawa's personal history, the Fallen Angels, the Pillar Division, demon supply and the final conflict—through covert interference.

It also changes the reader's understanding of earlier events. What first appears to be a series of unrelated school and demon incidents may have been shaped by actors who wanted particular people to develop, clash or become useful to a larger plan.

Core significance: The Company introduces manipulation as a force behind the plot, while its unresolved motives preserve uncertainty about how much of the story was truly under its control.
