01 / SERIES PROFILE

Overview

In an alternate world where the Holy Britannian Empire dominates much of the globe, exiled prince Lelouch Lamperouge obtains Geass, a power that allows him to command another person once. He adopts the masked identity Zero and leads a rebellion while pursuing a deeply personal objective.

Creator

Ichirō Ōkouchi and Gorō Taniguchi; original character designs by CLAMP.

Publication & release

Original television anime premiered in 2006, followed by a second season in 2008 and later film projects.

02 / SETTING

World & setting

The world is divided by imperial conquest, resistance movements and political blocs. Britannia's aristocracy, Area classifications and Knightmare Frame technology shape its military and social order.

10 / EXPANDED RESEARCH DOSSIER

Series analysis & world reference

Code Geass: Lelouch of the Rebellion is an original anime about Lelouch vi Britannia, an exiled prince who acquires Geass and leads a masked rebellion against the Holy Britannian Empire. The story combines tactical conflict, school-life concealment, political intrigue and questions about whether a just end can excuse coercive means. Lelouch's double identity drives the plot: the strategist Zero must operate while the student Lelouch maintains relationships inside the system he opposes.

World structure and institutions

The Holy Britannian Empire is an expansionist superpower whose colonial hierarchy shapes citizenship, geography and everyday life. Area 11 is the former Japan, and its population is subjected to imposed status categories. The Chinese Federation and the European Union provide alternative geopolitical centers. Political resistance includes competing factions whose goals are not always identical to Zero's.

Abilities, technology and operating rules

Geass is an individual supernatural ability granted through a contract, and each user's power has its own conditions and limitations. Lelouch's command-based Geass requires direct eye contact and becomes increasingly difficult to control. Knightmare Frames are military machines whose performance depends on design, pilots, tactics and logistical support; they are not simply interchangeable power levels.

Organizations, factions and relationships

The Black Knights evolve from a resistance group into a political-military organization. Britannian royal factions, the military, the Ashford Academy circle, the Chinese Federation's court and the Geass Order each have distinct internal interests. Zero's leadership depends on public symbolism and strategic secrecy, making information control a major organizational weapon.

Major themes and adaptation continuity

The series examines imperialism, racialized hierarchy, rebellion, political legitimacy, sacrifice, revenge, loyalty and the ethics of leadership. Lelouch is neither a neutral liberator nor a simple villain; his methods and consequences are central to the moral argument. The original television continuity, recap films and alternate film-sequel continuity should be clearly separated because they do not form one interchangeable chronology.
