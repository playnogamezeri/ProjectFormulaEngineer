Synopsis & Production Context

After Teikō Middle School's Generation of Miracles fractured under the weight of unmatched individual talent, invisible passer Tetsuya Kuroko joins humble Seirin High to become the 'Shadow' to American-trained ace Taiga Kagami. Together with Kiyoshi and Hyūga, they battle Kaijō, Shūtoku, Tōō, Yōsen, and Rakuzan to prove team basketball still reigns.

02World & SettingEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Set in contemporary Japan's high school basketball circuit (Tokyo preliminaries, Summer Interhigh, and the 50-team Winter Cup), pitting Seirin High against five powerhouse schools housing the Generation of Miracles.

Seirin High (Tokyo)

Second-year basketball program led by coach Riko Aida and founder Teppei Kiyoshi.

Teikō Middle School

Three-time national champion where the Generation of Miracles fractured.

Winter Cup Arena (Tokyo)

National elimination tournament where Seirin faces Tōō, Yōsen, Kaijō, and Rakuzan.

Powerhouse Campuses

Kaijō, Shūtoku, Tōō, Yōsen, and Kyoto's Rakuzan.

03Story & ChronologyEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Chronological Progression

Follows Seirin's Interhigh defeat to Aomine's Tōō, training camps, Winter Cup revenge wins over Tōō, Yōsen, Kaijō, and Akashi's Rakuzan, the Teikō flashback, and Vorpal Swords vs. Jabberwock in Last Game.

ANIME VS. SOURCE CONTINUITY DIFFERENCES: The anime introduces Tetsuya #2 earlier, condenses the 24-chapter Teikō flashback into Ep. 63–66, and alters the Extra Game ending in Last Game: in the manga Kagami stays at Seirin, whereas the film has Kagami depart for the U.S.

06Battle & Combat SystemEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Stylized high school basketball mechanics—five-position play, Misdirection, Emperor Eye, Zone/Direct Drive Zone, Formless Shot, and Perfect Copy—constrained by real stamina and adaptation.

Misdirection, Emperor Eye & Copy

Misdirection wears off over repeated exposure; Perfect Copy and Zone drain stamina rapidly.

Zone & Direct Drive Zone

Unlocks 100% individual focus—or full-team synchronization through Kuroko's eye-contact gate.

12PurposesEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Moral of the Story: No Prodigy Is Strong Enough to Play the Whole Court Alone

Kuroko's Basketball celebrates the invisible teammates who make stars shine and reminds prodigies why they fell in love with the game.

When the Shadow and Light trust their whole bench, even the most unstoppable solo talent learns the joy of passing the ball.
