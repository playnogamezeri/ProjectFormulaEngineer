EXPANDED RESEARCH / SERIES DOSSIER

World, mechanics and critical analysis

ONE’s coming-of-age supernatural comedy follows Shigeo Kageyama, nicknamed Mob, a boy with immense psychic ability who wants an ordinary life and struggles to understand his emotions. Working under the self-proclaimed spirit medium Reigen Arataka, Mob encounters spirits, fraudulent psychics, genuine espers and organizations seeking power. His emotional development matters more than the size of his psychic output.

World structure and institutions

The story takes place in modern Japan, where most people are unaware of the supernatural conflicts occurring around them. Spirits inhabit ordinary places, while psychic organizations operate in the shadows. The contrast between normal school life and hidden esper networks allows the series to examine how extraordinary ability can distort identity and social relationships.

Systems, rules and limitations

Psychic abilities include telekinesis, barriers, exorcism, energy absorption, astral projection and other specialized phenomena. Mob’s emotional percentage is a narrative representation of emotional pressure rather than a simple power meter. Different espers have distinct strengths and limitations; spiritual possession and spirit energy are not identical to human psychic talent.

Factions and organizational dynamics

The Body Improvement Club, Spirits and Such Consultation Office, Claw and various smaller esper groups represent contrasting responses to power. Reigen’s office is a business built around performance and interpersonal judgment, while Claw is an organized attempt to impose psychic dominance. Mob's classmates and family provide grounding outside the esper world.

Themes and adaptation continuity

Mob Psycho 100 explores emotional literacy, humility, self-worth, mentorship, manipulation, friendship and the danger of defining a person by talent. Mob’s restraint is not weakness; it is a deliberate ethical choice. The television anime adapts the manga’s complete central story, while OVAs and specials should be recorded as supplementary material rather than necessary replacements for the main seasons.
