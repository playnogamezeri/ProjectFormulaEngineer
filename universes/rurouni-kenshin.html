Synopsis & Production Context

In 1878 Meiji Tokyo, former imperial assassin Kenshin Himura wanders with a reverse-blade sword (sakabatō) vowing never to kill again. Sheltered at Kaoru Kamiya's dojo alongside Yahiko and Sanosuke, Kenshin confronts old Bakumatsu ghosts—Hajime Saitō, Makoto Shishio's Juppongatana in Kyoto, and finally Enishi Yukishiro's Jinchū revenge for Kenshin's late wife Tomoe.

02World & SettingEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Set in 1878 Meiji 11 Japan (Tokyo and Kyoto) amid rapid Westernization and the Haitōrei sword ban, with Bakumatsu flashbacks (1864–1868) contrasting lethal satsujinken with life-saving katsujinken.

Site of Kenshin's deepest despair and Enishi's final closure.

03Story & ChronologyEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Chronological Progression

Spans the Tokyo Arc (Oniwabanshū, Jin-e, Saitō), Kyoto Arc (Shishio & Juppongatana, Hiten Mitsurugi mastery), and the manga's Jinchū Arc (Tomoe Remembrance & Enishi), plus the Hokkaido Arc sequel.

ANIME VS. SOURCE CONTINUITY DIFFERENCES: The 1996 TV anime stopped adapting the manga after Kyoto (Ep. 62) and replaced the Jinchū Arc with filler (Shimabara, Black Knights, Feng Shui) before cancellation; Trust & Betrayal (1999) adapted only the Bakumatsu flashback. The 2023 LIDENFILMS remake faithfully adapts Tokyo and Kyoto (plus Act Zero & Sakabatō Shōgeki).

11Ending ExplainedEXPAND / COLLAPSE
SPOILER DISCLOSURE: Detailed resolution of Rurouni Kenshin across anime and source material.

In the canonical manga finale (Jinchū Arc), Kenshin defeats Enishi without killing him and Misao delivers Tomoe's diary so Enishi can heal. Four years later, Kenshin and Kaoru are married with son Kenji, and Kenshin passes his sakabatō to Yahiko.

12PurposesEXPAND / COLLAPSE

Moral of the Story: A Sword Exists to Protect Life, Not Replay Yesterday's Bloodshed

Rurouni Kenshin asks how someone stained by wartime violence can earn a peaceful tomorrow.

Through Kenshin's sakabatō vow and Yahiko's growth, the story insists that choosing mercy today breaks cycles of vengeance.
