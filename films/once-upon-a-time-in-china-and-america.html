01 / FILM IDENTITY

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Once Upon a Time in China and America (黃飛鴻之西域雄獅, "Wong Fei-hung: Lion of the Western Territory"), also known as Once Upon a Time in China VI, is a 1997 Hong Kong martial arts western directed by Sammo Hung — who also choreographed its fights — produced by Tsui Hark, and the sixth and final installment of the Once Upon a Time in China series. It marks Jet Li's return to the role of Wong Fei-hung, the Cantonese folk hero he had originated in the series' first three films, after Vincent Zhao carried the fourth and fifth. Wong travels to America with 13th Aunt (Rosamund Kwan) and his apprentice Clubfoot (Hung Yan-yan) to visit Bucktooth So (Power Chan), who has opened a San Francisco branch of Po-chi-lam, Wong's medicine clinic. Crossing the wilderness, the party rescues a thirsty cowboy, Billy (Jeff Wolfe), survives an ambush, and loses its carriage in a river — where Wong strikes his head on a rock and loses his memory. Adopted by a Native American tribe, the amnesiac master defeats a rival tribe's leader in single combat and becomes its champion, remembering nothing. In San Francisco, the corrupt mayor is legislating the Chinese migrants into misery and, sunk in debt, hires a Mexican bandit to rob the bank and frames Po-chi-lam for the crime. Wong's memory returns just in time for the gallows — and the frame-up collapses only because the mayor has short-changed his own bandit, who rides back into town to collect, exposing everything. Billy is elected mayor; Wong sails home. It is the series' strangest chapter — a kung fu western about memory, migration and who gets hanged for whose debts — and its final word on its hero: Fei-hung is Fei-hung even when Fei-hung does not know it.

RELEASE

February 1, 1997

Released in Hong Kong to positive reviews; the final installment of the six-film series, and Sammo Hung's last directorial effort until 2016's The Bodyguard.

THE RETURN

Li Resumes the Role

Li had left the series after Part III; Vincent Zhao played Wong in IV and V. Part VI is a homecoming — the original master, the original Aunt (Rosamund Kwan), one last journey.

THE GENRE

Kung Fu Western

The series that defined the modern wuxia epic ends in Monument Valley country: stagecoaches, tribes, a hanging judge's town, and a hero whose weapon is his schooling.

Place in the culture

The film lives in the culture as the series' coda and curiosity: the great nationalist saga of 1990s Hong Kong cinema — made across the handover years, about China meeting the West — concludes by sending its hero to the far West itself, to be judged by America's own frontier mythology. It is also a document of the genre's family: directed by Sammo Hung, produced by Tsui Hark, starring the role's originator, with Vincent Zhao's tenure respectfully closed by Li's return. For this Galaxy, the page stands at the head of the Wong Fei-hung descent: Parts III, II and I follow in this run, and the archive reads the series backward, from its American farewell to its 1991 beginning.

A series ends abroad

The production record places the film at a genuine crossroads: Tsui Hark's series had been Hong Kong cinema's defining historical epic of the decade, and its final chapter was handed not to Tsui but to Sammo Hung — the industry's other great action auteur — who took the hero further west than the series had ever gone. The archive records the handover of the director's chair alongside the film's own theme: the record notes that a saga about China negotiating with the modern world ends with its hero amnesiac in America, adopted by the continent's first peoples, and hanged — nearly — by its newest ones. The film's well-known afterlife includes the oft-repeated allegation that its kung-fu-western premise anticipated Jackie Chan's Shanghai Noon (2000); the archive files the resemblance without adjudicating it.

03 / NARRATIVE SUMMARY

Story Synopsis

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL STORY SUMMARY

In the early 20th century, Wong Fei-hung crosses the Pacific with 13th Aunt and Clubfoot to visit Bucktooth So, his apprentice, who has opened a Po-chi-lam clinic in San Francisco. Crossing the wilderness by carriage, they rescue a friendly cowboy named Billy, dying of thirst — and are ambushed by hostile Native Americans. The party escapes, but the carriage goes into a river; 13th Aunt and Clubfoot are pulled out and carried to So's clinic, while Wong, his head struck on a rock, vanishes into the water. He washes up in the keeping of a Native American tribe — alive, unhurt, and remembering nothing: not his name, not his school, not China. The tribe is at war with a stronger rival whose leader has wounded Fierce Eagle, the chief's son. The stranger who cannot remember being a master steps into the fight anyway, defeats the rival leader and half his men, and sends the enemy fleeing: the body remembers what the mind has lost.

In San Francisco, two campaigns run in parallel. The corrupt mayor is writing discriminatory laws to make the Chinese migrants' lives impossible, and Billy — the cowboy the party saved — fights him in the open, the one local voice against the ordinances. And Wong's companions search for their master. Wong makes his way to the city and, among his own people, his memory returns — at the price the film insists on: he forgets everything that happened during the amnesia, the tribe and its war wiped as cleanly as China had been. Meanwhile the mayor's debts come due. He hires a Mexican bandit to rob the town bank, frames the people of Po-chi-lam for the robbery, and plans to abscond with the loot. The frame holds: Wong and his companions are arrested and sentenced to hang.

The rescue, when it comes, is accounting. The bandit counts his pay and finds the mayor has shorted him — by hundreds of thousands — and rides back into town to claim the difference, exposing the whole arrangement in the middle of the execution. In the ensuing fight the mayor is killed; Wong captures the bandit and clears Po-chi-lam's name before the town that was about to hang him. The film closes in the key of the western it has become: Billy, the thirsty cowboy of the first reel, is elected the new mayor, and Wong Fei-hung, 13th Aunt and Clubfoot sail for China — the master's memory restored, his American chapter forgotten twice over, once by the river and once by the cure.

The full narrative summary is an editorial paraphrase of the documented accounts (Wikipedia's plot account), not dialogue or a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / STORY STRUCTURE

Story & Chronology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

The film unfolds as a voyage, an amnesia and a hanging. The sequence below follows the film's narrative order.

Act I — The river

Billy rescued from thirst; the ambush; the carriage lost; Wong's head, the rock, and the tribe that adopts a nameless master.

Act II — The town and the ordinances

The mayor's discriminatory laws; Billy's opposition; the tribal war won by a man who cannot remember learning to fight; memory restored, amnesia forgotten.

Act III — The robbery and the gallows

The mayor's debt and the hired bandit; Po-chi-lam framed; the sentence; the bandit's short count; the mayor killed; Billy elected; the voyage home.

THE MASTER

Wong Fei-hung

Jet Li. The Cantonese physician and kung fu master, folk hero of China, crossing an ocean to inspect a branch clinic — and spending the film's middle as a nameless tribal champion. Wong's function is the story's portrait of identity as practice rather than memory: stripped of name, school and country, he is still, in every gesture and intervention, exactly himself, which is the series' whole six-film argument compressed into one amnesia. The archive files him as the decade's most consequential master in film: a hero, verified, by amnesia.

13TH AUNT

Siu-kwan

Rosamund Kwan. Wong's romantic interest and the series' emissary of the modern: photographer, traveler, the partner who has crossed every border with him. Her function in this chapter is the story's portrait of the searcher: while the town legislates and the tribe wars, she and Clubfoot conduct the film's one unambiguously righteous campaign — find the master — and her recognition is the instrument of his cure. The archive files her as the decade's most consequential aunt in film: a beloved, recovered, by persistence.

THE APPRENTICE

Clubfoot

Hung Yan-yan. Wong's disciple, named for the foot the series' earlier chapters deformed and its hero healed, now the expedition's strong right arm. Clubfoot's function is the story's portrait of the school's continuity in exile: with the master lost, the apprentice is Po-chi-lam's senior representative in America, and the clinic's honor is carried by the student the master once cured. The archive files him as the decade's most consequential apprentice in film: a debt of healing, repaid, as escort duty.

THE COWBOY

Billy

Jeff Wolfe. The thirsty cowboy rescued in the wilderness, who becomes the Chinese community's one local defender and ends the film as the town's mayor. Billy's function is the story's portrait of the ally as the western's true hero-type: his qualification for office is established in the first reel — he is a man who was saved by strangers and spends the film repaying strangers — and the election is the town ratifying a debt. The archive files him as the decade's most consequential cowboy in film: a life debt, converted, into a constituency.

THE BRANCH MANAGER

"Bucktooth" So

Power Chan. Wong's apprentice, proprietor of the San Francisco Po-chi-lam whose opening is the expedition's whole purpose. So's function is the story's portrait of the diaspora's beachhead: the clinic is China planted in American soil — its medicine, its school, its protection — and everything the mayor does to the migrants is aimed, in miniature, at this one shopfront. The archive files him as the decade's most consequential branch manager in film: a franchise, defended, as a homeland.

THE MAYOR

The Mayor

The town's corrupt chief magistrate: legislating against the Chinese to please his voters, indebted past rescue, and finally a bank robber by proxy who frames his own scapegoats. The Mayor's function is the story's portrait of frontier authority as a debt structure: his racism is policy, his policy is theater, and his theater is financed by a robbery — the film's argument that the ordinances and the frame-up are the same act at different scales. The archive files him as the decade's most consequential mayor in film: a government, foreclosed, by its own bandit.

THE BANDIT

The Mexican Bandit

Joseph Sayah plays the bandit leader. The mayor's hired robber, whose discovery that he has been short-paid turns him into the prosecution's star witness at the gallows. The Bandit's function is the story's portrait of the mercenary as accidental auditor: he does not expose the frame-up from conscience but from arithmetic, and the film's justice arrives, as it so often does in this Galaxy, through a criminal's recount of his fee. The archive files him as the decade's most consequential bandit in film: a hanging, adjourned, by a pay dispute.

THE CHIEF'S SON

Fierce Eagle

The young warrior of the tribe that adopts Wong, wounded by the rival leader and avenged by the stranger who cannot remember his own name. Fierce Eagle's function is the story's portrait of the amnesia's evidence: the tribe takes Wong in as a foundling and keeps him as a champion, and the chief's son's injury is the occasion on which the master's body testifies to his identity before his mind can. The archive files him as the decade's most consequential chief's son in film: a wound, answered, by a guest.

THE RIVAL

The Rival Tribe's Leader

The stronger tribe's chief warrior, who wounds Fierce Eagle and is then defeated — with half his men — by the nameless stranger. The Leader's function is the story's portrait of the measuring instrument: his prowess is established at the tribe's expense precisely so that Wong's victory calibrates what the river could not wash away. The archive files him as the decade's most consequential rival in film: a champion, retired, by an amnesiac.

THE INSTITUTION

Po-chi-lam

Wong's medicine clinic and kung fu school, franchised to San Francisco and framed for bank robbery by the town's mayor. Po-chi-lam's function is the story's portrait of the institution as the migrant's portable homeland: it heals, teaches and shelters under one sign, which is why the mayor's campaign targets it — a people with an institution is harder to legislate into misery than a people without one. The archive files it as the decade's most consequential fictional clinic in film: a homeland, incorporated, under one roof.

THE CONDITION

The Amnesia

The head wound that erases Wong's memory of China — and is itself erased when the memory returns. The Amnesia's function is the story's portrait of the doubled forgetting: the film forgets the hero twice, once in each direction, and files its thesis in the symmetry — identity survives the loss of the past, but the price of recovering the past is losing the interval, tribe and all. The archive files it as the decade's most consequential fictional amnesia in film: a cure, billed, in the same currency as the disease.

THE SENTENCE

The Gallows

The hanging prepared for Wong and his companions on the mayor's framed evidence — interrupted by the bandit's return. The Gallows' function is the story's portrait of frontier law as the mayor's stage machinery: the trial is quick, the sentence theatrical, and the whole apparatus stands ready to convert a debt-ridden magistrate's robbery into a community's entertainment. The archive files it as the decade's most consequential fictional gallows in film: an execution, cancelled, by an invoice.

Three laws on one frontier

07 / WORLD & SETTING

World & Setting

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

The American West — the series' final frontier

The film's world is the mythic West of the early 20th century, at the moment the frontier is hardening into municipalities: wilderness and tribes beyond the town limits, ordinances and banks within them, and a gallows at the center where the two orders meet. Into this machinery comes a Chinese master with a clinic — the film's world is arranged as a three-way mirror, in which the continent's oldest peoples, its newest migrants and its self-appointed owners each hold a different theory of who belongs. The Setting's function in the record is the archive's portrait of the wuxia epic auditing the western on the western's own ground, and finding its hero more at home among the continent's first peoples than in its newest town. The archive files this West as the decade's most completely realized fictional frontier in film: a mythology, cross-examined, by its guest.

The river — where the master was filed away

The water that takes the carriage, strikes Wong's head on its rocks, and holds his past for the film's middle act. The River's function is the story's portrait of the border between the two halves of a life: everything before it is China, the school, the name; everything after it, until the cure, is pure conduct — and the film's experiment requires the interval to be exactly as long as a tribal war and exactly as forgotten as a dream. The archive files this river as the decade's most consequential fictional river in film: a memory, held, in escrow by the current.

08 / AMNESIA, THE FRAME-UP & THE FRONTIER LAW

Amnesia, the Frame-Up & the Frontier Law

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

The amnesia — identity as practice

The master's memory is gone; his mastery, ethics and courage are entirely intact. The amnesia's function is the story's portrait of the series' thesis run as a controlled experiment: remove everything Wong Fei-hung knows about being Wong Fei-hung, and observe that nothing essential changes — the man is his training, not his biography. The archive files the amnesia as the decade's most consequential fictional condition in film: a self, demonstrated, by subtraction.

The ordinances — legislation as harassment

The mayor's discriminatory laws, written to make the Chinese migrants' lives unlivable. The ordinances' function is the story's portrait of law's pettiest and most effective weapon: not the gallows but the regulation, the daily friction that prices a community out of a town without ever giving it a battle to win. The archive files the ordinances as the decade's most consequential fictional legislation in film: an expulsion, attempted, by paperwork.

The frame-up — the scapegoat mechanism

The mayor's robbery, billed to Po-chi-lam: a debt, a hired bandit, and a clinic full of convenient foreigners. The frame-up's function is the story's portrait of the mechanism by which corrupt authority converts its own crimes into its minorities' reputations — the town is offered a story in which the people it already resents are the criminals it already believes them to be. The archive files the frame-up as the decade's most consequential fictional frame-up in film: a robbery, attributed, to its witnesses.

The short count — the mercenary's audit

The bandit's discovery that the mayor has paid less than agreed, and his ride back to collect. The audit's function is the story's portrait of the conspiracy's structural weakness: a frame-up staffed by subcontractors is hostage to every subcontractor's arithmetic, and justice, in this film, is what happens when a criminal's invoice is disputed at the foot of a gallows. The archive files the short count as the decade's most consequential fictional audit in film: an execution, stayed, by a recount.

10 / THEMATIC ANALYSIS

Themes & Analysis

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

You are your training, not your biography

The film's governing theme is the amnesia's experiment: a master with no memory is still a master. The theme is worked out through the tribal war until the audience holds its doctrine of identity as practice: the parts of a person that survive the loss of their story are the parts they rehearsed — the skills in the hands, the ethics in the reflexes, the courage in the posture — which means a life is, in the end, the sum of what it practiced when it believed no summary would be taken. Its proposition reaches every person who fears losing their past in the audience. The reach is the story's question to every keeper of a precious biography: if the river took your memory tonight, what would the strangers who found you conclude you were — and is that conclusion the one your remembered self would have chosen?

The cure costs the interval

The film's second theme is the recovery's price: Wong regains China and loses the tribe, in a single transaction of memory. The theme is worked out in the doubled forgetting until the audience holds its doctrine of memory's zero sum: a self can hold only so much allegiance, and the restoration of an old identity is paid for with the interim one — the people who adopted the stranger are forgotten by the master as completely as the master was forgotten by himself. Its proposition reaches every person who has recovered from anything in the audience. The reach is the story's question to every survivor of an interval — an illness, an exile, a lost year: whom did the interim you belong to, and what do you owe the people who knew only that version, now that you cannot remember being him?

Discrimination is a debt instrument

The film's third theme is the mayor's ordinances: anti-Chinese legislation written by a man drowning in debt, culminating in a robbery billed to the discriminated. The theme is worked out from the regulations to the gallows until the audience holds its doctrine of the scapegoat's economics: majorities are taught to resent a minority most efficiently by an authority that needs the resentment — as cover for its thefts, as a market for its protection, as a story to explain its failures — and the ordinances are never the disease but the symptom of a ledger. Its proposition reaches every citizen of a scapegoating season in the audience. The reach is the story's question to every person handed a new regulation against a neighbor: what is your magistrate's balance sheet this quarter — and which of his debts is your resentment currently collateralizing?

The subcontractor's arithmetic saves the innocent

The film's fourth theme is the bandit's recount: justice arrives because a criminal was underpaid. The theme is worked out at the gallows until the audience holds its doctrine of the frame-up's weak joint: conspiracies staffed by hirelings are hostage to every hireling's invoice, and the corrupt, being corrupt in all directions at once, reliably underpay the very people positioned to expose them — which makes the payroll, in any investigation of a frame-up, a document as probative as the evidence bag. Its proposition reaches every investigator of an official story in the audience. The reach is the story's question to every person examining a too-tidy conviction: who was hired to build it, what were they promised, and — before anything else — were they paid in full?

11 / FULL SPOILER CONCLUSION

Ending Explained

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL THE ENDING

The Mexican bandit, discovering the mayor has paid him far less than agreed, returns to town to claim his money — exposing the bank-robbery frame-up in the middle of Wong's execution. In the ensuing fight the mayor is killed; Wong captures the bandit and clears Po-chi-lam's name. Billy is elected the new mayor, and Wong Fei-hung, 13th Aunt and Clubfoot return to China.

What the ending establishes

  • The bandit's return files the frame-up's cause of death: not evidence, not conscience, but a short count — the conspiracy underpaid its own exposure.
  • The mayor's death files corruption's payroll doctrine: the greed that robbed the bank also shorted the robber, and billed the magistrate for both.
  • Wong's capture of the bandit files the master's restored jurisdiction: the amnesiac who was judged by conduct now judges, and clears his school's name before the town that framed it.
  • Billy's election files the canteen's compound interest: a rescued cowboy becomes the migrants' mayor — the frontier's one clean succession.
  • The voyage home files the series' farewell: six films end with the master sailing east, his American chapter forgotten twice — once by the wound, once by the cure.

What remains uncertain

The documented accounts agree on the ending's shape; the tribe that adopted the stranger is left behind inside the forgotten interval, and the film does not say whether any word of the master's gratitude ever reached them. The archive files the uncertainty as the story's own last word on its theme: every recovered identity leaves a set of witnesses behind in the interval, testifying to a person the recovered self can no longer remember being — and the series ends owing them a debt it cannot pay, which may be the most honest ending a six-film saga ever filed.

12 / PURPOSES OF THE STORY

Moral of The Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Once Upon a Time in China and America uses a river, a tribe and a gallows to examine identity as practice, memory's zero sum, and the scapegoat's economics: a master who is himself without his biography; a cure that costs the interval; a mayor whose discriminatory laws are the theater over his debts; and a bandit whose recount of his fee saves the innocent at the scaffold. Through the clinic, the tribal camp and the town square, the film argues that a person is the sum of what they practiced; that restoring an old identity is paid for with the interim one; and that a frame-up staffed by subcontractors is hostage to every subcontractor's arithmetic.

Why tell this story?

Because the series that taught a generation what China was had one border left to cross: the Pacific, and the mythology waiting on its far shore. Sammo Hung's farewell chapter sends the folk hero into the western to be judged by the continent's oldest law and nearly hanged by its newest — and finds him, in both courts, exactly himself. The archive files the result as the saga's coda and this Galaxy's head of the Wong Fei-hung line: Parts III, II and I follow below, in this run's descent toward 1991.

1. Practice the self you would want found

The amnesia is the film's first counsel, for every keeper of a biography: your identity, audited by strangers, is your training. The counsel is a rehearsal doctrine with the tribal war as its standard: the master without a memory intervened, fought and healed exactly as the master with one would have, because decades of practice had moved his character below the reach of forgetting — so build yours at that depth, in the reflexes rather than the résumé, by rehearsing your ethics on small occasions until they no longer require your biography's permission. The river is not scheduled. Practice anyway.

2. Keep a record for the witnesses of your intervals

The doubled forgetting is the film's second counsel, for every survivor of a lost season: the people who knew the interim you cannot be repaid from inside the cure. The counsel is a gratitude doctrine with the tribe as its standard: when you recover — from illness, exile, disgrace, despair — send word back into the interval before it closes: thank the adopters, name what they carried, and leave a record by which the person you were among them is not wholly disowned by the person you resumed being. Wong sails home owing a tribe he cannot remember. Your intervals deserve a better filing than a river: a letter, sent while the cure is still warm.

3. Ask for the magistrate's balance sheet

The ordinances are the film's third counsel, for every citizen handed a scapegoat: resentment is a financial product, and its issuer has debts. The counsel is an audit doctrine with the mayor as its standard: when an authority invests heavily in teaching you to resent a neighbor — new regulations, new dangers, new stories — request the issuer's ledger before buying the issue: what is he stealing while you are resenting, whose robbery is your outrage scheduled to cover, and why does the campaign's intensity track his creditors' patience so precisely? The town's Chinese were framed for a bank job by the man who owed the bank. Check the frame's carpenter for a mortgage.

4. Audit the payroll of any tidy conviction

The short count is the film's fourth counsel, for every examiner of an official story: conspiracies are dissolved by their invoices. The counsel is an investigative doctrine with the gallows as its standard: when a conviction arrives too neatly — the guilty identified, the evidence complete, the public satisfied — subpoena the payroll before the evidence: who was hired, promised, threatened or shorted in the building of this story, because the frame-up's weakest joint is always a subcontractor's arithmetic, and the corrupt reliably cheat the very hands that could expose them. The bandit rode back for his difference and saved three innocent people by accident. Make his ride unnecessary: do the recount yourself, in advance, on paper.

5. Adopt strangers by conduct

The tribe is the film's fifth counsel, for every community judging a newcomer: the fairest court in the film has no records office. The counsel is an admission doctrine with Fierce Eagle's rescue as its standard: judge the stranger by the interval's evidence — what he does at your fire, in your war, at your table — rather than by the biography he arrives with or cannot produce, because every community's Wong Fei-hung problem is the same: the person at the gate may be a master whose papers are at the bottom of a river. The tribe that adopted by conduct gained a champion; the town that judged by category nearly hanged one. Choose your jurisdiction.

6. Let the rescued hold office

Billy's election is the film's sixth counsel, its benediction, for every community choosing its leaders: gratitude, properly compounded, is a qualification. The counsel is a succession doctrine with the canteen as its standard: the cowboy's whole platform was a debt remembered and repaid in public — he had been the stranger at the gate himself, and governed accordingly — and a town that elects its rescued gets magistrates who know, in their bodies, what the ordinances feel like from underneath. When your own community next fills an office, ask the candidates the film's question: when were you thirsty, and who gave you water — and what have you built, since, on the site of that drink?

The moral of the story is…

Practice the self you would want found; ask for the magistrate's balance sheet; audit the payroll of any tidy conviction — and adopt strangers by conduct.

Once Upon a Time in China and America teaches that a person is the sum of what they practiced; that restoring an old identity is paid for with the interim one; and that a frame-up staffed by subcontractors is hostage to every subcontractor's arithmetic.

The film's final question is therefore not whether Wong recovered — the film files that under "ask the river." It is what the strangers who found you tomorrow would conclude you are; which of your magistrates' debts your current resentment is collateralizing; and whether the people who knew you in your intervals have heard, from the recovered you, that they mattered.
