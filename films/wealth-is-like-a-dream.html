01 / FILM IDENTITY

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Wealth Is Like a Dream （富貴浮雲 — Fu Gui Fu Yun, "wealth and glory, floating clouds") is a 1948 Hong Kong Cantonese drama in which Bruce Lee, seven years old, plays a lost boy, with his father Lee Hoi-chuen in the cast. The title is a quotation worn smooth by two thousand years of use: in the Analects, Confucius files wealth and honor gained unrighteously as "floating clouds" to him — the classical world's most famous dismissal of riches, hung over a postwar melodrama in a city then filling with refugees who had watched fortunes evaporate exactly like weather. Of the film's plot, company and staging, no documentation survives in the English-language sources this archive could verify, and none is reconstructed here. What the record keeps is three facts and a distinction. The facts: the year, 1948, the second year of the Hong Kong industry's postwar rebirth; the boy, seven, in the part the University of British Columbia's course filmography names the "lost boy"; and the father, co-starring — the family's films being, at this stage, a father-and-son concern. The distinction: HKMDB's filmography of Lee opens with this film, marking it "[intro]" — his introduction — while the biographies place two earlier appearances before it (an infant's in 1941 and a runaway's in 1946). The archive files the discrepancy without resolving it beyond the record's own terms: to the catalogue that anchors the filmography, the career begins here, in 1948, with a lost boy under a title that calls wealth a cloud. It is difficult, the archive notes, to imagine a more prophetic first entry: the richest self-made legend in martial-arts cinema, introduced to the record by a film that says riches are weather.
