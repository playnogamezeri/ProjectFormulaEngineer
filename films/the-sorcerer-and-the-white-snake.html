01 / FILM IDENTITY

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

The Sorcerer and the White Snake (Chinese: 白蛇傳說之法海 — "Legend of the White Snake: Fahai"; previously titled It's Love and Madame White Snake, and released in some markets as The Emperor and the White Snake) is a 2011 Chinese–Hong Kong action fantasy film directed by Ching Siu-tung, the legendary action choreographer of A Chinese Ghost Story and Swordsman II, and based on the Chinese folk legend of the White Snake. Abbot Fahai (Jet Li) is the age's great demon hunter, sealing the demons he defeats inside Lei Feng Pagoda, assisted by his disciple Neng Ren (Wen Zhang). On the mountain, the snake demons Susu (Eva Huang) and Qingqing (Charlene Choi) spot the herbalist Xu Xian (Raymond Lam); when Qingqing's prank knocks him into the lake, Susu saves him with a kiss that passes her Vital Essence into his body — and falls in love. She takes human form, finds him in the city, and marries him, hiding what she is. Fahai, who has already spared her once because she used her powers to help save others during an epidemic, gives Xu Xian a demon-killing dagger and warns her to leave the human world; when she refuses, the law closes in — through a stabbing Xu Xian deals without knowing whom he strikes, a stolen spirit root, a possession, and the sisters' tidal wave against Jinshan temple — until Fahai must decide what his law is for, with his own disciple turning into a bat demon at his side. The Chinese title names the film for Fahai rather than the lovers, and the film honors the billing: it is the legend retold as the hunter's crisis.

RELEASE

Premiered out of competition at the 68th Venice International Film Festival on September 3, 2011, then released across China and Hong Kong.

Place in the culture

The film lives in the culture as the White Snake legend's blockbuster-effects incarnation — a HK$200 million production, filmed in 3-D, that grossed about US$32 million and divided audiences between its spectacle and its sentiment. For this Galaxy, its place is in Jet Li's gallery of authority figures played from inside their own doubt — the Emperor of Mulan, the sage of League of Gods, and here the monk — and in his partnership with Wen Zhang, whom Li personally invited to play Neng Ren: the second of their three films together, between Ocean Heaven's father and son and Badges of Fury's detectives. Li said of the shoot that he had never been so exhausted: "After fighting the White Snake, fight the Green Snake. After fighting the Green Snake, then fight the Demon… Everyday on set I was letting out a big sigh."

The disciple the star invited

The production's warmest record is a piece of casting: when the roles were announced in September 2010, Wen Zhang's presence as Neng Ren came with the note that Jet Li had personally invited him — the young actor Li had played father to in Ocean Heaven the year before. The invitation matters to the film's design: Fahai and Neng Ren's master-disciple bond is the mechanism by which the law's enforcer is made to feel his law's cost, and it is played by two actors whose real relationship runs the same direction. 03 / NARRATIVE SUMMARY

Story Synopsis

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL STORY SUMMARY

Abbot Fahai and his disciple Neng Ren open the film hunting an ice harpy on a snowy peak; she freezes Neng Ren solid before Fahai captures her with his demon trapper, and she is confined in the magic circle of Lei Feng Pagoda with the other sealed demons. Across the mountain, the snake demons Qingqing and Susu watch the herbalist Xu Xian gathering herbs; Qingqing frightens him into the lake for sport, and Susu, gentler, saves him with a kiss that lets her Vital Essence flow into his body. Hunting a bat demon that has claimed a victim, Fahai and Neng Ren meet Xu Xian, who ferries them by boat; Susu follows her fascination into the city, and she and Xu Xian find each other and fall in love. Neng Ren, helped by Qingqing to find the bat demon's lair, kills its cohorts but is bitten by the bat demon king — and begins, slowly, to turn into a bat demon himself, though he keeps his human tastes and qualities, to Qingqing's fascination.

Xu Xian and Susu marry. Fahai gives the bridegroom a dagger that can kill demons, confronts Susu, defeats her — and spares her, because she has used her powers to help save others, warning her to leave Xu Xian or die. She will not go. Fahai's monks attack the cottage; in the battle, Susu fights in snake form and is stabbed by Xu Xian, who does not know the snake is his wife. Gravely wounded, she escapes. Xu Xian, learning what he has done, steals into Lei Feng Pagoda with the help of Susu's mouse friend and retrieves the spirit root that could heal her — but the root is the seal that keeps the pagoda's demons confined, and in taking it he is possessed by them. Fahai's monks capture Xu Xian and begin the spells to banish the demons from his body. Susu, recovered, comes for her husband with Qingqing; refusing to believe Fahai's warning that the spell must not be broken, the sisters unleash a tidal wave on Jinshan. Susu frees Xu Xian — and finds the broken spell has taken his memory of her.

In the final battle, Fahai is at first defeated, questioning whether his driven demon-hunting has itself been a kind of blindness; then, empowered by his faith, he traps Susu in Lei Feng Pagoda. Susu repents and asks only to see Xu Xian one last time. Fahai lifts the pagoda and frees her briefly; her kiss restores Xu Xian's memory, and then she is drawn back into her prison. In the epilogue, Xu Xian gathers herbs around the pagoda where his wife, in snake form, is sealed — and Fahai walks the mountainside with Neng Ren, now fully a bat demon, tossing him an apple and remarking that he has grown used to his disciple's new look. Master and demon journey on together.

04 / STORY STRUCTURE

Story & Chronology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

The film unfolds from a hunt to a marriage to a flood. The sequence below follows the film's narrative order.

Act I — The kiss and the bite

The ice harpy is sealed; Susu saves Xu Xian with her Vital Essence; Neng Ren is bitten by the bat demon king and begins to change.

Act II — The marriage and the dagger

Susu weds Xu Xian in human form; Fahai spares her once, then arms the husband; Xu Xian unknowingly stabs his wife; the spirit root is stolen and Xu Xian possessed.

Act III — The flood and the pagoda

The sisters drown Jinshan in a tidal wave; the broken spell erases Xu Xian's memory; Fahai, chastened and then resolute, seals Susu in Lei Feng Pagoda — and grants her one last sight of her husband.

10 / THEMATIC ANALYSIS

Themes & Analysis

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

The category and the conduct — a law that files by species

The film's governing theme is Fahai's taxonomy: demons are sealed for what they are, in a world where what they do ranges from the Bat Demon King's predation to Susu's healing of an epidemic. The theme is worked out through the caseload until the audience holds its doctrine of the misfiled defendant: any rule that sorts by category rather than conduct will eventually imprison its Susu and employ its Xu Xian as the executioner — and the system's own keeper will feel the error first, because he is the one who has met the defendants. Its proposition reaches every keeper of a bright-line rule in the audience. The reach is the story's question to every enforcer of a clean taxonomy: which of your sealed files healed an epidemic — and do you know, or does your filing system prevent you from knowing?

The exception in the household — Neng Ren's bite

The film's second theme is the disciple's transformation: the law's crisis arrives not as an argument but as a beloved face changing species. The theme is worked out in Fahai's inability to seal his own assistant until the audience holds its doctrine of the domestic exception: principles are not reformed by counter-arguments but by counter-examples with names — the category fails precisely where the enforcer's love begins, and the honest response is to generalize the mercy, as Fahai finally does at the pagoda, rather than to hoard it as a private exemption. Its proposition reaches every principled person in the audience. The reach is the story's question to every keeper of a strict rule: when your Neng Ren turns, will you amend the law — or merely exempt your household and keep sealing strangers?

The dagger in the husband's hand — enforcement by proxy

The film's third theme is the gift of the dagger: a law that cannot reach its target arms the target's family. The theme is worked out in the cottage battle until the audience holds its doctrine of the deputized intimate: systems that recruit the people closest to the accused — as informants, as enforcers, as the hand on the blade — do not merely punish the accused; they corrupt the love that was the accused's defense, and bill the corruption to the deputized. Its proposition reaches every designer of an enforcement scheme in the audience. The reach is the story's question to every authority that has handed a husband a dagger: when he learns whom he stabbed, whose remorse will you accept as your policy's cost?

The lifted pagoda — mercy as the law's completion

The film's fourth theme is its ending gesture: the keeper opens his own prison, once, for the prisoner's last request. The theme is worked out in Fahai's arc from certainty through defeat to the lifting until the audience holds its doctrine of mercy as maintenance: a law that cannot be opened by its keeper's hand, in the case that indicts it, is not a law but a machine — and the keeper who lifts the pagoda does not weaken the seal; he certifies that a person, not a mechanism, is keeping it. Its proposition reaches every judge, warden and manager in the audience. The reach is the story's question to every keeper of a seal: when your Susu repents and asks one last sight of her life, will your hand be able to lift what it built?

11 / FULL SPOILER CONCLUSION

Ending Explained

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL THE ENDING

After the tidal wave and the broken spell — which leaves Xu Xian with no memory of his wife — Fahai, first defeated and questioning his own driven certainty, rises in his faith and traps Susu in Lei Feng Pagoda. Susu repents and asks to see Xu Xian one last time. Fahai lifts the pagoda and frees her briefly; her kiss restores Xu Xian's memory, and she is drawn back into her prison. In the epilogue, Xu Xian gathers herbs around the pagoda, and Fahai journeys on with Neng Ren — now fully a bat demon, and fully his disciple still.

What the ending establishes

  • The lifted pagoda files mercy's precedent: the law's keeper can open his own seal, and the law survives being opened by hand.
  • The restored memory files love's evidence as admissible at last — granted by the court's own act, one scene too late to change the sentence.
  • Neng Ren's retention files the reform's permanence: the exception is not sealed away but walks beside the master, eating an apple.
  • Xu Xian's herb-gathering files devotion's sentence: the husband's life is re-organized around the prison's perimeter.
  • Qingqing's departure files the verdict's cost beyond the courtroom: the witness generation resigns from love itself.

What remains uncertain

The legend's older tellings promise a distant future release for the White Snake; this film ends at the pagoda, with the seal in place and the mercy spent. Within this film, the archive files the uncertainty as the story's own last word on its theme: the law was amended in its keeper before it was amended in its text — which is how laws usually change, and how slowly.

12 / PURPOSES OF THE STORY

Moral of The Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

The Sorcerer and the White Snake uses China's most durable love legend to examine categorical law, deputized enforcement, and the education of an enforcer: a monk whose taxonomy seals healers and harpies in the same pagoda; a husband armed by the law against his own wife; a disciple whose transformation makes the master's household the law's exception; and a keeper who ends by lifting his own prison for mercy's sake. Through the lake, the cottage and the flooded temple, the film argues that rules sorted by category rather than conduct will imprison their best defendants; that an exception granted only to one's own household is not yet mercy; and that a law is completed, not weakened, when its keeper's hand can open it.

Why tell this story?

Because the White Snake legend is Chinese culture's longest-running trial of exactly this question — the demon who behaves better than the law — and each era restages it with its own Fahai. This era's is Jet Li: an enforcer whose doubt is the spectacle, in a film whose Chinese title honestly names him, not the lovers, as its subject. The archive files the result as the Galaxy's gentlest Jet Li film and its most searching portrait of authority.

1. File by conduct, not by category

Fahai's taxonomy is the film's first counsel, for every keeper of a bright-line rule: a system that seals by species will imprison its healers alongside its harpies, and will not notice, because noticing is what categories exist to prevent. The counsel is an evidence doctrine with the epidemic as its standard: build the review that asks what the defendant did — whom they healed, whom they harmed — into the sentence itself, because a law that cannot hear conduct will be educated by catastrophe instead, at flood scale. Susu saved the town. The file said snake. Fix the file.

2. Generalize your exceptions

Neng Ren is the film's second counsel, for every principled person whose rule has met its beloved exception: the mercy you grant your own household is a draft amendment, not a private arrangement. The counsel is an amendment doctrine with the apple as its standard: when you find you cannot apply your rule to your disciple, the honest sequel is to rewrite the rule for the strangers it still seals — otherwise your principle is a household privilege with a public relations office. Fahai's greatness is that he eventually lifts the pagoda for Susu too. Lift yours for the strangers.

3. Never arm the intimate

The dagger is the film's third counsel, for every authority designing an enforcement: deputizing the accused's family converts love into the law's instrument and bills the remorse to the deputized. The counsel is a proxy doctrine with the cottage as its standard: if your case cannot be made without putting a blade in the husband's hand, the weakness is in your case, not in his grip — enforce in your own name, with your own hands, and let households remain the one jurisdiction your law does not conscript. Xu Xian's memory came back. His innocence about the stabbing never did.

4. Keep a hand on your own seal

The lifted pagoda is the film's fourth counsel, for every judge, warden and keeper of a system: a seal you cannot open is a machine you serve, not a law you keep. The counsel is a mercy doctrine with the lifting as its standard: preserve, in every system you administer, a lawful way for its keeper to grant the exception the case demands — reviewed, recorded, rare — because the alternative is not rigor but brittleness, and brittle laws are not amended but shattered, usually by a flood. Fahai lifted his. Build the hinge into yours.

5. Let the caseload educate the keeper

Fahai's defeat and questioning are the film's fifth counsel, for every enforcer mid-career: he does not learn from a treatise but from his defendants — the healer he sealed, the disciple he could not. The counsel is an education doctrine with the mountainside as its standard: schedule the audit of your own certainty against your files' actual humans, because a keeper who never re-reads his sealed cases will be educated by them anyway, in a single afternoon, at the worst time. Fahai's tuition was a tidal wave. Yours can be a quiet review, if you start it yourself.

6. Journey on with your exceptions

The epilogue is the film's sixth counsel, its benediction, for every master whose household no longer fits his doctrine: Fahai does not retire his bat-demon disciple or his doubts — he walks the mountain with both, tossing an apple. The counsel is a companionship doctrine with the road as its standard: the people your categories failed are not your shame to be hidden but your teachers to be kept close, because a law's keeper is finally judged by who is still walking beside him. The master and the monster, sharing fruit. That is the film's last image of justice.

The moral of the story is…

File by conduct, not by category; generalize your exceptions; never arm the intimate — and keep a hand on your own seal.

The Sorcerer and the White Snake teaches that rules sorted by category will imprison their best defendants; that an exception granted only to one's own household is not yet mercy; and that a law is completed when its keeper's hand can open it.

The film's final question is therefore not whether Susu is sealed — the film files that under "ask the pagoda." It is which of your own sealed files healed an epidemic; whose transformation your household is quietly exempting; and whether the systems you keep have a hinge your hand can lift.
