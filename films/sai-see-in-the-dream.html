01 / FILM IDENTITY

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Sai See in the Dream （夢裡西施 — "Xi Shi in the dream"; also Meng Li Xi Shi) is a 1949 Hong Kong Cantonese film — catalogued across historical, comedy and drama — released May 6, 1949, and running a compact 56 minutes. It is adapted from a Cantonese opera treatment of the Xi Shi material (the source opera is credited to Liao Xia-huai in the catalogue record): Xi Shi — Sai See in Cantonese — being one of the Four Beauties of ancient China, the woman whose loveliness, in the legend, was itself a weapon of state, sent to beguile a king and topple his kingdom. The film's documented frame, preserved in MyDramaList's record from the Chinese catalogue, translates the legend into a contemporary key: a kind but impoverished playwright, living in a shabby house with a blind companion, rescues a mistreated singer named Li Shi — a woman abused and exploited by others, treated as a money-making tool. The singer is the modern Xi Shi: beauty as an asset other people operate. The dream of the title frames the transposition — the ancient beauty appearing in a poor man's present, where the exploitation is not a kingdom's strategy but a show business's bookkeeping. Bruce Lee, eight years old, stands in the cast in a support role, credited under one of his earliest screen names — "Yam Lee" (also rendered in the catalogues with his family-style credit, Siu Hoi-chuen Lee, "Lee Hoi-chuen Junior") — a year before The Kid would rename him for good. No further cast or production credits survive in the English-language sources this archive could verify, and none are reconstructed here. The archive files the picture as the early shelf's dream exhibit: a 56-minute opera-born fable about who owns a beautiful voice — answered, in its documented frame, by the two poorest men in the story, one of whom cannot even see the beauty he helps to free.
