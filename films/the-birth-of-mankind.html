01 / FILM IDENTITY

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

The Birth of Mankind （人類的誕生） is a 1946 Hong Kong Cantonese drama — a social melodrama of the industry's first postwar year — and Bruce Lee's first proper screen role: spotted, per the biographies, while visiting his father's sets, and cast at five (six by some reckonings) as a young runaway. The catalogue synopses (IMDb's and Plex's, in agreement) give the small plot entire: the boy runs away, becomes a pickpocket, causes mischief around town — and is run over by a truck, and dies. It is a remarkable first role by any measure: the future's most indestructible screen body enters the cinema as a child the city kills in traffic, in a cautionary melodrama whose title promises the birth of mankind and whose story delivers one small boy's death as its exhibit. Lee Hoi-chuen co-stars — the first of the father-and-son billings — and the boy is billed not under his own name but as "Little Lee Hoi Chuen": Little Hoi-chuen, his father's name with a diminutive, the dynasty's brand before the boy had one of his own. The cast also holds the filmography's most touching footnote: Chan Ling-chung, "Little Unicorn" — Unicorn Chan — another child actor on the picture, who became Lee's lifelong friend and appears, a quarter-century later, in Fist of Fury and The Way of the Dragon; and Yee Chau-sui, who four years later would play Uncle Ho to the same boy's Kid Cheung. The archive files the picture as the early shelf's nativity exhibit: mankind's birth, a runaway's death, and a five-year-old's first day at the family trade — billed, fittingly, as his father's miniature.
