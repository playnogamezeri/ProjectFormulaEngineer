01 / FILM IDENTITY

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Darling Girl (甜姐兒) is a 1957 Hong Kong Cantonese feature presented by Dafeng, directed by Ng Wui — the same director as The Thunderstorm (in this Galaxy), released the same year — and premiered December 6, 1957. It belongs to Bruce Lee's teenage period: he was seventeen, in his final full year of Hong Kong filmmaking before The Orphan and America, and the Hong Kong Movie DataBase credits his role with unusual specificity for so small a part — "student friend / dancing partner." The credit is a small biographical jewel: Lee was, in life, a champion dancer (he would win the Crown Colony Cha-Cha Championship in 1958), and the teenage filmography is dotted with the evidence that the studios knew it. The documented cast, per the Chinese Movie Database's transcription of the credit sequence, is headed by Cheung Ying (who played Chow Ping opposite Lee's Chow Chung in The Thunderstorm), Wen Lan, Leung Sing-Bo, Wu Hui, Yip Ping, Ding Ying, Lee Pang-fei and Cheung Ying-choi, with Lee listed among the additional cast. Beyond the credits, the accessible English-language record thins quickly: the Galaxy's own directory metadata states the position this archive adopts — "Available English-language descriptions are limited, so this entry records the film's place in his filmography without inventing plot details." What can be said with confidence is the film's place in the shelf: the second of Lee's two 1957 features, the lighter companion to The Thunderstorm's tragedy (some catalogues classify it as comedy; the Galaxy's directory files it as drama and romance), and a standing exhibit in the argument this whole section of the Galaxy makes — that the child actor's career was a full career, with its own genres, stock companies and minor credits, long before the world asked it to be a prologue. This file therefore does double duty: it documents a film, and it documents the documentation — what survives, where it is kept (HKMDB, the Chinese Movie Database, the standard Lee filmographies), and what an honest archive does when the trail ends at a credit line.
