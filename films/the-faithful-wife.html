01 / FILM IDENTITY

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

The Faithful Wife (守得雲開見月明) is a 1955 Hong Kong Cantonese drama written and directed by Chiang Wai-Kwong for the Tai Seng (Dasheng) Film Company, released June 24, 1955, and also known in English as Woman in Sorrow. Its Chinese title is not a name but a proverb: 守得雲開見月明 — "keep faith through the overcast, and you will see the moon when the clouds part" — the classical idiom for endurance rewarded, and the film's whole argument in seven characters: a wife's fidelity, held through a long sorrow (the alternate title's plain statement of the middle), until the sky clears. The cast is headed by Fong Yim-Fun — one of the era's defining Cantonese screen actresses, later an opera-film icon — with Kong Yat-Fan, Bowie Wu (Wu Fung), Tam Sin-Hung, Ma Siu-Ying, the veteran Lee Yuet-Ching (three hundred films across her career, and Sam's mother in The Orphan five years later), Chan Lap-Ban, Fung Ying-Seung and Yeung Yip-Wang; Bruce Lee, fourteen, is credited as Hung-jai (洪仔) in support. No plot synopsis for the film survives in the English-language sources this archive could verify — a fact this file states plainly, in the standing practice of this shelf's honest records (see Crime Killer, Darling Girl) — and the film's director supplies its most eloquent context instead: Chiang Wai-Kwong, who the following year would direct Too Late for Divorce (in this Galaxy), the melodrama of a singer who refuses an open door and takes the veil. The two films form a diptych of the era's female endurance — the wife who waits out the clouds, the widow who declines the clearing — made by one craftsman in consecutive seasons, with the same teenage actor in the margins of both households. The archive files the picture as the 1955 group's quietest exhibit: a proverb for a title, a sorrow for an alternate, and a moon it promises its heroine behind the weather.
