01 / FILM IDENTITY

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Crime Killer is a 1987 American independent action-crime film written, directed by and starring George Pan Andreas, who also edited the picture and performed stunts — a one-man production apparatus of the kind the 1980s home-video market made possible. Its catalogue synopsis, preserved by the databases that list it, is a single pulp paragraph: "Zeus is all business. A no-frills hard-nosed CIA agent under normal circumstances... a furious, unrelenting crime killer when rubbed the wrong way." Andreas plays Zeus; the supporting cast in surviving listings includes Carol Dingman (Shannon), David Dominguez (Willy), Athan Karras (Karras), Peter Malota (Imbrochim), June Wallace Kean (June), Julie Thompson (Emily) and Jack Bliesener, remarkably, as the President of the United States. Its tagline was "The war has just begun." The film's one durable claim on film history — and its reason for standing in this Galaxy — is a single line in its cast list, carried by IMDb and the film databases ever since: Brandon Lee — Gangster, opening scene (uncredited). In 1987 Lee was twenty-two, between his Hong Kong starring debut in Legacy of Rage (1986) and his American television work (Kung Fu: The Next Generation, Ohara), picking up the kind of uncredited day-player work that fills the early filmographies of actors between markets. The appearance is brief, word-of-mouth famous among his completist admirers, and — like the film around it — barely documented: no reliable scene-by-scene record of Crime Killer survives in the standard references, and this archive declines to invent one. What can be said is that the film is a genuine artifact of two histories at once: the micro-budget action industry that supplied the decade's video stores, in which a single determined hyphenate could write, direct, star in, edit and stunt-coordinate his own CIA picture; and the archaeology of a star who died at twenty-eight, whose every frame — down to an uncredited gangster in someone else's opening scene — is now catalogued like scripture. The archive files the film exactly as the Movie Galaxy's own directory describes it: a minor screen credit rather than a starring role, listed separately from Lee's feature performances, and preserved for the record rather than the repertoire.
