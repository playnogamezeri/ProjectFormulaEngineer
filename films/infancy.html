01 / FILM IDENTITY

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Infancy （人之初 — "man at his beginning," the opening words of the Three Character Classic: "People at birth are naturally good") is a 1951 Hong Kong Cantonese drama written and directed by Chun Kim, produced by the Grandview Film Company, and released April 12, 1951. IMDb's synopsis — the film's fullest English document, preserved in this file — files it as "a cautionary tale for parents," and its design is the era's child-rearing debate reduced to a controlled pair: two latchkey boys, classmates under one egalitarian tutor (Ng Cho-fan), reject his strict teachings together — and then fall in opposite directions, each through his own household's characteristic neglect. Rich kid Chuen-tsai (Lee Siu-fan), doted on by his father (Cheung Ying), squanders his time at home reading comic books and role-playing his cowboy hero; bought a brand-new western outfit, he hurts himself imitating the dangerous stunts of his fantasy. Poor kid Ngau-tsai (Bruce Lee, ten — HKMDB credits the role as 牛仔， "cowboy kid," the era's slang folded into a name) is beaten by his mother (Wong Man-lei) after a fracas between the boys, runs away from home, and roams the streets into petty theft to survive — until his luck runs out in the concrete jungle, when his flight from a purse-snatch takes an ominous turn. The pairing is the film's whole argument: wealth's neglect (indulgence, fantasy, an unsupervised room full of comics) and poverty's neglect (the beating, the street) are the same abdication in different budgets, and the tutor's rejected egalitarianism — both boys taught alike, strictly, as equals — is the road neither household takes. It is Chun Kim's first film in this Galaxy's Lee shelf, two years before The Guiding Light and A Mother's Tears made him the era's defining auditor of parents; the archive files Infancy as the laboratory's first experiment — nature pronounced good by the title's classic, nurture indicted by everything after it.

The film opens in the tutor's classroom: two adolescent boys — rich kid Chuen-tsai and poor kid Ngau-tsai — study under a strict, egalitarian tutor, and reject his teachings together; the film's premise is that what happens next is not the tutor's failure but the households'. The boys come to blows in a brief fracas, and the consequences divide by budget. Moral of The Story
