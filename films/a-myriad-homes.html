01 / FILM IDENTITY

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

A Myriad Homes （千萬人家 — "a thousand, ten thousand households"; also rendered A Home of a Million Gold) is a 1953 Hong Kong Cantonese drama directed by Chu Kei, written by Cheng Kang, produced by Ng Cho-fan, and released October 8, 1953, running 126 minutes — the longest of Lee's 1953 pictures. It is a Union Film Enterprises production (with the Wader Motion Picture & Development Company), which places it inside the cooperative's extraordinary 1953: the year of Family, Spring and In the Face of Demolition, when Union's artists — working at half or a third of their usual fees — made the housing-and-family condition of the refugee city into Cantonese cinema's defining subject. The title is the slate's thesis stated as a census: not one home, not the tenement's dozen rooms, but the myriad — the whole city's households as a single subject. The documented cast is the cooperative's ensemble at strength: Ng Cho-fan (who also produces — the cooperative's leading man literally underwriting the myriad), Lo Duen, Tsi Law-lin, Yung Siu-yi and Luolian Zi, with Bruce Lee, twelve, credited in HKMDB's filmography as B仔 — "B Boy," a child's part filed under a letter and a diminutive, the filmography's most anonymous credit of his career. No plot synopsis for the film survives in the English-language sources this archive could verify, and none is reconstructed here; the Galaxy's own directory files it among "the social and family dramas of postwar Cantonese cinema," and its company, its writer (Cheng Kang, who would become one of the Shaw Brothers' defining directors) and its title fix its territory precisely enough: the household, multiplied to the scale of a city, in the year Union Film made that multiplication its mission. The archive files it as the 1953 group's census exhibit: a film whose subject is announced by its arithmetic — and whose smallest credit, B Boy, would out-fame the myriad.
