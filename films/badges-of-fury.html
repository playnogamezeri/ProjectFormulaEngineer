01 / FILM IDENTITY

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Badges of Fury (Chinese: 不二神探， Bù èr shéntàn; also known as The One Detective) is a 2013 Chinese–Hong Kong action comedy directed by Wong Tsz-ming in his directorial debut, with action direction by Corey Yuen. In three days, three men are found dead in Hong Kong, each with a smile fixed on his face — the "Smile Murders." The case falls to Wang Bu'er (Wen Zhang), a young detective whose recklessness is the station's standing joke, and his partner Huang Feihong (Jet Li), a veteran inspector who appears every bit as muddle-headed as Wang — and is, in fact, the unit's concealed kung fu master, surfacing at the crucial moments to pull his partner out of the fire. The victims share one connection: all of them formerly dated Liu Jinshui (Liu Shishi), a budding actress, who becomes the prime suspect before the evidence clears her; suspicion shifts to her elder sister Dai Yiyi (Liu Yan), a onetime darts champion forever stealing Jinshui's boyfriends, and deepens when Jinshui's current fiancé Gao Min (Raymond Lam) proposes and immediately drops dead, smiling. A fortune teller declares Jinshui cursed. Wang's solution is to become the bait himself: he poses as Liu Jinshui's new boyfriend to lure the killer into striking at him. The film was Jet Li and Wen Zhang's third collaboration, after Ocean Heaven and The Sorcerer and the White Snake, and it carries one of the Galaxy's neatest jokes in its casting: Li's detective is named Huang Feihong （黃非紅） — a homonym spoof of Wong Fei-hung （黃飛鴻）, the folk hero Li himself played in the Once Upon a Time in China series.

THE NAME

Huang Feihong, Not Wong Fei-hung

Place in the culture

The film lives in the culture as a throwback: a late, unpretentious revival of the 1980s–90s Hong Kong police comedy, packed with star cameos (Wu Jing, Huang Xiaoming, Tong Dawei and dozens more pass through), received by critics as a disposable lark and by audiences, in China, as a solid summer ticket. For this Galaxy its place is fixed by its place in Jet Li's late career — the master as supporting player and secret weapon in a younger star's comedy — and by its hero's name, which files the whole Wong Fei-hung tradition as a punchline its greatest exponent was happy to tell on himself.

The archive records the film in that spirit: a lark, made by masters, on purpose.

03 / NARRATIVE SUMMARY

Story Synopsis

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL STORY SUMMARY

The film opens on a botched stakeout: the drug trafficker Chen Hu, vanished for seven years, resurfaces at a Scottish-themed party, and detectives Wang Bu'er and Huang Feihong — the latter days from retirement — give chase and lose him, to the fury of their team leader Angela and the police commissioner. Meanwhile Hong Kong is collecting a stranger case: a television star, a dancer and a real-estate manager are found dead in absurd circumstances, each corpse wearing a smile. Wang is the first to call it what it is — a serial murder case, the "Smile Murders" — and the first to find the pattern: every victim once dated Liu Jinshui, a rising actress. The police arrest Jinshui on her own film set and are forced to release her for lack of evidence. Suspicion migrates to her elder sister Dai Yiyi, a former darts champion with a voodoo streak and a lifelong habit of stealing Jinshui's boyfriends — and then Gao Min, Jinshui's latest, proposes to her and drops dead smiling on the spot.

Jinshui, hounded and now half-convinced by a fortune teller that she carries a curse, tries to leave Hong Kong and is stopped at the airport. The investigation's logic has by now cornered itself: the killer strikes at Jinshui's boyfriends, and the only way to bring him into the open is to give him a target. After a brief retreat to Hainan, Wang returns with the plan he has been building toward all along — he will pose as Liu Jinshui's new boyfriend, publicly, and wait for the Smile Murderer to come for him, with Huang Feihong's concealed mastery as the trap's hidden spring. The closer the detectives get to the truth, the accounts agree, the greater the danger the bait is in.

The mispriced veteran — mastery filed under comedy

The film's governing theme is Huang Feihong's filing error: the institution's most capable man is registered as its clown. The theme is worked out in rescue after rescue until the audience holds its doctrine of the mispriced elder: organizations systematically undervalue the veteran whose competence has become quiet, because quiet competence produces no paperwork — and they discover the error only in emergencies, which is to say at the worst exchange rate. Its proposition reaches every employer of aging masters in the audience. 12 / PURPOSES OF THE STORY

Moral of The Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Badges of Fury uses a serial case in a comedy precinct to examine mispriced competence, discounted observation, and courage in costume: a master filed as a fool who insures his partner's risks; a buffoon who reads the pattern first and volunteers as its next victim; an actress imprisoned by a curse she half-believes; and a killer whose authorship is a list. Through the precinct, the film set and the staged engagement, the film argues that institutions misprice their quiet elders and their loud clowns in opposite directions; that supernatural explanations are sold precisely where investigation gets hard; and that the bravest assignment in the building is usually the one that looks ridiculous.

Why tell this story?

Because the buddy-cop comedy is Hong Kong cinema's native form for saying serious things about work — who is valued, who is laughed at, who actually solves the case — and this late entry tells it with the genre's own greatest master playing the undervalued elder. The archive files the result as the Galaxy's lightest Jet Li entry and one of its slyest: a film whose hero's name is a joke about the heroism its star defined.

1. Audit your institution's filing errors

Huang Feihong is the film's first counsel, for every manager: your most capable veteran may be registered as your clown, because quiet mastery generates no paperwork. The counsel is an audit doctrine with the precinct as its standard: before the emergency, list what each of your elders has actually done at the crucial moments — the rescues, the saves, the quiet fixes — and reprice them by that ledger rather than by their meeting-room presence, because the emergency will conduct the audit anyway, at emergency rates. The department learned Huang's value mid-crisis. Learn yours in peacetime.

2. Let the clown present the pattern

Wang Bu'er is the film's second counsel, for every hierarchy: the discounted observer often reads the pattern first, because laughter is a form of attention and the laughed-at are rarely blinded by dignity. The counsel is a hearing doctrine with the case briefing as its standard: when your unit's joke offers a theory, grade the theory before the teller — the pattern he found was sitting in the victims' shared biography, visible to anyone, and invisible to everyone respectable. The corpses were smiling. So, eventually, was the buffoon.

3. Refuse the retail curse

The fortune teller is the film's third counsel, for every frightened person offered a supernatural verdict: a curse is a product whose function is to end your questions. The counsel is an explanation doctrine with Jinshui as its standard: when an account of your troubles requires no evidence and predicts nothing checkable, its price — fatalism — is the tell, because real explanations make the world more actionable, not less. Jinshui's case had a human author with a list. Yours, almost always, has a mechanism. Finish the investigation.

4. Volunteer for the ridiculous assignment

The bait is the film's fourth counsel, for every professional watching a dangerous, embarrassing job go unclaimed: the engagement plan is the story's bravest act precisely because it cannot be performed with dignity. The counsel is a courage doctrine with the staged proposal as its standard: when the work that would actually solve the problem looks silly, the silliness is usually the market price of everyone else's vanity — pay it, and you will find the job was priced in a currency you can afford. Wang got the girl's hand, professionally. He also got the killer's attention. Both were the point.

5. Insure the young; do not replace them

Huang's restraint is the film's fifth counsel, for every senior master working beside a junior's big moment: he does not take the case over — he positions himself to catch its worst second. The counsel is a mentorship doctrine with the trap's spring as its standard: the elder's highest use is insurance, not substitution, because a junior rescued from every risk learns nothing and a junior insured against catastrophe learns everything survivable. The master stayed in the walls. Let your juniors spring their traps with you in the walls, not at the desk.

6. Laugh at your own legend once in a while

The name Huang Feihong is the film's sixth counsel, its benediction, for every master with a famous past: Jet Li plays a one-character-off parody of his own definitive role, and the joke lands because he tells it on himself. The counsel is a legacy doctrine with the spoof name as its standard: a body of work that cannot survive its owner's affectionate mockery was a monument, not a living thing — the masters who endure are the ones who can file their own legend under comedy and keep working. The folk hero's name, respelled, still caught the case.

The moral of the story is…

Audit your institution's filing errors; let the clown present the pattern; refuse the retail curse — and volunteer for the ridiculous assignment.

Badges of Fury teaches that institutions misprice their quiet elders and their loud clowns in opposite directions; that supernatural explanations are sold where investigation gets hard; and that the bravest work usually looks ridiculous.

The film's final question is therefore not who the killer is — the film files that under "ask the trap." It is who your own precinct has filed under comedy who is actually carrying it; which of your discounted people's patterns you have not yet heard out; and what your curse cost you in unfinished investigation.
