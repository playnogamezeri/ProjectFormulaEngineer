01 / FILM IDENTITY

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

The Story of Fan Lei-fa （樊梨花； Fan Lihua) is a 1949 Hong Kong Cantonese opera film in which Bruce Lee, eight years old, appears in what HKMDB's filmography files as a "childhood cameo." Its subject is one of the great heroines of the Chinese stage: Fan Lihua, the legendary Tang-dynasty female general of the Xue Dingshan cycle — the warrior of the Western campaigns, a commander of supernatural skill (in the tales, a disciple of the goddess Lishan Laomu, mistress of magic and the sword) who defeats, marries and repeatedly rescues the hero Xue Dingshan, and stands in the culture as one of the four ancient heroines of the operatic canon. The role is the Cantonese opera's supreme female-warrior part — the dao ma dan, the sword-and-spear heroine — and the material was a repertory staple, filmed again and again; the most famous screen version, the Hong Kong Film Archive's restored gem The Story of Heroine Fan Lei-fa (1968) with Connie Chan Po-chu in her breakthrough warrior role, is a different production from this 1949 film, and this file is careful to keep the two apart. Of the 1949 version itself — its company, its Fan Lihua, its staging — no documentation survives in the English-language sources this archive could verify, and none is reconstructed here. What the record keeps is the cameo and the context: an opera film, in the industry that was the Lee family's own trade, in the same season as Sai See in the Dream — the eight-year-old opera star's son passing through the heroine's camp, in the art form that had employed his father for two decades. The archive files the picture as the early shelf's opera exhibit: the legend of China's great female general, carrying, in its smallest credit, the future of the male action star.

Story Synopsis

THE LEGEND THE FILM TELLS

The film's subject, in its traditional form: Fan Lihua, daughter of a frontier commander and disciple of the goddess Lishan Laomu, comes down from her mountain training with supernatural command of magic and arms to join the Tang campaigns in the west. She meets Xue Dingshan, the young hero of the expedition, in battle — defeats him, and, the tales delight in repeating, marries him across a cycle of captures, rescues, misunderstandings and reconciliations in which she is consistently the stronger general of the two. The heroine's legend is the opera's great argument that the expedition's true commander wears the female warrior's armor. The 1949 film's own staging of the legend is not documented in the English-language sources this archive could verify, and none is reconstructed here.
