Katie

A student and longtime haunting subject — the presence attached to her at eight, survived a house fire with her, and has now followed her into her first adult home — whose tragedy the film states in its first scene: she knows exactly what this is, says so plainly, and is not believed at the speed the thing requires.

01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Katie is the franchise's origin and its expert. The haunting is hers — attached to her since she was eight years old, through a childhood home that later burned — and she understands its rules better than anyone in the film: she hires the psychic, she hears the diagnosis, she forbids the Ouija board, she wants the demonologist called. Every correct decision in the film is hers, and every one of them is outvoted, deferred or undone by the man holding the camera. Her arc is the record of an expert being slowly overruled in her own haunting, until the thing that has waited twenty years for her finally collects.

ROLE

The subject and the expert

Protagonist; the person the demon is attached to, and the only person in the house who treats it as what it is.

DEFINING TRAIT

Clarity without authority

Katie names the danger accurately from the first scene and cannot make the naming stick — the film's horror is administrative before it is supernatural.

FATE

Taken

Theatrical ending: possessed, she kills Micah off-screen, returns bloodied to the doorway, smiles at the camera and lunges; the epilogue states her whereabouts are unknown.

Place in the story

Katie is the film's centre of knowledge and its centre of loss: the haunting's whole history is hers, and the franchise that follows — sisters, covenants, childhood footage — is the long footnoting of what this film leaves in her family's silence.

Featherston plays Katie as the film's adult: funny in the daylight scenes, precise about the danger, and increasingly alone inside her own correctness. The performance's discipline is that she never once performs "haunted heroine" — she performs a woman managing a chronic condition that her partner has decided to treat as content.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Katie drives up to the San Diego house in the film's first footage and states the premise herself: an evil presence has haunted her since childhood, and she suspects it is responsible for the strange happenings in their new home. At her request, psychic Dr. Fredrichs visits; his diagnosis is that she is haunted not by a ghost but by a demon — attached to her, not the house; feeding on negative energy; intent on tormenting her wherever she goes. He advises them not to communicate with it and to contact a demonologist, Dr. Johann Averies.

Micah films the nights. The phenomena escalate on schedule with his provocations: noises, lights, a moving door, then thuds, slamming, growls. Katie wakes in trances — standing beside the bed staring at Micah for two hours, wandering outside to the swing, remembering none of it. She is irate when Micah brings home a Ouija board against Fredrichs's explicit advice; the board later moves on its own and bursts into flame on the patio while they are out.

The haunting turns archival: Micah finds, in the attic, a burned photograph of Katie as a young girl — an artifact of the childhood home that burned, materialised in a house she moved into weeks ago. Katie finally calls the demonologist and learns he is out of the country. Fredrichs returns, senses the house's energy, and flees despite their pleas. On the later nights the entity drags Katie from the bed by an unseen force and bites her; Micah finds her gripping a cross until her palm bleeds, and burns the cross and the photograph.

On Night #21, Katie rises in a trance, stares at Micah for hours, goes downstairs and screams for him. Micah runs to help; his screams follow, then silence, then his body is hurled at the camera. Katie stands in the doorway, blood on her shirt, crawls to his body, sniffs it, looks up with a grin and lunges at the camera with a demonic snarl. The epilogue text states that Micah's body was discovered by police days later and that Katie's whereabouts are unknown. (The film's original ending — Katie returning alone with a knife and being shot by responding police — and its other alternate are recorded in the archive's sources; the theatrical ending above is the release version.)

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The expert in her own case

Katie has lived with this for two decades and it shows: she consults the right specialist, asks the right questions and sets the right prohibitions. The film's tragedy is that expertise without a veto is just narration.

Daylight warmth

The daytime Katie — laughing with Amber, teasing Micah — is the film's control condition: the haunting has not made her fragile, and the archive records her competence precisely so her overruling registers as loss.

Dread, accurately aimed

Her fear is never of the house or the dark in general; it is specific, historical and correct — she knows the thing is patient, and that patience is what the film's twenty-one nights are measuring.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
KNOWLEDGE

Twenty years of case history

She is the only living archive of the haunting's behaviour before 2006 — a record the film keeps in her dialogue and the franchise later excavates.

JUDGMENT

Correct protocol

Psychic consulted, communication refused, demonologist requested: her decisions are the film's control group against Micah's.

ENDURANCE

A childhood survived

She grew up as a demon's chosen subject and built an ordinary adult life on top of it — the film's quietest fact about her.

LIMIT

No authority in her own house

Every one of her correct decisions requires Micah's cooperation to execute, and the film is the record of that cooperation failing.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The burned photograph

The haunting's thesis in one prop: the past is not behind her, it is in the attic of the new house, singed. That Micah burns it — destroying evidence to feel safer — is the couple's whole division of labour in a single act.

The stare

Katie's trance-standing beside the bed, watching Micah sleep for hours, is the film's signature image: the haunting rehearsing its occupancy of her, in the posture of a wife.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Belief and veto

The film's real subject is not whether the demon exists but whose assessment governs the household. Katie is right about everything and empowered about nothing; the archive files her page as the franchise's founding document on that asymmetry — later films give her a covenant, a family and a history, but the asymmetry is already complete here.

The expert's ending

Her possession is staged as the expert's final overruling: the body that carried the knowledge is taken by the thing the knowledge was about. The grin at the camera is aimed at the audience's surrogate — the lens Micah hid behind — and the epilogue's "whereabouts unknown" leaves her where the franchise will spend years looking.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Featherston and the role

Katie Featherston, like Micah Sloat, was a near-unknown cast by Oren Peli for naturalism; the characters carry the actors' own first names, a found-footage convention the film uses to dissolve the line between performer and subject.

The endings

The film was shot with an original ending (Katie, alone and catatonic with a knife, is shot by police) that screened publicly once; after Paramount acquired the film, new endings were developed and the theatrical one — Micah's body at the camera, Katie's lunge — became the release version.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Katie became found-footage horror's defining subject: not a final girl but a case history, whose haunting predates and outlasts the film that documents it. The franchise built six further films substantially out of her file.

Her doorway grin is the series' emblem — the moment the documented becomes the documentarian's executioner.
