01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Chen Honghui is the principal comrade of Mulan (2020): a confident, ambitious recruit in Commander Tung's battalion who becomes "Hua Jun's" rival, sparring partner and closest friend in the camp — and, in the remake's restrained design, Mulan's love interest, built as an earned respect between soldiers rather than a romance with her commander (Honghui carries the second half of the 1998 Li Shang's divided role: the peer, not the superior). His film is the camp's story told at bunk level: the rivalry that becomes a partnership, the practice duel that becomes the battalion's standard, the lake scene where Hua Jun's strangeness baffles him, the avalanche from which Mulan pulls him back, and the return — when the expelled soldier rides in with a warning and a death sentence over her, Honghui is among the comrades who stand up for her before Tung decides. He matters because he is the disguise's closest witness: the person who knew Hua Jun best is the film's test of whether the truth about Mulan changes anything that matters — and his answer, standing, is the battalion's.

ROLE

Supporting — the comrade

The camp's peer-level heart: rival, partner, rescued, advocate — the disguise's nearest neighbor and the truth's first defender.

DEFINING TRAIT

Respect, earned in the open

Honghui's regard for Hua Jun is built entirely of witnessed merit — drills, duels, rescues — which is why it survives the revelation intact: it was never based on the disguise.

FATE

Standing at the return

He closes the war as a proven soldier of the battalion that saved the Emperor — the comrade whose advocacy helped authorize the unit he then served in.

Place in the story

Honghui is the film's ground truth about the camp: where Tung sees the battalion and the audience sees the disguise, Honghui sees the soldier beside him. The story positions him as the revelation's control group — if the truth about Mulan can survive anyone's scrutiny, it is the man who trained against her daily — and his standing at her return is staged as the battalion's verdict preceding its commander's.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Honghui arrives at Tung's camp as the recruits' early standard: confident, ambitious, already the man the others measure against — and "Hua Jun," the slight latecomer from the Hua household, is assigned as his training partner. Their rivalry is the camp's engine: paired drills, a heated practice duel, the daily competition by which the battalion sorts itself — and by which Hua Jun's chi-driven rise is registered, since it is Honghui's standard she first matches and then passes. The film builds the comradeship inside the competition: the two become the unit's closest pair, and the camp's small human scenes are theirs — including the lake, where Honghui, telling his smelly comrade to bathe, follows him to the water and is baffled by Hua Jun's frantic insistence on privacy: the disguise's nearest miss, played as comedy, with the audience alone in on the arithmetic.

The war promotes the comradeship to its working form. Deployed early against the Rouran advance, the battalion breaks under the trebuchets; Mulan's avalanche buries the Rourans — and Honghui, at the snow's edge, is pulled back by Mulan's hand: the rivalry's account settled in the currency that matters. Then the confession and expulsion remove Hua Jun from the battalion entirely, and Honghui's film becomes the waiting of the comrades — until the expelled soldier rides back into camp with a warning (the feint, the Emperor, the execution planned) and a capital charge over her head. The record is specific about the battalion's response: the soldiers she befriended stand up for her — Honghui among them, the closest comrade as the advocacy's natural head — and Tung, weighing the law against the men's testimony and her character, believes her. Honghui serves in the unit she leads to the Imperial City, in the palace fight that saves the Emperor. His film closes in the battalion's victory, the comrade's arc complete: the man who measured himself against Hua Jun has stood, in every sense the film offers, beside Mulan.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Ambition without envy

Honghui begins as the camp's standard and is overtaken by his own partner — and the film's quiet distinction is that the overtaking never curdles: his competitiveness is a measuring instrument, not a grievance, and his respect reprices itself as fast as the drills do.

Loyalty of the witnessed kind

His advocacy at her return is not romance or chivalry but testimony: he stands because he knows the soldier. The film writes him as the battalion's empiricist — his feelings are all evidence-based, which is why the revelation, when it reaches him, revises nothing he actually valued.

The baffled decency

The lake scene is his character in miniature: confronted with a comrade's inexplicable behavior, he withdraws — confused, a little hurt, and entirely without suspicion or cruelty. The film trusts that decency as the foundation the later standing is built on.

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

SKILL

Soldiering at the battalion's standard

He is the recruits' early benchmark and remains among its best: drills, duels and the mountain battle certify him as the unit's reliable second standard — the measure by which Mulan's rise is calibrated.

SKILL

The partnership

Paired with Mulan, the two are the battalion's most effective small unit — training partners whose coordination, built in rivalry, converts directly into battlefield function at the palace.

WEAKNESS

The nearest miss

He is the person most likely to discover the disguise and the one who comes closest (the lake) without seeing it: the film's gentlest irony is that the comrade's respect was itself the blindfold — he never looked for a woman because the soldier was sufficient.

WEAKNESS

A private in a generals' war

His advocacy matters because Tung permits it to: Honghui's ceiling in the film is the battalion's — he can stand, testify and fight, and the decisions remain his commander's. The film is honest about the rank of his heroism.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Narrative function: the revelation's control group

Honghui exists to test the film's central proposition at the closest range: that what Mulan earned as Hua Jun was real. If the nearest comrade's loyalty survives the truth unchanged in kind, the disguise is vindicated as a costume rather than a fraud — and his standing at the return is the experiment's published result, delivered before Tung's verdict so that the institution follows the men.

Investigation: the divided Li Shang

The record establishes that Honghui and Tung together inherit the 1998 captain's role — Honghui taking the peer-and-romance half. The archive's judgment: the division solves the original's chain-of-command romance problem at the price of the love story's altitude; the remake's restraint (the relationship closes as comradeship with a future implied rather than declared) was among the divided reception's most debated choices, praised as discipline and faulted as timidity in roughly equal measure.

Unresolved questions the film leaves about him

His future — with the battalion, and with Mulan, whose commission request hangs unanswered over both — is the film's gentlest open file. The record gives him no family, no home village, no post-war scene: the comrade exits the story as he entered it, in ranks.

Critical reception of the character

Yoson An's Honghui was received as the remake's warmth — the notices credited the camp material's life to the pairing, and the character's redesign (peer rather than commander) was noted as the film's most deliberate modernization, whatever the verdict on its restraint.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Performance

Yoson An plays Chen Honghui, cast as the remake's peer-level love interest. An's register — warmth inside competitiveness — carries the camp sequences, and the production's design relies on his credibility as both the standard Mulan must pass and the witness who must not see.

The character's design

Honghui was designed as the second heir of Li Shang: the comrade half, deliberately demoted from command so the romance could be a peer's. His creation also serves the remake's structural honesty — a battalion needs a face at bunk level, and the disguise needs a nearest neighbor to be in danger from.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Chen Honghui's legacy is the remake's quietest argument: that the love story of a soldier is a comradeship first — measured in drills, rescues and testimony — and that a revelation about the beloved revises nothing that was earned in the open. He stands in the legend's record as the first Mulan love interest who was her equal in rank, and the design's restraint has made him its most discussed modernization.
