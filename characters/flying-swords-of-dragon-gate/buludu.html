01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Buludu leads the Tatar caravan into Dragon Gate with the desert's own agenda: the buried Western Xia city is, to her people, not the court's treasure and not the Bureau's problem but an inheritance sleeping under their sands. The documented accounts name her differently — Wikipedia's cast list gives Chang Xiaowen, the caravan's leader; the Galaxy's film page and Sino-Cinema's review give Buludu (Burudu), the tribal princess — and this file keeps both names in the record rather than choosing between its sources. What the accounts agree on is her function and her ending: she clashes with the Bureau's undercover men, stands with the treasure alliance through the storm, and in the film's final movement joins the disguised Bu Cangzhou at the palace to poison Consort Wan — the outsider's claim, collected at the centre of the empire.

ROLE

The outsider claimant

Leader of the Tatar caravan; the treasure alliance’s third force and the palace plot’s co-author.

DEFINING TRAIT

The inheritance claim

Where the Depots hunt people and the bandits hunt gold, her party hunts its own patrimony in the buried city.

FATE

The palace poisoner

She ends the film allied with the false Yu Huatian, Consort Wan dead by their poison and the eunuchs’ influence dismantled.

Place in the story

Buludu is the film's reminder that the desert was never empty and never the court's: her caravan is the only party at the inn whose claim to the buried city is ancestral rather than acquisitive. The ending — the Tatar princess poisoning the Consort inside the palace — is the film's geography of power reversed.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

The Tatar caravan reaches the Dragon Gate Inn ahead of the storm, and the house's tensions ignite around it first: a conflict breaks out between the caravan and Tan Luzi's party — Bureau agents travelling undercover — ending in mutual poisoning, the inn's overture of violence. Yanqiu, hiding in the tunnels, discovers what the caravan's presence means: the Tatars are partners in Gu Shaotang's treasure alliance, waiting with the inn's people for the storm to uncover the Western Xia vault. When the siege breaks — the passcode fratricide, the open battle, the storm's arrival — the caravan fights as the alliance's own.

In the risen palace the alliance loots beside its enemies under truce, and survives the truce's collapse. Buludu's closing movement is the film's quietest coup: she allies with Bu Cangzhou — still wearing Yu Huatian's face and credentials — and together they reach Consort Wan and poison her, eliminating the purge's author and, with her, the eunuchs' political influence at court. The caravan that arrived as the inn's wild card leaves as the co-author of the empire's correction.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The claimant’s confidence

Interpretation: Buludu negotiates with empires from a caravan — her assurance is a people's, not a person's: the city under the sand was hers before it was anyone's plot point, and she behaves accordingly in every room.

Alliance without submission

She partners with Shaotang's people and later with Cangzhou without ever becoming anyone's subordinate in the record: the film's outsider keeps her own counsel and her own share.

The long revenge, administered coldly

Poison is the film's weapon of patience — hers and Cangzhou's closing choice is the desert's answer to the purge: not a duel but a dosage, delivered at the centre.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The buried city’s true heirs

The Western Xia vault is a dead empire's treasury under a living people's desert. Buludu's claim reframes the film's treasure plot as a restitution the other factions never think to make.

Poison as the periphery’s weapon

The film assigns its two poisons — the caravan's clash and the Consort's death — to its outsiders: the weapon of those who cannot duel empires in the open.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The ending’s quiet radicalism

Score the film's resolutions and Buludu's is the most consequential after Cangzhou's — and it is hers jointly: a Tatar caravan leader helps end the eunuch ascendancy at the Ming court. The archive reads the closing alliance (the outsider princess plus the rented face) as the film's thesis in miniature: the empire is corrected by the people its categories cannot see — the foreigner and the nobody.

The name in the record

The Chang Xiaowen / Buludu split is itself a small lesson in the film's subject: identity in the record depends on which office wrote it down. This file follows the archive's rule — both names kept, the discrepancy stated, neither erased.
