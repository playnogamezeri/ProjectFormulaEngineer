01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Hayes is the rescue party's professional core: Englehorn's first mate, a veteran whose army training and World War I combat experience are precisely why the captain gives him command of the search for Ann. Ashore he is everything Denham is not — methodical, unsentimental, honest about the odds — and his second command is quieter: he is teaching Jimmy, the ship's foundling, to read. Kong kills him in the attack on the log crossing, and the party's subsequent disintegration — the ravine, the losses, the turn toward Denham's capture scheme — is measured from the moment its commander falls.

ROLE

The rescue's commander

Supporting principal; first mate of the Venture and leader of Ann's rescue party, killed by Kong in the jungle.

DEFINING TRAIT

Integrity under command

A soldier's steadiness joined to a teacher's patience: the film gives him both the expedition's hardest job and its gentlest one.

FATE

Killed on the log

Dead — killed by Kong in the attack that shakes the rescue party into the ravine.

Place in the story

Hayes is the film's portrait of duty without illusion: he doubts the voyage, voices the crew's foreboding, and then leads the rescue to the standard anyway. His death is the expedition's hinge — after Hayes, the island sequences stop being a rescue and become a survival, and then a capture.

Parke plays Hayes with a quiet, total authority, and the production's design is deliberate: the cast record notes he leads the rescue because of his army training and combat experience gained during World War I. He is the only man aboard whose authority is earned in both the film's worlds — the ship's hierarchy and the jungle's.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Hayes is introduced as the Venture's first mate and its conscience of experience: when the crew speculates about the voyage's destination and its dangers, Hayes is among the voices — with Lumpy — whose foreboding prompts Englehorn to reconsider the charter. The warrant for Denham's arrest and the grounding overtake the question. When Ann is abducted from the ship, Englehorn organises the search and gives it to Hayes: the soldier leads the landing's second wave into the jungle, with Driscoll, Denham, Baxter, Preston, Herb and Jimmy — the apprentice Hayes could not, in the end, leave behind.

His command survives the stampede that kills Herb and scatters the party among the Brontosaurus and their Venatosaurus hunters, and it holds the remnant together as Baxter and some of the men turn back for the ship. Deeper in, the party crosses a fallen log over a ravine, and there Kong comes for them. Hayes is killed in the attack — Kong's assault shakes the survivors off the log into the ravine below, where the giant insects wait. The party he built finishes the island without him: rescued from the pit by Englehorn and Baxter, its survivors absorbed into Denham's capture, its purpose — Ann — achieved by Driscoll alone.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The soldier-teacher

Interpretation: Hayes's two functions are one character: the man who leads men into danger and the man who teaches a boy his letters are both doing the same work — bringing the unready through. The film's respect for him is total and unadorned.

Honest about the odds

He voices the crew's foreboding before the island and commands without false comfort on it. His integrity is the expedition's scarcest resource, and unlike Denham's charm it does not survive contact with Kong — the film's bleakest exchange rate.

Duty without ownership

Unlike Denham he wants nothing from the island; unlike Driscoll he is not there for love. He is there because it is his watch. The film offers him as the professional ideal Englehorn commands from the ship and Hayes embodies ashore.

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
COMMAND

A combat veteran's leadership

He organises and holds a mixed party of sailors and film crew through a stampede and a jungle pursuit — the expedition's only sustained field command.

EXPERIENCE

World War I

His army training is the documented reason Englehorn gives him the rescue; the island validates the choice even as it kills him.

TEACHING

The literacy of Jimmy

He is educating the ship's foundling — the expedition's one act of pure investment in a future it will not see.

WEAKNESS

The scale of the enemy

No human competence in the film is rated for Kong. Hayes's death is not a failure of his command; it is the island's arithmetic asserting itself.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The Great War's echo

A veteran of 1914–18 leads a party into a jungle killing ground in 1933: interpretation — Hayes is the film's figure for the generation that learned what expeditions cost, commanding one more, for an employer who learned nothing.

The teacher's monument

His literacy lessons outlive him in Jimmy: the film's smallest legacy and its most durable — the one thing taken from Skull Island that was worth bringing home was already aboard.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The hinge death

Hayes's death is the island narrative's structural turn: before it, a disciplined rescue with a commander and a chain of decisions; after it, a rabble in a ravine, a soloist in a lair, and a showman's capture scheme. The film spends its most competent man at the exact midpoint to make the second half leaderless by design.

Representation as respect

The film gives its Black first mate the expedition's integrity, its command and its moral centre without commentary — he is simply the best man aboard, and the record of the film's casting and writing treats that as unremarkable, which the archive records as one of the remake's quiet modernities.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Evan Parke

Parke's Hayes is built on stillness — the production needed a first mate whose authority never has to raise its voice, opposite Black's volubility and Kretschmann's command.

Written for the remake

The Hayes–Jimmy mentorship is the 2005 screenplay's construction, deepening the original film's functional rescue party into the remake's company of distinct men.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Hayes is remembered as the expedition's best man and its most mourned loss — the character through whom the film states that the island's dead were not redshirts but a company, with a commander, a schoolroom and a standard. His death on the log remains the island sequence's emotional hinge.
