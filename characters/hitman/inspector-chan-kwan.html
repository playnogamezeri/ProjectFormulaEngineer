01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Inspector Chan Kwan of the Hong Kong Security Bureau enters the film as the law's warning: he tells Fu and Lo not to pursue the King of Killers case further. He is, as the film presents it and as Fu eventually deduces, the King himself — the mysterious hitman whose assassination of the ex-yakuza boss Tsukamoto opened the story and put the US $100 million revenge-fund bounty on his own head. The documented cast record describes Chan as a capable and determined detective who is actually the dangerous vigilante King of Killers. His double position lets him supervise the manhunt for himself until the penthouse climax, where he arrives openly as the King to help Fu and Lo kill Eiji Tsukamoto and his men. With the fund's terms rewritten and the money divided, Chan retires — and recruits Fu to act as the new King of Killers.

ROLE

The King, under cover of the law

Antagonist-shaped ally; the hunt's quarry, supervisor and final arbiter in one man.

DEFINING TRAIT

Discipline of the double life

He warns his own hunters off, investigates his own crime, and betrays himself only once — a single slip of knowledge at a graduation party.

FATE

Retired, by his own appointment

Chan retires after the penthouse fight and recruits Fu as the new King; he shares in the division of the fund money.

Place in the story

Chan is the film's concealed centre: the title figure, present from the first act in his inspector's cover. His page also carries Uncle Leung's grievance from the King's side — Leung was the client-shaped plaintiff whose wartime loss the King's killing of Tsukamoto answered, via the promissory notes Leung forwarded as payment — and it records the honest limit of the archive: the sources document the King's Tsukamoto killing and his vigilante character, not a full catalogue of his earlier assassinations.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Before the film's present action, the King of Killers assassinated Tsukamoto, the wealthy ex-yakuza crime boss — a killing the old man had anticipated by establishing a revenge fund that now places a US $100 million bounty on the King. In his cover as Inspector Chan of the Hong Kong Security Bureau, Chan warns Fu and Lo, early in their partnership, not to pursue the case further — the quarry personally discouraging two of his hunters.

Chan remains at the hunt's edge while the promissory-note trail develops: Martin's discovery that Tsukamoto was forced to swallow wartime promissory notes, the sale of the codes to Eiji, and the failed rescue of Uncle Leung, whose forwarded payment implicated Lo as the King. Chan knows the implication is false — and at Kiki's graduation party, which Fu and Lo attend before their planned disappearance, he lets that knowledge slip in Fu's hearing: he knows Lo is not really the wanted assassin. Fu deduces the truth from the slip: the inspector is the real King.

In the climax, Fu and Lo stage their penthouse performance — Fu shooting Lo and demanding the bounty from Martin — and Martin reveals Eiji has rewritten the fund so the money flows to Eiji whoever kills the King. A massive fight ensues, and Chan arrives as the King to help Fu and Lo. Together they kill Eiji and all of his men, leaving Martin to rewrite the terms of the fund. Later, Chan is with Fu and Lo as they divide the money and discuss their future plans. Chan retires, and recruits Fu to act as the new King of Killers.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Patience as camouflage

Interpretation: Chan's cover is not a disguise but a temperament — he genuinely works the case, warns the hunters, attends the party. The double life holds because the inspector half is performed without strain; the film's one slip is a fact, not a mannerism, escaping.

The vigilante's restraint

Interpretation: the documented record frames the King as a vigilante and Chan as dangerous in that alter ego, while the plot shows him spending the film not killing — warning, watching, waiting — until the penthouse, where his violence is spent defending the two men hunting him. Read as interpretation: the King's discipline is the trait he is really recruiting for in Fu.

The retiring king

Interpretation: Chan treats an assassin's legend as an office with a succession plan. His retirement is the film's most bureaucratic gesture and its most revealing: the King was always, in his own mind, a post — one a Security Bureau man could hold, and hand over.

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

ASSASSINATION

The King's craft

He killed Tsukamoto — a guarded ex-yakuza boss — cleanly enough to leave a hundred-million-dollar mystery and no usable trail except the notes forced down the victim's throat.

COVER

Investigating himself

As inspector he warns off hunters, moves inside the case's official machinery, and is suspected by no one until Fu's deduction.

COMBAT

The penthouse arrival

Arriving openly as the King, he helps Fu and Lo kill Eiji and all of his men — the film's confirmation that the legend's skills are real.

WEAKNESS

Knowledge he should not have

His single documented slip — knowing Lo is innocent — is the thread Fu pulls. The perfect cover fails on a fact, not a fight.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The king inside the law

Interpretation: Chan embodies the film's central irony — the state's inspector and the underworld's legend are one man, and neither institution can see the other half. The bounty economy and the police procedural chase the same ghost through the same corridors.

Retirement as the rarest exit

Interpretation: hitmen in this genre die; Chan files paperwork, divides money and appoints a successor. Read as interpretation, his exit is the film's quiet argument that the King was an office of judgment all along — which is why it can be inherited rather than avenged.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the withheld centre

The plot is a search for a man who appears in its first act wearing a badge. Structurally, Chan converts a whodunit into a study of attention: every hunter reads documents — notes, terms, transfers — while the answer stands in the room using his own name. Fu's deduction is the film's thesis in miniature: the innocent reads people; the professionals read paper.

Honest-record note

The sources consulted document the King's identity, the Tsukamoto assassination, and the vigilante character of the alter ego. They do not document a fuller biography, a motive statement, or earlier contracts in detail; this page states those gaps rather than filling them, in line with the archive's fact discipline.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Simon Yam

Yam plays Chan; the documented cast record describes the role in one breath as capable detective and dangerous vigilante alter ego — the film's double part, cast in a single performance of restraint.

The title's referent

The film's Chinese title translates as King of Killers; the documented plot makes Chan, not its star Jet Li, the King for most of the running time — Li's Fu inherits the title only at the end.

Legacy & Significance
