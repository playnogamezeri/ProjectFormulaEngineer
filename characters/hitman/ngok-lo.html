01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Ngok Lo is seeking the US $100 million bounty on the King of Killers when he watches Fu fight the security staff at the revenge-fund lawyers' building, and hires the ex-soldier as his muscle on the spot. Lo outfits Fu, lets him live in his home, and tests him with a small contract — the full service of a self-appointed agent. But Lo is carrying the film's most dangerous secret, and it is not that he is the King: it is that, years earlier, out of pity for the veteran Uncle Leung, he arranged the payment channel through which the King's fee for killing Tsukamoto flowed into Lo's own bank account. The paper trail makes him look exactly like the man a hundred million dollars is hunting. His confession to Fu, the attack on his apartment, the staged shooting in Eiji's penthouse — in which Fu appears to kill him to claim the bounty — and the final division of the fund money complete the arc of a liar saved by the one truth he told the right person.

ROLE

The agent and false suspect

Fu's employer and host; the bounty hunt's middleman, and its most plausible innocent suspect.

DEFINING TRAIT

The self-described rat with one good deed

The documented cast record calls him a self-proclaimed "rat bastard" who makes the rent by any means necessary, a notorious liar and cheater — whose pity for Leung is the plot's hinge.

FATE

Alive, and paid

Lo survives the staged shooting and the penthouse fight, and is last seen dividing the fund money with Fu and Chan.

Place in the story

Lo is the film's engine of both comedy and jeopardy: he starts the partnership, and his backstory supplies the mystery's false solution. His page also carries Uncle Leung from Lo's side — Leung was Lo's acquaintance, the man Lo had been trying to locate, and the beneficiary of Lo's one documented act of pity — and Martin, the fund's lawyer, whose sale of the promissory-note codes sets Lo's danger in motion.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Lo is present at the orbit of the revenge-fund lawyers' meeting when Fu is thrown out and fights the security staff; impressed, Lo hires Fu as his muscle for his own attempt on the bounty. Inspector Chan warns them both off the case. Lo then runs Fu's induction: he takes on a small contract to kill a local gang member as a test of Fu's abilities, outfits him, and allows him to live in his home — where Fu meets Lo's daughter Kiki, the successful lawyer exasperated by her father's misdoings.

When Martin, the head lawyer of the revenge fund, discovers that Tsukamoto was forced to swallow old Chinese wartime promissory notes before his death and sells the serial codes to Eiji, Lo and Fu follow Eiji to the apartment complex — and find the suspect is Uncle Leung, an acquaintance Lo had previously been searching for. The rescue fails: despite Fu and Lo fighting off yakuza hitmen and a grenade-wielding assassin disguised as a priest, Leung dies of a heart attack during the escape and is shot in the head by Eiji.

Back at his apartment, Lo comes clean with Fu. Years ago he had met Leung and learned the old man was a veteran whose entire family was killed by the Japanese during the Second Sino-Japanese War; Leung wanted vengeance on his family's murderers, and his only wealth was the promissory notes. Taking pity, Lo told Leung to hold onto the notes and, in the event of the murder of the Japanese man he wanted dead, cash them in and forward the money to Lo's bank account. Lo had only recently learned that Tsukamoto was the man Leung wanted killed; once the King assassinated the crime lord, Leung forwarded the money as arranged — creating the paper trail that implicated Lo as the King.

Two bounty hunters attack Lo's apartment; Fu and Lo barely escape, and Lo plans to disappear to mainland China. Before leaving they attend Kiki's graduation party, where Fu deduces Chan is the real King. Then comes the penthouse: Fu and Lo go to meet Martin at Eiji's penthouse, where Fu shoots Lo and demands to cash in the bounty — the staged performance, played on a man both of them know is innocent, to force the fund's hand. Martin reveals Eiji has rewritten the terms so the money flows to Eiji whoever does the killing; the fight follows, Chan arrives as the King, Eiji and his men are killed, and Martin is left to rewrite the terms again. Lo is last seen with Fu and Chan, dividing the money and discussing future plans.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The professional liar

Interpretation: Lo's lying is documented as notorous and habitual — a business method. What makes the character work is that the film gives his dishonesty a single, load-bearing exception: the confession to Fu is complete, self-incriminating and unprompted by proof, and it is the act that saves his life.

Pity, administered badly

Interpretation: Lo's arrangement for Leung was kind and catastrophically designed — a payment channel from a murder victim's swallowed notes straight into Lo's account. Read as interpretation: Lo helps people the way he does everything, improvisationally, and the underworld reads improvisation as conspiracy.

The father

Interpretation: Kiki's exasperation is the record Lo cannot talk his way out of. His daughter the lawyer is the standing evidence of the life his trade embarrasses, and the graduation party — the legitimate world's ceremony — is where he chooses to spend his last evening before disappearing.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The paper trail as false scripture

Interpretation: everyone in the film reads documents — fund terms, note serials, bank transfers — and every document lies by omission. Lo is the human form of that theme: a man whose records describe an assassin and whose record, in full, describes a soft touch.

The agent's commission

Interpretation: Lo begins the film as the trade's middleman, taking positions in other men's violence. He ends it as a principal — shot at, fought beside, paid in full — the agent absorbed into the story he was only supposed to broker.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the false solution

Lo is the mystery's decoy answer, and the film constructs him carefully: motive-shaped finances, underworld fluency, a connection to the victim's grievance. The analysis the archive records is that the decoy works because Lo's world runs on surfaces — and that his confession scene is placed exactly where a guilty man's would be, so the audience must judge the same evidence the bounty hunters do.

The heart of the odd couple

Structurally, Lo supplies everything Fu lacks — contacts, clothes, patter, cynicism — while Fu supplies everything Lo lacks, principally a right arm and a clean conscience. Their partnership is the film's thesis about the trade: the agent and the weapon, each incomplete, dividing one whole survivor between them.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Lo's place in the Galaxy's records is the middleman redeemed by bookkeeping: the agent whose accounts finally balanced because he told the truth once, to the right apprentice. His page carries the full promissory-note mechanism — the film's central device — from the side of the man it nearly killed.
