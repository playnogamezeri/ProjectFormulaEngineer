01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Fu is a former soldier in a small Hong Kong gang when the King of Killers assassinates the ex-yakuza boss Tsukamoto and Tsukamoto's revenge fund puts US $100 million on the King's head. Fu tries to force his way into the lawyers' meeting about the fund, is thrown out by security, and — defending himself with conspicuous martial-arts skill — is spotted by the small-time criminal Ngok Lo, who hires him as muscle. What follows is an apprenticeship Fu never quite completes: Lo outfits him, houses him, and tests him with a small contract, while Fu's decency keeps interrupting the trade he is supposedly learning. By the ending, Fu has deduced the King's identity himself, staged a shooting of Lo in Eiji Tsukamoto's penthouse, fought beside the real King, and been recruited by the retiring Inspector Chan to act as the new King of Killers.

ROLE

The apprentice who inherits

Protagonist; the bounty hunt's least corrupt hunter, and its final beneficiary.

DEFINING TRAIT

Skill without cynicism

Documented as an extremely skilled melee fighter but a naive, inexperienced hitman in training — the gap between those two facts is the film.

FATE

The new King

Chan retires and recruits Fu to act as the new King of Killers; Fu is last seen dividing the fund money with Lo and Chan and discussing future plans.

Place in the story

Fu is the story's centre and its test case. Every other hunter is defined by what the bounty does to them; Fu is defined by what it fails to do. His pages in this archive also carry two figures who receive no page of their own: Uncle Leung, whose rescue Fu attempts and whose death mid-escape — a heart attack, followed by Eiji shooting the body in the head — Fu witnesses at close quarters; and the grenade-wielding assassin disguised as a priest, whom Fu fights during that rescue attempt in the apartment complex.

The documented record describes Fu in a deliberate contradiction: an extremely skilled melee fighter who is also naive and inexperienced as a hitman. The archive keeps both halves. Fu's fighting is never the problem the plot has to solve; his unsuitability for the moral economy of contract killing is — and the ending resolves that unsuitability not by curing it but by redefining the job around it, since the King he succeeds turns out to have been a vigilante whose victims were criminals.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Fu is introduced as a former soldier in a small gang that learns of the US $100 million bounty placed on the King of Killers after the assassination of Tsukamoto, the wealthy ex-yakuza crime boss who had established a revenge fund against exactly that eventuality. Fu attempts to enter the building where Tsukamoto's lawyers are discussing the fund's terms and is rebuffed by security; when he defends himself, his martial arts attract the attention of Ngok Lo, a seedy small-time criminal who is also seeking the bounty and hires Fu as his muscle. The pair are warned off the case by Inspector Chan of the Hong Kong Security Bureau.

Lo tests Fu's abilities by taking on a small contract to kill a local gang member, while also outfitting Fu and letting him live in his home. There Fu meets Kiki, Lo's daughter, a successful attorney ashamed of her father's activities. The hunt then turns on evidence: Martin, the head lawyer administering the revenge fund, discovers that before his death Tsukamoto was forced to swallow old Chinese wartime promissory notes, and sells the notes' serial codes to the highest bidder — Eiji Tsukamoto, the dead man's grandson and new head of the family. Fu and Lo follow Eiji to a small apartment complex and find the suspect is Uncle Leung, an old man and an acquaintance Lo had previously been trying to locate.

The rescue of Leung is the film's central failure. Fighting off multiple yakuza hitmen and a grenade-wielding assassin disguised as a priest, Fu and Lo cannot extract him: Leung suffers a heart attack and dies during the escape, and Eiji then shoots the dead man in the head. Back at Lo's apartment, Lo comes clean about the paper trail that implicates him as the King — years earlier he had pitied Leung, a veteran whose whole family was killed by the Japanese during the Second Sino-Japanese War, and had arranged for Leung to cash in the promissory notes and forward the money to Lo's account once the man Leung wanted dead was killed. After an attack on the apartment by two bounty hunters, Fu and Lo plan to disappear to mainland China, pausing to attend Kiki's graduation party — where Fu meets Inspector Chan again and deduces, from Chan's slip that he knows Lo is not really the wanted assassin, that Chan himself is the real King.

Fu and Lo then go to Eiji's penthouse to meet Martin, where Fu shoots Lo and demands to cash in on the bounty — a performance, staged for Martin and Eiji's apparatus, since Lo is not the King and Fu knows it. Martin informs them that Eiji has changed the terms of the fund so that whoever kills the King, Eiji receives the money. A massive fight ensues; Chan arrives as the King to help Fu and Lo; Eiji and all his men are killed; and Martin is left to rewrite the terms of the fund. In the closing movement, Fu, Lo and Chan are seen together dividing up the money and discussing their future plans. Chan retires, and recruits Fu to act as the new King of Killers.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The soldier out of his element

Interpretation: Fu's competence is physical and his innocence is procedural — he knows how to fight and not how the underworld prices, lies and files paperwork. The comedy and the tension both live in that mismatch: he keeps treating a bounty hunt as if it were a mission with an objective, while everyone around him treats it as a market.

Decency as a professional defect — and qualification

Interpretation: in the trade Fu is entering, hesitation about killing is a defect. The film's reversal is that the one killing office worth inheriting — the King's — turns out to have been run on exactly Fu's instincts: its documented victims were criminals. What disqualifies him from the market qualifies him for the crown.

Loyalty learned fast

Interpretation: Fu's bond with Lo begins as an employment contract and ends with a staged shooting that only works because the trust underneath it is real. His arc with Kiki and with Chan follows the same pattern: Fu commits first and understands the arrangement afterwards.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
MARTIAL ARTS

Exceptional melee skill

His fighting is what gets him hired: security staff at the lawyers' building cannot handle him, and Lo recruits the skill on sight. It carries him through the apartment-complex rescue attempt and the penthouse fight.

DEDUCTION

The man who spots the King

Fu alone deduces Chan's identity, from a single slip at Kiki's graduation party — that Chan knows Lo is not the wanted assassin. The film gives its best detective beat to its supposed innocent.

PERFORMANCE

The staged shooting

In the penthouse Fu shoots Lo and demands the bounty, playing the mercenary convincingly enough to draw out Martin's revelation about the fund's rewritten terms.

WEAKNESS

Inexperience in the trade

Documented as naive and inexperienced as a hitman in training: the small contract Lo uses to test him, the outfit, the lodging and the coaching all mark how much of the profession Fu does not yet know.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The crown nobody honest wanted

Interpretation: the King of Killers is the film's central symbol of a reputation detached from its man — a bounty magnet, a paper trail, a title that can be falsely pinned on Lo and finally transferred to Fu. That the title passes by recruitment rather than by killing its holder is the film's verdict on the bounty economy built around it.

The mainland innocent in the Hong Kong market

Interpretation: Fu the ex-soldier enters a city where everything — information, loyalty, the fund itself — is for sale, and his refusal to become fluent in that language is what the story rewards. Read as interpretation, not as a documented statement of intent.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the apprentice as audit

Fu's naivety is an auditing device: because he understands nothing of the trade, every part of it — agents, tests, information sales, fund terms — has to be explained in his hearing, and the audience receives the underworld's operating manual through his induction. The analysis here is structural: remove Fu's innocence and the film loses both its exposition engine and its moral baseline.

The succession problem

The ending solves a vigilante's succession the way institutions do: the incumbent retires and appoints. That Chan recruits Fu — the hunter who deduced him rather than the one who nearly killed for the bounty — frames the King's office, as this film presents it, as a trust to be handed to the man whose instincts match its undocumented charter: its victims were criminals.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Jet Li

Li plays Fu; the documented cast record describes the role as the film's main character — an extremely skilled melee fighter and naive hitman in training who eventually becomes the new King. The film was directed by Stephen Tung and released in Hong Kong on 3 April 1998.

The American edition

In 2002 an edited version with a hip-hop soundtrack was released on DVD in the United States as Contract Killer; reviewer Scott Hill of PopMatters called that edit a diluted reissue and advised watching the original. The archive's Fu is the 1998 film's Fu, not the re-edit's reframing.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Fu's significance in the Galaxy's records is the succession: he is the rare action protagonist who ends his film by being appointed to the legend he spent it hunting.
