01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

When the King of Killers assassinates the ex-yakuza boss Tsukamoto, the dead man's power-hungry grandson Eiji becomes the new head of the Tsukamoto family — and one of the bounty hunters after the US $100 million revenge fund. Eiji hunts with the family's resources: when Martin, the fund's head lawyer, discovers that Tsukamoto was forced to swallow old Chinese wartime promissory notes and sells the serial codes, Eiji pays the highest amount and learns them first. The trail leads him to Uncle Leung's apartment complex, where he arrives over Fu and Lo's failed rescue to shoot the already-dead Leung in the head. His masterstroke is legal, not martial: he changes the terms of the fund so that no matter who kills the King of Killers, Eiji receives the money. The rigging is exposed in his own penthouse, in a massive fight in which Inspector Chan arrives as the King; Eiji and all of his men are killed, and Martin is left to rewrite the terms once more.

ROLE

The heir as saboteur

Principal antagonist; head of the Tsukamoto family and the fund's secret re-drafter.

DEFINING TRAIT

Inheritance as architecture

Documented as ruthless, focused and power-hungry — his signature act is not a killing but a clause: whoever wins, he is paid.

FATE

Killed in his own penthouse

Eiji and all of his men are killed in the penthouse fight by Fu, Lo and Chan arriving as the King.

Place in the story

Eiji is the bounty's purest product: the hunter who understands the fund better than the fighting. His page also records his grandfather Tsukamoto from the heir's side — the dead boss whose foresight built the fund Eiji corrupts — and notes that Tsukamoto receives no standalone page in this run: he is assassinated before the film's present action and never appears alive in the documented plot, an inciting corpse on the Ben Hildebrand precedent, covered inside Eiji's, Fu's and Chan's pages.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Eiji is established after his grandfather's assassination: the deceased's power-hungry grandson becomes the new head of the Tsukamoto family and one of the bounty hunters seeking the King of Killers under the US $100 million revenge fund.

His first documented advantage is purchased. Martin, the head lawyer in charge of the fund, discovers that before his death Tsukamoto was forced to swallow old Chinese wartime promissory notes, and sells the information about the notes' serial codes to the bounty hunters; Eiji, willing to pay the highest amount, is the first to learn the codes. Fu and Lo follow Eiji to the small apartment complex where the suspect, Uncle Leung, is living.

At the complex, Fu and Lo fight off multiple yakuza hitmen and a grenade-wielding assassin disguised as a priest but cannot extract Leung, who suffers a heart attack and dies during the escape. Eiji's documented contribution to the scene is its bleakest beat: he shoots the dead Leung in the head. The plaintiff whose grievance started the whole machinery is disposed of by the heir whose fund claimed to serve it.

Eiji's decisive act happens off the battlefield: he changes the terms of the revenge fund so that no matter who kills the King of Killers, he will be the one who receives the money. When Fu and Lo come to his penthouse to meet Martin — Fu shooting Lo and demanding to cash in on the bounty — Martin informs them of Eiji's rewritten terms. A massive fight ensues in the penthouse; Inspector Chan arrives as the King to help Fu and Lo; and they kill Eiji and all of his men, leaving Martin to rewrite the terms of the fund.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Contempt as policy

Interpretation: shooting a corpse is not strategy; it is a signature. Read as interpretation, Eiji's treatment of Leung's body announces how he values the grievance his fund claims to honour — as an asset, worthless once spent.

The house always wins

Interpretation: Eiji's rewritten clause is the character in one sentence: he does not need to find the King, outfight the King, or even survive the King — he needs only to own the paperwork through which everyone else's violence is paid. His ambition is architectural, and the film answers it architecturally, with a lawyer rewriting the terms over his body.

Focus without loyalty

Interpretation: the record calls him ruthless and focused; the plot shows the focus aimed entirely at the fund, never at the grandfather whose death the fund exists to avenge.

He outbids every other hunter for Martin's promissory-note information and reaches Leung first.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The will, redrafted by its beneficiary

Interpretation: Tsukamoto's fund was foresight — a dead man's attempt to govern the future. Eiji's clause is the same instrument inverted: the future governed for the governor. The film's final rewrite, performed by Martin over Eiji's defeat, restores the document to the survivors — paperwork as the story's true battlefield.

The corpse shot twice by history

Interpretation: Leung dies of his heart and is shot by Eiji; Tsukamoto dies by the King and is governed by Eiji. Read as interpretation, Eiji's arc is a war on the dead — the two old men whose grievances and fortunes he treats as raw material.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the antagonist as administrator

Eiji rarely needs to be the best fighter in a film full of them; his menace is institutional. The analysis the archive records: Hitman splits its villainy between spectacle (hitmen, grenades, disguises) and structure (terms, codes, accounts), and Eiji owns the structural half — which is why his defeat requires both a fight and a redrafting.

The heir versus the apprentice

Structurally, Eiji and Fu are the film's paired successors: one inherits a family and a fund by blood and corrupts both; the other is handed a crown by appointment, for the instincts the first lacks. The penthouse fight is the two successions settling which principle governs the story's world.
