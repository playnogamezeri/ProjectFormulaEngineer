01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Todd holds the smallest account in the rumor economy and the only clean one. In eighth grade (by the record, at eight years old in the closet of a birthday party), he and Olive were locked in a room to kiss; he was not ready, asked her to lie that they had, and she did. Years later, when the school convicts Olive on a reputation built of identical favours, Todd is the one person who discounts the file — because he is living proof of what her lies are actually for. His return at the film's end, on a lawnmower, in quotation of the teen films Olive loves, is the past arriving to testify.

ROLE

Love interest; character witness

The romance is almost an afterthought; his narrative office is testimony — he is the evidence that Olive's lying has always been a form of protection.

DEFINING TRAIT

The kept receipt

He remembers being lied for, and what it cost her, and he has organised his opinion of her around the debt ever since.

FATE

The deferred kiss, collected

He arrives beneath her window as the webcast ends; they kiss, and ride off on the lawnmower.

Place in the story

Todd is the film's control experiment: give one person complete knowledge of an early Olive lie, and watch him become immune to all later ones. Everyone else in the school knows Olive's reputation; Todd alone knows her record.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Todd's biography in the film is mostly prologue and verdict. The prologue: a childhood party, a locked room, an expected kiss, a boy not ready and a girl who covers for him without being asked twice — the founding favour of Olive's career, years before the career. In the film's present he surfaces at the edges of the rumor economy as the Woodchuck mascot and as Olive's old crush, conspicuously not among the customers for her fictitious exploits and not among Marianne's congregation either.

His decisive scene is a confession to match hers: he tells Olive he does not believe a word of the school's file, and produces his evidence — her lie for him, kept all these years. It is the first time anyone in the film evaluates Olive by her history rather than her headlines, and it arms her endgame: if her lies were protection, the truth can be, too. When her webcast signs off with a tease about Todd and her virginity, he answers in kind — arriving under her window on a lawnmower, in the grand-gesture grammar of the 1980s films she reveres. They kiss, and ride off together.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Loyalty as memory

Interpretation: Todd's constancy is not passion but bookkeeping — he has simply never updated the entry. The film presents this as the rarest form of intelligence available in a high school: remembering what a person did before it was expensive.

The unembarrassed romantic

He performs the ending's gesture at full sincerity and miniature scale — a lawnmower, not a sports car — and the film frames the smallness as the sincerity's proof.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The mascot costume

Todd is professionally hidden inside the Woodchuck suit while Olive is professionally exposed by hers — the scarlet A. Interpretation: the film pairs two costumes, one that conceals a person and one that advertises a fiction, and gives the romance to the pair who can see past both.

The lawnmower

The teen-film grand gesture deliberately underpowered: sincerity measured by willingness to look ridiculous, not by budget.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Why the film needs him

Without Todd, Olive's webcast is a defendant's uncorroborated statement. Todd is the corroboration prepared in advance: the film plants his memory in the prologue so that, by the finale, at least one verdict besides Olive's own has acquitted her. His function is evidentiary before it is romantic.

The gender mirror

The founding favour reverses the film's central transaction: at eight, Olive spends reputation to protect a boy's unreadiness; at seventeen, boys spend gift cards to purchase her readiness. Todd is the only male character whose debt to her runs the other way.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Casting

Penn Badgley, then best known for Gossip Girl, plays Todd; the cited ScreenRant guide lists him second in the film's cast and identifies Todd as Olive's main love interest and one of the only people who does not believe the rumors.

The homage ending

Todd's arrival is staged as a deliberate quotation of the grand gestures of 1980s teen cinema — the genre Olive name-checks throughout — miniaturised to a ride-on lawnmower, a joke the film plays as sincerity rather than parody.
