01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Adam is the expedition's organiser and its most obedient patient. Dumped by his latest girlfriend in a 2010 that has emptied his house, he stages the Kodiak trip as Lou's intervention and is assigned, in 1986, the founding act of his own romantic damage: break up with Jenny, publicly, and take a fork in the eye for it. The forty-year-old inside the teenager cannot do it — the cruelty that was once reflex is now unperformable — and Jenny, impatient, performs the breakup herself, fork included. Freed from his script by its failure, Adam spends the night with April, a music journalist passing through Winterfest, talking until morning: the first woman in his life who is not a manoeuvre. In Lou's edited 2010, she is his wife.

ROLE

Protagonist; the organiser

The trip is his plan and the doctrine is his instinct: Adam is the friend who believes the rules exist to be followed, in travel and in love.

DEFINING TRAIT

The failed re-enactment

His growth is measured by an inability: he can no longer perform the cruelty his younger self invented, and the failure is the film's kindest verdict on him.

FATE

Married to April

In the new timeline he wakes to the life the failed fork bought him: the woman from the one unscripted night, kept.

Place in the story

Adam is the film's control subject for its experiment: give a man his worst evening back and see whether he repeats it. Lou edits; Nick asserts; Adam simply declines to be the person he was, and the archive treats his page as the record of change at its least theatrical — a refusal, a conversation, a marriage.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Adam's 2010 is an emptied house: the girlfriend gone, the furniture gone, the nephew in the basement playing Second Life. When Lou is hospitalised, Adam organises the Kodiak Valley expedition — part reunion, part suicide watch — and leads the drinking that ends in the tub. In 1986, in his younger body, he accepts the doctrine and his assignment: Jenny, the great love, must be broken up with, publicly and cruelly, and the fork must go in his eye, or the timeline frays. He finds he cannot. Jenny, who has her own momentum, ends the relationship herself — and the fork arrives anyway, wielded by the injured party rather than suffered as a script: the timeline gets its wound, reassigned.

Unscripted, Adam drifts to the concert and meets April, a music journalist in town for Winterfest, and spends the night in the film's most deliberate anti-climax: talking. She is the first person in either of his timelines who meets the forty-year-old rather than the performance. When the endgame comes — Blaine's cabin, the recovered Chernobly, the vortex — Adam makes the expedition's most generous offer: he volunteers to stay in 1986 with Lou. Lou throws him into the vortex. He surfaces in Lou's edited 2010 to find April beside him as his wife, and the emptied house refilled by a life he did not plan but finally chose in the only hour he stopped planning.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The manager of other people's crises

Interpretation: Adam's instinct is organisational — trips arranged, doctrines enforced, friends monitored — and it functions in his own life as avoidance. The film cures him by making his management fail: the fork unperformed, the night unplanned, the friend unstayed-with.

Cruelty outgrown

The breakup he invented at seventeen — public, utensil-assisted — is the founding document of a romantic life spent re-living it. His inability to repeat it is not weakness but evidence: the manoeuvre's author no longer exists, and the film lets the discovery arrive as relief.

The listener

His night with April is talk, and the film insists on the precision: what Adam has never done with a woman is the thing he does all night. His arc is a conversation.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The fork

A utensil as a founding document: the archive reads it as the film's emblem of scripted cruelty — a tiny, domestic instrument with which a boy once ended a love affair and organised twenty-four years of repetitions. That it still finds his eye, wielded by Jenny, is the timeline's joke: the wound was always coming; only its author changed.

The emptied and refilled house

Adam's domestic state is the film's barometer: furniture burned in the first timeline, a marriage in the second — the same rooms, audited twice.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Change by refusal

The film gives each traveller a mode of revision: Lou edits, Nick asserts, Jacob witnesses. Adam's is refusal — he simply cannot perform the assigned cruelty — and the archive reads it as the film's most realistic psychology: people rarely become new by deciding; they discover, at the moment of repetition, that the old act no longer fits their hands. The fork scene is a man finding out he has changed by failing to change back.

The organiser organised

Adam spends the film arranging other people's salvation — Lou's trip, the doctrine's enforcement — and is saved entirely by other people's arrangements: Jenny's initiative, April's conversation, Lou's throw. The analysis: the film's kindness to him is structural; the manager is the one character not permitted to manage his own rescue.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Adam's page is the archive's record of the gentlest time-travel cure in the Galaxy: a man sent back to his worst evening who discovers he cannot be its author anymore.
