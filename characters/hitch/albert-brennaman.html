01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Albert Brennaman is the film's evidence. An accountant at the firm that serves Allegra Cole's fortune, he has loved her across a conference table for years at a distance of about six feet and several social classes, and he hires the date doctor as a drowning man hires a swimming instructor. The coaching rebuilds his presentation — wardrobe, posture, the choreography of approach — and the film is meticulous about what actually works: not the choreography, but the moment in a finance meeting when Albert, defending a position that touches Allegra's interests against his own boss, forgets to perform entirely and is simply, publicly, competently himself. She notices. The courtship that follows is the method's showroom success until the exposé reclassifies it as a fraud in print; Albert, arrested vandalising a newsstand in the fallout, ends the film's argument by delivering its thesis to Hitch himself — you treat love like a job — and then winning Allegra back the only way the film endorses: unscripted, in person, as himself. The wedding is his graduation and the method's true review.

ROLE

The case study

The client whose courtship is the film's experiment — and whose result refutes its designer's theory.

DEFINING TRAIT

Sincerity under the terror

Albert's defining quality is that his love is entirely genuine and entirely unpresentable — the film's work is the slow removal of the presentation, not the manufacture of the feeling.

FATE

Married

He ends the film as Allegra's husband, the wedding attended by his coach and his exposé's author: the experiment's result, published in vows.

Place in the story

Albert is the Hitch batch's laboratory: every claim the method makes is tested on him, and his file is where the film's verdict is recorded. His page carries the courtship entire — the coaching, the meeting, the exposé's blast, and the thesis he delivers to his own doctor.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Albert is introduced at the exact distance of his problem: a conference table, across which Allegra Cole's fortune is administered by his firm and her presence administers his composure. He hires Hitch in the spirit of a man purchasing a ladder, and the coaching begins its rebuild — the wardrobe, the stance, the engineered first encounters, the dance of approach rehearsed like a merger. The film's honesty about the process is its signature: the choreography helps, visibly, and is not what works. What works arrives uninvited, in a finance meeting, where a recommendation touching Allegra's interests is being waved through and Albert — the room's most junior conscience — stands up to his boss and argues the point on its merits, in front of her. Allegra notices the accountant. The archive files the meeting as the film's thesis in embryo: the coached man, succeeding at the precise moment the coaching falls away.

The courtship proceeds as the method's showroom piece — dates, signals, the protocol observed — until Sara's exposé reclassifies the whole production in print: the heiress learns from a gossip column that her suitor had professional help, and the revelation's implication (that the feeling itself was manufactured) breaks them. Albert's response is the fallout's most human document: he vandalises a newsstand selling the paper and is arrested, the sweetest man in the film in handcuffs for littering his own heartbreak across a sidewalk. His recovery begins as an accusation — confronting Hitch, he names the method's flaw from the client's side: Hitch treats love as a job, a formula, a thing with steps, and the steps are what just failed. It is the pupil's verdict that sends the doctor to Allegra's door to argue the case — and Albert's own final approach is the film's last piece of evidence: unscripted, unrehearsed, himself at full size, he wins her back. They marry. The wedding is staged as the method's closing review: the client and the heiress, the coach and the columnist, all in one crowd — the experiment's result, dancing.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The genuine article

Interpretation: Albert is the film's control case for sincerity — his feeling never needs manufacturing, only unburying. The archive reads his arc as the method's quiet refutation from inside its own success story: everything the coaching adds is scaffolding, and the building was always there.

Courage, domain-specific

He is terrified of Allegra and fearless in a spreadsheet dispute — the film's point being that his courage was never absent, only misfiled: the meeting works because it lets him be brave in his native register, in her presence, once.

The meeting

Interpretation: Albert's stand is the archive's emblem of the film's whole doctrine — a man becomes visible to the woman he loves not when his presentation peaks but when it lapses, in the one room where he was never acting. 09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the experiment and its result

Albert is the method's only fully documented trial, and the film designs his data to refute the method's self-understanding: the coached variables (wardrobe, choreography) correlate with progress, but the decisive events (the meeting, the final approach) are the uncoached ones. The analysis the archive records is that Albert's function is evidentiary — he is the proof, produced in court by both sides, that what Hitch sells is confidence and what his clients supply is everything else.
