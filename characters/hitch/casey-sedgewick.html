01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Casey Sedgewick is the Hitch batch's ground-level file: the person the plot happens to before it happens to anyone else. She is Sara Melas's friend and colleague, dating in the same New York the professionals farm, and her brief entanglement with Vance Munson — a polished operator pursuing an evening, not a woman — leaves her burned in the way that starts everything: she tells Sara, Sara hears the date doctor's name in the ash, and the investigation begins. Casey herself has no method, no column and no legend; she has the experience both professions monetise. The film keeps her at the story's edge thereafter — present in its world, at the speed-dating event where the truth is finally told in her hearing's vicinity — and this file follows the record's proportion, noting plainly that her function is to be the cost, itemised in a person, that the film's two professionals spend their arcs learning to count.

Place in the story

Casey is the Hitch batch's reminder of stakes: without her burn there is no investigation, no exposé, no plot — the entire machinery of both professionals is set in motion by one undefended person's bad evening. Her page is deliberately kept to the documented record, as the honest-record treatment requires.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Casey's documented course is short and load-bearing. She is established in Sara's orbit — the friend and colleague with whom the columnist is a person rather than a byline — dating in the city the film farms. Vance Munson enters her life with his polish and his requirement; the encounter, conducted on his terms and concluded on his schedule, leaves her burned, and her account of it to Sara contains the detail that changes the film: the name of the date doctor, dropped by Vance as the supposed source of his technique. Sara's loyalty does the rest — the investigation, the legend, the exposé all descend from Casey's bad evening and her telling of it.

Thereafter the record keeps her at the level the film keeps her: within Sara's world, present at its edges — including the speed-dating milieu where the confrontation that corrects the record takes place — without a closing set-piece of her own. This file does not promote her beyond the documented record. What can be said, and is said in the analysis, is structural: Casey is the only person in the film who pays for the dating economy without earning from it in any currency — no method sold, no column printed, no wedding danced at centre — and the film's two professionals both begin their educations at her expense: Hitch's ethic is defined against the man who burned her, and Sara's investigation is begun for her sake. Her page stands as the ledger entry both of them were, in their different offices, failing to keep.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the stakes, personified

Casey exists so the film's professionals have something their work is about. The analysis the archive records is that she is the test both leads initially fail in opposite directions — Hitch's ethic already excludes her burner but cannot protect her; Sara's loyalty avenges her with a story that is wrong in the one fact that matters to her case — and the plot is the two of them slowly becoming worthy of the errand she set them.
