01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Alex Hitchens has industrialised courtship's first mile: for men referred to him in confidence — men who genuinely want the woman, not the evening — he designs the approach, the dates, the signals, with one inviolable protocol: the kiss, when it comes, is hers to initiate. The practice is ethical by its own code and effective by the film's evidence, and it is also, the flashbacks confess, a scar with a business model: the college girlfriend he found with another man, flowers in his hand, taught him that unscripted feeling is an ambush, and he has been scripting everyone's feelings — never his own — ever since. The film's plot is the method meeting its two counterexamples: a client who succeeds by ceasing to perform, and a woman, Sara Melas, on whom every technique fails in sequence because she is the first person he has wanted without a script. His education ends where his practice began: the admission that his clients won by being themselves, that he only ever supplied confidence — and that his own life requires the unscripted thing itself.

ROLE

The date doctor

Protagonist; the method's author and its final refutation.

DEFINING TRAIT

Control, in the service of other people's courage

Hitch's genius is real — he reads rooms, signals and timing like a clinician — and it is entirely outward-facing: his own romantic life is a waiting room he never calls his own number in.

FATE

Unscripted, at last

He ends the film exposed, forgiven, and in a relationship he did not engineer — dancing at his clients' wedding with the woman his method could not process.

Place in the story

Hitch is the film's centre and its thesis in a suit: the method works, the method is insufficient, and the distance between those two facts is the story. His page carries both the practice's operations and its author's repair.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Hitch's practice is introduced as an urban legend with an office: men are referred, vetted for sincerity, and coached through courtship's opening — the approach engineered, the dates designed, the signals taught — under the protocol that the first kiss must be hers. The film's case study is Albert Brennaman, an accountant in love with a client of his firm, the heiress Allegra Cole; under Hitch's coaching Albert is rebuilt from apology into presence, most decisively in a finance meeting where he stands up to his own boss on a matter touching Allegra's interests and wins her notice with the first unscripted thing he has ever done at work — a beat the film files carefully, because it is the method's secret showing early: the coached man succeeds at the moment he stops performing.

Hitch's own courtship runs as the method's controlled demolition. Sara Melas, a gossip columnist, enters his life as a subject who will not be processed: his engineered encounters misfire, his signals jam, his legendary timing deserts him in scene after scene of escalating physical comedy — the doctor, symptomatic at last. He conceals the practice behind the word "consultant," and the concealment is the bomb the plot lights: Sara, investigating the date-doctor legend after her friend Casey is burned by Vance Munson — a man Hitch refused to coach, who nonetheless lets Sara believe he was a client — connects the legend to the man she is falling for, and publishes the exposé. The blast takes out everything: Albert and Allegra break over the revelation that a professional was involved, Albert is arrested vandalising a newsstand, and Hitch's anonymous practice has a face in the papers.

The repair is conducted in the film's two confrontations. At a speed-dating event Hitch finally tells Sara the whole truth — the refusal of Vance, the practice's actual ethic, that men like Vance are precisely why his services exist — and Albert, in the companion scene, delivers the client's verdict on the method: Hitch treats love as a job, a formula, a product. Hitch carries the verdict to Allegra herself, and in arguing Albert's case discovers his own: his clients did not win because of the scripts; they won in the moments they became themselves, and his contribution was never the choreography — it was the confidence. The method's author files its true mechanism at last, and applies it unscripted to his own case. The film ends at Albert and Allegra's wedding, Hitch and Sara together in the crowd: the date doctor, retired into the condition he spent a career simulating for other men.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The professional romantic

Interpretation: Hitch's tragedy and comedy are the same fact — he believes in love completely and participates in it not at all. The archive reads the practice as a cathedral built by a man standing outside it: every client he sends through the door is a proof of the faith and an exhibit of the exile.

Ethics as architecture

His refusals define him as much as his methods — Vance is turned away on sight, because the practice exists for men who want the woman, and the film's whole third act hangs on that refusal having happened off Sara's record.

The clinician's eye

Signals, timing, rooms, rivals: Hitch reads a courtship the way a doctor reads a chart, which is the title's whole claim.

DESIGN

The engineered encounter

Approaches, venues, exits, contingencies: his dates are productions with the machinery hidden, at a standard his own courtship never once achieves.

Symbolism & Themes

The three-date protocol

Interpretation: Hitch's method is the archive's emblem of courtship as engineering — sincere in aim, effective in practice, and finally insufficient in kind. The film's respect for it is what makes its verdict land: the method is not exposed as a fraud but graduated from, by its author, in public, at cost.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the expert, educated

Hitch is the rare romantic-comedy protagonist whose expertise is genuine, which relocates the genre's question: not can he learn to love, but can the man who teaches it survive its actual syllabus — concealment punished, scripts failing, the truth told at a speed-dating microphone. The analysis the archive records is that the film's real subject is professionalisation itself: any human mystery, competently packaged, will eventually be invoiced to its packager, and the invoice is the plot.
