01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Neville's fifth year is the DA's thesis proven on its hardest case. He arrives as the saga's designated struggler — forgetful, clumsy, the boy whose parents' fate (tortured into permanent madness by Bellatrix Lestrange and her companions) is the war's quietest horror, visited in a closed ward at St Mungo's where his mother gives him sweet wrappers he keeps. The DA changes the file: it is Neville who finds the Room of Requirement, Neville whose progress Hermione cites as the Army's vindication, Neville who — after the Azkaban breakout frees his parents' torturers — trains with a ferocity the film marks as grief converted to syllabus. In the Department of Mysteries he stands in the same room as Bellatrix, hears her taunt him with his parents, is tortured with the Cruciatus to pressure Harry — and does not break the line: he tells Harry not to surrender the prophecy. The comic relief ends the film as a soldier with a broken nose and an unbroken position.

ROLE

The proof

Gryffindor fifth-year; the DA's most improved member; one of the six at the Ministry; Bellatrix's living accusation.

DEFINING TRAIT

Grief converted

Neville's transformation is not confidence trickery — it is mourning with a curriculum: every spell he masters is a visit to St Mungo's, weaponized.

FATE

Unbroken

Tortured by his parents' torturer in the Death Eaters' hands, he refuses the exchange — the prophecy for his pain — and survives the battle, bloodied and standing.

Place in the story

Neville is the film's argument about who wars are fought by: not only the chosen and the brilliant, but the dismissed — the boy the prophecy might have named, as the saga's deep record notes, who becomes a soldier by the slower road of work.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Neville's war began before he could speak: his Auror parents were tortured into madness by Death Eaters seeking Voldemort after his fall, and he visits them in St Mungo's closed ward — where his mother, not knowing him, presses sweet wrappers into his hand, which he keeps. The film carries this as his foundation: the saga's most private grief, borne without self-pity.

He joins the DA among its first recruits and becomes its miracle of documentation: he finds the Room of Requirement — the institution's home is the struggler's discovery — and under Harry's teaching his disarming spell, then his whole repertoire, comes online. Hermione cites his progress as the Army's proof of concept.

The Azkaban breakout changes his training's temperature: Bellatrix Lestrange is free, and Neville's practice acquires the ferocity the film notes without underlining — the boy is preparing, spell by spell, for a woman he has never met and has always known.

He answers the lure without hesitation — one of the six on the thestrals — and in the Department of Mysteries the preparation meets its subject: captured with the others, he is held before Bellatrix, who recognizes the Longbottom boy and offers his parents' treatment as a taunt and then a demonstration. Under the Cruciatus, with Harry pressed to trade the prophecy for his pain, Neville's line in the film is refusal: do not give it to them. He survives the battle — nose broken, wand snapped in the fighting, position unbroken — the DA's proof, filed in blood.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The slow courage

Neville's bravery is never spontaneous combustion; it is attendance — showing up to practice, to the flight, to the taunt — by a boy who knows exactly what the enemy does to people, better than anyone in the room.

The keeper of wrappers

His tenderness toward his mother's gifts — rubbish to anyone else, relics to him — is the character in miniature: loyalty to what the world files as worthless, including, for years, himself.

From the class's slowest start to a Mysteries-grade duellist in one school year — the film's documented rate of improvement, and its argument about teaching.

Symbolism & Themes

The sweet wrappers

A madwoman's gifts, kept as treasure: the saga's emblem of love surviving the mind that can no longer file it — and of Neville's whole doctrine of worth.

The found room

The school's most dismissed boy discovers the resistance's headquarters: Hogwarts gives its hidden room to the student who needed it most, which is the room's stated rule.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The other chosen one

The saga's deep record — known to readers of the prophecy's full terms — is that Neville, born a day apart, might have been the prophecy's subject had Voldemort chosen differently. Analysis: the film plays the echo without stating it: two boys in the Hall of Prophecy, one marked by the enemy's choice, one by his servants' — and the unmarked one holds the line like the marked one's equal.

Teaching as the film's verdict

Neville is the DA's evidence in the film's argument with Umbridge's syllabus: given a real teacher and a real subject, the school's worst student becomes a soldier in a year. Lewis's incremental performance is the proof's documentation.
