Severus Snape

The new Defence Against the Dark Arts teacher — bound by an Unbreakable Vow to a desperate mother, serving two masters' incompatible orders, and carrying, behind the stillest face in the series, the film's entire concealed architecture: he is the Half-Blood Prince, and he is the man on the tower.

01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Snape is the film's black box. It opens with him at Spinner's End, receiving Narcissa Malfoy and Bellatrix, and making — under Bellatrix's wand and her sister's desperation — an Unbreakable Vow: to watch over Draco, protect him, and complete his task if the boy cannot. To the sisters he presents himself as Voldemort's spy inside the Order; to the Order, whose trust in him rests entirely on Dumbledore's word, he is Dumbledore's spy inside the Death Eaters. The film adjudicates neither account. It shows him healing Draco from Harry's curse, confiscating the Prince's book without recognising it in public, arriving on the tower in the held breath after Draco's failure — and casting the Killing Curse. Fleeing, he stops Harry's curses with contemptuous ease and leaves him with the year's last revelation: the textbook Harry has been studying all year is his.

ROLE

The concealed architecture

Principal; the vow-bound protector, the war's double-facing agent, the Prince, and the headmaster's killer — in an order the film declines to sort.

DEFINING TRAIT

Opacity as armour

Rickman plays him at his most opaque, which is the film's point: every faction reads its own Snape into the stillness, and the stillness accommodates them all.

FATE

Fled, with the Death Eaters

He kills Dumbledore, defeats Harry without injuring him, and escapes — his true account, whatever it is, carried out of the film unopened.

Place in the story

Snape is the film's locked room: the plot's most consequential acts (the vow, the healing, the curse) are all his, and its central question — whose man is he? — is the one it refuses to answer. The archive files his page as the film files him: documented in action, withheld in allegiance, with his own claims recorded as claims.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

At Spinner's End, before term begins, Narcissa Malfoy comes to him in secret: Voldemort has tasked her son Draco with a mission designed, she believes, to kill him by failure, in punishment for his father's disgrace. Snape receives her with Bellatrix watching and answers her plea with the Unbreakable Vow — he will protect the boy and, if it comes to it, perform the task himself. To Bellatrix's interrogation he presents his account of himself: the Dark Lord's plant in Dumbledore's confidence, patient for sixteen years. The film offers the account as his testimony, and offers nothing to settle it against.

At Hogwarts he takes the Defence post he has coveted for a decade, and the year proceeds under his double shadow. He heals Draco after the bathroom duel, recognising his own curse's work; he confiscates Harry's Potions book — the Prince's own annotated copy — in an interrogation the film plays as a near-miss of self-recognition. The Order's trust in him is stated, by Lupin among others, as resting on Dumbledore's: the headmaster trusts Snape, therefore the Order does. It is the only credential the film ever shows for him, and it is a dead man's by the end.

On the tower he arrives in the held breath: Draco wandless-hearted, the other Death Eaters baying, Dumbledore disarmed and speaking his name. Snape casts the Killing Curse, and the headmaster falls. In the flight across the grounds he dismantles Harry's attacks with a contempt that never becomes injury — he will not let the others harm Harry either, claiming him for the Dark Lord — and delivers the year's final revelation to the boy who has been his unwitting pupil: he is the Half-Blood Prince, and Harry has been using his book. Then he is gone, and the film closes his file exactly there.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The stillness

Interpretation: Snape's opacity is not a personality trait but a professional instrument maintained at visible cost — the film's one tell is how much effort the stillness takes in the vow scene's close-ups.

Contempt as cover

His cruelty to Harry is performed at full volume in public and modulated, in the flight, to precise non-lethality: he defeats the boy like a teacher correcting form. The archive records the modulation as data and declines, with the film, to total it.

The vow's weight

Whatever his allegiance, the oath is real magic with a death price: he has bound himself to protect the boy sent to kill his master, or to do the killing. The film lets that bind stand as his year's definition, in place of an allegiance it will not state.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
DUELLING

The master at ease

He dismantles Harry's best curses in the flight without effort or injury — the saga's most economical display of superiority.

HEALING

The counter-curse

He saves Draco from Sectumsempra — his own invention — singing the wounds closed: the Prince recognising his work.

OCCLUMENCY AND COVER

The unreadable man

Sixteen years, by his account, inside one camp or the other: whichever way the truth runs, the performance has never cracked.

WEAKNESS

The vow

He has mortgaged his life to a mother's desperation: fail Draco, and the oath kills him. His freedom of action is the film's most expensive casualty.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The still face

Interpretation: Snape is the film's argument that a person can be perfectly legible in action and perfectly illegible in meaning — every faction's certainty about him is shown to be a reading of the same unreadable text.

The textbook

Interpretation: the Prince's book makes Snape Harry's secret teacher in the same year he becomes his open enemy — the saga's most poisonous irony, delivered as a parting line.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the locked room at the plot's centre

The film is constructed so that its climax is uninterpretable without a fact it withholds: if Snape is Voldemort's, the tower is a triumph of the long game; if Dumbledore's, it is something else entirely, and the film gives the audience Dumbledore's spoken name as the only key, uncut. The archive therefore files his actions with unusual care and his allegiance not at all — the claims on record (his own, to the sisters; the Order's, resting on Dumbledore) are preserved as claims, which is where this film leaves every honest account of him.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Alan Rickman

Rickman's Snape reaches its saga summit here; the production staged the tower sequence as the film's climax with his arrival held to the last possible beat. The character posters released for the film placed Snape among its six principals — Harry, Dumbledore, Ron, Hermione, Draco and Snape — the marketing's own reading of the year's architecture.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Snape's Half-Blood Prince is the saga's locked room at its most consequential: the vow kept, the headmaster killed, the book revealed, the allegiance unopened. In the Galaxy's records his page stands exactly as the film leaves him — the most documented and least settled file in the series, awaiting a later film's evidence.
