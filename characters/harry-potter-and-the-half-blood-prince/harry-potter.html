01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Harry's sixth year is an apprenticeship in the past. Dumbledore takes him out of the classroom and into the Pensieve, walking him through Tom Riddle's orphanage, his schoolboy charm and the one memory — Slughorn's, doctored out of shame — that holds the key to Voldemort's survival. Meanwhile Harry acquires the Half-Blood Prince's annotated textbook and becomes, overnight, a Potions star; falls for Ginny Weasley; and nurses a conviction nobody will certify, that Draco Malfoy has been given a mission. He is right, and it does not help: the year ends in the cave, at the tower, and over a locket that turns out to be fake — with Harry announcing he will not be coming back to Hogwarts.

ROLE

The student of the enemy

Protagonist; Dumbledore's apprentice in the war's history, the Prince's unwitting pupil, and the tower's hidden witness.

DEFINING TRAIT

The series' best listener

Radcliffe plays him with a new adult attentiveness: this year Harry wins by hearing people — Slughorn, Riddle, Dumbledore — rather than by out-duelling them.

FATE

The apprenticeship ended by murder

He retrieves a fake Horcrux, watches Snape kill Dumbledore, and ends the film resolved to leave school and hunt the real ones.

Place in the story

Harry is the film's vessel: the memories are shown to him, the book is found by him, the mission is confided to him. The archive keeps his page as the year's register — every other principal (headmaster, double agent, broken assassin, ashamed teacher, the boy Riddle was) is filed under what Harry is shown of them.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

The year opens in a London under quiet attack — the Millennium Bridge destroyed, Ollivander taken — and Dumbledore collects Harry personally, first to recruit Horace Slughorn (using Harry, the famous survivor, as the bait the old collector cannot refuse) and then to begin the real curriculum: private lessons in which Harry dives into collected memories of Tom Riddle. He sees the orphanage, the stolen trinkets and the boy who could hurt people; he sees the teenage Riddle charm Slughorn; and he is shown the doctored memory in which Slughorn's voice drowns the question that matters. Dumbledore sets the year's assignment: retrieve the true memory. Meanwhile the Half-Blood Prince's textbook makes Harry a Potions prodigy — its marginalia win him a vial of Felix Felicis — and its curses nearly make him a killer: duelling Draco in the bathroom, he uses Sectumsempra, a spell annotated "for enemies," and watches in horror as Draco bleeds on the flooded floor until Snape intervenes.

Harry's parallel conviction — that Draco is on a mission for Voldemort — is dismissed by everyone, including Ron and Hermione, even as the evidence accumulates: Katie Bell cursed by a necklace, Ron poisoned by mead meant for Dumbledore. Harry saves Ron with a bezoar from the Prince's own marginalia. His romance with Ginny builds through the year and arrives, after the Quidditch final, in the common room; it is Ginny who hides the Prince's book in the Room of Requirement, among a generation's lost things, beside a Vanishing Cabinet Harry does not yet understand.

The memory is won at last at Aragog's funeral, Harry lubricated by Felix Felicis, drawing the truth out of Slughorn's shame: the boy Riddle asked how a soul could be split into seven. Dumbledore confirms the arithmetic of horror — Horcruxes, two already destroyed — and takes Harry to the cave to take a third. Harry forces the potion down the headmaster's throat as ordered, fights the Inferi with fire, and brings them home to a tower under the Dark Mark. Ordered to hide, he watches Draco disarm Dumbledore and fail to kill him — and Snape arrive and do it. Harry's pursuit of Snape ends with the revelation spat back at him: the Half-Blood Prince is Snape himself. Over the fake locket's note — signed R.A.B. — Harry tells Ron and Hermione he will not return to Hogwarts. The hunt is the school now.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The listener

Interpretation: this film's Harry wins his objectives by attention — to memories, to an old teacher's shame, to a dying man's instructions. The saga's hero is being retrained from duellist to investigator, and the retraining is Dumbledore's real bequest.

Right, and uncertified

His Draco conviction is correct and unusable: the film is a study in the loneliness of accurate suspicion, and Harry's frustration at the adults' trust in Snape is the year's most prophetic emotion — whatever its object turns out to be worth.

Obedience as the wound

The cave and the tower both require him to obey orders that horrify him — feed the poison, hide and watch. The film locates his maturity precisely there: he does the unforgivable things he was told to do, and carries them.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
POTIONS, BORROWED

The Prince's marginalia

With the annotated book he is Slughorn's star and Felix's winner; the skill is real but it is a dead boy's homework, and the film keeps the debt visible.

DEFENSIVE MAGIC

The duellist of the DA

A proven combat wizard — and the author, once, of Sectumsempra's horror, which teaches him the year's other curriculum: what his weapons do.

FELIX FELICIS

Luck, administered

The won vial spends itself perfectly at Aragog's funeral: the right words, the right hour, the memory surrendered.

WEAKNESS

The hidden witness

His obedience at the tower saves his life and costs him the chance to intervene: the film leaves him with a front-row seat at the murder he most wanted to prevent.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The two teachers

Interpretation: Harry is educated this year by a dead boy's textbook and a dying man's memories — the Prince and Dumbledore, the war's two hidden authors, both teaching him in absentia. The film's climax reveals they were the same war all along.

The fake locket

Interpretation: the year's whole labour purchases a counterfeit and a stranger's initials — the story's way of saying the hunt will be longer than the schooling, and mostly unwitnessed.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the archive's reader

Harry's role this film is receptive: he is shown things. The structure makes him the audience's surrogate in a new register — not the boy things happen to, but the investigator evidence is laid before — and the ending promotes him accordingly: the student who has read the enemy's biography declares himself the hunt's author. The archive records the tower as the design's price: the reader must watch the book's author murdered by its other author, hidden, obedient, and unable to footnote it.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Daniel Radcliffe

Radcliffe's sixth film in the role; the production staged the cave sequence as the year's set piece, with Harry's forced ministration of the potion played as the mentor bond's darkest hour. Dumbledore's funeral, a set piece of the novel, was removed from the film as not fitting its design — the mourning is carried instead by the raised wands of the school.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This Harry is the saga's hinge: the schoolboy becomes the hunt's commander by declaration, over a fake locket, in a school that has just buried its headmaster. In the Galaxy's records his page stands as the apprenticeship's diploma — written by two hidden teachers, and signed in the tower's aftermath by the student who watched.
