01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Mia Wallace is the wife of Los Angeles crime boss Marsellus Wallace and, before that, an aspiring actress whose pilot — a show about female secret agents — was not picked up. While Marsellus is away, he instructs his hitman Vincent Vega to take her to dinner. The evening at Jack Rabbit Slim's, a 1950s-themed restaurant, is all charged surfaces: milkshakes, a twist contest they win, and conversation that circles the prohibition hanging over the whole night — including the rumour that Marsellus threw a man from a fourth-storey window for giving Mia a foot massage, a story Mia corrects: the man never gave her one. Back home, while Vincent is in the bathroom, Mia finds the heroin in his jacket pocket, mistakes it for cocaine, snorts it, and overdoses. Vincent rushes her to his dealer Lance's house, where she is revived by an adrenaline shot to the heart. The two agree never to tell Marsellus — a secret the film leaves standing.

ROLE

The guarded centre

Title figure of the film's second storyline ('Vincent Vega and Marsellus Wallace's Wife'); the person around whom the underworld's most dangerous etiquette is organised.

DEFINING TRAIT

Boredom as hazard

Everything that nearly kills Mia is a product of her appetite for sensation inside a gilded restriction — the Galaxy card's reading, kept: she is the story's portrait of boredom as the underworld's true hazard.

FATE

Alive; the secret kept

Revived at Lance's house; she and Vincent agree Marsellus never hears of the night, and within this film, he does not.

Place in the story

Mia is the film's poster and its pressure gauge: she appears in essentially one chapter, yet Marsellus's power, Vincent's doom-laden carelessness and the underworld's whole code of possession are all measured against her safety. Thurman's performance was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Story chronology: Mia's chapter follows the apartment/diner morning. Marsellus, going out of town, instructs Vincent Vega to take his wife to dinner. Vincent — having bought heroin from Lance and shot up — collects her. At Jack Rabbit Slim's, a 1950s-themed restaurant, they order, talk and enter the twist contest, which they win. Their conversation includes Mia's acting past — her unaired pilot — and the underworld rumour that Marsellus threw a man, Tony Rocky Horror in the AFI account's rendering, out of a window for giving her a foot massage; Mia states the man never gave her a foot massage.

Returning to the Wallace house, Mia goes through Vincent's jacket while he is in the bathroom and finds his heroin. Mistaking it for cocaine, she snorts it and overdoses — collapsing as Vincent emerges. He rushes her to Lance's house, where, after frantic argument in the household, she is revived by injecting adrenaline directly into her heart. Shaken, Vincent and Mia agree that Marsellus will never be told about the incident. That agreement is where the film leaves her: alive, restored, and the keeper of the night's secret alongside the man whose drugs nearly killed her.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The interviewer

Interpretation: Mia runs her escort like an audition in reverse — questions, tests, a dance contest — appraising the man her husband sent. The archive reads her wit as an instrument of a person whose world permits her almost no other instruments.

Appetite under glass

Interpretation: milkshakes, music, cocaine-that-isn't: her desires are all small, immediate and physical, set against the enormous abstract prohibition that organises her life. The overdose is that collision, not a character defect in isolation — though the archive notes she takes an unknown powder from a hitman's pocket, and the film does not excuse the recklessness; it prices it.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
SKILL

Performance

A trained actress manquée: she commands Jack Rabbit Slim's floor, wins its twist contest with Vincent, and conducts an entire evening as a staged event.

SKILL

Conversation as control

She sets every topic, corrects the record on the foot-massage rumour, and keeps her escort precisely at the distance she chooses — until the jacket pocket.

LIMIT

The sealed life

Her movements, escort and reputation are her husband's property in the underworld's accounting; the film's one night of relative freedom nearly kills her, which the archive records as the system's design, not her failure alone.

07 / CHARACTER ARC

Character Arc

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
The assignment

Introduced as an instruction — take my wife out — before she is introduced as a person; the chapter's work is reversing that order.

The evening

Jack Rabbit Slim's: milkshakes, the pilot story, the rumour corrected, the twist contest won — Mia at maximum command of her narrow stage.

The overdose

Her escort's heroin, her mistake, her collapse: the night's prohibition and its hazard turn out to be the same man.

The pact

Revived by adrenaline, she agrees with Vincent to bury the night — the film's most consequential secret, kept by its two least powerful keepers.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The five-dollar milkshake

Interpretation: the film's emblem of her world — an ordinary pleasure at an absurd markup, questioned ('You don't put bourbon in it or anything?') and then pronounced worth it. Desire, priced by the house.

The adrenaline shot

Interpretation: her resurrection is the film's most literal — a needle to the heart, administered by amateurs, that works. Set beside Jules's spiritual resurrection the same story-day earlier, the archive reads the pair as the film's two revivals: one of the body, one of the soul, both mediated by Vincent's presence and only one of them learned from.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

One chapter, whole film

Analysis: Mia's screen story is a single evening, yet she organises three men's fates — Marsellus's authority, Vincent's doom (the secret he carries to Butch's apartment), and the audience's memory of the film itself, via its poster. The archive notes the economy: Tarantino builds the film's most famous woman out of one dinner, one dance and one emergency.

The rumour, handled with care

Analysis / fact discipline: the foot-massage story is presented in the film as underworld rumour, and Mia's own correction — that the man never gave her a foot massage — is the only first-person evidence offered. This page records the rumour as rumour and her correction as the record, and draws no conclusion about what, if anything, Marsellus did to the man.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Mia Wallace on the poster — black bob, cigarette, pulp novel — became the visual signature of 1990s independent cinema, and Thurman's performance its defining femme fatale turn, per the Galaxy card's assessment, which the archive keeps. Her chapter is the film's most excerpted: the dance and the overdose are its two most imitated sequences.

In the Galaxy she anchors the archive's guarded-centre line: the character with the least freedom and the largest consequences, whose survival depends on a secret the film, unusually, allows to stay kept.
