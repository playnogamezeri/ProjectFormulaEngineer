01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Pumpkin — real name Ringo — and his girlfriend Honey Bunny (Yolanda) are small-time robbers eating in a Los Angeles diner, reviewing their trade: liquor stores, gas stations, the risks of robbing places with too many witnesses or too little cash. Pumpkin proposes the diner itself — a restaurant, he argues, is unprepared, insured, and full of wallets. They kiss, draw their guns, and the film freezes on Honey Bunny's scream — cutting away for two hours. When the film returns to the diner, the robbery collides with Jules and Vincent's breakfast: Pumpkin demands the briefcase at Jules's table, and finds himself disarmed in every sense — Jules holds him at gunpoint, re-reads his own Ezekiel passage as a sermon on the life Pumpkin is standing in, buys back his own wallet's money as a gift, and sends the couple out of the diner alive, with the till money and the customers' wallets, but without the briefcase and without a shot fired at them.

ROLE

The amateur at the professionals' table

Co-author of the film's frame: his robbery opens and closes the picture, and its resolution is the proof of Jules's conversion.

DEFINING TRAIT

The theorist robber

Pumpkin robs by argument — cost-benefit, risk classes, a business plan — and is undone by meeting a man who argues better, about larger stakes, with a gun already drawn.

FATE

Alive; released with the money

Jules lets the couple keep the robbery takings and his own wallet money, and they leave the diner — the film's only robbers to profit and survive.

Place in the story

Pumpkin is the frame's human half: the film begins inside his reasoning and ends inside his education. The Galaxy card's reading stands — his diner heist collides with a metaphysical event he cannot process, and his survival is the evidence it happened.

Tarantino wrote the part with Roth in mind — Roth had starred in Reservoir Dogs — though TriStar's Mike Medavoy preferred Johnny Depp or Christian Slater, per the cast record. Roth, who used an American accent in Reservoir Dogs, uses his natural London accent here; early in development Tarantino had even considered Roth as Vincent opposite Gary Oldman as Jules.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

The film opens on Pumpkin and Honey Bunny's breakfast conversation: a review of their robberies and their risks, ending in Pumpkin's proposal to rob the diner itself. They declare their love, draw their weapons, and announce the holdup — Honey Bunny screaming the terms — as the film cuts away. In story terms, the robbery waits while the film tells its other stories; in the diner's own timeline, Jules and Vincent arrive for breakfast after the Wolfe cleanup, and Jules is mid-conversation about retirement when the robbery resumes around them.

Pumpkin works the room, collecting wallets, and reaches Jules's table, where the briefcase sits. He demands it. Jules — Vincent being in the bathroom — complies with the wallet collection, then turns the table: he holds Pumpkin at gunpoint, and with Honey Bunny's gun now on him and Vincent returned with his gun on her, Jules talks. He recites his Ezekiel passage and gives it its new reading, tells Pumpkin he is buying his life — he lets the couple keep the money, including the contents of his own wallet, which he hands over as a gift rather than a surrender — and keeps the briefcase, which he says is not Pumpkin's to take. The standoff dissolves; Pumpkin and Honey Bunny leave the diner with their takings, alive.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The rationalist out-argued

Interpretation: Pumpkin's whole self is a cost-benefit analysis, and Jules beats him on his own field — the sermon's logic is actuarial as much as spiritual: the life priced, the exit priced, the gift priced. The archive reads his stunned compliance as a theorist recognising a better theory.

Love as a working partnership

Interpretation: the pet names and the kiss before the guns are the character's warmth — he and Yolanda are the film's most conventionally affectionate couple, which is precisely why Jules's mercy can reach him: there is a life visible on the other side of the robbery.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
SKILL

Target selection

His diner theory is, on its own terms, sound — the robbery proceeds without resistance from staff or customers until it reaches the one table in Los Angeles it should have skipped.

SKILL

Room control

He collects a diner full of wallets efficiently and without violence — a competent small-time operator, as far as the record goes.

LIMIT

No read on people

His analysis prices premises, not patrons: he cannot see that the quiet man with the briefcase is the most dangerous object in the room, nor that the briefcase is defended by a theology, not a salary.

07 / CHARACTER ARC

Character Arc

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
The proposal

Breakfast as a business meeting: the diner itself selected as the day's target, on impeccable small-time logic.

The freeze-frame

Guns drawn, love declared, Honey Bunny screaming — the film's opening image, held for two hours.

The last table

The briefcase demanded; the table turned; a gun at his head and a passage being re-read at him.

The release

He leaves with the money, without the briefcase, and alive — the only character in the film paid to go away by the man he was robbing.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The frame

Interpretation: Pumpkin's robbery is the film's parentheses — the story begins and ends inside his worst professional decision, and everything between the parentheses is, in part, the explanation of why he survives it. The archive reads the structure as Jules's sermon given a two-hour footnote.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The audience surrogate, armed

Analysis: Pumpkin is positioned exactly where the audience is — a genre consumer who thinks he knows how diner robberies go — and Jules's sermon is delivered to him as much as to us. His survival is the film's proof-of-concept for mercy, staged on the character least able to demand it and most able to publicise it: he will tell this story, the film implies, for the rest of his life.

10 / KEY MOMENTS

Key Moments

The theory of diners

The opening conversation: robbery as a trade being rationalised, over coffee, by its two practitioners.

The holdup announced

Love declared, guns out, Honey Bunny's scream — and the freeze-frame that opens the film.

The briefcase demanded

At Jules's table he reaches for the one object in the room that is defended by faith.

The sermon received

Held at gunpoint, he hears the Ezekiel passage re-read — and is paid, spared and dismissed.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Roth, as written

Per the cast record, Tarantino wrote Ringo for Tim Roth against studio preference for Depp or Slater, and Roth plays him in his natural London accent — a deliberate contrast with his Reservoir Dogs American.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Pumpkin's half of the diner frame is half of the film's most famous structure, and his stunned face under Jules's sermon is one of its defining images. In the Galaxy he anchors the archive's amateur line: the small-time theorist whose robbery became a conversion's evidence.
