01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Yolanda — Honey Bunny to her boyfriend Ringo (Pumpkin) — is a small-time robber whose half of the diner frame is pure voltage. In the prologue she listens to Pumpkin's theory of robbing the diner they are sitting in, declares her love, and announces the holdup at a scream, gun in hand: the image the film freezes on. In the epilogue, when Jules turns the tables on Pumpkin and holds him at gunpoint, it is Honey Bunny who escalates — hysterical, gun pointed at Jules, screaming at him to let her man go — while Vincent returns from the bathroom and points his gun at her. The standoff has four weapons and one negotiator, and Jules's resolution is engineered around her: he keeps his voice and his gun steady, addresses Pumpkin's reason, and lets the couple leave with the money before her panic can fire the room.

ROLE

The variable

The sermon's counterweight, per the Galaxy card: Jules's negotiation succeeds by routing around her — the standoff's outcome is decided by whether the room's most volatile element can be kept from firing.

DEFINING TRAIT

Panic as presence

Plummer plays her, per the Galaxy card, with a feral, trembling intensity: her love and her terror are the same instrument at full volume.

FATE

Alive; released with Pumpkin

She leaves the diner with Ringo and the takings, unshot and unarrested — Jules's mercy extends to her precisely because it was designed around her.

Place in the story

Honey Bunny is the frame's exposed wire: the film opens on her scream and nearly ends on her trigger finger. Her page is kept honest about the record's size — she exists in one location and one continuous event — and argues that within that event she carries the climax's suspense almost alone.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Honey Bunny's documented story is the diner, in two parts. Prologue: over breakfast, she and Pumpkin review their robbery trade and its risks; Pumpkin proposes robbing the diner itself. They exchange declarations — the pet names are theirs — draw their guns, and she announces the holdup, screaming the terms to the room as the film freezes and cuts away.

Epilogue: the robbery proceeds — wallets collected — until Pumpkin reaches Jules's table and demands the briefcase. Jules holds Pumpkin at gunpoint; Honey Bunny, hysterical, points her gun at Jules and screams for her man's release. Vincent returns from the bathroom and points his gun at her, completing the standoff. Jules defuses it by negotiation with Pumpkin — the passage re-read, the money conceded, the briefcase kept — and the couple are allowed to leave with the takings. She exits the film as she entered it: at Pumpkin's side, at full volume, alive by a stranger's theology.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Devotion at full volume

Interpretation: everything Honey Bunny feels arrives at maximum amplitude — love, loyalty, terror. The archive reads her not as comic relief but as the film's honesty about armed robbery: this is what the adrenaline in the room actually sounds like, and every professional in the scene is managing her weather.

The loyalist

Interpretation: her hysteria is not cowardice — she holds her position and her aim at a man holding her boyfriend at gunpoint, outnumbered by professionals. The film gives her the robber's version of courage: she does not run, she screams and stays.

Interpretation: Pumpkin and Honey Bunny — the film's most domestic names belong to its most volatile couple. 09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The suspense is hers

Analysis: structurally, the epilogue's tension does not live in whether Jules can outdraw Pumpkin — he already has — but in whether Honey Bunny can be kept from firing. The film hands its climax's trigger to its least powerful character, and the archive reads that as the sermon's real test: Jules's conversion is proven not by sparing the man he controls, but by saving the woman he doesn't.

A one-location file, honestly kept

Analysis / honest record: Yolanda exists in a single continuous event; the sources give her no backstory, no surname beyond the film's usage, and no later fate. This page documents the event at full weight and invents nothing around it.
