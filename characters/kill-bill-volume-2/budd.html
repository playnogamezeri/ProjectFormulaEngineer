01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Budd is the saga's ground truth: what the life pays, after the life. Once Sidewinder of the Deadly Vipers, he is found bouncing at a strip club where the boss berates him over a flooded toilet, living in a desert trailer with his drinking and his grievances itemised — the sword Bill gave him (he tells Bill he pawned it for $250; he is lying, and the lie is the brothers' whole relationship in one cruelty), and a philosophy no other Viper permits himself: that they deserve Beatrix's revenge. He is also, not coincidentally, the only target who beats her. Forewarned by Bill, Budd meets the Black Mamba's ambush with a rock-salt shotgun, sedates her, sells her sword for a million dollars and buries her alive with a working man's thoroughness — flashlight provided, out of a killer's vestigial courtesy. Madsen plays him, per the Galaxy card, ruined and philosophical at once. His death is the saga's most squalid: counting Elle's million in his trailer, opened case, a black mamba to the face — the Sidewinder, killed by a snake, mid-payment, in the profession's true afterlife: not honour, but a cash transaction gone wrong.

ROLE

The honest ruin

Principal supporting; the only Viper who defeats Beatrix, and the saga's portrait of the life's pension plan.

DEFINING TRAIT

Deservedness, admitted

He alone of the squad grants the justice of her campaign — "we deserve to die" — and then fights her anyway, with the best result of anyone: his philosophy and his professionalism are both genuine.

FATE

Bitten at the till

Killed by Elle's black mamba hidden in the sword money: the Sidewinder, by a snake, in his own trailer.

Place in the story

Budd is the saga's reality principle: against Bill's aesthetics and Elle's connoisseurship he sets the strip club, the toilet, the trailer and the rock salt — the underworld as shift work. His victory over Beatrix is the film's respect for the principle: the ruined man, taken seriously, is the most dangerous one.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Budd's biography is told backwards from his trailer: Sidewinder, Deadly Viper, Bill's brother — and now a bouncer in Barstow, twenty minutes late to an empty club, being fired by Larry Gomez over a flooded toilet and taking the shift back on sufferance. The film gives his ruin no single cause; it is presented as the life's ordinary sediment — the drinking, the desert, the grievance against Bill kept like a pet. When Bill visits to warn him that Beatrix lives and the list is moving, Budd delivers the saga's most honest line of dialogue: they deserve her revenge, all of them; perhaps they should let her have it. He salts the wound with the sword — the Hanzo blade Bill gave him, priceless, which he claims to have pawned for $250. (He has not; the sword is in a golf bag in the trailer, as the Elle fight later proves. The lie is aimed at Bill's heart, and it lands.)

Then the ruined man goes to work. Beatrix comes at night to ambush him; Budd, forewarned and waiting behind his own door, receives her with both barrels of rock salt — the saga's only clean defeat of its heroine — sedates her, and conducts the profession's most businesslike evening: her sword sold to Elle by phone for a million dollars, cash; and her body disposed of with a craftsman's thoroughness, nailed into a coffin and buried in the grave of Paula Schulz, with a flashlight left in the coffin — a killer's courtesy, or a working man's completeness. The burial fails, as the film's middle hour shows. Days later Elle arrives with the money case; Budd, counting his million at the trailer table, opens it onto a black mamba — Elle's payment in full — and dies of the bite in his own home, the Sidewinder killed by the saga's smallest snake while the woman he buried watches from the dark outside, denied her kill. His death, reported to Beatrix by Elle mid-fight, draws the saga's most professional epitaph: distaste that Elle did it that way — the list had an etiquette, and Budd, of all of them, had earned a cleaner line on it.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The philosopher of the trailer park

Interpretation: Budd's honesty is his ruin's compensation — having lost everything the life promised, he can afford the truth about it, and his "we deserve to die" is the saga's only unforced moral judgment from inside the squad. The film rewards the honesty with competence: he is the best of them at the actual work, still.

The brother's cruelty

The pawned-sword lie is his one indulgence: a small, precise cruelty aimed at Bill, maintained at the cost of the truth, in a life that has given up every other luxury. Madsen plays it as a man spending his last coin — the hatred of his brother, kept like a pet, fed once more.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
THE AMBUSH

Rock salt and patience

He defeats the saga's heroine outright — forewarning, position, the right load for the range, sedation, disposal: a complete professional performance by the squad's most ruined member.

TRUTH-TELLING

The deservedness doctrine

His moral clarity is a tactical asset: a man who has priced his own death correctly cannot be frightened, rushed or flattered — Elle's snake is the only argument that gets past him, and it is not an argument.

WEAKNESS

The money

A lifetime's ruin has left exactly one appetite unguarded: he opens the case. The million dollars — the sword's price, his retirement, the life's arrears — is the bait his philosophy cannot refuse, and the film kills him with his own invoice.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The Sidewinder and the mamba

Budd's codename is fulfilled by his death: the snake-named man is killed by the saga's actual snake — and not even his own adversary's (Beatrix is the Black Mamba; Elle merely delivers one). Interpretation: the film's codenames are horoscopes; the squad dies inside its own heraldry.

The unpawned sword

The Hanzo blade in the golf bag is the trailer's buried truth: he kept the gift and lied about it, which means the ruin is not total — the brother's bond survives in the one object he could not sell and could not admit to keeping.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The reality principle

Budd's chapters are the saga's tonal floor — strip club, trailer, cemetery dirt — against which Bill's aesthetics and Pai Mei's mythology are measured. The film's wager is that the floor is where the truth lives: his deservedness doctrine is the only moral accounting the squad ever produces, and it comes from the man at the bottom.

Madsen's economy

The performance is famously unhurried — Madsen drawls the philosophy and ambles the ambush — and the archive notes the discipline: any visible effort would break the character's premise, that the most dangerous man in the saga is the one who has stopped trying to be.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Michael Madsen

Madsen, Tarantino's Mr. Blonde a decade earlier, was cast as the saga's ruin-in-residence; the performance's drawl and bulk are the character's biography, and the director has cited Budd as the saga's most truthful figure.

The rock salt

The ambush's load — non-lethal by design — is the character's professionalism in one detail: Budd needs her buried, not shot, and the film's most practical killer chooses his ammunition like a man who has read the whole assignment.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Budd endures as the saga's most quoted minor tragedy: the only man who beat the Bride, the only Viper who admitted the bill was just, and the profession's definitive pensioner — killed not by his enemy but by his payment, in a trailer, counting it.
