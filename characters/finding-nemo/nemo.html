01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Nemo is the sole survivor of a barracuda — one damaged egg out of hundreds, born with a small fin his father calls his lucky one — and he has been raised inside a promise: nothing will ever happen to him. On his first day of school the promise becomes a humiliation, and his defiance — swimming out to touch a boat at the drop-off, to prove he can — delivers him into a diver's net and a dentist's tank in Sydney. There the film's second education begins: under Gill, a scarred escape artist who sees in Nemo the gang's one chance, the protected child becomes an initiate, an engineer of escapes, and finally — in the harbour, organising a net full of terrified strangers to swim down together — the rescuer of the film's climax. The title says he is the one who is found; the film's finer point is that he is also, by the end, a finder.

ROLE

The sought

Co-protagonist; the missing son whose parallel captivity is the film's second narrative.

DEFINING TRAIT

Defiance becoming capability

The same impulse that gets him captured — the refusal to be only protected — becomes, under Gill's tuition, the competence that frees a net.

FATE

The rescuer

He escapes the tank, reunites with his father, and leads the trawler's catch to freedom — then goes back to school, waved off like any other kid.

Place in the story

Nemo's page is the film's second ledger: everything Marlin learns in the ocean, Nemo learns in the tank, in miniature and at higher stakes. The archive treats the two educations as one story told in two classrooms — the current and the aquarium — which is the film's structural signature.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Nemo hatches the sole survivor of his clutch, his right fin stunted from the damage to his egg, and is raised by Marlin inside the vow. He grows up eager — for school, for the drop-off, for the world his father describes only in warnings. On the first day, Marlin's hovering humiliates him before his classmates; when his father tries to withdraw him from the excursion, Nemo swims out to the speedboat anchored off the drop-off and touches its hull in open defiance. A diver's net takes him in front of Marlin's eyes.

He wakes in a fish tank in the Sydney dental office of Philip Sherman — collected as a gift for the dentist's niece. The Tank Gang receives him: led by Gill, a scarred Moorish idol taken from the ocean young, who has spent his captivity engineering escapes. The gang's news is a death sentence with a date: Nemo is to be given to Darla, Sherman's niece, whose previous fish have died in her care. Gill's plan makes Nemo its instrument — only he is small enough to enter the filter tube and jam it with a pebble, dirtying the tank until Sherman bags the fish for cleaning, when they can roll to the window and the harbour. Nemo's first attempt nearly kills him in the machinery; Gill, who sent him, is left with the guilt of a commander.

The second education completes itself by news: Nigel the pelican brings the tank word of Marlin's journey — the legend the turtles spread has reached Sydney — and Nemo, learning his father crossed the ocean for him, jams the filter successfully. The plan is foiled by technology: Sherman cleans the tank overnight and installs a new filtration system. Then Darla arrives. Nemo plays dead to escape her bag; in the office chaos, with Nigel's delivery of Marlin unfolding in the same minutes, Gill gets him to the sink and down the drain to the harbour. Reunited with Marlin — by Dory's finding — he faces the film's last test inside a trawler's net, where Dory and a school of groupers are trapped: Nemo persuades his father to let him enter the net, leads the catch to swim down in unison, and breaks the trawler's gear. Weeks later he is dropped at school like any other child — which is the film's definition of a miracle.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The defiance, correctly read

Interpretation: Nemo's boat-touching is usually filed as disobedience; the film files it as a referendum. A child whose world is a list of prohibitions will eventually test the largest one, and the father's humiliation of him in public chose the moment. The tank teaches him the defiance's better form: risk, in a cause, with a plan.

Gill's pupil

Under the gang he acquires what the anemone could not give him: a trade, a crew, and a commander who apologises when his plan nearly kills him. Interpretation: Nemo's growth is apprenticeship — competence transferred by a scarred professional who treats him as capable, the film's counter-model to protection.

The organiser

His climax is not an escape but a rescue of strangers, conducted by persuasion inside a net. The archive records the promotion plainly: the protected child ends the film protecting a crowd.

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

SWIMMING

The lucky fin, sufficient

His small fin is the film's standing refutation of his father's file on him: he crosses the tank's machinery, the harbour and the net on it.

ENGINEERING

The filter plan

Twice enters the filter tube — the gang's only operative small enough — and the second time succeeds.

LEADERSHIP

The net

Persuades a trawler's catch of strangers to swim down together: command, exercised at seven years old, in the film's climax.

WEAKNESS

The referendum impulse

His defiance, unplanned, is what the divers collect on; the film is honest that the trait and the gift are one trait at two ages.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The lucky fin

Interpretation: Marlin's euphemism for the damage is the film's gentlest lie and its thesis in miniature — the fin is not lucky, it is simply his, and the story ends when everyone, father included, stops pricing it. Nemo's competence is never despite the fin in the film's accounting; it is merely alongside it, unremarked at last.

The tank as the anemone

Interpretation: Nemo trades one enclosure for another — his father's glass for the dentist's — and the film's symmetry is exact: he escapes the second by capability, and the first by his father's education. Both cages open in the same week.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the parallel education

The analysis the archive records: the film cross-cuts two curricula — the ocean teaching the father to let go, the tank teaching the son to hold on (to a plan, a crew, a nerve). They graduate in the same scene, in the net, where each must trust the other's new competence simultaneously. The structure is the meaning: neither education is sufficient alone.

The child as agent

Against the era's kidnapped-child plots, Nemo is given the captivity's engineering, its intelligence (via Nigel) and its climax. The archive notes the discipline: he never stops being seven — his plans are Gill's, his courage is a child's — but the film consistently lets him act, which is what his father's arc must learn to permit.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Alexander Gould

Gould, a child actor, voices Nemo; the production cast for unforced naturalism, and his register anchors the tank sequences' kid-among-convicts design.

The deleted Gill

The documented production record includes a deleted scene in which Gill's invented backstory (a place called Bad Luck Bay) is exposed as a lie by a storybook — cut material, recorded here as production history, not film fact.

Production

Nemo's tank world was designed from Stanton's childhood memory of a dentist's aquarium — the assumption, which became the film, that the fish were from the ocean and wanted to go home.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Nemo became one of the most recognised child characters in animation — the small-finned clownfish on a generation's lunchboxes — and, within the Galaxy record, the standard for the captive-as-agent: a sought child whose own education carries half the film. His net rescue remains the picture's defining image of competence transferred from father to son and back again.
