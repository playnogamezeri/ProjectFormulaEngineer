01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Coral appears in one sequence of Finding Nemo, and the film is the consequence of it. She is Marlin's wife, newly housed in an anemone above the drop-off, mother of a clutch of eggs days from hatching — playful where Marlin is already careful, the parent who teases him about his worrying and names, among the hundreds of possible names, the one she likes: Nemo. When the barracuda comes, Marlin's instinct is the anemone; Coral's is the eggs. She charges back toward them, against his grip and the predator, and the film cuts to her husband waking alone. Her page follows the archive's rule for figures who never return: she is the plot's emotional core and appears in person at its origin, and her file states the fragmentary nature of her presence rather than inventing her a life beyond the four minutes the film gives her — which are, by the film's own construction, the four minutes everything else is about.

ROLE

The origin

The film's first loss; the mother whose death organises every subsequent scene.

DEFINING TRAIT

The charge toward the eggs

Her one decisive act is the film's founding asymmetry: she runs toward the danger her husband survives by fleeing, and the saga is his life in the aftermath of her choice.

FATE

Killed by the barracuda

She dies defending the clutch in the opening sequence; one damaged egg survives her.

Every rule Marlin enforces, every warning he gives, is a memorial practice addressed to her — and the son she named carries her choice of name into every scene.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Coral and Marlin have just moved into their anemone on the reef's edge, above the drop-off, with their first clutch of eggs hidden below — hundreds of them, days from hatching. The opening sequence is the marriage in miniature: the new home admired, the view debated (Marlin already anxious about the neighbourhood's exposure; Coral amused by him), the children discussed. She favours one name among the possibilities — Nemo. Then the water stills, the reef empties, and the barracuda is there.

Marlin's move is to pull her into the anemone. Coral breaks from him and charges down toward the eggs. The barracuda strikes; Marlin, fighting, is knocked unconscious against the coral. He wakes to silence: Coral gone, the clutch gone, the ledge empty — except for one egg, damaged, in the shadows below, which he cradles and names with her name for it. The film never shows Coral again, and never stops showing her: the vow Marlin swears over the egg is her monument, and the story is its hundred-minute consequence.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The unafraid parent

Interpretation: in the marriage's only scenes, Coral is the risk-tolerant one — she chose the exposed home, laughs at the worrying, and runs toward the eggs. The film's irony is structural: the cautious parent survives to raise the child in caution, and the brave one's bravery is inherited by the son as defiance.

The naming

Her one bequest that survives intact is a word: Nemo. Interpretation: the film gives the dead mother the title itself — the story is named with her choice, spoken by her widower over the egg she died defending.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The charge toward the eggs

Interpretation: the film's founding asymmetry — one parent flees to survive, one charges to protect, and the survivor raises the child in the philosophy of the one who fled, while the child grows into the temperament of the one who charged. The whole saga is contained in the parents' two directions in a single second.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the absent centre

The analysis the archive records follows this run's rule for such figures: Coral is kept because she is the plot's emotional core and appears in person at its origin — the Helen Wick precedent, in an anemone. Her function is to be the specific shape of Marlin's grief: not a generic lost wife but a woman whose one recorded instinct was the opposite of his, which is why his parenting is an argument with her as much as a memorial to her.

The front-loaded death

The production record documents Stanton's decision to show Coral's death at the beginning rather than reveal it in flashback — so that the audience carries it, as Marlin does, through every scene. The archive records the consequence: her four minutes are the film's most efficient, because the remaining hundred are spent inside them.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Elizabeth Perkins

Perkins voices Coral; the role is four minutes long and the production treated it as the film's foundation — cast, per the record, for warmth that could survive its own brevity.

The flashback decision

Stanton originally planned to reveal Coral's death in flashbacks and reversed the design, judging that a late reveal would leave nothing to uncover; the death opens the film instead.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Coral stands in the Galaxy record as animation's model of the absent centre — the character whose four minutes organise a classic — and as the first half of the diptych (her charge, his perimeter) that the film's whole meaning hangs on. Her name for her son is the title the world knows the story by.
