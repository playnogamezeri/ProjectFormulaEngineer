01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Autumn has one scene, no surname, no history and the film's last word — her name. She is a fellow applicant at the architecture interview Tom attends on day 500, Wednesday, May 23: they talk in the waiting room, discover they share his favourite spot in the city and his dislike of the parking lots that spoil it, and when he invites her for coffee afterwards she politely declines — then changes her mind. He asks her name. "Autumn." The on-screen clock winds back to its start. The film's honesty about her is total and is the point: she is not a character but a season arriving, and everything the audience projects onto her — rescue, repetition, hope, doom — is the audience performing Tom's own founding act, casting a stranger from a shared taste and a laugh, in a waiting room, in one scene. Her page records the scene exactly, and the readings as readings.

ROLE

The next season; the final word

The film's closing figure: not a resolution but a rotation — the counter reset, the weather changing, the question of what Tom has learned handed to the audience.

DEFINING TRAIT

Arrival

She exists in the record as pure beginning: a shared favourite spot, a changed mind about coffee, a name that is a season. The film gives her nothing else because anything else would be Tom's edit starting again.

FATE

Unrecorded, deliberately

The film ends at her name. Whether she is Tom's future, his relapse or a stranger he has coffee with once is withheld by design, and this page invents nothing beyond the waiting room.

Place in the story

Autumn is the Galaxy's smallest page and its most pointed: the archive treats her file as the record of a single scene that contains the whole film — the casting impulse, the audience's complicity in it, and a clock rewinding over both.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Autumn's documented course is one scene, and this page gives it entire. Day 500: Tom Hansen, out of the greeting card company, portfolio in hand, attends an interview at an architecture firm. In the waiting room is another applicant. They talk. She knows his favourite spot in the city — the bench, the buildings — and shares his opinion of the parking lots that spoil the view. As he is called in, he invites her for coffee afterwards. She politely declines. Then she changes her mind, and accepts. He asks her name, and she gives the film its last word: Autumn. The on-screen day counter — the film's memory-interface, which has jumped across 500 days out of order — winds back to its start. End of record. The archive notes what the scene withholds with the same care as what it shows: no surname, no history, no indication of what the coffee leads to, and no evidence about Tom's state of mind beyond the invitation itself — extended, this time, by a man who has quit the wrong job, done the re-reading his sister prescribed, and wished his Summer happiness and meant it. Whether that man casts Autumn or meets her is the question the film ends by asking its audience, having spent 500 days teaching them what casting looks like.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The seasons

Summer and Autumn are the film's calendar made personal: the symbol of its whole philosophy — that what Tom experienced as destiny was weather, that weather is real while it lasts, and that its turning is not a betrayal but a climate.

The rewound clock

The day counter returning to zero over her name is the film's final image of memory: the archive reads it as neither promise nor warning but as the instrument itself, reset — what gets recorded on it this time is the open question the audience is left holding.

The audience, cast

The archive's reading: Autumn's scene is the film's final experiment, run on the viewer. After 500 days of instruction in Tom's error — casting a stranger from shared taste and a feeling of recognition — the film presents a stranger, a shared taste and a recognition, and stops. Audiences who find the ending hopeful and audiences who find it ominous are both reporting their own casting decisions, which is the film's point and its last joke: the experiment's final subject is the person watching.
