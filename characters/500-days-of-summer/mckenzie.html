01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

McKenzie is the film's prime mover disguised as its comic relief: without his drunken karaoke-night announcement — Tom's private feelings, broadcast to the office and to Summer herself — there is no relationship, no 500 days, no film. The Galaxy's film page calls him the office's cheerful agent of chaos, and his function across the picture is consistent: he is the friend whose loyalty expresses itself as detonation. He blurts the secret that starts it, joins the coalition pressing Tom to define it, and counsels him through the wreckage with the friend's classic mixture of loyalty and uselessness. The film is fond of him and honest about him: his one great intervention works by accident, against the wishes of everyone it concerns, and the archive files him as the Galaxy's patron of the unplanned — the proof that destinies, when they arrive, usually arrive drunk and holding a microphone.

ROLE

The catalyst; the chorus

The relationship's accidental founder and Tom's longest-serving commentator: he starts the plot and then narrates its sidelines with the other friends.

DEFINING TRAIT

Loyalty as detonation

His help arrives as exposure — secrets announced, questions forced — the Galaxy's film page's agent of chaos, whose every intervention is an uncontrolled disclosure in a good cause.

FATE

The survivor of the office

He remains at the card company Tom quits, in the ordinary world of the chorus: the film leaves him where it found him, which is his function's definition.

Place in the story

McKenzie is the Galaxy's patron of the unplanned beginning: the archive treats his page as the record of the film's first cause — a microphone, a drunk friend, and a destiny that needed both.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

McKenzie's documented course is a chorus part with one solo. He is established in the office's daily life — a fellow writer at the greeting card company, Tom's drinking companion, one of the two friends who constitute Tom's adult counsel. His solo comes early and decides everything: at the office karaoke night, drunk, he announces to the room — and therefore to Summer — that Tom is in love with her. The disclosure is a catastrophe of method and a triumph of outcome: Summer, informed, acts on her own initiative, and the relationship Tom's destiny-script had scheduled as fate begins instead as a leak. The archive records the film's quiet irony without underlining: the most fated-feeling romance in the Galaxy's collection was started by a drunk man with a microphone, and its hero's metaphysics never recovers the information.

Thereafter McKenzie returns to the chorus: he joins the friends' campaign to make Tom ask Summer where they stand, drinks with him through the golden days and the grey ones, and counsels him after the breakup in the register the Galaxy's film page identifies — loyalty and uselessness in the classic proportions. He is present at the office Tom melts down in and quits; he remains in the ordinary world the hero exits. His file closes with the chorus's traditional epitaph: he was there at the beginning, and the beginning was his fault, and nobody in the film — least of all Tom — would have it otherwise.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The blurter

Interpretation: McKenzie has no internal editor, which makes him the film's one reliable disclosure mechanism in a story full of withheld things — Tom's feelings, Summer's engagement, the relationship's actual state. His chaos is a solvent: whatever he touches becomes public.

Loyalty, uncalibrated

His interventions are all for Tom and none of them are measured: he will announce the secret, force the question, and prescribe for the heartbreak with the same cheerful total commitment. The film's fondness for him is the fondness for a force of nature that happens to be on your side.

The chorus's realism

Beneath the chaos he is one of the film's ordinary people — a job, a bar, a friend in trouble — and his counsel's uselessness is the ordinary kind: he has no better map of love than Tom does, only a louder voice.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
THE DISCLOSURE

Secrets, detonated

One karaoke announcement accomplishes what Tom's months of longing could not: the relationship exists because McKenzie cannot keep a secret while drunk. As a plot instrument he is unmatched in the film.

PRESENCE

The friend who shows up

Bars, offices, interventions, aftermaths: his reliability is attendance. In a film about a man editing his memories, McKenzie is unedited — always in the room, saying the thing.

WEAKNESS

No calibration

The same instrument that starts the relationship is useless for steering it: his counsel is generic, his timing is alcoholic, and his one great success is indistinguishable from an accident — a fact the film files fondly and exactly.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The microphone

Destiny's instrument in this film is a karaoke mic in a drunk friend's hand: the symbol of the story's whole anti-metaphysics — beginnings are not written in stars but announced, badly, in bars.

The chorus

McKenzie and Paul are the film's Greek chorus in office clothes — commenting, warning, failing to alter anything except the one thing McKenzie alters by accident: the symbol of friendship's real powers, which are attendance and detonation.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The accidental founder

The archive's reading: the film's argument about fate is completed by McKenzie's existence. Tom experiences the relationship as destiny; the audience watches its actual causal chain — a new assistant, a shared song, a drunk's announcement — and the chain is made of accidents at every link. McKenzie is the chain's loudest link, and his page records him as the standing refutation, inside the film itself, of its hero's philosophy: nothing was written; a man with a microphone was thirsty.
