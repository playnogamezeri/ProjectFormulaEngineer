01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Logan — James Howlett, the Wolverine — is dying in 2029, and the film is the account of what he does with the remainder. The adamantium bonded to his skeleton is poisoning him as his healing factor fades; he drives a limousine in El Paso, hustles for medication, and hides Charles Xavier in a Mexican smelting plant, saving for a boat that would put the old man's seizures safely out to sea. Then Gabriela López offers him fifty thousand dollars to drive a child to North Dakota, and the Wolverine's last story turns out to be a father's.

ROLE

The last Wolverine

Protagonist; the film is his farewell and his only act of fatherhood.

DEFINING TRAIT

Endurance outliving purpose

He has survived everything except obsolescence; the film asks what a weapon does when the wars end and the weapon remains.

FATE

Dies at Eden's edge

Impaled by X-24 on a fallen tree, he dies holding his daughter's hand — "So this is what it feels like."

Place in the story

Logan is the film, in the way few protagonists are: its western structure, its elegy for the mutant age and its argument about legacy all run through his body — the poisoned metal inside it, the daughter cloned from it, the clone built to replace it. The Galaxy's other X-Men records belong to a younger man; this page is the same man at the end of the account.

Jackman's final performance in the role he had played for seventeen years is built on subtraction: the physique diminished, the voice roughened, the famous rage arriving slower and costing more each time. The archive notes the critical consensus documented at release — this was received as the definitive Wolverine performance precisely because it is the first one about weakness.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

By 2029 no new mutants have been born in twenty-five years — Transigen's gene-suppressing additives in the food supply saw to that — and the X-Men are gone: a year before the film, one of Charles Xavier's seizures, his dementing brain losing control of the most powerful telepathy on Earth, killed several of them. Logan, his healing factor fading and the adamantium in his bones slowly poisoning him, works as a chauffeur and cares for Xavier in hiding with Caliban, saving to buy a boat — the Sunseeker — where Xavier's seizures could harm no one.

Gabriela López, a Transigen nurse, offers him $50,000 to escort her and an eleven-year-old girl, Laura, to "Eden" in North Dakota. Logan stalls; Gabriela is murdered by Donald Pierce before the trip begins, and Laura is found stowed in his limo. In the escape from the plant, Caliban is captured. From Gabriela's phone video the truth arrives: Transigen bred mutant children from DNA samples as weapons — the X-23 programme — and, its X-24 perfected, ordered the children terminated. The nurses helped some escape. Laura was bred from Logan's DNA. She is his daughter.

Logan drives north with Xavier and Laura, disbelieving: Eden, he discovers, appears in Laura's X-Men comic books, and he tells her it is fiction — the stories were never real the way she needs them to be. In Oklahoma City a Xavier seizure freezes a hotel; Logan kills the Reavers who come through it one by one and gets them out. On the highway they help the Munson family and accept a dinner invitation Logan argues against — the film's one warm room — and that night X-24, a feral young clone of Logan himself, comes for Laura. X-24 kills Xavier and the Munsons before Will Munson pins it with his truck; Logan escapes with Laura and Xavier's body, and buries the professor by a roadside lake.

Laura drives the unconscious Logan to a doctor and then on to North Dakota, where Eden proves real: the escaped children are gathered, preparing to cross into Canada. Logan, refusing to go further, sends her on — then, as Rice and the Reavers close in on the children, takes the last of the nurses' serum to restore his healing for a few burning minutes and runs into the forest. He kills Rice and cuts through the Reavers; X-24 impales him on the stump of a fallen tree. Laura kills X-24 with the adamantium bullet Logan had carried for himself. He dies holding her hand, telling her not to become what they made her, and naming the feeling: "So this is what it feels like." The children bury him. Laura turns the cross on his grave sideways, into an X.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

A man rationing himself

Interpretation: Logan's defining behaviour in the film is refusal — of the job, of the girl, of the word "daughter," of Eden. Each refusal is a man measuring what he has left and declining to spend it. The arc is the refusals failing, one by one, against a child who simply assumes he will come.

Guilt as his last superpower

He carries the adamantium bullet — an exit he has considered and not taken — and the film reads his survival as penance rather than luck. Interpretation: Logan does not believe he deserves the boat, the sea or the quiet; he believes he deserves the plant, the bottle and the professor's care, and he performs all three like duties.

Fatherhood recognised late

He never says the word until the end, and then only by implication — but he teaches Laura the film's whole curriculum: how to drive, when to run, what the comics got wrong, and finally, in his last sentence to her, what not to become. Interpretation: the film's Logan is a man who can only express love as instruction and sacrifice, and it costs him his life to say the rest with his hand.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
HEALING FACTOR

Failing

Once near-instant, now slow and incomplete: wounds linger, scars accumulate, and the adamantium his body once tolerated is killing him.

ADAMANTIUM SKELETON & CLAWS

The poison and the weapon

Three retractable claws per hand; the same metal that made him unbreakable is the agent of his death.

COMBAT

Two centuries of it

Even diminished, he kills a hotel floor of Reavers while frozen victims stand around him, and, serum-burning, cuts through the forest force.

WEAKNESS

Mortality, arriving

Age, poison, drink and despair: the film's Logan can be hurt by things that once would not have slowed him, and X-24 — himself, young — is beyond him without help.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The western

The film quotes Shane openly — Xavier watches it in the hotel, and its dialogue returns at Logan's grave. Interpretation: Logan is placed deliberately in the gunfighter's elegy: the killer who cannot outlive the age he made, dying so that children can live in a world that will not need him.

The cross turned to an X

Laura's adjustment of his grave marker is the film's thesis in one gesture: the faith he never held is set aside, and the identity he ran from — the X — is what his daughter chooses to remember him by.

Adamantium

The metal is his biography: what the world put into him to make him a weapon is what kills him, slowly, once his body can no longer forgive it. Interpretation: the film argues that what was done to Logan was always a death sentence; it just took the healing factor's failure to read it.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the elegy that closes the series

Logan was designed as a farewell — to Jackman's Wolverine, to Stewart's Xavier, and to the Fox X-Men era's first generation. The analysis the archive records is that the film earns the elegy structurally: it does not resurrect the old world for one last ride but prices its absence into every scene — the empty roads, the comic books as scripture, the children bred from relics.

The investigation: what killed the mutants?

The film answers its own mystery in layers: no villain wiped them out in battle; a corporation adjusted the food supply, a great mind broke, and time finished the work. The archive notes this as the film's bleakest device — extinction as administration, which is why its antagonist is a project manager and its weapon is a product line.

10 / KEY MOMENTS

Key Moments

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
The limo fight

Thieves try to strip his car; a slower, drunker Wolverine than any before puts them down and is winded by it — the film's whole premise in its first scene.

The hotel seizure

A frozen casino of victims; Logan staggering through them, killing the Reavers one by one, to reach the old man at the centre.

The Munson dinner

Grace, laughter and a working tap: the life Logan is shown, for one evening, exactly too late.

Burying Xavier

A eulogy that collapses into rage at a broken water pump — grief with nowhere grammatical to go.

"So this is what it feels like"

Death, named by the man who spent two centuries unable to have it; his daughter's hand in his.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Hugh Jackman

Jackman announced Logan as his final Wolverine performance after seventeen years and nine films in the role; directed by James Mangold (who also directed The Wolverine), the film premiered at the Berlin International Film Festival on February 17, 2017 and released March 3, 2017.

An R-rated western

The film was made as an R-rated, western-inflected drama — its Shane references are explicit in the text — and was received as a landmark of the superhero genre, including an Academy Award nomination for its screenplay, a first for a live-action superhero film.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Logan closed the founding era of the modern superhero film: the Wolverine who began the Fox franchise in 2000 ended it by dying of old age, poison and fatherhood in 2017. Within the Galaxy it stands as the X-Men records' terminus — every earlier mutant page in this archive is, in story terms, a document of the world this film buries.
