01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

X-24 is Transigen's final product: a clone of Logan in his physical prime, grown to adulthood without a childhood, feral, mute with rage, and controlled — insofar as it is controlled — by Rice's handlers and a green serum that stokes its fury. It exists because the X-23 children disappointed their maker by becoming people. X-24 is the correction: all of the Wolverine's lethality with none of the Wolverine. It kills Charles Xavier in the dark, wearing Logan's face, and it kills Logan himself in the forest — before Laura, the obsolete model, puts her father's bullet through its head.

ROLE

The final weapon

Antagonist of the last act; Logan's replacement and executioner.

DEFINING TRAIT

Rage without biography

It has Logan's body and none of his two centuries: no mercy, no memory, no off-switch of its own.

FATE

Shot by Laura

Killed by the adamantium bullet through the head, moments after killing Logan.

Place in the story

X-24 is the film's argument made flesh. Everything Logan believes about himself — that the weapon is all he is — stands in the farmhouse doorway wearing his young face; everything he hopes — that the person matters more than the product — is what the clone lacks. Their two fights bookend the film's final movement, and the clone wins the second one. It takes the daughter to settle the question.

Jackman plays both Logan and X-24 — the film's central doubling is literal in the casting. The archive notes the performance distinction: X-24 moves like Logan's body remembers moving, without the economy age taught it; there is no thought in it, and Jackman plays the absence as carefully as he plays the man.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

X-24 was grown by Transigen as the successor to the X-23 children: where they were bred from many mutants and raised — disastrously, from the company's view — by nurses who mothered them, X-24 was produced from Logan's genome alone and raised as equipment. It is Logan in his prime: the healing, the claws, the strength, with the personality left uninstalled. Rice keeps it as the programme's proof and its enforcement of last resort.

Its debut is the Munson farmhouse. Sent in at night while Logan is away dealing with the farm's water dispute, X-24 enters the house and is met by Xavier — who, confused in the dark, takes it for Logan and speaks to it about the Sunseeker and the life they were going to have. X-24 runs him through. It kills the Munson family, overpowers the returning Logan — the older body cannot match its own younger one — and takes Laura. Only Will Munson's dying act, pinning it with his truck, and Caliban's grenades in the confusion, break its hold; Logan and Laura escape.

In the forest below Eden, X-24 is unleashed for the final assault on the children. Logan, burning the last of the serum to restore his own healing for minutes, fights it as an equal and is losing as the strength fades; X-24 drags him onto the stump of a fallen tree and impales him on its broken branches. As it turns for Laura, she shoots it through the head with the adamantium bullet — the one Logan had carried for himself. The replacement dies beside the original, killed by the generation it was built to replace.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

A person-shaped absence

Interpretation: X-24 has no psychology to analyse in the ordinary sense, and the film knows it — that is the horror it embodies. What can be recorded is the shape of the lack: no hesitation, no recognition, no response to Xavier's voice speaking Logan's name to it. It is appetite and instruction, nothing else.

The handlers' leash

It is not even autonomous evil: Rice's people dose it, direct it and steady it. Interpretation: X-24 is the only character in the film with literally no choices, which makes it less a villain than a verdict on its makers — the film saves its moral accounting for them.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The self, unburdened

Interpretation: X-24 is Logan's worst belief about himself, manufactured: that he is only a weapon, and the man was an accident of time. The film answers through Laura — the same genome, raised by love — and lets the two products fight over the original's body.

Youth as horror

Every other character in the film is diminished by age; X-24 alone is at full strength, and it is the monster. The film inverts the genre's worship of prime condition: here, the prime is the obscenity, and the ruin is the hero.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the thesis antagonist

X-24 exists so Logan can fight his own file. The analysis the archive records is that the clone converts the film's theme into choreography: experience versus specification, the person versus the product — and the film is honest enough to let specification win the fight and lose the war, because the daughter is standing behind it with the bullet.

Why it cannot be the final killer and the final victim by Logan's hand

Logan cannot kill X-24 — the film is exact about this: the old man lacks the strength. The killing must be Laura's, because the argument is generational: the future disposes of the replacement, not the past. The archive notes this as the film's most disciplined structural choice.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Hugh Jackman, twice

Jackman performs both roles; the farmhouse and forest fights were staged as the film's climactic doubling, with the clone distinguished by movement and silence rather than by makeup alone.

A comics echo

X-24 has no direct comics counterpart; its nearest relative is Albert, the android Wolverine duplicate of the comics — the film's version replaces the robot with a clone, in keeping with its genetic programme plot.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

X-24 is the Fox era's final monster: the Wolverine with the man removed, killed by the Wolverine with the childhood restored. In the Galaxy's records it stands as the counter-argument to every Weapon X page before it — proof, written by its makers and refuted by its death, that the weapon was never the interesting part.
