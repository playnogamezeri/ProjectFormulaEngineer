01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Tint is introduced at his recreation: prisoners forced to run a mine-seeded paddy while he watches, smoking, with a gambler's mild interest. The film's portrait of the Burmese junta is concentrated in that patience — this is not a raging tyrant but an administrator of atrocity, a major for whom massacre is a unit activity and prisoners are a resource: boys drafted, women held, villages taxed in blood. He takes the missionaries as inventory. He loses them to a night infiltration, pursues them to the river with everything he has, and dies there at the end of Rambo's machete — the film's one killing it stages as a sentence rather than a battle.

ROLE

The local terror

Antagonist; Tatmadaw major commanding the region's campaign of fear.

DEFINING TRAIT

Cruelty as routine

Tint's sadism is scheduled, delegated and watched at leisure — the film's argument that the junta's horror is bureaucratic before it is personal.

FATE

The machete

His pursuit is annihilated at the river; fleeing, he is killed by Rambo personally.

Place in the story

Tint is why the film exists: Stallone built the picture around the Karen war, and Tint is that war's face at unit level — the officer whose amusements are the documentary the opening newsreels promised.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Tint is introduced commanding his unit's campaign in the Karen region during the Saffron Revolution period: settlements pillaged, innocents slaughtered as instruction, teenage boys abducted for drafting, women held as sex slaves. His signature recreation is documented in the film's opening movement — prisoners forced across a mine-infested marsh, then gunned down, while he watches with a cigarette.

His unit attacks the Karen village the missionaries are serving: the villagers are massacred, two missionaries killed, and the survivors — Sarah and Burnett among them — taken to his camp as prisoners.

The camp is his administration's portrait: prisoners staked and tormented as routine, the unit's recreation budget spent on cruelty. For ten days the captives are his inventory.

The night rescue empties his cells. Tint pursues with his full force to the river, where the fugitives are cornered — until Rambo's .50-calibre stand and the Karen rebels' arrival convert the pursuit into a rout.

Tint flees the collapse and is killed by Rambo with a machete — the film's final sentence, delivered by the ferryman in person.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The administrator

Tint's evil is organizational: he schedules, delegates and spectates. Interpretation: the film indicts the system through the temperament — a sadist with a staff is a policy, not a personality.

The gambler's calm

The mined paddy is watched as sport with odds. His composure never breaks until the river — the first event in the film his administration did not schedule.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The documentary function

Tint is written less as a character than as evidence: Stallone's film is an advocacy piece about the Karen war, and its villain is the war's documented method wearing one face. Analysis: Khin's restraint serves the function — the performance never competes with the record it is presenting.

The personal sentence

In a film of industrial-scale violence, Tint's death is deliberately small — one man, one blade, no audience. The archive records the staging as the film's distinction between battle and judgment.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Performance

Maung Maung Khin, a Burmese actor, plays Tint; the production's use of Burmese and Karen performers and its Thai locations were part of its stated documentary intent.

The record

The character's campaign — pillage, drafting of boys, hostage-taking — follows the plot record's documented account of the unit's methods, drawn from the conflict's real reporting as the film presents it.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Tint remains the Rambo series' most historically grounded villain: not a mastermind but a major, doing the junta's paperwork in blood — and the film's advocacy is remembered through his face.
