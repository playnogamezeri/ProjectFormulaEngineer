01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Voldemort barely needs to appear in Part 1, and that is the point the film keeps making. His coup is complete in the opening movement: the Ministry falls, Hogwarts is occupied, the press and the radio are his, and a Taboo on his own name turns the country's speech into a tripwire. He is free, therefore, to pursue his private obsession — the wand problem. His own wand shares a core with Harry's and cannot kill him; he commandeers Lucius Malfoy's, duels Harry in the sky, and spends the film interrogating wand-makers and a prisoner in Nurmengard for the location of the Elder Wand. The film's last shot is his reward: Dumbledore's tomb cracked open, the wand in his hand, lightning called down in triumph.

ROLE

Antagonist; the state

He is no longer a raider but a government: his evil in this film is administrative — registries, courts, lists — while his person hunts a relic.

DEFINING TRAIT

Delegated terror

Others torture, register and snatch in his name; his own appearances are reserved for the wand quest and the humiliation of his servants.

FATE

The Elder Wand, taken

He ends the film holding the most powerful wand in the world, stolen from his dead enemy's grave — believing the wand problem solved.

Place in the story

Voldemort is the film's climate rather than its weather events: every scene the trio inhabit is shaped by his pressure systems — the Taboo, the Snatchers, the fallen Ministry, the hostage cellars. The Galaxy's film page calls him a weather system; the archive's page records the meteorology of a completed coup.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

The film opens at his table: Snape reports the date of Harry's move, and Voldemort — before his assembled Death Eaters — takes Lucius Malfoy's wand as his instrument, humiliating his host in the act of borrowing his weapon. In the Seven Potters ambush he duels Harry in the sky; the borrowed wand fails against Harry's, whose golden fire acts of its own accord, and Harry escapes. Thereafter Voldemort withdraws from the field war — which his state is winning without him — into the wand investigation: Ollivander, already his prisoner, is tortured for the truth about the twin cores; Gregorovitch, the foreign wand-maker, is hunted and killed after his memory is read; and at Nurmengard, Voldemort confronts the imprisoned Grindelwald, who laughs at him and tells him the Elder Wand lies with Dumbledore.

His regime's war proceeds in parallel and mostly off his own screen: the Ministry falls and Scrimgeour dies; the Registration Commission processes Muggle-borns; the Taboo on his name delivers the careless to Snatchers; Luna Lovegood is held in his host's cellar as a lever on her father's newspaper. When the trio are brought to Malfoy Manor, Voldemort is summoned — and arrives too late: the prisoners have escaped and his host's household is in disgrace. The film's final scene is his alone: the white tomb on the lake's shore, the lid cracked, Dumbledore's corpse, and the Elder Wand raised to a sky that answers with lightning.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The connoisseur of humiliation

Interpretation: Voldemort's pleasures in this film are administrative and social — the borrowed wand, the silenced table, the host ruined in his own house. His cruelty has been bureaucratised along with his state; he now prefers his victims processed.

The obsessive antiquarian

Beneath the coup, the old schoolboy hunger: a legendary wand, a grave to rob, a rival's relic to own. The film's irony is structural — the master of Britain spends his victory chasing a stick, while three teenagers in a tent dismantle his immortality.

Contempt as intelligence failure

He cannot model love, loyalty or elves: Dobby's rescue is the kind of event his worldview cannot predict, and the film keeps arranging his defeats along exactly that blind spot.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
THE STATE

A government of fear

Ministry, courts, press, radio and a name-Taboo: his power in this film is infrastructural, and the trio survive by being unfindable, not by outfighting it.

LEGILIMENCY AND INTERROGATION

The wand investigation

He reads Gregorovitch's memory, tortures Ollivander, and extracts the Elder Wand's location from Grindelwald: detective work by atrocity.

DUELLING

Still the apex predator

The sky duel is the film's one direct clash, and even it is decided by wand-lore rather than skill — a defeat his whole quest exists to correct.

WEAKNESS

The blind spot shaped like other people

House-elves, goblins, love and loyalty do not exist in his model of the world; the film's escapes keep walking through the gap.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The Taboo

His name as infrastructure: speak it and the state arrives. Interpretation: the film's purest image of totalitarianism — language itself wired as an alarm.

The borrowed wand

A tyrant reduced to borrowing his servant's weapon at his own table: his power's completeness and its one defect, in a single gesture.

The robbed grave

The film ends with a theft from a corpse: the master of the living world still shopping among the dead for the means to kill a boy.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Absence as characterisation

Voldemort's screen time in Part 1 is small and the film is more his than anyone's: the archive's reading is that the saga has graduated its villain from monster to climate — the condition every other character's scene is weather inside. His delegation is not a diminishment but the final form of his power, and the film's horror is that the climate works: registries, courts and lists, run by clerks.

The two quests

The film's structure is a race between relic-hunts: his for a wand that kills, theirs for objects that unmake him. The analysis the archive draws: Voldemort's quest is acquisitive (one more thing to own) and the trio's is subtractive (things to destroy) — the saga's whole moral geometry in two itineraries, and his ends this film ahead, holding the wand over a grave.
