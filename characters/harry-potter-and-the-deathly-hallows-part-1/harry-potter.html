01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Harry opens the film losing his legal existence: the protective spell breaks with his seventeenth birthday, the Ministry falls within weeks, and the boy who was the wizarding world's mascot becomes its most wanted fugitive, with a price on his head and his face on the radio's lists of the missing. Dumbledore's mission — find the Horcruxes, destroy them — comes with no locations, no method and one tool he cannot obtain. Part 1 is Harry stripped of every institution that ever carried him: no school, no Order escort after the first flight, no wand by Christmas. What remains is the mission, the locket's poison, and two friends — one of whom leaves.

ROLE

Protagonist; the hunted

The war's object and its only appointed executioner: Voldemort hunts him while he hunts the pieces of Voldemort.

DEFINING TRAIT

Endurance without a map

His heroism in this film is not victory but continuation — camping, hiding, failing, and not stopping.

FATE

Captured, rescued, and burying his rescuer

The film ends with Harry digging Dobby's grave by hand at Shell Cottage while Voldemort takes the Elder Wand from Dumbledore's tomb.

Place in the story

Harry is the film's centre of gravity in a new way: previous films orbited his discoveries; this one orbits his attrition. The Galaxy's film page calls him a leader by default and a failure by his own accounting; the archive treats his page as the record of what the saga's heroism costs when it is reduced to three teenagers, a tent and a task with no instructions.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

The film opens with Harry's protection expiring. As the Dursleys depart and the Order flies him out under Polyjuice cover — six decoy Harrys in the sky — the ambush costs Mad-Eye Moody and Hedwig their lives, and Harry duels Voldemort himself in the air, his wand acting of its own accord. At the Burrow, Minister Scrimgeour delivers Dumbledore's bequests: a Deluminator for Ron, Beedle the Bard for Hermione, the first Snitch for Harry, and a sword that has gone missing. At Bill and Fleur's wedding the news lands that the Ministry has fallen and Scrimgeour is dead; the trio flee mid-celebration into a London café and then to Grimmauld Place, where they identify R.A.B. as Sirius's brother Regulus and trace the real locket to Dolores Umbridge.

Disguised by Polyjuice, they infiltrate the Ministry, recover the locket from Umbridge's courtroom, and escape into the wilderness with Grimmauld Place compromised and Ron splinched. The camping odyssey that follows is the film's long centre: the locket, worn in turns, amplifies every grievance; Harry's visions of Voldemort hunting a wand-maker's secret grow harder to refuse; and after a blazing row — about food, about progress, about Ron's family in danger while Harry has no plan — Ron walks out. Harry and Hermione go on alone to Godric's Hollow, where Bathilda Bagshot proves to be Nagini in disguise; escaping, Hermione's rebounding spell breaks Harry's wand. In the Forest of Dean, a silver doe leads Harry to the Sword of Gryffindor at the bottom of a frozen pond; the locket tries to strangle him as he dives, and Ron — returned at the decisive hour — pulls him out and destroys the locket with the sword.

Reunited, the trio visit Xenophilius Lovegood, learn the Tale of the Three Brothers and the meaning of the Hallows sign, and escape his betrayal by moments. Days later, Harry speaks Voldemort's name — Tabooed — and Snatchers take them to Malfoy Manor, where Bellatrix tortures Hermione over the sword and Dobby, summoned by Harry's plea to the mirror shard, rescues the prisoners from the cellar. On the beach at Shell Cottage, Dobby dies of Bellatrix's thrown knife in Harry's arms. Harry digs the grave himself, without magic. The film's last image intercuts the burial's aftermath with Voldemort breaking open Dumbledore's tomb and taking the Elder Wand.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The mission as identity

Interpretation: Harry has outsourced his purpose to a dead man's instructions, and the film is the record of the instructions running out. His despair in the tent is not fear of Voldemort but fear that Dumbledore left him nothing — a crisis of faith the saga has been building since the cave.

The refusal to be carried

He digs Dobby's grave by hand, declining magic for the labour: the film's clearest statement of his ethic. What love did for him, he does with his hands; the boy who was always rescued insists on being, this once, the one who does the burying properly.

Visions as temptation

His link to Voldemort's mind — once a weapon — is here an addiction the film treats seriously: the Hallows hunt, glimpsed through Voldemort's eyes, offers him a rival quest with clearer prizes than the Horcruxes' fog.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
DEFENCE AND DUELLING

Battle-proven

He survives the Seven Potters ambush and duels Voldemort in mid-air; his wand's independent action against Voldemort is recorded as the film presents it — a phenomenon neither side understands.

THE CONNECTION

Voldemort's mind, unwillingly

His visions supply the film's intelligence about the Elder Wand hunt — and its chief temptation.

ENDURANCE

The camping war

His real weapon in Part 1 is continuation: months of cold, hunger and failure without defection.

WEAKNESS

No plan, and he knows it

Ron's accusation is the film's own: the mission has a destination and no route, and Harry's leadership consists of refusing to admit the difference.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The tent

The saga's smallest set: a whole civilisation — books, wards, a radio — carried in a beaded bag and pitched in the rain. Interpretation: Part 1 argues that what the trio are defending is portable, and fits in a tent, which is why the regime with all its ministries cannot find it.

The grave dug by hand

The anti-spell: in a film of wand-work, the most important labour is manual. The archive reads it as Harry's graduation — the rescued child performing, at last, the rites himself.

The broken wand

His own wand, broken by a friend's rebounding spell in a haunted house: the hero disarmed by accident, not by the enemy — Part 1's thesis that this war will be survived, not won, by its hero.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

A hero audited by attrition

Part 1 is the saga's only film in which Harry achieves almost nothing on schedule: one Horcrux destroyed, by Ron, with a sword found by an anonymous benefactor. The archive's reading: the film is deliberately auditing the chosen-one structure — strip the hero of school, mentor, wand and plan, and see whether the story still calls him its centre. It does, but on new evidence: endurance and the loyalty he inspires, not competence.

The Hallows temptation

The film plants a rival relic-quest inside the Horcrux quest and lets Harry feel its pull through Voldemort's own visions — the archive notes the symmetry: both hunters are chasing a wand, and Harry's susceptibility to the shinier mission is the film's most honest piece of characterisation, preparing the choice the saga's finale will demand of him.

10 / KEY MOMENTS

Key Moments

The Seven Potters

Six friends wearing his face in a night sky: the war's first bill — Moody and Hedwig — presented before the titles have settled.

The dance in the tent

One radio song, danced without romance or dialogue: the partnership, restated in the mission's worst week.

The frozen pond

The sword under the ice, the locket at his throat, and Ron's hand: the quest's restoration staged as a drowning survived.

The cellar plea

Harry begging a shard of mirror for help — faith in a dead man's gift, answered by a free elf.

The grave

"Here lies Dobby, a free elf": the headstone Harry insists on, and the hands he insists on digging with.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

The split film

Part 1 is the first of two films adapted from the 2007 novel, directed by David Yates from Steve Kloves's screenplay, per the film's Wikipedia record; its worldwide opening weekend of $330 million was the highest opening of 2010, as the same record notes. The split lets this instalment end mid-quest — an unresolved structure the production chose over compression.

Radcliffe's fugitive year

The Galaxy's film page records the performance's register: a leader by default and a failure by his own accounting. The archive adds that the film's camping centrepiece — long dialogue scenes in a tent — was the production's acknowledged gamble: the saga's spectacle budget spent on weather and waiting.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Harry's Part 1 file is the archive's record of the hero at minimum: no wand, no plan, no school, one Horcrux destroyed by someone else's hand. Within the Galaxy it stands as the saga's study of endurance as heroism — and as the necessary darkness before Part 2's battle, in which every debt this film records (Dobby's grave, the doe's mystery, the wand in the tomb) comes due.
