01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Daniel is the prequel's tragic engine: a good provider whose every virtue is routed through systems — insurance, contractors, cameras — in a situation where the only functioning instruments are a housekeeper's sage and a teenager's search history. He answers the break-in with a six-camera installation, answers Martine's correct diagnosis by firing her, answers his wife's knowledge by reviewing footage, and is converted, at last, by the footage of Kristi dragged into the basement — one escalation too late. What he does with his conversion is the film's moral crater: he recalls Martine, exorcises his wife, and — over his daughter's explicit protest — deliberately transfers the demon to Katie, burning a photograph of his sister-in-law as a child to redirect a creditor onto an innocent woman who trusts his family. It works. His wife is saved, his son is kept, his house is quiet for three weeks. Then the transferred debt walks back through his front door wearing Katie, and kills him in his own hallway.

ROLE

The husband; the decision-maker

Every consequential choice in the film is his — the cameras, the firing, the exorcism, the transfer — and the film is the ledger of their prices.

DEFINING TRAIT

Reasonableness as a system

He manages: the Galaxy's film page's diagnosis. His failure is not stupidity but category — a homeowner's toolkit applied to a creditor from before his grandfather was born.

FATE

Killed by his own solution

The possessed Katie breaks into his house on October 9, 2006 and kills him: the transfer, returning its invoice to its author.

Place in the story

Daniel is the Galaxy's study of the reasonable man in an unreasonable house: the archive treats his page as the record of a conversion that came one dragging too late, and of the bargain he struck with the converted man's desperation.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Daniel's documented course begins in competence: a prosperous Carlsbad household, a new son, a blended family at ease. The break-in — trashed rooms, locked doors, nothing taken but a necklace — is processed as a security problem, and solved as one: a professional six-camera installation. When Martine, the housekeeper, reads the house on instinct and burns sage against what she names as malevolent, Daniel fires her for superstition — the film's first priced decision, the correct authority dismissed by the reasonable man. The incidents proceed in his system's own footage: the pool cleaner relocated, pans dropping, doors slamming on Ali. Kristi tells him the house is haunted; Daniel reviews the footage and dismisses the claim — the system's owner, auditing the evidence his system exists to gather, and filing it as malfunction.

The conversion is forced on him by escalation. Abby, the dog who has been reporting the presence for weeks, is attacked and seizes; at the vet, the demon takes its hour with Kristi. The following day's footage — his wife dragged down the stairs into the basement, the bite mark, the stranger in the nursery — leaves the reasonable man no category left. He recalls Martine. She prepares the cross; the exorcism is performed in a night of darkness and shaking, Daniel forcing the presence out of his wife in the basement with the cross while she attacks him. And then the decision the film will not forgive: rather than let the creditor keep its claim on his house, Daniel — told the demon can be passed — burns a photograph of young Katie and transfers it to his wife's sister, over Ali's protests that it is unfair to Katie. Three weeks later Katie visits, laughing, reporting strange things beginning at her house. On October 9, the night after Micah is killed, the possessed Katie enters the Rey house and kills Daniel and Kristi and takes Hunter. His body is found by his daughter on October 12.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The manager

Interpretation: Daniel's love language is logistics — provision, installation, response. The film's sympathy and its indictment are the same observation: he never stops working on the problem; he simply works on it in a grammar the problem does not speak, until the night it teaches him its own.

Conversion, total and instant

When the footage finally lands, he does not hedge — he recalls the fired housekeeper, funds the exorcism, walks into the basement with a cross. The archive reads his conversion as an engineer's: the gauge is believed the moment it is read, and every prior reading is discarded without self-pity.

The utilitarian abyss

His transfer decision is made in the open, against his daughter's stated objection, with a provider's logic: my wife, my son, this house — against a sister-in-law's safety, unconsulted. The film presents it as the reasonable man's final category error: other people, filed as externalities.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
RESOURCES

The provider's arsenal

Money, contractors, a six-camera system installed in days, a housekeeper recalled on demand: Daniel can mobilise a household like a small firm, and does.

THE EXORCISM

Performed by hand

Whatever the cross's theology, the basement confrontation is his: he carries the instrument into the dark, survives the possessed Kristi's attack, and completes the rite while the house shakes — the manager, doing the fieldwork himself at the end.

DECISIVENESS

No half-measures

Firing, installing, exorcising, transferring: every decision is executed completely and immediately. The film's horror is that decisiveness without knowledge is just a faster route to the crater.

WEAKNESS

The dismissed authorities

His two correct informants are a housekeeper he fires and a wife he audits: the reasonable man's filter passes footage and fails testimony, and the haunting prices the filter precisely.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The six cameras

Surveillance as the reasonable man's theology — if it is recorded, it is managed: the film's irony is that the system documents everything and prevents nothing, a perfect archive of a family being collected.

The burned photograph

A childhood picture of Katie, burned to redirect a demon: the symbol of the transfer's true nature — an act of sympathetic magic performed by a man who fired his housekeeper for superstition, when the superstition was aimed at someone else.

The pool cleaner

The presence's first signature is a malfunctioning appliance, filed by Daniel as a defect: the symbol of his whole epistemology — the supernatural, processed as a maintenance ticket.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The transfer as the franchise's original sin

The archive's central file on Daniel: every later event in the series' chronology — Katie's possession, Micah's death, Hunter's taking — flows from a decision made in this film's basement by a man protecting his family. The analysis: the film constructs him carefully as no monster, because its horror depends on the transfer being the act of a good provider at the end of his categories. The demon does not corrupt Daniel; it invoices him, and he pays with somebody else.

Reasonableness as an ally of the haunting

Each of Daniel's reasonable acts buys the presence time: the cameras normalise the incidents as data, the firing removes the countermeasure, the audit delays belief until the possession is accomplished. The analysis reads the film as a systems critique — the haunting is not opposed by his competence but routed through it, the way the demon is finally routed through his exorcism into his sister-in-law.

Behind the Character

Boland's Daniel

Brian Boland plays Daniel; the film is directed by Tod Williams, per the film's record. The archive notes the casting's discipline: Boland is an unfamiliar face playing an everyman without tics, so the audience's identification is total by the time the basement decision arrives — the transfer horrifies because the man performing it has been, until that night, the viewer's own surrogate.
