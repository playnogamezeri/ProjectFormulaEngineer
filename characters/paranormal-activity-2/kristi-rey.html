01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Kristi is the prequel's haunted centre: the sister the demon reaches first, in the house where the debt comes due. She opens the film bringing her newborn son Hunter home — the first male born to her family line since the 1930s, though nobody says so for an hour — and the film builds its dread on the corruption of her warmth: the kitchen laughter, the sisterly confidences, the mother at the crib. Underneath it, Kristi carries the sisters' buried childhood: she and Katie were tormented by something when they were small, and their lifelong pact was silence. The haunting breaks the pact in stages — disbelief performed for her husband, terror confided to her sister, a dragging into the basement that the cameras record and she does not remember — until the presence takes her over entirely and has to be exorcised out of her by her husband's desperate bargain, which saves her life and spends her sister's. Her documented end is the franchise's bleakest accounting: saved, restored, memory wiped clean — and killed weeks later by the possessed Katie, in the house the demon was transferred out of her to reach.

ROLE

The haunted centre; the first host

The demon's point of entry into the Rey household and its first possessed body in the film: the haunting's address is her family line, and its door is her.

DEFINING TRAIT

Knowledge, withheld

She knows what this is before anyone — the sisters' childhood saw to that — and her silence, maintained for her family's peace, is the haunting's first ally and the film's saddest mechanism.

FATE

Saved, then collected

Exorcised by transfer, with no memory of the possession — and killed by the possessed Katie on the night of October 9, 2006, when the debt returns to the house for Hunter.

Place in the story

Kristi is the prequel's heart and its first casualty: the archive treats her page as the record of the sisters' buried childhood coming due — what she knew, what it cost her to say it, and what was done to her while the cameras watched.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Kristi's documented course opens with a homecoming: home-video footage of her bringing newborn Hunter home from the hospital, into the Carlsbad house, the family complete — Daniel, stepdaughter Ali, housekeeper Martine, the dog Abby. A year later the house is found trashed in a staged break-in, doors locked, nothing taken but a necklace Katie had given her — and Hunter's nursery left untouched, the detail the film plants and refuses to underline. Daniel installs the six-camera system that becomes the film's narrator. The incidents accumulate in the system's grammar: the pool cleaner out of the pool, pans falling, doors moving — and Kristi, alone among the adults, does not need convincing. She and Katie talk, in the film's most quietly loaded conversation, about being tormented by a demon when they were children; Katie, furious, demands she not pay the events attention, insisting engagement makes it worse. Kristi confides anyway — to her sister, the one person who already knows — and obeys the sisters' old law in public: she performs disbelief for Daniel while the house escalates around her.

The escalation takes her. While Daniel and Ali rush the attacked dog to the vet, Kristi is alone with Hunter when the demon assaults her — dragged down the stairs and into the basement, where she remains for over an hour before emerging dazed, and possessed. The following day she is a stranger in her own house: bedridden, then found sitting in the nursery with a bite mark on her thigh, ordering Ali away from the baby in a voice the family does not recognise. The footage of the dragging finally converts Daniel; Martine's cross exorcises the presence in a night of shaking darkness — with the documented mercy that Kristi will retain no memory of having been possessed. She is put to bed restored, and the demon is burned out of the house's account into a photograph of young Katie. Three weeks of peace follow. On October 9, the night after Micah is killed, the possessed Katie enters the Rey house, kills Daniel and Kristi, and takes Hunter. Ali, returning from a school trip on October 12, finds the bodies. Kristi's file closes where the film's text does: the debt, collected from the woman it was never finally about.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The hearth

Interpretation: Kristi's warmth is the household's operating system — the kitchen, the laughter, the sisterly ease — and the film corrupts it deliberately, possession by possession, because the demon's campaign is against the family's centre of gravity, not its perimeter.

The keeper of the buried thing

She has managed the childhood haunting for decades by the sisters' method — silence — and her tragedy is that the method was also a mercy she believed in: saying it, she had been taught by experience, feeds it. Her silence is not denial; it is an old, failed protocol, faithfully kept.

The mother at the crib

Every escalation is routed through Hunter — the untouched nursery, the attacks timed to her absences from him, the possessed Kristi stationed at his window. Her motherhood is the haunting's terrain, and her defence of him, while herself, never wavers.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
RECOGNITION

The first to know

She identifies the haunting before the footage convinces anyone, because she has met it before: the film's only adult whose epistemology starts from the truth — and whose tragedy is that she has been trained out of acting on it.

ENDURANCE

The dragging, survived

She survives a physical assault and an hour in the basement that leaves her possessed rather than dead: the demon wants the door, not the corpse, and her body bears the campaign's worst violence in the film.

THE CONFIDENCE

Truth, told once

Her kitchen conversation with Katie — the childhood, named aloud — is the film's exposition and its emotional core: the buried thing, spoken by the only two people alive who know it.

WEAKNESS

The sisters' law

Silence as survival strategy leaves her undefended in the present tense: she cannot recruit her husband, cannot act on her knowledge, and cannot even warn at full strength — the protocol that protected her childhood delivers her adulthood.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The untouched nursery

The break-in trashes every room but the baby's: the film's thesis in one staging decision — the house was never the target, and its most violent night was a courtesy call.

The basement

She is dragged to the house's buried room and kept there an hour: the symbol of the sisters' own architecture — the childhood, stored below, that the whole film is about the return of.

The wiped memory

Martine's mercy — she will not remember being possessed — is the film's bleakest kindness: Kristi is restored to exactly the ignorance that left her undefended, and dies inside it.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The first host, not the target

The archive's reading: the film's cruelest structural fact is that Kristi is a conduit — the demon possesses the mother to stand beside the crib, and the family's rescue transfers it to the aunt for the same reason. Kristi's page records her as the campaign's terrain rather than its object, and notes that the film knows the injustice: it gives her its warmest footage and its most violent, and lets the text card, not a character, speak her death.

Silence as the family religion

The sisters' pact — don't talk about it, don't engage, it feeds on attention — is the film's study of a coping strategy becoming a curse's infrastructure. The analysis: Kristi is the pact's most faithful keeper and its clearest victim, and her one act of speech, the kitchen confidence, is answered by Katie with the pact itself — the religion, defending itself through the person she trusted with breaking it.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Grayden's Kristi

Sprague Grayden plays Kristi; the film is directed by Tod Williams, written by Michael R. Perry, Christopher Landon and Tom Pabst, per the film's record. The archive notes the role's prequel difficulty: Kristi must be a complete person in her own film and a known casualty in the audience's — Grayden plays the warmth without foreshadowing, which is what makes the possession sequences read as occupation rather than transformation.
