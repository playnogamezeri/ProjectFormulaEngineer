01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Ali is the prequel's investigator and its surviving conscience. A teenager in a blended family — Daniel's daughter by a prior marriage, Kristi's stepdaughter, Hunter's half-sister — she meets the haunting the way her generation meets everything: with a camera in her hand and a search engine open. Locked outside by a slamming door, she starts keeping records; researching the incidents, she assembles the film's entire mythology — the demon bargain, the firstborn son, Hunter as the first male of the line since the 1930s — from old accounts and family shape, and delivers it to adults who use her research and ignore her judgement. Left alone with the possessed Kristi, she survives the nursery; shown the dragging footage, she arms her father at last; and at the transfer, she is the only person in the film who says the true thing — it is unfair to Katie — and is overruled. The film's closing text is hers: away on a school trip while her family is killed, she comes home on October 12, 2006 and finds the bodies.

ROLE

The researcher; the survivor

The household's functioning investigator and its final witness: the mythology is hers, the protest is hers, and the discovery is hers.

DEFINING TRAIT

An epistemology equal to the house

She tests, films and reads — the Galaxy's film page's portrait — while the adults manage and deny: the teenager as the family's only scientist.

FATE

The discoverer

Away on a school trip during the killings, she returns on October 12 to find her father and stepmother dead and her brother gone: the survivor, handed the ledger.

Place in the story

Ali is the Galaxy's model of the found-footage investigator: the archive treats her page as the record of the household's one working mind — what she found, what she said, and what it cost to be right in a house that priced her as a child.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Ali's documented course begins in the ordinary: video diaries, her friend Brad on the other side of the camera, a teenager's flippant register in a prosperous blended household. The haunting recruits her early and personally — lured outside by banging, she is locked out by the slamming door — and her response defines her: she starts investigating. Where her father installs cameras and files malfunctions, Ali researches. Her reading assembles, piece by piece, the account the whole franchise stands on: that a person may bargain with a demon for wealth or power at the price of a firstborn son; that an unfulfilled bargain leaves the demon attached to the family line; and that Hunter — her baby half-brother — is the first male born to Kristi's side of the family since the 1930s. The mythology arrives in the film through a teenager's search history, and the archive records that as a deliberate piece of casting-by-generation: the house's truth is found by the person with the least authority to act on it.

Her investigation is not armchair work. She is in the house for its worst day — left alone with Kristi after the possession, hearing the scratching at the basement door, finding the word meus cut into it, facing the stranger in the nursery who orders her away from the baby in her stepmother's voice. It is Ali who, terrified, calls her father home, and Ali's showing him the dragging footage that finally converts him. At the endgame she performs the film's one act of moral reasoning: told the demon will be passed to Katie, she protests — it is unfair to Katie — and is overruled by her father, in her own kitchen, on the record. The film then removes her, by a school trip, from the killings she predicted in everything but detail: the closing text records her return on October 12, 2006, to the bodies of her father and stepmother, and to an empty nursery. Her friend Brad, the confidant of the video diaries and the Ouija session, is covered inside this page; the film leaves him in the ordinary world Ali can no longer rejoin.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The scientist of the house

Interpretation: Ali's method is the film's only functioning one — observe, record, research, hypothesise — and she applies it without her father's filter or her stepmother's silence. Her tragedy is jurisdictional: the house's best mind has a minor's authority.

Curiosity, honestly priced

The Ouija session is hers — the investigator's one act of recklessness, conducted with Brad as a game with a scientist's notebook — and the film files it as the tax on her method: she opens the same doors she documents, and the house notices her back.

The conscience, in plain words

Her protest at the transfer is not theological or strategic; it is a fairness claim, made by a teenager to her father, and it is the film's verdict in advance. She loses the argument and is proven right by the ending — the archive records her as the Reys' Cassandra, with the additional cruelty that Cassandra was believed about nothing and Ali is believed about everything except the one thing that mattered.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The word in the door

Meus — the Latin for mine — scratched into the basement door she opens: the creditor's whole claim in one word, found by the household's only reader. The archive notes she is the one who finds it because she is the one who goes and looks.

The search history

A franchise's mythology delivered through a teenager's browser: the symbol of the film's generational argument — the old knowledge is all still there, in the open, for whoever is frightened enough to read it.

The school trip

She is saved by the most ordinary mechanism in the film — a school excursion: the symbol of the normal world's indifference, which removes her from the killing floor by timetable and returns her by timetable to the aftermath.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Believed about everything but the verdict

The archive's central file on Ali: the adults adopt her facts wholesale — the bargain, the debt, the transfer's mechanics all come from her research — and reject only her ethics. The analysis: the film stages a precise division of a minor's knowledge into the usable and the ignorable, and the ending is the ignorable part's revenge. Her page records her as the franchise's most complete intelligence and its least empowered, by design.

The survivor as the series' memory

Ali is left alive with the whole account — the research, the protest, the bodies, the empty nursery — and no one to file it with: the film's closing text gives her discovery the flat grammar of a police report. The analysis reads her as the franchise's designated rememberer, the counterpart to the sisters' pact of silence: the generation that writes it down, arriving one film too late to be heeded.
