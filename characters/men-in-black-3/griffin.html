01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Griffin is the film's wonder and its conscience: an Archanan refugee whose people the Boglodites destroyed, who sees every possible future at once — the deaths and the survivals superimposed — and who carries the ArcNet, the shield his species' survivors built, to the one morning in Earth's history where it can be deployed. He experiences the plot the way the audience wishes it could: as a field of branching outcomes, most of them terrible, with a narrow bright path through Cape Canaveral that he must guide two agents along without ever being able to simply tell them why. His gentleness is the film's counterweight to Boris's arithmetic: the being who sees everything chooses, at every branch, kindness.

ROLE

The witness; the shield's keeper

He carries the ArcNet, reads the futures, and steers the mission — the plot's navigator, who can see every road and walk only one.

DEFINING TRAIT

Wonder under omniscience

Griffin has seen everything that could happen and remains delighted by what does: his awe at a baseball game, at a pie, at a kindness, is the film's argument that knowledge need not curdle into Boris's arithmetic.

FATE

The bright branch

He delivers the ArcNet, shows the colonel the future that makes a believer of him, and withdraws — assuring J that history will be restored once K takes Boris's arm.

Place in the story

Griffin is where the film keeps its metaphysics and its heart in one small alien: the only character who experiences the story the way the audience does — as suspense over known stakes — raised to the power of simultaneity. His page records the witness's labour: to see the colonel's death in every branch and still walk him, kindly, to the tower.

Griffin's identity is spectatorship raised to a vocation: he is the universe's audience, seated in every seat at once. The film defines him against Boris precisely — both are last-of-kind survivors of Boglodite appetites; one responds with a ledger of vengeance, the other with a shield and a habit of delight. Identity, in Griffin, is what omniscience does to gentleness: nothing. It survives.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Griffin comes to Earth a refugee: the Boglodites destroyed his planet, and its survivors entrusted him — the witness who could see where the shield would be needed — with the ArcNet, a device that can seal a world against invasion. In 1969 he is in New York, seeing the day's branches: in most of them, Boris kills K, the shield never flies, and Earth follows his own world into the Boglodites' maw.

The agents find him through Warhol at the Factory; sensing young Boris's attack, Griffin flees — promising the ArcNet at the next meeting — and is found again at Shea Stadium, where he watches the Miracle Mets with the wonder of a being for whom even certainties are delicious. Young Boris captures him; J and K rescue him; and Griffin surrenders the ArcNet with its instructions: it must ride Apollo 11 into orbit, on July 16, at Cape Canaveral, in the narrow hours where all the bright branches run.

At the Cape, arrested with the agents by the military, Griffin performs his kindest miracle: he shows Colonel Edwards the future — the invasion, the stakes, the mission's meaning — in a single act of witnessed evidence. The colonel believes, and escorts them to the tower. Griffin leaves the agents there with his assurance and his law: history will be restored once K takes Boris's arm. He does not stay for the tower, the deaths, or the child — the witness withdraws, having steered the chord to its bright note, and the film's last sight of his kind of knowledge is J's diner-table peace in 2012, a future Griffin saw and chose.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Anxious delight

Griffin is frightened constantly and delighted constantly, in superposition — he has seen his own death in most branches and a baseball game's beauty in all of them, and he attends to both with the same trembling attention. Interpretation: he is the film's proof that foreknowledge is not the opposite of faith but its most expensive form: he believes in the bright branch while watching the dark ones.

Tact as a superpower

He cannot simply announce the future — the branches react to telling — so his guidance is all indirection: a promise here, a shown vision there, an assurance at parting. His gentleness is operational: the witness must not crush the very choices he is steering toward.

The shield

His charge and his people's legacy: a planetary defence that must be in orbit by a morning's deadline, or never.

STEERING

He nudges events — a meeting promised, a vision shown — with the lightest possible hand, the only hand omniscience is permitted.

He can see the colonel's death in the bright branch too, and cannot prevent it: his gift shows him exactly which sorrows are load-bearing. The film's gentlest character carries its heaviest knowledge.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The audience as a character

Griffin experiences the film as its viewers do — knowing the stakes, fearing the branches, hoping for the bright one — and the film makes that spectatorship its moral centre. Interpretation: he is the argument that watching, done with Griffin's attention and love, is itself an act with consequences: the colonel believes because a witness showed him he was witnessed.

The shield, not the sword

His people's answer to extinction is a defence that saves a world they will never see. Against Boris's consumption, the Archanans' legacy is pure protection — the film's two last-of-kinds are its thesis and antithesis.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the navigation system with a soul

The plot needs an exposition engine for its time mechanics, and the screenplay's grace is to make the engine the film's most beloved character. Analysis: Griffin's scenes are where the film's tone is set — wonder, anxiety, pie — and his showing to the colonel is its ethical peak: the one conversion in the film achieved by evidence rather than force, staged as an act of trust between a doomed soldier and a frightened god.

Stuhlbarg's register

Michael Stuhlbarg plays Griffin in a trembling, delighted key that the documented reception singled out as the film's heart. The archive notes the construction: omniscience performed as vulnerability — the most powerful being in the film is its gentlest, and the performance never lets the audience forget the price of the sight.
