01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Cobb is an extractor: a corporate thief who enters shared dreams and steals the secrets his marks keep in their subconscious. He is also a fugitive from a murder charge he did not commit in the manner alleged — his wife Mal, convinced by an idea he planted in her that their world was not real, carried the conviction back into reality and killed herself to escape it, having first arranged the evidence to frame him. Exiled from his children, he accepts the profession's impossible job — inception, planting an idea so it grows as the mark's own — in exchange for the one payment that matters: Saito's promise to make the charge disappear. The film is the job, and the job is a descent through three dream levels into limbo, where Cobb must finally confront the wife he keeps, the idea he planted, and the guilt that has been sabotaging his work from inside his own mind.

ROLE

Protagonist; the extractor

The team's leader and its liability in one person: his skill runs the job and his subconscious keeps trying to kill it.

DEFINING TRAIT

Guilt with a security clearance

Every level the team descends, his mind smuggles Mal in with them. His secret is the mission's true cargo, and Ariadne is the only colleague who audits it.

FATE

Home — as far as the film will certify

He wakes on the plane, passes the border, and embraces his children while his totem spins, and wobbles, unobserved: the film ends before the top falls or doesn't.

Place in the story

Cobb is the film's argument that the most dangerous idea is the one you plant in someone you love. The Galaxy's film page calls him a man serving a life sentence in his own architecture; the archive treats his page as the record of the sentence — its construction in limbo, its enforcement by Mal's projection, and its commutation, if that is what the ending is, in a garden with two children's faces finally turned toward him.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

The film opens with Cobb washed up on a shore before an aged Saito — a scene the story will take its whole length to explain — then cuts to the job that explains him: an extraction inside Saito's dream castle, where the team's work is undone by two failures, Nash's carpet woven from memory in the wrong material, and Mal, Cobb's projection of his dead wife, who appears in his dreams to sabotage him as reliably as gravity. Saito, who has staged the job as an audition, offers the real commission: perform inception on Robert Fischer — plant the idea that he should dissolve his father's empire — and Saito will clear the murder charge that keeps Cobb from his children in America. Cobb assembles the team: Arthur, his partner; Eames, a forger; Yusuf, a chemist whose sedative can hold three levels stable; and Ariadne, Miles's architecture student, to build what Cobb no longer dares to.

What Cobb withholds from the team is the job's true risk profile, and Ariadne excavates it: following him into his dreams, she finds his private elevator of kept memories and, at its heart, Mal — who stabs the intruder and wakes her. The backstory arrives in instalments. Cobb and Mal, experimenting in their youth, sank into limbo and spent some fifty years of dream time building a world of their own. To persuade her to leave, Cobb found the totem she had hidden in a safe in their house — a spinning top she had locked away to forget — and set it spinning: the inception that told her, forever after, that her world was not real. It worked in limbo and it worked at home. Mal, certain that reality was another level, arranged her own refutation: a trashed hotel room across the street, a letter to her attorney alleging Cobb's threats, and a jump performed in front of him, framed as his crime. He fled the country; she got the death she believed was a waking.

The Fischer job descends through Yusuf's rain-drowned city, Arthur's hotel and Eames's snow fortress, with Mal sabotaging at depth — in the third level she shoots Fischer, dropping him into limbo, and Cobb and Ariadne follow to retrieve him. In limbo Cobb finally tells the whole truth aloud, to Ariadne and to Mal: that he knows how the idea got into her, that the woman before him is a shade — brilliant, beautiful and insufficient — and that he cannot stay with her because he has children in a world she refused. Ariadne shoots the projection; Fischer is revived above; and Cobb remains in limbo to find Saito, who has died in the levels above and grown old in the depths. The film returns to its opening shore: Cobb, reminding the old man of a promise made by two younger men, and the two of them taking hold of the means to wake. Cobb wakes on the plane. Saito makes a phone call. Immigration waves him through. At home he spins the top on the table — and walks away from it, out to the garden, to his children's turned faces. The camera leaves the top spinning, wobbling, unresolved.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The controlled man, leaking

Interpretation: Cobb's professionalism is real and insufficient: he can run a three-level operation inside a militarised subconscious and cannot run one night's sleep without his wife arriving. The film's tension is the gap between the competence he sells and the cargo he smuggles.

The author of the idea

His guilt is not survivor's guilt in the simple sense: he performed the inception that killed her, knowing the technique and not its half-life. His basement visits are a man returning to the scene of an experiment he cannot unpublish.

The father as compass

Every decision he makes is denominated in two children's faces — which he refuses, in dreams, to look at directly, because looking would confirm whether the dream is false. The ending's turn of the faces is the film's whole emotional budget, spent in one shot.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
EXTRACTION

The trade's best thief

Layered dreams, improvised gambits — the Mr. Charles play, in which he convinces Fischer he is his own subconscious's security chief, is the profession's masterstroke, performed mid-collapse.

DREAM ARCHITECTURE (FORMER)

The retired builder

He was the trade's leading architect and can no longer build: his subconscious populates his constructions with Mal. His abstention is the team's founding constraint — and Ariadne's job description.

LIMBO EXPERIENCE

The only guide down

Fifty years of subjective time in the unconstructed deep: Cobb is the team's sole veteran of the place the job keeps threatening to send them, which makes him both its map and its warning.

WEAKNESS

The projection

Mal is his subconscious's sabotage made personal — she knows his plans because she is his plan, and the deeper the level, the stronger her vote.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The spinning top

A totem that was his wife's before it was his — the archive reads the ending's power as biographical, not mechanical: Cobb walks away from the instrument because its verdict has stopped being the question. Whether it falls is the audience's problem, deliberately.

The elevator of memories

Cobb's kept past, filed by floor — the beach house, the hotel, the children's birthdays — with limbo in the basement: his architecture confesses what his mouth will not, that he has built his guilt a building and rides down to it nightly.

The children's faces

Withheld all film — turned away, cropped, refused — and granted in the last shot: the symbol of the reality Cobb will accept on faith, which is the film's definition of waking up.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The ending, as documented debate

The final shot's ambiguity is the film's most famous open file, and the archive records the positions without settling them: the top wobbles; the cut is to black; the children's clothes and ages have been argued over frame by frame by the film's audience for years. Christopher Nolan's own documented comment — made at Princeton's 2015 commencement, per the EBSCO record — is that the point is Cobb's indifference: he has his children and no longer cares which level he is on. The archive files this as the director's reading, not a verdict: the film withholds the top's fall with intent.

Inception as autobiography

Cobb performs on Fischer, redemptively, the exact operation he performed on Mal, fatally: an idea planted to feel like the mark's own. The analysis: the film's structure is a man re-running his crime as a cure — this time building the idea out of the mark's own materials (the pinwheel, the father's word) and this time staying to confess his authorship to the one shade who can accuse him. The job is the therapy, and both are heists.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Nolan's decade

The film was written and directed by Christopher Nolan, who produced it with Emma Thomas; per the film's Wikipedia record it grossed $839.4 million worldwide, was the fourth-highest-grossing film of 2010, and won four Academy Awards (Cinematography, Sound Editing, Sound Mixing, Visual Effects). Nolan has said the ending's ambiguity is intentional and that its subject is Cobb's indifference to the verdict — the Princeton remark recorded in the EBSCO account.

DiCaprio's Cobb

The Galaxy's film page frames the performance around the sentence Cobb serves in his own architecture; the archive notes the production's central demand on its lead — that the same man must be credible as the trade's best thief and as a sleep-deprived fugitive whose own mind is the job's chief saboteur.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Cobb became the defining protagonist of the dream-heist: the thief whose loot is his own absolution. Within the Galaxy his page stands as the collection's study of an idea's half-life — the man who proved inception possible on the woman he loved, and spent the rest of the film, and possibly the rest of his life, inside the result.
