01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Riza Stavros is an intergalactic arms dealer, played by Rebecca Ferguson, operating from an island fortress with her bodyguard Luca Brasi. She is also Agent H’s ex-girlfriend — a history the film spends as currency. When the stolen star-weapon lands in her hands, M and H come to her fortress to take it back; they leave with it only because Luca, whom Molly Wright rescued as a child in Brooklyn, repays his debt at the gate.

ROLE

The market’s queen

Riza is what the star-weapon is worth outside the agency: the private market for world-ending devices, wearing evening dress.

DEFINING TRAIT

Romance as an old account

Her history with H is neither forgiven nor forgotten; it is itemized. Every courtesy in the fortress sequence is a negotiation between two people pricing their past.

ARC IN ONE LINE

From holder of the weapon to spectator of its use

She possesses the film’s MacGuffin at its midpoint and loses it without a battle — undone by her own bodyguard’s older loyalty.

Place in the story

Riza’s place is the film’s middle-act mirror: against MIB’s institutional custody of dangerous things, she is private custody — elegant, fortified, and for sale. Her loss of the weapon is the plot’s moral bookkeeping: the fortress, the army and the ex-lover’s leverage are all outbid by a kindness a child did in Brooklyn in 1996.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Riza’s history with H predates the film: the two were lovers, and the relationship ended in the grievance-and-attraction equilibrium the fortress sequence plays on. She has built, in the meantime, an arms empire with an island fortress and Luca Brasi — a mountainous alien — as her bodyguard.

The star-weapon, stolen from the agents en route, lands with Riza. M and H infiltrate her fortress to recover it and are captured. The negotiation that follows runs on the old account between Riza and H — until the fortress’s own arithmetic changes: Luca recognizes Molly Wright. He is the small alien she helped escape in Brooklyn in 1996, grown into Riza’s mountain of a bodyguard, and his debt outranks his employment. He lets the agents leave with the weapon.

Riza exits the film in possession of everything she started with except the weapon and the bodyguard’s undivided loyalty — the market’s queen, outbid by a childhood kindness she never knew was on her books.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Elegance as fortification

Riza’s menace is social: the fortress is a salon, the negotiation a dinner party with consequences. She is the film’s most civilized dangerous person, which the film treats as the most dangerous kind.

The ex as professional

Her scenes with H are conducted as a trade summit between former lovers — every pleasantry a bid, every memory a valuation. The film never resolves the account, because the account is the relationship.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The market versus the gift

Riza prices everything in the film — weapons, loyalty, love. The fortress falls to the one transaction in the story that was never priced: a child helping a stranger out a window.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Narrative function: the middle-act mirror

Riza exists to give the weapon a price and the agents a heist, but her structural work is thematic: she is custody-by-purchase set against custody-by-oath (Pawny) and custody-by-institution (MIB). The film’s sympathies are ranked by how each holder loses or keeps the star — and Riza, who bought everything she has, is the only one outbid.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Riza Stavros is International’s most efficient piece of world-building: one character establishing that beyond MIB’s custody disputes there is a market, a price list and a queen — and that the film’s moral economy outranks all three. Her unresolved account with H remains one of the film’s deliberate loose ends.
