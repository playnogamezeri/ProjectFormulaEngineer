01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Aaron is Girl World's disputed province: a decent senior in Cady's calculus class whose entire plot function is jurisdictional — he is Regina's ex, which under Plastics law makes him her permanent territory, and Cady's crush, which makes him the infiltration's first casualty and the sabotage's first objective. The film is fair to him in a way the regime is not: he is genuinely decent — kind to the new girl, honest about the relationship Regina reclaims as policy, and the one person at Cady's party who tells her the truth about herself in plain words: she is no better than Regina. Bennett plays him, per the Galaxy card, as the school's straight line — the decent boy whose verdict matters precisely because he has no ministry, no archive and no agenda: when the province speaks, both empires listen. He ends the film at Spring Fling with Cady, the jurisdiction finally settled by the only method Girl World's peace process recognises: the girl stopped lying about math, and about everything else.

ROLE

The disputed province

Principal supporting; the campaign's first objective, its mirror, and its certificate of reform.

DEFINING TRAIT

Decency without office

He holds no power in Girl World and wants none — which is exactly why his single verdict ("no better than Regina") is the film's most damaging sentence: it is the only one issued without an agenda.

FATE

Settled, at Spring Fling

He ends the film dancing with the reformed Cady: the province, self-governing at last.

Place in the story

Aaron is the film's control for honesty among its principals: every other major character is running an operation; he is running a calculus class. His function is to be the person the operations are about, who turns out to have been a person all along.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Aaron's biography is mostly other people's paperwork: he dated Regina, it ended, and Plastics law converted the ending into a permanent claim — the ex-boyfriend rule, explained to Cady by the court as constitutional fact. He meets Cady in calculus and is simply nice to her — the new girl, the questions about class — while Cady, tutored by the infiltration's needs, begins failing math on purpose to manufacture his tutoring: the film's most quietly damning sabotage is the one she runs on herself, for him. Regina's Halloween reclaiming — the promised good word converted, in front of Cady, into a kiss and a resumed relationship — is done to him as much as to her: he is the territory on which the policy is demonstrated, and his discomfort is the scene's honest register.

The campaign's first cut is his separation from Regina — the truth, told selectively by Cady, about Regina's Shane Oman affair — and Aaron exits the regime's jurisdiction into Cady's orbit, where the orbit's own corruption is proceeding on schedule. At Cady's party, drunk and cornered, she finally tells him the truth about the math — she was failing on purpose, for his attention — and Aaron delivers the film's plainest sentence: she has become no better than Regina. He leaves; she vomits on the evening's whole design. It is the verdict the narration had been avoiding, issued by the one person with nothing to gain from it, and the film treats it as the conversion's floor: everything Cady reforms afterwards is dated from his sentence. The peace process returns him at Spring Fling — the crown broken, the math honest, the girl reformed — and the province, at last, chooses its own government.

Interpretation: Aaron's decency is defined negatively by the film — no archive, no ministry, no operation — and its single deployment (the party verdict) is devastating precisely because the film has established that he does not issue sentences. 08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The failed math

Cady's deliberate F is the film's most intimate symbol of the cover story's cost: she sacrifices the one subject she is genuinely gifted in — her real self's home ground — to the operation, and her reform is dated from the grade's recovery. Aaron is the symbol's address: the person the sacrifice was performed for, who values the honest version.

The province

His treatment by Plastics law — a person as permanent territory — is the film's jurisprudence in one rule: the ex-boyfriend law is Girl World's constitution at its most feudal, and his Spring Fling consent is its repeal, enacted.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The straight line's function

Aaron is the film's measuring instrument: because he never performs, his readings are trusted — the audience prices Cady's fall by his face at the party more than by any narration. The archive notes the screenplay's discipline in rationing him: a more active Aaron would collapse the instrument into a character, and the verdict would lose its calibration.

Bennett's restraint

The performance is deliberately the film's least stylised — a decency played at conversational level among ministries and choruses — which is why the party scene plays as documentary: the one unperformed person tells the performers the truth.

Bennett's Aaron became the film's most durable romantic-lead citation — the performance's restraint was the production's design: the province had to be worth two empires' war without ever raising an army of his own.

The calculus device
