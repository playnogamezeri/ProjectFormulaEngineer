01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Dodge Landon is the cage system's id: where his father administers, Dodge enjoys. The San Bruno shelter's chief guard by nepotism, he runs the corridors as a small kingdom of casual cruelty — the hose turned on cages for sport, the cattle prod flourished as punctuation, the apes' fear harvested as entertainment for himself and his friends, whom he tours through the facility like a prince of a very small country. His function in Caesar's education is essential and fatal: the household taught the founder love, the yard taught him politics, and Dodge teaches him the final lesson — what the enemy is, personally, at arm's length. Their confrontation in the uprising is the film's most famous exchange of power: Dodge raises the prod, Caesar seizes his arm and speaks — 'No!' — and Dodge dies by his own weapon in the struggle, electrocuted, the regime's cruelty collecting its bill from its own hand.

Place in the story

Dodge is the human antagonist at Caesar's own scale — not the company, not the law, but the man with the prod in the corridor — and the film prices the revolution's first death accordingly: it is his. Within the set, his page pairs with his father's as the regime's two storeys, corridor and office.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Dodge's film is a corridor patrol. Installed by his father as the shelter's chief guard, he treats the post as an inheritance of the recreational kind: the apes are his audience and his toys. The film builds his regime in vignettes — the hose, the taunts, the prod's threat flourished at cages, the after-hours tour for his friends in which the facility's charges are exhibited as a freak show with himself as ringmaster. Caesar's file in Dodge's ledger is special from the start: the new inmate is clever, defiant in small ways, and — the unforgivable thing — unimpressed. The pocketknife Caesar lifts from him is the regime's first security breach, invisible to its owner; the yard's politics proceed under his nose, in a language of glances and cookies he cannot read.

The uprising collects him personally. In the breakout's opening confrontation Dodge faces the troop he has tormented — dosed, organised, and led — and raises the cattle prod on its leader as he has a hundred times. Caesar catches his arm, and speaks: 'No!' — the first word of the new age, addressed to its most deserving recipient. In the struggle the prod discharges into Dodge himself, and the shelter's chief guard dies by his own instrument in his own corridor, the revolution's first fatality and its most exact piece of bookkeeping. The film leaves his body to the facility his father managed: the regime's two storeys, emptied in a morning.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The hobbyist of fear

Interpretation: Dodge's abuses are leisure, which the archive reads as the film's most precise institutional observation — the cage system's worst product is not its prisoners' suffering but its guards': the small, licensed pleasure of the prod, farmed in a young man who might otherwise merely have been ordinary.

Nepotism’s prince

His authority is inherited and therefore never examined — by himself least of all. He mistakes the facility for a kingdom and its charges for a court, and the performance of mastery is the only mastery he has.

The unimpressed inmate

Caesar's refusal to fear him correctly is the one text Dodge cannot stop reading: his escalations track, precisely, the growth of the one inmate who audits him back.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The prod

Dodge's cattle prod is the cage system's whole philosophy in a hand tool — pain, administered at a distance, by the unaccountable — and its discharge into its owner is the film's most diagrammatic justice: the instrument's logic, completed.

The first word’s addressee

That 'No!' is spoken to Dodge is the saga's founding courtesy: the new age's first sentence is not a proclamation but a refusal, delivered to the exact man who had made refusal necessary. The archive reads the address as constitutional — ape speech begins as an objection to cruelty, at arm's length.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The banality, junior division

The archive pairs Dodge with his father as the film's two-storey theory of institutional evil: the office supplies the shrug, the corridor supplies the appetite, and neither believes himself the story's villain. Dodge is the more frightening storey precisely for his smallness — the film argues that cages do their deepest damage not to the caged but to the keepers they train.

Felton’s casting as citation

Casting the decade's defining schoolboy bully as the shelter's tyrant is the production's own footnote: the audience arrives knowing this face in this function, and the film spends the recognition on making Dodge's ordinariness — a young man, a job, a prod — legible as the horror it is.
