01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Abduwali Muse is the film's second captain and its moral wager: that the audience can be made to feel for the hijacker as deeply as for his hostage. A former fisherman in a collapsed economy, he is sent to sea by a Somali faction boss to do a job — take a ship, ransom it for insurance millions — and he pursues it with a striver's total commitment. He bluffs, adapts, welds a ladder overnight, takes the Alabama's bridge, refuses the safe's $30,000 because his orders price the prize in millions, survives being held at knifepoint by the crew, and in the lifeboat holds his fracturing gang together by force of insistence: he has come too far and will not surrender. The film gives him dreams — America, a car — and a negotiator's patience, and then takes him off the board with a lie about clan elders. Barkhad Abdi, in his acting debut, won the BAFTA for the performance.

ROLE

Pirate leader; the other captain

Antagonist and co-protagonist; Muse's gang of four against the Alabama, the Navy, and his own men's despair.

DEFINING TRAIT

The striver's commitment

Muse treats piracy as the one promotion his economy offers; his refusal to surrender is ambition, not fanaticism — which is what makes it tragic.

FATE

Arrested by a ruse

Drawn off the lifeboat by the promise of clan elders, taken into custody; the title cards record his conviction for piracy and a 33-year sentence.

Place in the story

Muse is the film's argument that the hijacking has two captains and one collision course. Every structure mirrors: he commands a crew, performs authority, manages a volatile subordinate, and answers to powers offstage — a warlord's quota against Phillips's insurers and Navy. His page is the record of the Galaxy's most humanized antagonist of 2013, and of the debut performance that carried him.

Abdi was chosen from more than 700 participants at a 2011 casting call in Minneapolis, with no acting experience and, by his own account, no prior ambition to act; he was paid $65,000 for the film. The performance — all appetite, intelligence and starvation-thin intensity — earned the BAFTA Award for Best Supporting Actor and nominations from the Academy, the Golden Globes and the Screen Actors Guild. Reviewers singled out the face and the sympathy: a hijacker the film makes the audience feel for, who does not want anyone hurt and must work to rein in his own most violent man.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Muse is introduced on the Somali shore, ordered to sea by his boss, a local faction leader, assembling his gang — Najee, Bilal, Elmi among them — and choosing his target with a professional's eye: the Maersk Alabama, travelling alone rather than in the protected convoy. In the first approach, his skiff is one of two; Phillips's bluff radio call turns the other away, and Muse's engine drowns in the Alabama's wake. He returns the next day better equipped — two outboard engines, a ladder welded overnight — and boards.

On the bridge he takes command with the film's most quoted declaration — he is the captain now — and sets his terms: not the safe's $30,000, but the ship and crew ransomed for millions in insurance money, per his orders. The ship fights back by disappearing: power killed, crew hidden, and Muse searching the dark engine room alone after Bilal's feet are cut on the corridor glass. The crew takes him at knifepoint. The arranged exchange — Muse freed, the pirates to depart in the lifeboat — is rewritten by Najee, who refuses to board without Phillips; the lifeboat launches with all five.

In the lifeboat Muse's command frays: the khat wears off, the mother ship is lost, and the USS Bainbridge arrives with orders to keep him from the coast by any means. Negotiators cannot move him; he asserts he has come too far and will not surrender, even as more ships gather. He holds his gang together through Phillips's escape attempt and recapture, and his authority is the only structure the boat has. The Navy's answer is a story pitched to his hopes: Somali clan elders are coming to negotiate. Muse boards the Bainbridge — and is arrested. His three men die by simultaneous sniper fire moments later. The title cards close his file: convicted of piracy, serving 33 years in Terre Haute.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The striver

Muse's menace is inseparable from his aspiration. He speaks of America and buying a car; he runs his hijacking like a man auditioning for a life. Interpretation: the film casts piracy as his economy's only ladder, and Muse as the climber who can see the top of it — a ransom that would make him somebody — from inside a sealed boat.

Command under scarcity

Like Phillips, he performs authority for armed men whose obedience is conditional; unlike Phillips, his crew's discipline is pharmacological and expiring with the khat. His leadership's deterioration across the lifeboat days is the film's second clock.

Restraint, comparatively

Reviewers noted that Muse does not want anyone hurt and works to rein in Najee's violence. The restraint is real and insufficient: he built the situation in which Najee's violence operates, a distinction the film holds without flinching.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The other shore of globalization

Muse symbolises the economy Phillips worries about in his driveway, from the other side: the same system that prices the Alabama's cargo prices Muse's labour at nothing, and piracy is the arbitrage.

The captaincy

His seizure of the title — captain — is the film's bleakest joke about ambition: the rank is real, the ship is a lifeboat, and the fleet answering to the other captain is the largest on earth.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The sympathy wager

The film's boldest construction is Muse's interiority: dreams, restraint, a boss, a price he did not set. Critics registered the wager's success — the hijacker made relatable without being acquitted. The analysis the archive files is that the sympathy is structural, not sentimental: Muse is given Phillips's exact professional virtues (adaptation, command, endurance) inside a criminal economy, so the collision reads as two systems meeting, not good meeting evil.

Removed by a story

Muse is never outfought. He is out-narrated — the elders ruse succeeds because it is the one development his ambition cannot afford to disbelieve. The film's intelligence apparatus wins not with the snipers but with a fiction pitched precisely to its target, and Muse's arrest, bloodless and polite, is the coldest beat in his file.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

A debut from a casting call

Abdi and his fellow Somali actors were chosen from more than 700 participants at a 2011 Minneapolis casting call; Abdi had no acting experience. The pirate actors trained for some six weeks — swimming, fighting, weapons and the skiff — before shooting, and were kept apart from Hanks until the bridge scene.

The BAFTA

The performance won the BAFTA Award for Best Supporting Actor, with Academy Award, Golden Globe and SAG nominations alongside — the most decorated debut in the Galaxy's 2013 run. Abdi has said he was paid $65,000 for the film and returned to work in his brother's shop afterwards.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Muse is the decade's defining screen pirate — not a caricature but a case study, and the performance that proved a casting call in Minneapolis could out-act Hollywood. Within the Galaxy he anchors 2013's theme of mirrored captains, and his file remains the archive's fullest portrait of an antagonist whose tragedy is ambition in a ruined market.

The real Muse's sentence, preserved in the film's title cards, closes the record the performance opened: the striver's story ends in an Indiana federal prison, while the film of it made its actor's career.
