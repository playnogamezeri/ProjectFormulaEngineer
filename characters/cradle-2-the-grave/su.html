01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Su enters the film as an interruption: a voice on a wiretap, then a man in the vault, ordering Anthony Fait's crew to leave the diamonds and walk away. He is a Taiwanese intelligence agent tracking a bag of black diamonds — in truth synthetic plutonium in gemstone form — stolen by a rogue former colleague, Ling, and now scattered through Los Angeles by a heist that was never about jewels. When Ling kidnaps Fait's daughter to force the stones' return, Su's mission and Fait's fatherhood align, and the agent who began the film arresting the thief ends it fighting beside him. The Galaxy film page names his function exactly: the professional whose mission keeps being interrupted by the human facts inside it.

ROLE

The agent

Co-protagonist; Taiwanese intelligence, hunting the stones and the man who stole them first.

DEFINING TRAIT

Mission, interrupted by people

He opens the film as pure procedure — a voice, a warning, a fight — and is steadily converted into a partner and a rescuer.

FATE

The debt, settled

He kills Ling with the weapon itself, rescues Vanessa, and lets Fait walk away a promised-reformed man.

Place in the story

Su is the film's imported centre of gravity: the Hong Kong action tradition, in the person of Jet Li, set down inside a Los Angeles heist picture. His page is the film's spine — the stones, the villain and the partnership all originate in his mission.

Li plays Su at the still, economical register that is his signature in the Bartkowiak films — the Galaxy record notes the taciturn professionalism — and the archive observes that the performance is built on withholding: Su's warmth is rationed so strictly that its appearances (the partnership, the rescue) register as events. 03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Su, a Taiwanese intelligence agent, is tracking a consignment of black diamonds — synthetic plutonium disguised as gemstones — connected to his former colleague Ling. He intercepts Anthony Fait's call to the French broker Christophe and moves on the crew's diamond heist in progress, phoning Fait mid-robbery to order him to leave the stones in the vault before the police arrive. Fait refuses. In the escape past a SWAT blockade, Su fights the crewman Miles and recovers his share of the take — and is dismayed to find Miles's bag holds no black diamonds.

That night Su and Fait collide in person. Ling phones Fait demanding the stones; two of Ling's henchmen attack; Su helps Fait defeat them. The alliance is sealed by the second call: Ling has kidnapped Fait's eight-year-old daughter, Vanessa. Su and Fait now hunt together — first for the stones, which the fixer Archie has already surrendered to gangsters under threat, and then through the jailed crime lord 'Jump' Chambers's organisation, in whose nightclub office the stones are hidden in a bubble bath. To find the gangsters, Su enters an underground fight club as a combatant — a cage bout against fifteen fighters — while Archie identifies their man by his ring. They reach the nightclub too late: Ling's men have taken the stones first.

On the drive to the hangar where Ling will auction the stones to flown-in arms buyers, Su tells Fait the buried history: he and Ling were childhood best friends who joined the government together, until Ling's greed led him to murder their entire task team; Su alone escaped. At the airfield, while the crew fights Ling's team and Vanessa is rescued, Su faces Ling alone. He forces a capsule of the synthetic plutonium down Ling's throat and breaks it in his neck — the weapon killing its broker from inside. With the police arriving, Su lets Fait make his promise to quit crime and go home to his daughter.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Procedure first

Interpretation: Su's opening acts are all protocol — intercept, warn, recover, report. The film's interest is in watching protocol fail to contain the situation: the stones matter less, scene by scene, than the kidnapped child.

The survivor's economy

He is the one man who walked out of Ling's massacre of their team. Interpretation: his stillness is a survivor's discipline — nothing wasted, nothing shown — and it is why his final violence, when it comes, is so absolute.

Loyalty, transferred

He begins the film hunting Fait and ends it covering him. The archive reads the transfer plainly: Su recognises in Fait the thing Ling discarded — a man whose word to his own people holds.

Defeats Miles in the vault escape, two of Ling's henchmen in the street, and fifteen combatants in the underground cage — the film's benchmark of capability.

INTELLIGENCE CRAFT

Intercepts and informants

Wiretaps the Christophe call, works Archie and the fight-club informant, and reads the auction's logistics from flight schedules.

IMPROVISATION

His killing of Ling weaponises the merchandise itself — the plutonium capsule forced and broken in the broker's neck.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The black diamonds

Interpretation: Su is the only principal who knows from the start what the stones are — value as pure disguise — and his arc is the transfer of that knowledge into action: everyone else in the film is pricing the object wrong.

The cage

Interpretation: the fight club strips the agent of every institutional tool — no badge, no wire, no team — and leaves the man. It is the film's portrait of what Su is when the mission cannot help him.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the professional, humanised by interruption

The analysis the archive records follows the Galaxy page's own reading: Su's story is a mission repeatedly interrupted by human facts — a thief who is a father, a partner who was a friend, a weapon that is a kidnapped child's price. Each interruption moves him further from procedure and closer to the film's actual subject, which is what professionals owe the people inside their operations.

The third Bartkowiak panel

Su completes the trilogy of Bartkowiak–Silver films (Romeo Must Die, Exit Wounds, Cradle 2 the Grave) in which Li's stillness is set against an American urban ensemble. The archive notes the formula's consistency: the agent's economy of movement and speech is the fixed point around which the louder film organises itself.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Jet Li

Li stars as Su in the third of director Andrzej Bartkowiak's martial-arts/hip-hop films produced by Joel Silver; the cage-fight sequence took ten days to shoot.

The name in the record

Wikipedia's cast list gives the character as "Su Duncan"; the film itself uses Su, and his page keeps the surname flagged as a cast-record detail.

Production

Filmed in Los Angeles from March 2002; released February 28, 2003, debuting at number one in North America and grossing about $56 million worldwide.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Su stands in the Galaxy record as the Jet Li run's representative of Li's Hollywood period — the still agent amid the noise — and as the co-anchor, with Fait, of the era's defining action hybrid: Hong Kong martial arts inside an American hip-hop heist film. The capsule killing remains one of the period's most remembered villain deaths.

Within the film, his arc — procedure converted into partnership — is the template the Bartkowiak trilogy refined across three pictures, and this is its final, most complete statement.
