01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Inspector Cheng enters the plot as a victim — taken hostage by Kung Wei during the collapsed arms deal — and is exchanged for his own subordinate and girlfriend, Anne Fong, who volunteers in his place. Thereafter Cheng functions as the establishment's ear, stopped: Fong brings him the truth, that Wei is an undercover officer, and Cheng will not credit it. Acting instead on procedure, he reports young Ku as missing — against Fong's wishes — to lure Wei out. The publicity draws Po as reliably as it draws Wei, the boy reads it as betrayal and runs, and Po collects him: the film's worst outcome is authored not by its villain but by its chain of command.

ROLE

The superior

Fong's boss and boyfriend; the Hong Kong police's senior voice in the film, and its most consequential failure.

DEFINING TRAIT

Disbelief with authority

Anyone can doubt Fong; only Cheng can convert doubt into policy — a media lure built out of an eight-year-old.

FATE

Sidelined by his own gambit

The documented plot gives him no part in the boat climax; the rescue is executed by Fong, Wei, Ku and Darkie's warning, around his decision rather than through it.

Place in the story

Cheng is the film's quiet indictment, as the Galaxy card files him: the truth about Wei exists inside his service, spoken by his best investigator, and is refused at the level of the org chart. His page is deliberately kept at the size of his documented record — a supporting figure whose two decisions (disbelief, publicity) outweigh his screen time.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Cheng is taken hostage by Kung Wei when the arms deal between Po's gang and foreign criminals goes awry. His subordinate Anne Fong — his girlfriend — volunteers herself as a hostage in his exchange, and in Wei's custody attempts to crash the getaway car before Wei escapes. Cheng is thus freed by the substitution that begins Fong's investigation.

Fong travels to Beijing, befriends Wei's family, and deduces that Wei is a police officer working undercover. She brings Ku to Hong Kong and informs Cheng of her findings. Cheng does not act on her theory. Instead, against Fong's wishes, he reports Ku as missing in order to lure out Wei — a decision that attracts publicity from local media and the attention of both Wei and Po. Ku, believing Fong to have betrayed him, escapes her apartment and is picked up by Po, setting up the penthouse confrontation and the boat climax, in which the documented plot assigns Cheng no part.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Procedure over testimony

Interpretation: Cheng trusts instruments — a missing-child report, media pressure — over the judgment of the officer he knows best. The archive reads him as the film's study in how institutions prefer a legible error to an illegible truth.

The exchanged man

Interpretation: his first scene defines his ratio to Fong: she steps into danger for him; he later steps over her judgment for procedure. The film never gives him a scene that reverses the exchange, and the archive records that absence as the character.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
SKILL

Rank

His authority can convert a theory into an operation overnight — the missing-child lure is mounted on his word, over his own investigator's objection.

SKILL

Media leverage

The gambit works exactly as designed at the level of attention: local media publicise the boy, and every interested party — Wei, Po — sees it.

LIMIT

Judgment

The gambit fails at the level of consequence: it delivers the child to the gang. The film's audit of Cheng is that his tools all function and his decisions all misfire.

LIMIT

Crediting his own people

Fong's deduction — the film's one complete piece of analysis — dies on his desk. No source consulted shows him revising the refusal.

07 / CHARACTER ARC

Character Arc

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
The hostage

Taken by Wei at the collapsed deal; freed by Fong's self-substitution — the film opens his account in her debt.

The refusal

Fong reports that Wei is an undercover officer; Cheng will not credit his own investigator's solved case.

The gambit

Against her wishes he publicises Ku as missing to bait Wei — and baits Po with the same hook.

The consequence

The boy runs, Po collects him, and the climax is staffed entirely by people working around Cheng's decision.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The missing-child report

Interpretation: the institution's fiction answering the service's fiction — Wei's cover is a manufactured criminal; Cheng's lure is a manufactured emergency. The archive reads the parallel as the film's bleakest symmetry: both fictions spend the same child.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The deafness at the top

Analysis: the film distributes knowledge with care — Darkie knows the gang, Fong knows the truth, Ku knows the cost — and places its one refusal at the rank where refusal becomes policy. Cheng is not written as corrupt or stupid; he is written as senior, which the film treats as the more dangerous condition.

An honest record of a thin file

Analysis / honest record: Cheng's documented plot presence is two movements — the hostage exchange and the missing-child decision — plus his relationship to Fong, on which the sources differ in tense. This page builds no further scenes, motives or redemption for him, because the record consulted contains none.
