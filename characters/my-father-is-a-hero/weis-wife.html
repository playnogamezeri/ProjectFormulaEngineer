01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

She is never named in the documented record — the sources call her only Wei's wife, Ku's mother. She has severe asthma, and she is raising an eight-year-old martial-arts student whose father has been arrested as a criminal in front of the family's world. Whether she was ever told the truth about the undercover assignment, the documented plot does not say: the film presents a wife and son who believe the disgrace, and her scenes are played inside that belief. When Hong Kong detective Anne Fong befriends the family in Beijing, it is in Fong's company that the wife suffers her fatal asthma attack — and her dying requests, that Fong deliver a letter to Wei and take care of Ku, are the hinge on which the whole second half turns.

ROLE

The home front

The cover's domestic cost centre; her illness is the clock the plot races, and her letter is its turning document.

DEFINING TRAIT

Authorship from a sickbed

She performs the film's two most consequential acts — choosing Fong, and writing to Wei — while dying, and both acts outmanoeuvre every institution in the story.

FATE

Dies of a fatal asthma attack

In Beijing, during Fong's stay, with her requests spoken and her son provided for as well as she can provide.

Place in the story

The Galaxy card files her as the story's portrait of the cover's home front, and the archive concurs, with one sharpening: she is also its hidden author. The letter she sends is the document the film is named for in Chinese — a letter to daddy — and its instruction, trust Fong, is the order no superior in the film ever gives.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Wei's wife is introduced inside the assignment's domestic wreckage: her husband, a mainland police officer, is sent undercover against a Hong Kong gang, and the cover requires his arrest as a criminal. She is left with severe asthma, an eight-year-old son, and a disgrace she must live inside; the documented plot records the family's belief that Wei is a criminal, and does not record her being told otherwise. Her son is teased at school by bullies who believe his father is a real criminal; the one thread to the absent man is a beeper number Wei gave the boy.

Hong Kong detective Anne Fong, working from a photograph of Wei, travels to Beijing and befriends the family while deducing Wei's true role. During Fong's stay, the wife suffers a fatal asthma attack. Before dying, she makes two requests of the detective: deliver a letter to Wei, and take care of Ku. Wei, deep in Po's gang, receives word that his wife has died and in grief almost blows his cover. The letter itself reaches him later, in Fong's Hong Kong apartment, and its message — that he should trust Fong — converts the hunt into the alliance that finishes the film. Ku travels to Hong Kong in Fong's care, as his mother arranged.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Provision under ignorance

Interpretation: everything she does is done without the information the audience has. Judged inside her knowledge — a criminal husband, a failing body, a son of eight — her choices (endure, then delegate with precision) are the film's most rational acts.

The chooser of Fong

Interpretation: she reads the detective correctly in days, where Fong's own service misreads everyone for the whole film. The archive reads her deathbed delegation as an act of the same detection Fong practises — applied to a person, not a case.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
SKILL

Judgment of people

She identifies the one trustworthy adult in her son's vicinity and binds her to the family with a letter and a charge — correctly, as the climax proves.

SKILL

The letter

Her written instruction to Wei — trust Fong — accomplishes what no order, signal or beeper message in the film manages: it changes an undercover officer's conduct at the decisive moment.

LIMIT

Asthma and absence

Her body is the plot's clock: severe asthma, a husband unreachable, a service that has classified her out of her own marriage's facts. The record gives her no scene of confrontation or explanation — her power is exercised entirely through arrangement.

07 / CHARACTER ARC

Character Arc

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
The disgrace endured

Left inside the cover story with an asthmatic body and an eight-year-old, holding a household together under a public shame she cannot answer.

The detective received

Fong arrives investigating her husband; the wife receives her — and, the archive notes in interpretation, reads her faster than anyone in the film reads anyone.

The deathbed arrangements

A fatal asthma attack; two requests — a letter delivered, a son cared for — that restructure the plot more completely than any police operation in it.

The letter's journey

Posthumously she directs the endgame: her instruction to trust Fong reaches Wei in Hong Kong and turns hunter and hunted into the team that boards the boat.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The letter to daddy

Interpretation: the film's Chinese title names it for her document. The archive reads the letter as the cover's antithesis — the one communication in the story that is unclassified, complete, and true, written by the person with the least access to the truth.

Breath, failing and held

Interpretation: the film's breath motif is hers first — asthma takes her breath in Beijing; her son's breathing exercise saves his in Hong Kong. Mother and son are linked by the film's central physiology: what she could not hold, he was trained to.

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Why she has a page

Analysis / debated selection: the batch debated this page — she appears only in the Beijing movement and dies mid-film. The archive includes her because the documented record gives her the plot's two hinge acts (the letter; the guardianship charge) and because her husband's and son's pages both route through her. The counter-consideration — thin screen record — is answered in the honest-record mode: her page states the thinness rather than padding it.

What she knew

Analysis / honest record: the sources consulted do not establish whether she knew Wei was undercover. The plot presents the family believing him a criminal; her letter's content, beyond the instruction to trust Fong, is not documented in the sources. This page records both limits in place and builds nothing on either side of them.

The uncredited centre

Analysis: her absence from the principal cast lists — and the single review credit naming Nancy She Nan-Nan as the boy's mother — is flagged in the batch report as a film-page matter: the Galaxy card, like Wikipedia's cast list, carries no performer for the role. The archive does not correct the card; it records the gap.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

The casting record

Wikipedia's cast list for the film names Li, Mui, Xie Miao, Yu Rongguang, Damian Lau, Corey Yuen and Blackie Ko, with no entry for the wife; the Galaxy card likewise credits no performer. Frame Rated's review, consulted for this page, names Nancy She Nan-Nan as Ku's mother and notes her background as a National Theatre of China stage actress — the archive cites that as one review's credit, the fullest the record offered.

The title

The film's Chinese title translates as 'Letter to Daddy' — the wife's letter is not a subplot but the title object, which the archive takes as production-level confirmation of her structural centrality.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

She is the Galaxy's standing case for the uncredited centre: a character with no name in the record, no place in the principal cast lists, and authorship of the plot's decisive document. Her file also anchors the film's breath motif, completed by her son aboard the boat.

Within this film's pages she is the fixed point three other files cite — Wei's grief, Ku's guardianship, Fong's charge all originate in her two deathbed requests.
