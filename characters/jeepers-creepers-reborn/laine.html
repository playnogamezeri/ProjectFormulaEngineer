01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Laine is the protagonist of Jeepers Creepers: Reborn (2022), the reboot of the Creeper franchise directed by Timo Vuorensola. She travels to Louisiana's Horror Hound festival as the skeptic half of a couple — her boyfriend Chase is the horror obsessive; she is along for the trip — carrying two secrets that structure the film: she believes she is pregnant, and from the moment she touches an artifact in a roadside gift shop, she is receiving premonitions. The Creeper, newly awakened for its twenty-three days of feeding, is hunting her specifically: the local cult that worships the creature has marked her unborn child for a ritual the film shows in her visions but never fully explains — an honest gap this file records rather than fills. Laine's arc is the film's one complete line: from dragged-along girlfriend to abducted offering to the strategist who lures the creature out, blinds it with its own shuriken, and drops a weather vane through it. She survives. The creature, elsewhere, regenerates.

ROLE

Protagonist — the chosen

The cult's intended offering and the Creeper's specific target: the plot's machinery — festival, raffle, plantation house — exists to deliver her.

DEFINING TRAIT

Reception

Visions, premonitions and an unnatural gift with the creature's own weapon: Laine is tuned to the Creeper's frequency before she knows the station exists.

FATE

Survivor

She escapes the altar, blinds the creature, engineers its impalement, and leaves Louisiana alive with Chase — while the Creeper, elsewhere, pulls itself back together.

Place in the story

Laine is the film's center of gravity in a way its other characters are not: the festival is bait for her, the raffle is rigged for her, the house is an altar for her. The film positions her as the reboot's thesis about its own franchise — the legend's victims used to be chosen by appetite; this one is chosen by design, and the design is what she has to out-think.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Laine's film begins on the road in 2021: she and Chase are driving to the first Horror Hound festival in rural Louisiana, a trip that is his pilgrimage and her concession. Two facts travel with her unspoken — Chase is carrying a ring, planning to propose; Laine believes she is pregnant and has told no one. The Creeper, meanwhile, has awakened for its cycle and begun feeding to build its strength. At a gift shop stop, Laine touches a mysterious artifact and receives the first premonition; the shop's owner, Lady Manilla, gives the couple cryptic messages that read, in retrospect, as a marking. At the hotel, Laine's attempt to take a pregnancy test is interrupted by a crow slamming into the window — the film's omen machinery announcing itself.

At the festival, the tuning deepens: Laine picks up a strange shuriken and discovers she is abnormally skilled at throwing it — a weapon recognizing its user, the film implies, without explaining. The Creeper arrives and begins hunting, knocking out the area's internet connectivity with its truck. Laine and Chase enter a raffle hosted by the festival's organizer, Madame Carnage, for a night in an abandoned plantation house converted into an escape room — and Laine begins having visions of herself in a strange ritual inside that house. They win, of course. A camera crew and the local guide Stu accompany them to document the prize.

In the 18th-century graveyard on the approach, the film's two plots collide in one gesture: as Chase proposes, the Creeper kills the cameraman and abducts Laine. She wakes bound to a table in the house, where the Creeper pierces her abdomen with a knife — the ritual begun. It is interrupted by accident: inside the house, the trapped group feuds, Stu fires his pistol into the air, and the sound draws the Creeper off its altar to hunt the house instead — killing the group one by one until only Stu and Chase remain alive. Laine frees herself, joins them, and tells Chase the truth at last: she is pregnant, and the Creeper has targeted her because of it. The survivors also piece together the frame around them — Madame Carnage, Lady Manilla and others have been worshipping the Creeper and luring victims to the house for its consumption; the festival is the cult's harvest machinery.

The counterattack is Laine's design: she will lure the Creeper outside while Chase and Stu, on the roof, push the house's weather vane down onto it. It works in the film's most complete sequence — Laine blinds the creature with its own shuriken, the vane falls and impales it, and a murder of crows descends to consume the Creeper's body (killing Stu in the same motion, knocking him from the roof — the film does not explain the crows' allegiance, and this file records the ambiguity as the film leaves it). Laine and Chase depart. Elsewhere, the Creeper regenerates and roars: the cycle's paperwork, filed for the sequel the film was designed to start.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The skeptic in the fan's world

Laine is the film's one non-believer at a horror festival — dragged along, dry about the legend, unimpressed by the merchandise — which makes her the audience's surrogate in reverse: the person who treats the Creeper as a joke is the one it has chosen. Her arc's first movement is belief arriving as evidence, in visions she cannot file away.

Secrecy as self-protection

She carries the pregnancy unspoken through two acts — through the road trip, the festival, the raffle — a privacy the film treats sympathetically: it is the one fact about her that is hers, in a story where a cult, a creature and a boyfriend's ring are all making plans for her body. Her disclosure, when it comes, is tactical as well as emotional: she tells Chase when the information becomes a weapon.

Composure under design

Abducted mid-proposal, bound to an altar, pierced — Laine's response pattern is the final girl's classic one, executed cleanly: free herself, find the others, and convert her status from offering to bait on her own terms. The weather-vane plan is hers; the film's climax is a victim redesigning the ritual's ending.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
SKILL

Premonition

From the gift-shop artifact onward, Laine receives visions of the ritual and the house — the cult's design leaking to its victim in advance. The film never explains the gift's source; this file records it as presented, unexplained.

SKILL

The shuriken

She handles the Creeper's own thrown weapon with abnormal, untrained skill — and uses it, at the climax, to blind the creature: the one offensive capability the film grants her, and it is the monster's.

SKILL

Field strategy

The weather-vane trap — lure, elevation, gravity — is her plan, built from the house's own architecture in minutes, under hunt conditions.

WEAKNESS

Chosen

Her connection to the Creeper is a targeting system she cannot switch off: the visions that warn her also mark her, and the pregnancy that motivates her survival is the precise reason she was harvested.

WEAKNESS

Unarmed among professionals of the hunt

Until the shuriken, Laine has no weapon, no training and no allies who believe her — the film's first two acts isolate her inside a festival full of people dressed as the thing that is actually coming.

07 / CHARACTER ARC

Character Arc

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
Beginning state — the dragged-along skeptic

A passenger on her boyfriend's pilgrimage, carrying an unspoken pregnancy into a festival built on a legend she does not believe.

The tuning — artifact, omen, shuriken

The gift shop marks her, the crow announces her, and the creature's weapon recognizes her hand: belief arrives as a series of facts she cannot file away.

The altar — abduction mid-proposal

Taken at the exact second of the ring, bound, and pierced: the chosen victim at the ritual's center, saved by a panicked gunshot she did not arrange.

The redesign — confession and trap

She frees herself, tells Chase the truth, identifies the cult, and converts herself from offering to bait — the plan, the lure and the blinded creature are all hers.

End state — the survivor

Out of Louisiana with Chase, alive, her secret spoken — while the creature she impaled pulls itself back together elsewhere, for a cycle the film leaves open.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The interrupted proposal — ordinary life, twice ambushed

The ring and the pregnancy test are the film's twin emblems of the future Laine and Chase were scheduling; the Creeper interrupts both — a crow through the test, an abduction through the proposal. The symbolism is the reboot's thesis: horror, in this film, is what happens to other people's plans for your body.

The weather vane — the house's own instrument

She kills the creature with the plantation house's furniture — the altar's architecture turned executioner. As interpretation: the cult built the trap, stocked it and consecrated it, and its own roof-ornament is what falls; the film's one poetic justice is architectural.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Narrative function: the final girl as strategist

Laine performs the genre's classic function with the reboot's one modernization: she is not merely the last one standing but the plan's author — the climax is her design, executed by others. The film's structure (visions as intelligence, the shuriken as capability, the trap as conclusion) makes her the only character whose information and agency both grow across the runtime.

Investigation: the honest gaps

This file records, rather than repairs, the film's unexplained machinery: the ritual's purpose for the unborn child is shown in visions but never stated; the source of Laine's premonitions and shuriken-skill is implied (a connection to the Creeper) but never defined; the crows — omens, servants, scavengers — kill Stu and consume the Creeper without the film declaring their allegiance. The archive's judgment is that these are gaps in the film, not mysteries this page may solve by invention.

Unresolved questions the film leaves about her

Whether the Creeper's regeneration renews its claim on her child; what the cult's survivors intend; what she tells the authorities — the film's closing movement gives her a departure, not an epilogue. The reboot was conceived as the first part of a new trilogy; no sequel has materialized in the documented record, and Laine's story stands, as released, at the Louisiana state line.

Critical reception of the character

The film was critically panned on release — for production values, lack of scares and performances — and Laine's file inherits that verdict honestly: Sydney Craven carries the reboot's only complete arc, and the reviews' general judgment was that the material around the arc did not rise to it. The character's design (chosen mother, prescient victim, trap-designer) is documented here as the film's most considered construction, whatever the execution's reception.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Performance

Sydney Craven leads the reboot as Laine — a final-girl performance built on the skeptic-to-strategist line the screenplay gives her. The film was released worldwide by Screen Media Films on September 19, 2022.

The character's design

Laine was written as the reboot's new center: where the original films' protagonists stumbled onto the Creeper, she is scheduled for it — the cult, the raffle and the ritual make her the first Creeper protagonist who is a target before she is a witness. The pregnancy-as-prize construction is the reboot's signature addition to the franchise's machinery, and its least explained — a gap this file has recorded rather than decorated.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Laine's legacy within the franchise is structural: she is the reboot's proof-of-concept for a Creeper story organized around a chosen victim rather than a wandering one — the legend with a liturgy. The film's reception has kept that legacy provisional; as the first part of an uncontinued trilogy, her file closes mid-sentence, with the creature regenerated and the claim on her child unresolved.

Within this Galaxy, Laine's file is the archive's record of the reboot's center: her card — "the chosen · protagonist" — is a designation; this page documents the visions, the altar and the weather vane that made the designation a survival story.
