01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Chase is the co-protagonist of Jeepers Creepers: Reborn (2022): a self-described paranormal fanatic and horror fanboy for whom the trip to Louisiana's first Horror Hound festival is a pilgrimage — and the Creeper, whose legend he knows by heart, is the main attraction. He travels with a plan his girlfriend does not know about: he means to propose. The film is, in large part, his education. The legend he consumed as entertainment turns out to be a working harvest — a cult, a rigged raffle, an altar — and the education's tuition is everything the festival takes from the people around him. Chase's function is the audience's mirror: he is the horror fan inside the horror film, and the film spends its runtime converting his enthusiasm into comprehension. He survives — proposes, in effect, twice: once interrupted by the abduction, and once in everything he does afterward — and leaves Louisiana with Laine, the ring's question answered by conduct rather than ceremony.

ROLE

Co-protagonist — the fan

The legend's consumer, educated by contact: his arc is the film's commentary on its own audience, played from inside.

DEFINING TRAIT

Enthusiasm, retrained

Chase begins as the man who knows the Creeper's cycle by heart and ends as the man on the roof pushing the weather vane — knowledge converted, at cost, into use.

FATE

Survivor

He loses the crew, the guide and the ceremony, and keeps the girl: out of Louisiana with Laine, the proposal's answer delivered in the only currency the night accepted.

Place in the story

Chase is the film's mediation layer: through him, the reboot addresses the franchise's own fanbase — the people who attend Horror Hound in the story and the people who bought tickets to it in fact. His position is the film's most self-aware: the fan is neither mocked nor spared, but educated — the legend is real, the festival is a trap, and fandom is not armor.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Chase's film begins behind the wheel in 2021, driving his girlfriend Laine to the first Horror Hound festival in rural Louisiana — a trip he has framed, privately, as the most important of his life: he is carrying an engagement ring, and the festival of his favorite legend is to be the backdrop of his proposal. His knowledge is encyclopedic and entirely recreational: the Creeper's cycle — every twenty-three years, twenty-three days of feeding — is, to him, the best story in the world. Laine's gift-shop premonition and Lady Manilla's cryptic courtesies pass through his attention as atmosphere; the crow through the hotel window is, to the fan, production design.

At the festival he is in his element — and his element is a trap being baited around his girlfriend. The Creeper's arrival, the severed internet, the raffle for the plantation-house night: Chase experiences the machinery as the best weekend of his life, up to the moment in the 18th-century graveyard where the machinery's purpose stands up in front of him. As he proposes — ring out, on the approach to the house — the Creeper kills the cameraman and takes Laine. The fan's education begins at that exact frame: the legend, in person, interrupting the most real moment of his life to collect its offering.

Inside the house, Chase is one of the hunted group the Creeper works through methodically — the crew, the guests, picked off around him — until only he, Stu and the escaped Laine remain. There the film gives him its two defining transactions: Laine's confession (she is pregnant; the creature wants the child) recasts his entire weekend in one sentence, and her plan gives him his post: on the roof, with Stu, to push the weather vane down on her signal. He holds the position, the vane falls true, the crows finish the creature and take Stu — and Chase comes down from the roof a survivor, which the film defines precisely: the fan who stayed. He and Laine depart Louisiana together. Elsewhere, the Creeper regenerates — the legend, as Chase now knows from the inside, keeping its own schedule.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The fan's joy, genuine

The film never makes Chase's enthusiasm contemptible: his delight in the legend and the festival is the most uncomplicated pleasure in the movie, and its destruction is staged as a loss, not a comeuppance. He is the audience's better self — the one who came for fun.

Romantic, in the practical tense

His proposal plan is a fan's production — the festival, the moment, the backdrop — and the film's gentlest judgment is that the production fails and the romance doesn't: everything Chase does after the abduction is the proposal, restated in the only language the night accepts.

Comprehension, arriving at speed

Chase's arc is measured in recalibrations: legend to machinery, festival to harvest, girlfriend's silence to pregnancy. He is never the last to understand once the facts are real — the fan's training, it turns out, included pattern recognition; it just had the patterns' prices wrong.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
SKILL

Legend literacy

He knows the Creeper's cycle, habits and history cold — useless as protection, decisive as orientation: Chase is the one person in the house who never wastes a minute doubting what is hunting them.

SKILL

Execution under direction

The roof work — position, timing, the vane — is his: the plan is Laine's, but its heaviest moving part is the fan's arms, and they do not fail her.

WEAKNESS

The fan's blind spot

For two acts he reads real omens as atmosphere — the gift shop, the crow, the cryptic shopkeeper — because his genre training files them as entertainment. The film's sharpest irony is his: expertise in the legend delayed his recognition of the event.

WEAKNESS

No combat capability

Against the creature he is, like everyone in the house, meat with opinions: his survival is owed to Laine's design, Stu's accident and the crows' appetite — a dependency the film never disguises.

07 / CHARACTER ARC

Character Arc

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
Beginning state — the pilgrim

Driving to the festival of his favorite legend, ring in pocket, treating omens as décor: enthusiasm at its maximum, comprehension at zero.

The festival — inside the bait

Every delight is machinery: the severed internet, the raffle, the house. Chase enjoys the trap's showroom while it closes around Laine.

The graveyard — the education begins

Mid-proposal, the legend takes his girlfriend and kills the cameraman: the fan's genre and his life become the same text.

The house — confession and conversion

Laine's truth recasts the weekend; her plan gives him the roof; his legend-knowledge becomes orientation and his arms become the trap's engine.

End state — the survivor

Down from the roof, out of Louisiana, with Laine: the fan who learned the price of the story and stayed in it anyway.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The ring — the fan's production, the man's proof

Chase's proposal is staged twice: once as production (the festival backdrop, interrupted) and once as conduct (the house, the roof, the departure). As interpretation, the ring is the film's measure of its co-protagonist — the object matters less than the second performance of its meaning.

The festival — fandom as harvest

Horror Hound gathers the legend's consumers in one place for the legend's convenience: the film's most pointed symbolism is industrial — an audience assembled by its own appetite, processed by the thing it came to celebrate. Chase is the symbol's human face: appetite, educated.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Narrative function: the audience's surrogate, educated

Chase exists so the reboot can address its own viewers from inside the frame: his trajectory — consumer, witness, participant, survivor — is the film's model of what it wants its audience to undergo in miniature. The construction is the reboot's most deliberate: the fan is the only character whose relationship to the Creeper predates the film, and the film is the story of that relationship's correction.

Investigation: the proposal's placement

The archive notes the screenplay's cruelest structural decision — the abduction interrupts the proposal at its exact apex, ring visible — and its function: it fuses the film's two plots (the couple's future, the cult's design) into a single contested object, Laine, at a single contested second. Every later beat (the altar, the confession, the roof) is that second, litigated.

Unresolved questions the film leaves about him

The ring's final disposition — whether the proposal is ever completed on screen — is left, in the documented accounts, to the departure's implication; the film's interest is the conduct, not the ceremony. His account of the night to Officer Edwards and the law is the closing movement's business, not his arc's. The uncontinued trilogy leaves his future with Laine — and the child — at the state line.

Critical reception of the character

The film's pans covered its performances generally, and this file records Chase's reception inside that verdict: Imran Adams plays the fan's education as the script writes it, and the character's design — the audience's surrogate, corrected — remains the reboot's most legible idea, whatever the execution's notices.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Performance

Imran Adams co-leads as Chase, playing the horror fan from inside the horror film. The production's framing of the couple — his fandom, her skepticism — is the reboot's entry device, and Adams carries the education half of it.

The character's design

Chase was written as the franchise's first fan-protagonist: a character whose knowledge of the Creeper comes from the same place the audience's does. The design's wager is that the education of such a character is the reboot's story — the legend's price list, taught to its most devoted consumer.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Chase's legacy is the reboot's mirror: he stands in the franchise's record as the fan who got the full tour — festival, altar, roof — and whose survival certifies the film's one optimistic claim, that enthusiasm can be educated into courage at survivable cost. The uncontinued trilogy leaves the certification provisional, like everything else in this film's file.
