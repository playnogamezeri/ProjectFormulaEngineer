01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Stu is the principal supporting character of Jeepers Creepers: Reborn (2022): the local tour guide attached to the camera crew documenting the raffle winners' night at the plantation house. He is the film's ordinary man — no fandom, no visions, no cult, no plan — and its cruelest case study. Twice, Stu is the hinge of the plot: his pistol shot, fired into the air in panic during the trapped group's feud, draws the Creeper away from Laine's altar at the ritual's apex, saving her life by accident; and his hands, with Chase's, push the weather vane that impales the creature. His reward is the film's bleakest beat: at the moment of the kill, the murder of crows that descends to consume the Creeper knocks Stu from the roof to his death. Stu matters because he is the reboot's control sample for heroism — an unremarkable man who does, by accident and by labor, the two most consequential things in the film, and whose file closes with the archive's standing note for such characters: the victory had his name on the work and the crows had his name on the bill.

ROLE

Supporting — the guide

Local knowledge attached to the prize crew: the house's working man, and the plot's accidental hinge — twice.

DEFINING TRAIT

Ordinariness under load

No gifts, no legend-knowledge, no arc of belief: Stu simply stays, shoots, climbs and pushes — the film's measure of what an unadorned person contributes to a haunted house.

FATE

Killed at the victory

Knocked from the roof by the crows as the weather vane falls: dead at the exact second of the success he engineered.

Place in the story

Stu is the film's ground level inside the house: where Laine is chosen and Chase is a fan, Stu is employed — the local hired to guide visitors through a legend he works beside without consuming or worshipping it. The film positions him as the third point of its triangle of relations to the Creeper (victim, fan, worker), and then gives the worker the plot's two decisive actions and its most unjust death.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Stu enters the film as staff: when Laine and Chase win Madame Carnage's raffle — a night in the abandoned plantation house, converted into an escape room — a camera crew accompanies them to document the prize, and Stu, the local tour guide, goes with them. Through the 18th-century graveyard on the approach, the night stops being a promotion: the Creeper kills the cameraman and abducts Laine in mid-proposal, and Stu is inside the house with the survivors when the hunt begins — a working man locked into the legend he guides tourists around.

His first decisive act is an accident of panic. As the trapped group feuds over their situation, Stu fires his pistol into the air — and the shot, meant for no one, does the most important work of the film: it draws the Creeper's attention away from the altar, where Laine lies bound with the ritual begun and her abdomen pierced. The creature leaves its offering to hunt the noise. The archive records the beat as the reboot's thesis in miniature: the cult's ceremony, generations in the preparing, is interrupted by a frightened employee's ceiling shot.

The Creeper then hunts the house methodically, killing the group until only Stu and Chase remain — and Laine, freed, joins them with the truth and a plan: she will lure the creature outside; Chase and Stu, from the roof, will push the weather vane down onto it. Stu climbs, takes his position, and pushes true: the vane impales the Creeper. In the same motion, the murder of crows descends on the creature's body — and knocks Stu from the roof to his death. The film offers no explanation for the crows' targeting, and this file records his death as it stands: the victory's engineer, killed by the victory's cleanup crew, in the victory's own second.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Working composure, honestly limited

Stu is not brave in the fan's or the final girl's register: he panics, he feuds, he fires at the ceiling. The film's respect for him is that none of it stops him being useful — his panic saves Laine and his labor kills the Creeper, a curriculum vitae no other character in the film matches.

The local's pragmatism

He works beside the legend without consuming it (Chase) or worshipping it (the cult): to Stu the Creeper country is a job site. The film treats this third relation — employment — as the sanest one available, and then charges it the highest price in the cast.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
SKILL

Local knowledge

The guide's familiarity with the house, the grounds and the country is the crew's infrastructure — the reason a local is attached to the prize at all.

SKILL

The pistol

The film's most consequential firearm discharge is his: unaimed, upward, and perfectly effective — a skill the record credits to luck and the archive credits to the film's design.

SKILL

Roof work

The vane push — position, timing, strength — is executed by Stu and Chase together; the trap's success is half his.

WEAKNESS

Ordinariness

No gifts, no armor, no one's plan includes his survival: in a house where the chosen and the fan both have narrative protection, the guide has a job description, and the crows collect accordingly.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The ceiling shot — grace as accident

The film's decisive rescue is performed by panic, upward, at nothing: as interpretation, Stu's shot is the reboot's one theological statement — in a story of cults, rituals and designs, the offering is saved by an accident, and the trap is completed by a workman. Design belongs to the cult; deliverance belongs to nobody's plan.

The roof — labor's altitude

The killing blow is delivered from the roof, by hands, using the building's own furniture: the film's climax is literally manual labor, and its cost is literally the laborer. The symbolism is the archive's reading, flagged as such: the festival's staff die at the festival's climax, unlisted in its program.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Narrative function: the hinge

Stu exists to turn the plot twice — off the altar, onto the vane — while belonging to none of its factions. His function is mechanical in the best sense: he is the reboot's proof that its machinery (cult, ritual, trap) is operated, interrupted and completed by ordinary labor, and his death is the film's refusal to let that proof be sentimental.

Investigation: the crows' second victim

The archive records the film's most debated beat as an open file: the crows consume the Creeper and kill Stu in the same descent, and the film assigns them no allegiance that would explain either act. Whether Stu's death is the flock's indifference, its price, or its error is unanswerable from the documented record — this file notes that the film gives its most useful man its least explained death, and leaves the disproportion standing.

Unresolved questions the film leaves about him

Everything beyond the roof: no surname, no family, no one shown mourning him. The survivors' account to the law is the film's closing business; Stu's own file closes, as the everyman's does in this franchise, at the scene.

Critical reception of the character

Within the film's general panning, Stu's thread was not singled out — which this file records as consistent with the character: Pete Brooke plays the guide as written, a working man in a haunted house, and the performance's unadorned quality is the role's design rather than its failure.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Performance

Pete Brooke plays Stu, the reboot's everyman. The role's design — panic that saves, labor that kills, death that goes unexplained — makes him the film's most economical character: two actions, one death, no mythology.

The character's design

Stu was written as the triangle's third point: against the cult's worship and Chase's fandom, the guide's employment is the film's portrait of the ordinary economy that runs beside a legend — tours, prizes, documentation — and of what that economy's workers pay when the legend keeps its appointments.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Stu's legacy is the reboot's footnote of honor: the man who saved the protagonist by accident and killed the god by labor, and whose name the victory does not carry. In a franchise of cycles and chosen victims, his file is the archive's record that the machinery of horror runs on staff — and bills them.
