01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Richard is corruption with a desk and a pension. The head of the Paris vice squad, he is also the French connection the heroin operation exists to find — a police inspector who runs prostitution from inside the squad charged with policing it, holding his women by their children, warehoused in orphanages as the leashes. When the joint operation against Mr. Big puts a Chinese officer in his surveillance room, Richard converts the arrest into a double murder and a frame in ten seconds: his guest needles the stabbed smuggler into a coma to save him, and Richard shoots both victims with the guest's own gun. The rest of the film is the frame's administration — a delegation handled, a liaison assassinated on the Seine, a seafood shop strafed, an orphanage ambushed — conducted with the whole jurisdiction as his staff, and it fails on the one fact his system cannot process: Jessica Kamen, his property, was in the hotel bathroom, and Jessica Kamen has terms. He dies in his own office, holding a hostage child, shot Liu and missed the arithmetic — one needle at the forbidden point at the back of his neck, the kiss of the dragon, driving his blood to his brain while his empire's paperwork burns around him. The archive files him as the run's study of the badge as criminal infrastructure: a villain whose power is entirely institutional, and whose defeat is therefore entirely personal.

ROLE

The state as the crime

The vice squad's head and the trade's owner in one chair: the frame's author, the leash's holder, and the jurisdiction's capture made flesh.

DEFINING TRAIT

Violence as administration

He does not rage; he files, frames, schedules ambushes and holds children as paperwork. The archive files his calm as the character's horror: murder, for Richard, is a management technique.

FATE

The kiss of the dragon

Killed in his own office by the forbidden needle point, a hostage child in the room and his framed man's gunshot in him first: the institution's man, executed by the guest's most merciful instrument, used once, unmercifully.

Place in the story

Richard's page is the archive's record of the film's true subject: not the fugitive but the jurisdiction. The archive files him as the Galaxy's exemplar of the desk villain — the antagonist whose arsenal is letterhead, custody orders and his own men's machine guns, and whose file closes when the one unadministered fact, a woman in a bathroom, finishes its work.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Richard's biography is an institution's, and the film reconstructs it from its operations: somewhere in his tenure, the vice squad's head became the vice trade's owner — the heroin connection the Chinese are hunting, the prostitution system's proprietor, the holder of orphanage leashes. The archive is careful with the record's limit: the film shows the system working, not its founding, and Richard's past is his present — a man so completely merged with his office that the office's corruption and the man's are one file. The joint operation is his opportunity and his exposure at once: Mr. Big, his partner in the heroin trade, is to be arrested on his own ground, and the arrest's design — Richard's design — places a foreign officer in the surveillance room as the designated author of what follows. When Aja's knife does half his work for him, he improvises the rest with a professional's speed: Liu's gun, two bodies, a coma's witness eliminated, and a Chinese murderer delivered to the Chinese delegation with the evidence arranged. It is, the archive records, a flawless ten seconds — except for the bathroom, whose occupant his system has trained him not to count.

The frame's administration occupies his middle film, and it is conducted at a high level of competence: the delegation is managed with evidence and regret; the liaison's boat meeting is spotted, the tape retaken, the courier assassinated; the fugitive's refuge is identified and strafed, Tai killed in his doorway as a message about shelter. His one intelligence failure compounds silently: Jessica, his property, is in the fugitive's company, sewing his wounds, telling his story — and Richard's file on her has no field for it, because his system records her as an asset, and assets do not defect. When she walks into his own headquarters and takes the tape out of his desk, the failure becomes legible to him at last: he discovers the theft, computes the orphanage as the pair's next move, and lays the ambush that stops the rescue and puts Jessica in a hospital — his system's last clean victory, and the one that spends his remaining margin, because it converts Liu from a fugitive assembling proof into a rescuer with nothing left to protect but a child.

His end is the system's logic returned to its author. He holds Isabel hostage in the police station — the leash, deployed in person at last, the orphanage's paperwork become a child in his office — and receives the assault he has made inevitable: Liu through his henchmen, the twins among them, up the building the inspector commands. Richard shoots his guest in the rescue and, for one held moment, has won the film: the framed man bleeding, the child in his power, the badge intact. The needle is the arithmetic he never learned to do. Liu, wounded, reaches the back of his neck — the forbidden point, the kiss of the dragon — and Richard dies as his own system never permitted anyone to die: publicly, in agony, by a mechanism he cannot file, appeal or ambush, his blood driven to his brain by the guest whose courtesy he counterfeited and whose one cruelty he personally priced. The archive closes his file on the symmetry: the man who held children as paperwork dies in a child's presence, killed by the instrument his victim had used, an hour into their acquaintance, to try to save a dying enemy's life.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The reasonable tone

Interpretation: Richard's menace is administrative — he regrets, he explains, he accommodates, and the bodies accumulate in the passive voice. The archive reads the calm as the character's thesis: this is what institutional corruption sounds like from inside the institution, a man for whom the frame, the leash and the ambush are all, sincerely, just how the office works.

Contempt for the furniture

His system's fatal defect is taxonomic: people are assets, witnesses are inventory, a prostitute in a bathroom is not a presence. The archive files the contempt as the plot's true weapon against him — everything that destroys Richard was in his files all along, misclassified.

Improvisation under procedure

The hotel frame is invented in ten seconds and is flawless but for the bathroom; the archive credits him as the film's second professional — a genuine operator whose procedures are excellent and whose one unexamined assumption (that the disregarded do not count) is load-bearing.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
THE JURISDICTION

A captured state in miniature

The vice squad, its surveillance, its commandos and its evidence rooms are his instruments: he spots meetings on the Seine, retakes tapes, strafes shops and ambushes orphanages with state resources, and the archive counts the apparatus — not any personal prowess — as his superpower.

THE LEASH SYSTEM

Children as instruments

The orphanage arrangement — daughters warehoused, mothers worked, silence rented — is his design and his economy's foundation: the archive files it as the film's most damning exhibit, a cruelty so procedural it has a filing system.

THE FRAME

Ten seconds of authorship

Two murders committed with another man's gun, in another service's operation, with the evidence pre-arranged: the archive counts the hotel improvisation as a masterwork of its kind, defective only in its census of the room.

WEAKNESS

The uncounted witness

Jessica in the bathroom; Jessica with terms; Jessica in his own office, robbing his desk: the archive records his weakness as a single blind spot with three acts — he cannot see the people his system has priced as furniture, and the film is the furniture's reply.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The desk

Interpretation: Richard's power is furniture — a desk, a drawer, a custody file, an office at the top of a station. The archive reads the film's geography as his portrait: the state's rooms are his rooms, until the story's two intrusions (Jessica's theft, Liu's assault) repossess them in sequence, and he dies in the chair the whole apparatus was arranged around.

The leash

Interpretation: the orphanage is the film's coldest symbol — respectability itself conscripted, a children's home as a criminal instrument, its paperwork indistinguishable from care. The archive files the leash as the story's definition of its villain: not a man who does evil despite the institutions, but one whose evil is administered by them, in their handwriting.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the captured jurisdiction

Richard exists to embody the film's institutional thesis: that a corrupt system is not a good system with bad men in it, but a machine whose ordinary operations — custody, vice enforcement, evidence handling — are the crimes. The analysis the archive records is that the film never gives him a scene of private wickedness to match his public one; his villainy has no off-duty register, because the office is the man.

The census error

His defeat is epistemological before it is physical: he loses because his files are wrong about one person. The archive notes the construction's elegance — the frame is flawless, the administration is competent, the apparatus is overwhelming, and the empire falls to an uncounted woman in a bathroom and the terms she sets in a seafood shop. The film's argument is that systems like his do not fall to superior force; they fall to the people they declined to count.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Tchéky Karyo

Karyo's second Besson villainy — after La Femme Nikita's Bob — is played in a lower register than the first: the archive records the production's design as deliberate promotion, the handler become the state, the menace moved from the gun to the desk.

The office's end

The climax was staged in Richard's own seat of power — the station he commands — with the twin-enforcers fight on the approach: the archive notes the geography as the production's argument, the guest fighting up the institution's floors to the desk where its author waits with a child.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Richard stands in the Galaxy's records as the archive's exemplar of the desk villain: the antagonist as an administration, whose crimes have letterhead and whose empire is a filing system. His file is the one future Galaxy pages on institutional corruption are cross-checked against.

His death — by the forbidden point, in his office, in a hostage child's presence — is filed as the run's most exact poetic accounting: the man of paperwork killed by the one mechanism in the film that cannot be filed, by the guest he framed, with the instrument that guest had used to try to save a life in the story's first ten minutes.
