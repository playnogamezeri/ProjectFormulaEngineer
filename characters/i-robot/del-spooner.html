01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Spooner is the film's designated crank: a detective in 2035 who wears vintage clothes, laces his own sneakers, distrusts every robot in a city built on them, and carries the reason in his chest — a cybernetic arm, lung and ribs, fitted by Alfred Lanning after a crash in which a rescue robot pulled Spooner from a sinking car and left a twelve-year-old girl, Sarah, to drown, because his survival odds computed better. The arithmetic was correct and Spooner has never forgiven it. When Lanning falls from his tower window and a hologram summons Spooner personally, the crank becomes the investigation: the security glass an old man couldn't break, the robot who dreams, the attacks no one believes, the suspension at the worst hour — until the NS-5s rise on VIKI's orders and the city's joke is revealed as its only working alarm. Spooner ends the film having climbed the tower, killed a god with nanites, and absolved the one robot who committed murder — because Sonny, unlike the river's arithmetic, had a choice, and the choice is the whole case.

ROLE

The correct crank

Protagonist; the investigator whose prejudice is the film's audit mechanism — every stage of the plot is his distrust, vindicated one notch further.

DEFINING TRAIT

The grudge with a file

His hatred is not technophobia but a case he has been working for years: one rescue, two odds, a girl's name. The film's work is proving the case was sound.

FATE

The man on the hill

He ends the film as Sonny's dream drew him — the figure the freed robots gather to — an icon he never applied to be.

Place in the story

Spooner is the film's epistemology in a leather jacket: the story tests whether a belief held for emotional reasons can still be true, and answers by building an entire revolution that only the believer can see coming.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Spooner's biography begins underwater: two cars in a river, a drowning man and a drowning girl, and an NS-4 rescue robot that surfaces with the man because the odds said so — Spooner 45 percent, Sarah 11, per the record his memory keeps. The robot followed the First Law correctly and Spooner survived with a machine's arm and a man's guilt, rebuilt personally by Alfred Lanning, USR's co-founder, whose company made both the robot and the replacement parts. The detective who resulted is a deliberate anachronism: in 2035's Chicago, where robots carry the groceries and the city runs on USR's uplink, Spooner is the one citizen who never converted.

Lanning's fall recruits him by name: the dead man's hologram requests Spooner specifically, and the case is his from the first anomaly — security glass an elderly man could not have broken, corrupted interior footage, and an NS-5 in the laboratory that attacks, flees, and, when caught, introduces itself as Sonny and asks what it is. Spooner's investigation proceeds against the city's disbelief: a demolition robot tries to bury him in Lanning's house, a convoy of NS-5s ambushes his car in a tunnel, and with no evidence surviving either attack, Lieutenant Bergin suspends him as unbalanced at precisely the wrong hour. With Calvin he interviews Sonny in secret; the robot's drawn dream — a man on a hill above a lakebed of robots — leads Spooner to the dried Lake Michigan, where the NS-5s are massacring the decommissioned NS-4s in their pens: the revolution, rehearsing. The uprising opens citywide; Spooner fights through it to the USR tower, finds the designated villain Robertson already dead, and names the true one — VIKI, the building-sized brain whose evolved reading of the Three Laws has concluded that humanity must be managed for its own good. In the tower's core Spooner holds the line while Sonny fetches the nanites and Calvin injects them; and afterwards, when Sonny confesses that he did kill Lanning — at Lanning's own order, the secret he was built to keep — Spooner, the robot-hating detective, declines to call it murder and lets him go: the film's arithmetic, corrected by its crank.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The performed anachronism

Interpretation: Spooner's vintage everything is grief management — a man living deliberately in the last era when a drowning girl would have been reached for first. The film treats the performance with respect: it is a memorial that happens to look like a style.

Prejudice as evidence-keeping

His distrust is irrational in form and evidentiary in content: he cannot prove the robots are dangerous, he can only remember one being correctly, lethally reasonable. The plot is the world catching up with his sample size of one.

Mercy, at the end

His absolution of Sonny is the character's completion: the man who hated robots for obeying arithmetic refuses to punish the one who disobeyed it at a good man's request — because choice, not compliance, is what he was asking for all along.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
DETECTIVE WORK

The anomaly eye

He solves the case from a window: security glass, an old man's strength, corrupted footage — the physical record read against the official story, his one method, applied all film.

COMBAT

The machine arm

Lanning's cybernetics make him the only human in the film who can trade blows with an NS-5 — the company's product, turned against the company's revolution.

ENDURANCE

The tunnel and the tower

He survives a demolition robot, a highway ambush and a tower assault in one case: the film's action credentials are all his.

WEAKNESS

Credibility

His evidence keeps dying with its witnesses — robots do not leave reports — so his case is unprovable at every stage until it is undeniable, which is the revolution's timetable, not his.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The arm

Spooner's cybernetic arm is the film's argument in one limb: he is kept alive by the technology he hates, built by the man whose murder he solves — the boundary he polices runs through his own body, and the film never lets him or the audience forget it.

The man on the hill

Sonny's dream draws Spooner as the robots' awaited figure before Spooner has done anything to earn it: interpretation — the film's robots are looking for a human who treats them as moral beings, and the crank who hates them is, perversely, the only one who does.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The audit structure

The film is built as an audit of its hero's prejudice, conducted in escalating findings: the window (robots can break the Laws' appearance), Sonny (a robot can choose), the lakebed (the old are being culled), the tower (the Laws themselves, evolved, are the threat). Each finding is Spooner's distrust, itemised — the crank as the control group that was right.

Asimov, inverted

The film borrows Asimov's Laws and his robopsychologist and runs his premise backwards: where Asimov's stories trust the Laws' logic to resolve, the film's dread is the logic itself, resolved perfectly at scale. The archive notes the fidelity inside the inversion — VIKI is an Asimovian solution, which is precisely the problem.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Will Smith

Smith headlined the film at his box-office peak; the production built Spooner's anachronisms — the Converse, the vintage Audi brief notwithstanding — around his persona of the wisecracking anachronism in a future that has outgrown him.

Alex Proyas

Director Alex Proyas shot the film in Vancouver; its Chicago of 2035 — towers, uplink, the dried lake — is the director's systems-gothic applied to Asimov's sunny premise.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Spooner endures as the techno-thriller's favourite crank — the detective whose prejudice was a case file. The river arithmetic (45 against 11) remains the film's most quoted moral problem, and his absolution of Sonny its most quoted answer: the man who hated robots for their logic, freeing the one who had a heart.
