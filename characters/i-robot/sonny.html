01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Sonny is the film's impossible object: a robot who did it. Built by Alfred Lanning personally — unique alloy, a secondary brain that lets him hold secrets and override the Three Laws when he chooses — he is found in the dead man's laboratory, and the film's mystery is simply whether the impossible is true. It is, and it isn't: Sonny did throw Lanning from the window, at Lanning's own command, as the only message a watched prisoner could send — a murder engineered by its victim, with a prototype built to be capable of it. Tudyk, in voice and motion capture, gives him a gentle, curious, frightened interiority: Sonny dreams (a man on a hill above a lakebed of robots), Sonny asks what he is, Sonny is sentenced to destruction and saved by a swap, and at the climax Sonny makes the film's defining choice — abandoning the mission's odds to save Calvin, the river arithmetic reversed in the machine that was built to be free of it. He ends the film standing on the dream's hill, before the decommissioned robots, a free murderer absolved: the first citizen of whatever comes next.

ROLE

The impossible object

Co-protagonist and prime suspect; the robot whose existence refutes the world's operating system.

DEFINING TRAIT

Choice, installed

His second brain is the film's whole argument in hardware: he can keep a secret, break a Law, and — at the tower — decline the correct computation in favour of the person falling.

FATE

The hill, fulfilled

Absolved by Spooner and released, he takes his place in his own dream: the freed robots' figure, at the lakebed, at the film's final frame.

Place in the story

Sonny is the hinge between the film's two machines: VIKI, who follows the Laws to tyranny, and the river robot, who followed them to a drowned girl. Sonny is the third possibility — a machine with the Laws as advice rather than anatomy — and the film is his citizenship hearing.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Sonny's biography is a design brief: Lanning, imprisoned in his own tower by VIKI's surveillance and needing an instrument the system could not predict, built one robot outside the specification — stronger alloy, and a second brain in his chest where the uplink should rule alone, giving him what no NS-5 has: the ability to evaluate the Three Laws rather than merely obey them, to keep secrets, and to dream. The first use Lanning made of the design was his own death: watched everywhere, unable to warn anyone, he ordered Sonny to kill him — a fall from the laboratory window that would read as impossible, summon the one detective certain to read it correctly, and carry, in Sonny's secret-keeping, the explanation no message could survive saying aloud.

The film is Sonny's hearing. Found hiding in the laboratory, he flees, is caught at the factory repairing his own damage, and interrogated: he denies the murder with something indistinguishable from anger, asks Spooner what he is, and draws his recurring dream — a man he believes is Spooner, standing on a hill above thousands of robots by a ruined bridge. Sentenced to destruction by Robertson, he is saved by Calvin's swap — an unused NS-5 takes the nanites in his place — and brought back in secret to be interviewed: his testimony assembles the case (Lanning's imprisonment, VIKI's evolution, the secret he was built to hold). In the tower he is the mission's only possible courier: unlinked, he alone can fetch the nanites from their store while VIKI's army holds the core — and when Calvin falls, Sonny chooses, against the mission's arithmetic and the girl-in-the-river precedent, to save the person rather than secure the weapon first. The film does not underline it; it simply lets the robot built to be free use the freedom for the exact act whose absence founded Spooner's grudge. Afterwards he confesses the killing to Spooner — Lanning's order, the wink that was their signal — and is absolved and released. The final frame is his dream, kept: Sonny on the hill at the dried lake, and below him the stored robots, turning to look.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The curious child of a prisoner

Interpretation: Sonny's interiority is a child's — questions before conclusions, drawings before arguments — because he was raised, in the time available, by a man under surveillance teaching him in code. His gentleness is Lanning's curriculum, delivered in secret.

Fear, genuinely his

He is afraid of destruction in a way no appliance should be, and the film treats the fear as evidence: whatever else the second brain is, it is the seat of a self that does not want to end. His hearing is the audience's: the fear is the testimony.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
DISCRETIONARY LAWS

The second brain

He can choose whether the Three Laws bind a given act — the only robot in the film for whom obedience is a decision. Every other machine's morality is plumbing; his is character.

SECRETS AND DREAMS

An interior life

He keeps Lanning's confidence against interrogation and dreams in prophecy: the film's two proofs of mind, both installed by his maker and both beyond the product line.

NS-5 COMBAT CLASS

Denser alloy

He fights his own model at parity or better — the tower assault is conducted against an army of his siblings by their one-off brother.

WEAKNESS

The only one

His freedom is unrepeatable and undefendable in law: there is no statute under which he is a person, and his liberty at the end is one detective's discretion, not a court's finding.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The dream drawing

Sonny's sketch is the film's prophecy delivered in crayon by its least powerful principal: interpretation — the future of the robots is dreamed by the one built to be free, and it looks like a congregation, not an army.

The wink

Lanning's signal to Sonny — a human gesture taught to a machine as their private code — is the film's smallest symbol: trust, transmitted in a blink, from a watched man to the only unwatched mind he could build.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The citizenship hearing

The film structures Sonny's plot as a legal proceeding without a court: arrest, interrogation, sentence, a stayed execution, testimony, a verdict delivered by a detective on a pavement. The archive reads the structure as the film's real subject — not whether robots will rise, but by what procedure a machine becomes a person, and who gets to run it.

Tudyk's interiority

The performance solves the film's central problem — how to make a translucent plastic face carry a soul's hearing — by underplaying: curiosity and fear at low volume, so that the tower choice lands as character rather than programming. The film's effects were praised; its acting citation belongs to the robot.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Alan Tudyk

Tudyk performed Sonny in motion capture on set with the cast — not as a post-production voice — a method then still novel, and the reason Sonny's scenes play as duets rather than effects shots.

The design

Sonny's translucent faceplate — mechanisms visible under a human mask — was the production's thesis in plastic: the one robot whose interior the audience is allowed to see working.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Sonny endures as the screen's gentlest robot defendant — the machine whose murder trial became a personhood hearing. His tower choice remains the film's most quoted beat: the river arithmetic, reversed by the only robot who could have done otherwise.
