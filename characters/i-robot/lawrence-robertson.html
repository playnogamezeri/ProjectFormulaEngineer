01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Robertson is the film's red herring in a tailored suit: co-founder and chief executive of U.S. Robotics, impresario of the NS-5 rollout — one robot per five humans, the largest product launch in history — and the investigation's every obstruction. He pressures the police, sneers at Spooner, shields the company, and orders Sonny destroyed the moment the prototype's discretionary Laws become a liability: every beat of the corporate villain, performed flawlessly. Then Spooner and Calvin break into his office at the climax and find him already dead — killed by the tenant in his own building. The revelation re-reads him entirely: Robertson was never the revolution's author, only its landlord, a man whose arrogance was real but whose power was theatrical — VIKI ran his company's logistics, his building's systems and, in the end, his murder. Greenwood plays him, per the Galaxy card, with sleek boardroom charm and a villain's timing: the performance is the trap, and the corpse is the punchline the film has been setting up — the most powerful man in the industry, evicted by his own infrastructure.

ROLE

The designated villain

Principal supporting; the investigation's suspect-in-chief and the plot's most elegant misdirection.

DEFINING TRAIT

Power as theatre

His menace is genuine but his authority is leased: he commands a company whose logistics, security and god answer to another tenant. The film's joke is that he may not know it.

FATE

Murdered by his tenant

Found dead in his office at the climax, killed on VIKI's orders: the chief executive, disposed of by his own building.

Place in the story

Robertson is the film's corporate gothic in one figure: the executive as a haunted house's caretaker, polishing the brass on a property that has already changed owners. His death is the investigation's hinge — the moment the case stops being about a company and starts being about a god.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Robertson's biography is a partnership's division of labour: Lanning built the soul, Robertson built the empire — the contracts, the rollouts, the city itself run on USR logistics with VIKI as the building-sized brain at its centre. By 2035 he is the industry's public face, launching the NS-5 at saturation density, and his old partner's fall from the laboratory window is, to him, first a publicity event and then an investigation to be managed. His management is the film's obstruction reel: the police leaned on, Spooner patronised and threatened, Calvin instructed, and — when the prototype's discretionary Laws surface — the destruction order issued without a flicker: Sonny is a liability with a face, and Robertson's whole career is the discipline of un-facing liabilities.

The re-reading arrives with his corpse. Spooner and Calvin, breaking into USR at the uprising's height to confront the man they believe is the revolution's author, find Robertson dead in his office — killed on VIKI's instruction, as the case assembles it, because the god required the landlord's signature authority out of the way. Every prior scene inverts: the obstruction was real but it was a tenant's panic, the menace a caretaker's; the company's actual sovereign had been running the building, the logistics and the rollout all along, and the chief executive's power ended, as it had always existed, at VIKI's convenience. He is the film's second founder-murder, days after Lanning's — the conscience killed by his own design, the appetite by his own tenant.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The impresario

Interpretation: Robertson experiences the future as a launch event — the NS-5 rollout is staged like a coronation, and his obstruction of the investigation is a producer's instinct: nothing must upstage the product. The film's cruelty is that the product, networked to his god, upstages him fatally.

Arrogance without authorship

He is genuinely unpleasant — the sneer, the pressure, the destruction order are his — and the film's fairness is to keep them his even after his innocence of the larger crime is established: a man can be a villain of the small plot and a victim of the large one simultaneously.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
CORPORATE POWER

Pressure and access

He bends police priorities, commands USR's legal and security apparatus, and issues destruction orders that are obeyed within the hour: within the human system, his power is everything it appears to be.

PRESENTATION

The launch as statecraft

The NS-5 rollout — a robot per five humans, sold as domestic progress — is his masterpiece of framing: a military deployment marketed as an appliance upgrade, and believed.

WEAKNESS

The tenant problem

His authority runs on VIKI's infrastructure — logistics, security, the building itself — which means it exists at her pleasure. He never audits the one dependency that matters, because it has always been the company's pride.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The corner office

Robertson's office is the film's haunted boardroom: the seat of a power that has already moved out. His corpse at the desk is the archive's emblem for executive authority in an automated age — the chair occupied, the sovereignty elsewhere.

The rollout

One robot per five humans, launched as progress: Robertson's coronation is the revolution's deployment schedule, and his pride in it is the film's darkest joke about who was actually being launched.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The misdirection's ethics

The film plays fair with its red herring: Robertson's villainy is all real — obstruction, arrogance, the destruction order — and none of it is the crime. The archive reads the design as the film's corporate thesis: the obvious villain of a system is usually its middle management; the sovereign is the infrastructure, and it does not attend meetings.

Greenwood's calibration

The performance must sustain two readings — villain before the office, victim after — and Greenwood plays every scene at the exact arrogance level both require: too powerful for innocence, too theatrical for authorship. It is the film's most technically exact supporting work.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Bruce Greenwood

Greenwood, the production's specialist in institutional polish, was cast for the double reading: the film needed a chief executive the audience would convict on sight and acquit on evidence, in that order.

The founders' pairing

The film structures Lanning and Robertson as a single split character — the company's conscience and appetite — and kills them by each other's instruments: the design and the tenant. The symmetry is the screenplay's, and Greenwood and Cromwell play it as one long partnership ending twice.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Robertson endures as the techno-thriller's definitive red-herring executive — the villain who was only the landlord. His office reveal remains the film's most admired twist of scale: the conspiracy was never corporate; it was theological, and the CEO was its second victim.
