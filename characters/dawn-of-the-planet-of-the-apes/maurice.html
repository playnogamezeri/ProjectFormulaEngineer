01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Maurice is the ape nation's memory with a teaching post. In Rise he was the circus orangutan who signed 'hurt' to a frightened young Caesar and became his first friend; ten years on, he is the colony's schoolmaster, teaching ape children their letters under the painted law, and the king's oldest counsel — the one ape whose advice arrives as recollection. Konoval, who plays him across the saga, gives Maurice the film's slowest, kindest physicality: an elder whose authority is entirely moral. When the coup cages him with the loyalists, the film measures Koba's reign by that single image: a government that must imprison its schoolmaster has announced itself.

Memory as office

FATE

The conscience, uncaged

Imprisoned by Koba, freed by Blue Eyes, he ends the film restored with the nation as the war begins.

Place in the story

Maurice is Dawn's continuity of soul: the character through whom the saga remembers being small, caged and kind. His page is brief in incident and large in function — the record of the figure both later films and Kingdom's mythology treat as the law's living footnote.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Maurice's Dawn is a life's work visible in one set: the school in the trees, where the nation's children learn beneath the painted law — Ape Not Kill Ape — in lessons he leads by sign. He is Caesar's confidant in the film's quiet conferences: it is Maurice the king talks with about the vanished humans, Maurice whose counsel frames the council's choices, the elder whose memory of the sanctuary years is the nation's constitution before it was written down. When Alexander, Malcolm's son, opens his sketchbook in the village, it is Maurice's face the boy draws — the second diplomacy finding its subject in the nation's kindest citizen, the orangutan sitting for his portrait like a headmaster humouring a promising pupil.

The coup prices him exactly: Koba's first government act, after the assault, is to cage the loyalists, and Maurice is caged with Rocket and Luca — the schoolmaster in a cell, the nation's memory under lock, in the film's most economical image of what the usurpation is. He is freed by Blue Eyes' hand, the heir opening the conscience's cage as his first act of state.

He ends the film restored among the nation as it gathers for war — the schoolmaster whose classroom the war will empty, carrying the law in memory now that its wall is burned. The saga will keep him at Caesar's side to the end of the king's story; Dawn is where his office is defined: to remember, accurately and kindly, what the nation was founded to never repeat.

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The elder's patience

Interpretation: Maurice's counsel is never urgent, which is its authority: he is the one figure in the film whose timescale is the nation's whole life, and his calm is the long view, embodied.

Kindness as policy

Interpretation: the schoolmaster's gentleness is not temperament but curriculum — he is teaching the first free generation what the nation is for, and the lesson plan is himself.

The witness

He sat for Alexander's portrait: the archive reads the sitting as his diplomacy — an elder offering his face to the other nation's child, trusting the drawing to travel where arguments cannot.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The classroom under the law

Interpretation: Maurice teaches children beneath the words Ape Not Kill Ape — the nation's whole constitution staged as a schoolroom, law and literacy in one image. The coup must cage the teacher because the classroom is the one institution that out-argues it.

The portrait

Interpretation: Alexander's drawing of Maurice is the peace's most durable artefact — outlasting the dam, the treaty and the tower — because it is the only instrument in the film made by looking carefully at the other side's face.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the barometer

Dawn uses Maurice the way classical tragedy uses a chorus elder: regimes are priced by their treatment of him. The analysis the archive records is that his small incident count is the point — a character whose function is to be the nation's memory cannot also be its swordsman, and the film protects the purity of the office by keeping his hands clean of everything but chalk, counsel and a cage door opened from the other side.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Maurice becomes the saga's permanent elder — the figure later films place at Caesar's side as memory and mercy, and whose tradition Kingdom's Raka, centuries on, unknowingly continues as the last keeper of the law's meaning. His Dawn page is where that office is founded.

Within the Galaxy, his file is the archive's purest study of the elder as institution: a character whose entire plot is other people's education, and whose imprisonment is a regime's confession.
