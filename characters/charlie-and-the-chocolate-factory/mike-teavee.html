01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Mike Teavee is the tour's critic: a boy raised by screens into a connoisseur's contempt for everything not on one. He found his ticket the modern way — by analysing the contest's distribution mathematically and buying a single bar — and announced on camera that he hates chocolate, which makes him the only contestant who scorns the prize he won. On the tour he is a one-boy review section, filing every wonder under stupid, until the Television Room offers him the one exhibit in his own medium: Wonka Vision, which teleports chocolate by television so the viewer can pull it from the screen. Mike, who understands television better than anyone in the building including its inventor, demonstrates it on himself — and is received, action-figure-sized, in a viewer's hand. The factory stretches him in the taffy puller as its remedy; he leaves enormously tall and thin: the critic, reformatted by his medium.

ROLE

The fourth elimination

The tour's exhibit of contempt; the screen's native, processed by it.

DEFINING TRAIT

Scorn as a formation

Mike's contempt is not discernment; it is a reflex installed by a childhood of screens, which has taught him that everything is content and most content is beneath him.

FATE

Stretched

He leaves the factory extremely tall and thin from the taffy puller: the record shows the scorn surviving the reformatting.

Place in the story

Mike is the exam's exhibit on the mediated appetite — the child who consumes the world at one remove and is consumed by the remove itself. His page is the most contemporary of the eliminations and the film's most prophetic.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Mike is introduced inside his natural habitat: a Denver living room arranged around screens, where he explains his ticket to the cameras — he does not eat chocolate (he hates it); he modelled the contest's shipping pattern, identified the likely bar, and bought one. The Golden Ticket, for Mike, is not luck or appetite but a solved system, and his prize is the tour he proceeds to despise professionally. Room by room he files the factory's wonders as stupid, its host as a fraud, and its other children as exhibits — the critic's tour, conducted at a slouch.

The Television Room is where the factory finally speaks his language. Wonka Vision transmits a chocolate bar by television signal, enlarged for the camera and received small, to be pulled from the screen and eaten — the tour's one miracle in Mike's native medium. His critique is instant and, on its own terms, correct: if the apparatus can send chocolate, it can send anything; the inventor has been thinking too small. So Mike demonstrates, vaulting onto the platform and transmitting himself before anyone can stop the broadcast. He arrives in the receiving set at action-figure scale, is plucked from the screen by an Oompa-Loompa, and delivers his review of teleportation from inside a hand. The factory's remedy is the taffy puller: stretched, over-corrected, and sent home enormously tall and thin — the boy who lived at one remove from everything, reformatted by the remove itself, his scorn the only dimension the process could not change.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Contempt as a screen effect

Interpretation: Mike's scorn is the posture of a permanent audience — everything is watched, rated and dismissed, nothing is entered. The archive reads his elimination as the posture's logical end: the boy who insists on experiencing the world through a screen is finally, literally, put inside one and handed around.

Intelligence, ungoverned

He is genuinely the cleverest of the four — the ticket-by-algorithm is the contest's only intellectual solution — and the film is precise that cleverness is no governor at all: his is the only elimination committed as a product demonstration.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The boy in the screen

Interpretation: Mike's miniature is the archive's emblem of mediated childhood — a person reduced to the size of his representation, carried in a hand, reviewed by his own parents at arm's length. The taffy-stretch that follows is the film's bleak joke about correction: the factory can resize the boy but not the posture.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the contemporary elimination

Dahl wrote Mike for the television age and the film sharpens him for the algorithmic one — the ticket found by data, the world received by screen. The analysis the archive records is that his chapter is the exam's most prophetic: the appetite it processes is not for food, goods or glory but for distance itself, and it is the only appetite the factory answers by granting it completely.
