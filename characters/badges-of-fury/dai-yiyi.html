01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Dai Yiyi enters the Smile Murders as the obvious suspect the moment her sister is cleared — and the reviews consulted for this archive name the mechanism honestly: she is bandied as the obvious suspect until revealed, predictably, as the red herring. The construction is deliberate. Yiyi, played by Liu Yan (credited in the cast listing as Ada Liu), is Jinshui's elder half-sister, a former darts champion who practices voodoo and has made a career of taking her younger sister's boyfriends — including, by the YesAsia account, the very men now dying. A sister with a rivalry, a weapon-adjacent sport and a curse practice is suspicion's perfect meal; the film serves her to the investigation precisely so the investigation can learn, a second time, that the profile is not the proof.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Yiyi's biography, as the sources give it, is a profile assembled by suspicion. She is Liu Jinshui's elder half-sister. She was a darts champion. She practices voodoo. And she has a documented habit that places her inside the case: she steals her sister's boyfriends — the men who dated Jinshui also dated Yiyi, per the YesAsia account, which makes her the pattern's second constant, standing one chair over from the first.

When the Smile Murders make Jinshui the prime suspect, Yiyi is the shadow theory; when Wang Bu'er's investigation clears the actress, the shadow becomes the focus: Wang shifts his focus onto her sister Dai Yiyi, among others, in the synopses' own sequence. The reviewers consulted are blunt about what she is for: the jealous elder sister is bandied as the obvious suspect until she is predictably revealed as the red herring — with a secondary suspicion, in one account, attaching to the life-insurance policies connected to her acquaintances. The documented trail then moves to its terminus, the bait plan, in which Wang poses as Jinshui's boyfriend to lure the actual murderer out — a plan that exists because neither sister was the answer.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The rival sister

Yiyi's documented conduct is appropriation: the boyfriends she takes are her sister's, which the film frames as a career of rivalry. Interpretation: she is written as appetite with a target — the sister as the one audience whose possessions are worth taking — and the investigation simply prices that appetite as motive.

The occult as self-fashioning

The voodoo practice, like the darts championship, is a fact the film leaves undeveloped and suspicion develops eagerly. On the page's honest reading, both are self-fashioning — a persona of dangerous glamour — that the case converts into evidence without ever testing.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

Suspicion's gravity

Yiyi symbolises how investigations actually fall: toward the person whose biography rhymes with the crime. The film builds her as a rhyme — darts, curses, rivalry — and then declines to make the rhyme a reason.

The sister as mirror

She is Jinshui's inverted reflection: the sister who takes instead of loses, performs instead of endures — the pattern's second constant, priced by the same logic that mispriced the first.

The red herring, documented as such

Unusually for this archive, the sources do the analysis themselves: reviewers name Yiyi the red herring in print, predictably revealed. The file's interest is structural — the film spends its second suspect to teach its detectives and its audience the same lesson twice, that profile is not proof, before sending its detective into the pattern as bait because neither profile produced the killer.

One review adds a suspicion the synopses omit: the frequency of life-insurance policies attached to her acquaintances. The archive records it as a review-level detail of the suspicion around her — part of her documented profile as a suspect, not a finding about her conduct.
