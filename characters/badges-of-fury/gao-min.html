Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Gao Min is a single-scene character with a structural job no lead could do: he is the pattern's proof of life. Played by Raymond Lam in a cameo, he is Liu Jinshui's latest boyfriend — intense, per one review's account — and the cast listing preserves the beat that defines him: he is one of the victims of the Smiling Murder, who died during his second proposal to Liu Jinshui. The earlier victims could be pattern or coincidence, history or curse; Gao Min dies in the investigation's present tense, mid-proposal, in front of the case — converting the actress's file from a suspicious past into an active, watching threat, and setting up the bait plan that answers him: if standing in the boyfriend's place is fatal, a detective will stand there on purpose.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Gao Min's documented biography is brief and complete. He is Liu Jinshui's current boyfriend — the intense new man between the two sisters, in one review's phrase, which means a smiling death is likely in his future. He proposes marriage to Jinshui; the cast listing specifies that the fatal proposal was his second. During it, he dies — with the signature smile of the Smiling Murder fixed on his face.

The death's function is documented on the Galaxy's own film page: his death, mid-proposal, converts suspicion into certainty that the killer is real, active and watching the actress's life. The earlier victims belonged to Jinshui's past and could sustain rival theories — curse, coincidence, the sister. Gao Min belongs to her present, and his corpse retires the theories: someone is monitoring the actress's romantic life in real time and killing the men who enter it, on the occasion of their deepest commitment to her. The investigation's answer follows from his scene directly: Wang Bu'er will pose as her boyfriend — the bait plan is Gao Min's death, answered with a volunteer.

The case's epistemology turns on Gao Min. Victims one through four establish a pattern in the past tense, compatible with curse and coincidence; Gao Min establishes an agent in the present tense — watching, timing, choosing the proposal as the occasion. The analysis the archive files is that his scene is the investigation's true founding: before it, a mystery about a woman's history; after it, a manhunt with a method, answered by the bait.
