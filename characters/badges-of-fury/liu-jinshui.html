01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Liu Jinshui is the Smile Murders' fixed point: a budding actress, played by Liu Shishi, every one of whose former boyfriends begins dying with a grin — a television star during filming, a dancer mid-dance, a diver, then a fourth man found in concrete. The investigation's logic makes her first its prime suspect: she is the pattern's only constant. Wang Bu'er's work then clears her — she is found to be innocent — and reclassifies her as what she always was: the pattern's centre and its intended audience, a woman being sent messages in corpses. Around her the film arranges its supporting machinery: a sister who took her boyfriends and is suspected in her place, a cousin who stands by the family, a fortune teller who pronounces her cursed, and a detective who finally stands in the dead men's place as bait.

Innocence under interpretation

Place in the story

Jinshui is the film's still centre: the detectives orbit, the sister schemes, the victims accumulate, and all of it is addressed to her. Her page records the investigation's most human file — the suspect who was a addressee — and the archive is careful with her exactly where the film's other characters are not: the sources establish her innocence and her peril, and nothing beyond them is claimed here.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Jinshui enters the film as a rising actress whose past becomes evidence. The Smile Murders take, in three days, three men she once dated — the television star Li Tianci, dead during filming; the ballroom dancer Yao Yiwei, dead mid-dance; the diver Zheng Jun — each corpse grinning, the media naming the pattern. The overlap is her, and the police logic is immediate: the actress is the constant, so the actress is the suspect. A fourth victim, found embedded in a block of concrete with the signature smile, makes her the prime suspect formally.

Wang Bu'er's investigation then does the thing the pattern did not expect: it clears her. She is found to be innocent — the synopses state it plainly — and the case re-reads her position: not the murders' author but their address. The corpses are messages sent to a woman through the men she loved. Around the cleared centre the machinery rearranges: suspicion shifts to her elder half-sister Dai Yiyi, who had taken Jinshui's boyfriends for herself and whose profile fits every suspicion the actress's did not; her cousin Liu Jun escorts her to a fortune teller, who examines her and pronounces her cursed — the supernatural reading she half-believes, in a household besieged by press and police.

Her latest boyfriend, Gao Min, proposes marriage — for the second time, per the cast listing — and dies smiling in the act, the pattern confirming itself at her feet. The investigation's documented answer is the bait plan: Wang Bu'er will pose as her new boyfriend, standing publicly in the dead men's place to lure the killer out. The record consulted for this archive ends there — Jinshui's life as the trap's ground, the danger rising — and this file ends with it.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Endurance as the role

Jinshui's documented actions are few and her situation is total: suspected by police, besieged by press, pronounced cursed, and bereaved on a schedule. Interpretation: the film casts her as the still point not because she lacks agency but because the pattern has confiscated it — every move she makes is read as evidence, so the performance becomes one of bearing readings.

The half-belief

The fortune teller's verdict — cursed — is the one explanation offered to her that fits her experience, and the Galaxy's film page records her as half-convinced. It is the file's most human detail: the innocent centre, offered superstition because the investigation can offer her only procedure.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The addressee

Jinshui symbolises the person a pattern is for: the murders are correspondence, and she is the mailbox. The film's horror-comedy premise is that a woman's romantic history can be converted, without her participation, into someone else's text.

The curse as folk epistemology

The fortune teller's verdict symbolises what rushes in when evidence stalls: the black market of explanation, selling the frightened a story that at least fits her fear.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Suspect to centre: the reclassification

Jinshui's arc is the investigation learning to read. The police begin with the constant and call it a culprit; the evidence promotes her to addressee; the bait plan finally treats her as the ground on which the killer can be met. The analysis the archive files is that her innocence is not a twist but a correction — the film's one genuinely procedural movement, performed by its least procedural detective.

The gender of the pattern

The sources' own summaries make the structure plain: a woman's ex-boyfriends die, and the woman is suspected, besieged and pronounced cursed before she is protected. The comedy is period Hong Kong register; the file underneath it — whose life gets read as evidence — is the film's most durable document.
