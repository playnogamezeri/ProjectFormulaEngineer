01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Huang Feihong is top billing in a supporting role, and the film knows it: Jet Li plays the veteran half of a buddy-cop pairing whose actual lead is Wen Zhang's Wang Bu'er. Huang's function is the portrait of mastery in its comic register — a decorated inspector near retirement, more visibly concerned with his stock portfolio than with procedure, who lets his young partner charge, blunder and theorise, and then, without fail and without hurry, arrives at the crucial moment with the kung fu the performance of incompetence was concealing. Around the Smile Murders — three, then four of actress Liu Jinshui's former boyfriends dead and grinning — Huang is the case's insurance policy: the partner whose apparent muddle is a leash held loosely on purpose.

ROLE

Veteran inspector; the hidden master

Supporting lead; Wang Bu'er's partner and rescuer, the unit's real martial artist beneath a performed fog.

DEFINING TRAIT

Mastery as camouflage

Huang's incompetence is a costume worn over competence — the film's joke is that the costume is optional and the competence is not.

FATE

On the case, at the bait

The documented record leaves him where the investigation turns dangerous: Wang posing as the actress's boyfriend to lure the killer out, Huang at his side.

Place in the story

Huang is the film's contract with its audience: Jet Li's name sells the ticket, and Huang is how the film pays it off without surrendering the lead to him — a legend in the passenger seat, intervening by the fight. His page is also the Galaxy's neatest naming record: Li, who played the historical Wong Fei-hung across the Once Upon a Time in China series, here plays a modern inspector whose name is a one-character homonym spoof of the hero — 黃非紅 for 黃飛鴻 — a joke the archive files exactly as the sources document it.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Huang is introduced as the department's decorated veteran and its problem: paired with the young Wang Bu'er under their superior Angela, the two are chaos agents whose ordinary work — the opening stakeout of the drug trafficker Chen Hu, vanished seven years — bookends the department's low opinion of them. Huang's own priorities appear divided between the job and his stock portfolio, and his manner is as muddle-headed as his partner's. The film's premise, stated in its synopses, is that the muddle is a surface: in reality Huang is the real master of kung fu, and at the most crucial moments he helps Wang out of sticky situations, without fail.

The case that defines the film arrives as the Smile Murders: in three days, three men die with grins fixed on their faces — a movie star during filming, a ballroom dancer mid-dance, a diver — and the media names the pattern. Wang declares it serial murder; the overlap among the victims is actress Liu Jinshui, who dated them all. A fourth victim, found embedded in concrete and still smiling, makes Liu the prime suspect. Huang works the case in his register — present, unhurried, and decisive in the fight scenes that interrupt the investigation, including set pieces against the likes of Chen Hu's world of traffickers and strongmen that the reviews note sit beside the main plot as showcases.

Suspicion moves from Liu Jinshui — found innocent — to her elder half-sister Dai Yiyi, among others. The investigation's documented terminus, in the sources consulted for this archive, is the bait plan: Wang decides to pose as Liu Jinshui's boyfriend to lure the murderer out, and the closer he gets to the truth, the greater the danger he is in. Huang's established function defines his place in that endgame — the master at the clown's shoulder, arriving at the crucial moment — but this file records the plan and the peril, not an outcome the sources do not document.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The performed fog

Huang's muddle-headedness is best read as a veteran's economy: he has nothing left to prove, a retirement to protect and a portfolio to watch, so he lets the young man spend the energy. Interpretation: the performance is not humility but triage — he intervenes only where intervention is decisive.

Loyalty at the crucial moment

The synopses' formula — without fail, at the most crucial moments — is a character definition: Huang's partnership is not supervision but rescue. He does not correct Wang's method; he insures its consequences.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The name as epitaph and joke

Huang Feihong symbolises the afterlife of a legend: the folk hero's name, respelled by one character into a near-homonym, now belongs to a tired inspector watching stocks. The film is Li auditing his own most famous role at comic distance.

Mastery in retirement

He represents skill without ambition — the master who has stopped auditioning, in a film full of people (actors, dancers, detectives) still desperate to be seen.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Top billing, supporting function

The film's most documented structural fact is its inversion: Li is the poster, Wen Zhang is the protagonist. Huang is engineered accordingly — a character who must be able to dominate any scene he enters and absent himself from the plot's labour without the film collapsing. The performed-fog device is the engineering: it licenses the star to be both the best fighter in the room and the least busy detective in it.

A favour, documented

YesAsia's review records the production's open secret: Li took the role in regard for producer Chui Po-chu (Chui Bo-Chu), his longtime collaborator — the film is directed by her son, Wong Tsz-ming, in his directorial debut, with Corey Yuen as action director. Huang's looseness is the performance of an actor doing a kindness at full professionalism: the fights are real even when the plot is a lark.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Third collaboration

Badges of Fury is Li and Wen Zhang's third film together, after Ocean Heaven and The Sorcerer and the White Snake — both earlier films stand in this Galaxy, where the pairing ran dramatic and fantastical; here it is rebuilt as a buddy-cop double act with the senior partner deliberately downshifted.

The favour and the debut

The film is Wong Tsz-ming's directorial debut; producer Chui Po-chu is his mother and Li's longtime collaborator, and Li's participation is recorded by reviewers as a favour to her. Corey Yuen served as action director, and the film was released on 21 June 2013.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Huang Feihong is the Galaxy's comic coda to Jet Li's defining role: the actor who carried Wong Fei-hung through the Once Upon a Time in China series closes the name's account with a homonym, a badge and a stock portfolio. Within the 2013 run of this archive he stands as the veteran pole — mastery retired into camouflage, in the year's most cameo-stuffed Hong Kong comedy.
