01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Hiccup Horrendous Haddock III is the protagonist of How to Train Your Dragon: The Hidden World (2019), voiced by Jay Baruchel. A year after becoming Chief of Berk, he has built his father’s village into a dragon utopia — and overfilled it. Facing the hunter Grimmel, who has all but exterminated the Night Furies, Hiccup leads his people in search of the Hidden World, the dragons’ ancestral refuge, and must finally decide whether loving dragons means keeping them or freeing them.

ROLE

Chief of Berk; dragon rider

Hiccup leads the rescue raids, the migration and the farewell — every stage of Berk’s dragon century ends in his hands.

DEFINING TRAIT

Invention in service of mercy

His genius is mechanical — prosthetic tails, flight suits, an automatic tailfin — but its purpose is always relational: each device is built to give a dragon, or a friend, a freedom they did not have.

ARC IN ONE LINE

From holding on to letting go

He begins trying to keep every dragon safe beside him and ends by releasing them all, keeping only the secret and the statues.

Place in the story

Hiccup is the trilogy’s moral instrument, and this film is his graduation. The first film taught him dragons were not monsters; the second taught him leadership costs; this one teaches him stewardship’s limit — that the Hidden World is not his to enter, and Berk’s safety cannot be built on dragons remaining targets. His arc is completed by a device: the automatic tailfin that lets Toothless fly without him, the kindest and cruelest thing he ever builds.

Hiccup’s file across the trilogy is a single argument about what strength is for. The scrawny inventor who shot down a Night Fury and then could not kill it became chief by the opposite method of his father’s: where Stoick ruled by force, Hiccup rules by understanding — and this film tests whether understanding includes understanding when to stop.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

A year after becoming chieftain, Hiccup and the riders run rescue raids freeing captured dragons, and Berk has swollen into an overcrowded dragon utopia that draws constant hunter attacks. Hiccup dreams of the Hidden World his father once sought — a refuge at the world’s edge where dragons could be safe forever.

The warlords whose dragons Berk keeps freeing hire Grimmel the Grisly, the hunter who boasts of killing nearly every Night Fury, and give him a captive Light Fury as bait for Toothless. When Grimmel visits Hiccup at night demanding Toothless, Hiccup’s prepared ambush fails; Grimmel’s Deathgrippers burn his house and part of Berk. Hiccup rallies the tribe to abandon Berk and migrate in search of the Hidden World.

On the new island — New Berk — Hiccup builds Toothless an automatic tailfin so his friend can court the Light Fury unaccompanied. Toothless flies off with her; searching for him, Hiccup and Astrid find the Hidden World itself and see Toothless crowned alpha beside his mate — and see the resident dragons attack the human intruders. Hiccup understands: his people would never be safe there, and dragons beside humans will always be hunted.

Grimmel, having followed the released Ruffnut back, captures Toothless and the Light Fury and uses Toothless’s alpha authority to take Berk’s whole flock. Hiccup leads the riders in wingsuits against Grimmel’s armada. In the free-fall climax, Hiccup frees the drugged Light Fury to save the falling Toothless, kicks free of his own prosthetic leg to drop the clinging Grimmel into the sea, and is caught by the Light Fury herself — saved by a dragon who chose to come back.

On the cliff at New Berk, Hiccup accepts the arithmetic and Berk releases its dragons, rider by rider. His goodbye to Toothless mirrors their first meeting: the offered hand, the closed eyes. Months later he marries Astrid. A decade on, he sails his wife and children to the edge of the world, where an older Toothless — now a father — recognizes the offered hand, and the two families share one flight before the dragons return to hiding.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The engineer of empathy

Hiccup solves emotional problems with devices and strategic problems with empathy — the automatic tailfin is both at once: a machine whose entire function is to make himself unnecessary.

Chiefly doubt

Unlike Stoick, Hiccup leads by questioning himself in public. His migration decision is a gamble he argues for rather than decrees, and the film lets it nearly fail before it succeeds.

Love as release

His defining maturation is learning that protection and possession are opposites. Every earlier film rewarded him for holding on — to Toothless, to Berk, to his father’s approval. This one rewards the open hand.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
INVENTION

Master inventor and smith-trained engineer

Designs the automatic tailfin, flight suits and dragon armor; his workshop output drives the trilogy’s technology.

RIDING

The first dragon rider

His bond with Toothless remains the template of rider-dragon coordination; he also flies gliding wingsuits in the armada assault.

LEADERSHIP

Chief of Berk

Moves an entire tribe across the sea on an argument, and later persuades it to surrender the dragons at the center of its identity.

WEAKNESS

The refusal to let go

For most of the film his utopia is a hoard: every rescued dragon kept, every risk answered with more Berk. Grimmel’s whole strategy prices that attachment.

07 / CHARACTER ARC

Character Arc

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
The utopia keeper

Hiccup opens as rescuer-in-chief of an overfull Berk, answering every threat by bringing more dragons home.

The migration gamble

Burned out of his house by Grimmel, he stakes the tribe on his father’s legend of the Hidden World.

The tailfin

He builds Toothless the means to leave him — and watches his friend choose the wild and a mate.

The intruder’s lesson

In the Hidden World he is attacked as an intruder and understands the separation must be total.

The farewell and the secret

He releases the dragons, marries Astrid, and becomes keeper of the secret until the world is ready — the trilogy’s last chiefly duty.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The open hand

Hiccup’s gesture — palm out, eyes closed, first offered to a dragon who could have killed him — bookends the trilogy. In the farewell and the reunion it is the same sentence: I trust you; you are free.

The prosthetic exchange

He lost a leg to the bond; Toothless lost a tailfin to it. The automatic tailfin settles the account: each has now given the other back a wholeness that does not require the giver.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Narrative function: the steward who resigns

Hiccup’s arc completes the trilogy’s thesis that dominion is the failure mode. The chief who began by shooting a dragon down ends by standing between dragons and the entire human world — as their secret-keeper, not their owner.

Investigation: the Grimmel duel as mirror

The climax is staged as an argument between two men hanging from dragons in free fall: the hunter who clings to Hiccup’s prosthetic leg — literally holding onto the hero’s wound — and the hero who lets go of the leg, the dragon and the era in a single sequence of releases. The archive reads the staging as deliberate: Grimmel dies of grasping; Hiccup lives by release.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Voice

Jay Baruchel completes the trilogy as Hiccup, having voiced him from a teenage outcast to a married chief; the character’s aging across three films is carried substantially in the performance’s deepening register.

Production note

The film is written and directed by Dean DeBlois and closes the trilogy he shaped from Cressida Cowell’s book series; the farewell structure — dragons departing into legend — follows the books’ framing elegy, in which Hiccup narrates from a world where dragons have vanished.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Hiccup stands as modern animation’s defining portrait of a hero whose power is relinquishment. The Hidden World completes him as the chief who ended the dragon age to save the dragons — a protagonist whose climax is a goodbye, and whose victory condition is being forgotten by history alongside them.
