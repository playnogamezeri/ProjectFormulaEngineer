01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Hattori Hanzō makes the finest swords in the world and has not made one for 28 years. Retired to a small Okinawa sushi bar, bound by a vow — sworn, the film implies, against the uses his blades were put to — he keeps his masterpieces in an attic like a retired army. The Bride comes to him as a supplicant: she needs a sword, and she is honest about the errand — revenge against his own former pupil, Bill. Hanzō's response is the volume's consecration scene: the vow breaks, the forge relights, and a month later he presents her with the finest sword he has ever made, with a warning that is also a blessing — if Bill encounters her, he will need all his skills; and if, on her journey, she should encounter God, God will be cut. He is the campaign's armourer and its moral witness: the master who judges his own pupil deserving of the blade.

Between the kitchen and the Blue Leaves, his attic and forge give the campaign its legitimacy: the old world of the sword formally endorses the Bride's errand against its own pupil.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Hanzō's biography in this volume is a vow and its breaking. The world's greatest living swordsmith, he forged the blades of an era — and Bill, the Bride's target, was his pupil. Twenty-eight years before the film, he swore never to forge another sword, and retired to run a small sushi bar in Okinawa, his masterpieces racked in the attic above it. The Bride arrives posing first as a tourist, then as a supplicant: she asks to hold his work, speaks the errand plainly, and names the target. The name changes the room. Hanzō takes her to the attic, lets her handle the retired blades, and delivers his verdict on his own pupil by accepting the commission: one month.

The month at the forge is the film's craft montage, and the presentation is a ceremony: the sword delivered with ritual gravity, its quality stated without ego — his finest work — and its purpose blessed in the volume's most quoted benediction: if she encounters God on her journey, God will be cut. The blade goes to Tokyo and proves the claim at the House of Blue Leaves, where even O-Ren Ishii, dying, verifies its authenticity. Hanzō remains in Okinawa; his war is fought by proxy, in steel.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The held verdict

Interpretation: Hanzō's 28-year vow reads, in retrospect, less like pacifism than like a sentence suspended — he stopped making swords because of what his best pupil became, and he resumes for exactly one purpose: the correction of that pupil. His retirement was a judgement waiting for its instrument.

Craft as morality

He speaks of swords the way priests speak of sacraments — the making is the meaning. Interpretation: in a film of quotations, Hanzō is the one character for whom the genre's central object is not a prop but a responsibility, and his scenes slow the film to the tempo of that belief.

The comic surface

His sushi-bar persona — henpecked theatre with his assistant — is a retired god playing house. The archive records the design: the volume's deepest authority is introduced as its broadest comedy, so that the attic's gravity arrives unannounced.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The attic of swords

Interpretation: a rack of masterpieces above a sushi bar is the film's image of a retired era — the age of the sword, stored over the age of commerce. The Bride's errand briefly reopens the attic; the volume's Tokyo night is what the old world does when it is consulted once more.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: legitimacy, conferred

The analysis the archive records: revenge films must decide whether their avenger is righteous or merely relentless, and this one outsources the verdict to the genre's highest authority. Hanzō's commission — a month, a masterpiece, a blessing — is the old world's ruling that the Bride's cause is just and Bill's life is forfeit. Every later set piece is fought with that ruling in her hand, literally.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Hattori Hanzō stands in the Galaxy record as the screen swordsmith archetype perfected — the retired master, the broken vow, the masterpiece — and Chiba's benediction entered the language.
