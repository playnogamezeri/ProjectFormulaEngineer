01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Bill does not show his face in Volume 1, and he is everywhere in it. He is the Deadly Vipers' leader, codename Snake Charmer; the Bride's mentor and lover; the father of her unborn child; and the man who, having ordered his squad to massacre her wedding rehearsal, delivers the head shot himself — after she tells him the baby is his. His volume is conducted by telephone and implication: he aborts Elle Driver's hospital assassination at the last moment because killing the comatose Bride would be dishonourable — the code of the man who shot her at her wedding — and he receives the campaign's progress through a mutilated courier. The film's last exchange is his: interrogating Sofie Fatale, he asks whether the Bride knows her daughter is alive. With one question the volume's whole engine is reclassified: the child the Bride believes dead, whose loss fuels the list, has been alive in Bill's keeping all along.

ROLE

The antagonist in absentia

The list's final name and its author; the volume's unseen centre.

DEFINING TRAIT

The code of the dishonourable honourable man

He massacres a wedding and aborts a mercy-killing on grounds of honour within the same story: his ethics are real, personal, and entirely his own invention.

FATE

Awaiting name five

Untouched in this volume; holding the saga's secret — the daughter — as his last card.

Place in the story

Bill's page is the archive's record of a villain constructed from restraint: a voice, hands, a handkerchief wiped across a blade, and a telephone's etiquette. The film saves his face for Volume 2 and spends this volume making his absence the largest presence in every room.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Bill's history in this volume is delivered in pieces. He built and led the Deadly Viper Assassination Squad; he trained the Bride (and, before her, was himself the pupil of Hattori Hanzō and others); he was her lover. When she left the life — pregnant, engaged to an ordinary man — Bill's answer was the chapel: the squad's massacre of the wedding rehearsal in El Paso in 1999, nine dead, and Bill himself administering the final shot to the Bride's head after she tells him the child is his.

His second intervention is the volume's strangest mercy. With the Bride comatose, Elle Driver infiltrates the hospital to euthanise her by injection; Bill telephones at the last moment and aborts the mission — killing her while she is defenceless, he judges, would be dishonourable, a verdict delivered by the man who shot her at her own wedding rehearsal. The Bride survives four years on her murderer's etiquette. When she wakes and begins the list, the campaign's news reaches Bill through its courier: Sofie Fatale, delivered to a hospital minus an arm and full of the Bride's message. The volume closes with Bill at Sofie's bedside, asking the question that reclassifies the saga: does the Bride know her daughter is alive?

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Honour, self-authored

Interpretation: Bill's code is not hypocrisy but sovereignty — he recognises no standard but his own rulings, and his rulings are absolute: a wedding may be massacred (she wronged him), a coma may not be (she cannot fight). The horror of the character is that the code is sincerely held and entirely self-serving, in proportions he never has to examine.

The paternal register

He speaks to his assassins, his victim and his courier in the same calm, fatherly cadence. Interpretation: Bill's power in this volume is tonal — Carradine's voice makes every scene he telephones into a family matter, which is his claim on all of them.

The keeper of the child

His raising of the daughter in secret is the volume's withheld fact. Interpretation: whether it is love, leverage or both is Volume 2's question; this volume establishes only that he kept her — the one mercy and the one theft, in a single act.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The unseen face

Interpretation: withholding Bill's face for a full volume makes him what he is to the Bride — not a man she can picture but a voice that raised her, loved her and shot her. The audience is put inside her epistemology: everyone in this saga knows Bill's voice better than his face.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the absent centre of a revenge film

The analysis the archive records: the volume's boldest construction is its villain's rationing. Bill is built from a handkerchief, a telephone call and a question — and the question retroactively rewrites the film the audience has just watched, converting a revenge plot into a custody tragedy without reshooting a frame. Carradine's casting — the star of Kung Fu, television's wandering monk — loads the voice with the genre's own history, Tarantino's quotation method applied to a person.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Bill stands in the Galaxy record as the modern screen villain's study in absence — a saga's whole gravity generated by a voice and a code — and his closing question remains one of the great volume-ending lines: eleven words that reclassify everything before them.
