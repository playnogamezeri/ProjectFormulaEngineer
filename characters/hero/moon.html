01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Moon is Hero's smallest principal and its clearest measure of what narration costs. She is Broken Sword's pupil at the calligraphy school — a young swordswoman with twin blades and a hopeless, unhidden love for her master. The film's versions spend her three ways. In Nameless's red lie she is seduced by Sword in his despair, then murdered by Flying Snow when she seeks revenge for him: collateral of great people's stories, killed by the plot's need for a triangle. In the King's blue reasoning she is the lovers' mourner-messenger, delivering Sword's sword to Nameless with the plea that the two swords remain united even in death. In the white truth she does none of it: she serves at the school, witnesses the conspiracy's quarrels, and survives — the only figure at the school left standing when the story ends. Her page is the archive's record of the difference between a person and a plot device, kept in the film's own three colours.

ROLE

The pupil and the footnote

The school's youngest principal; her treatment across the versions is the film's index of each narrator's honesty.

DEFINING TRAIT

Devotion without a claim

She loves her master openly and asks for nothing; the lie punishes her for it, the truth leaves her in peace.

FATE

Alive in the truth

Murdered only inside Nameless's fabrication. In the King's version she is the swords' messenger; in the truth she remains at the school, the conspiracy's sole surviving witness there.

Place in the story

Moon's page is deliberately the archive's most version-conscious file in this film: she is the character a careless summary kills. The Galaxy's own card for her states the design — in every version told in the throne room she is a footnote — and her page exists to give the footnote its full text.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Moon's documented life is the school's daily round: she serves Broken Sword and Flying Snow at the calligraphy school in the besieged Zhao city, studies the sword under her master, and loves him — a fact the film treats as common knowledge, carried by her with a pupil's devotion and no strategy at all. When Nameless comes to the school to commission the scroll and open the conspiracy, Moon is part of the household he enters: present at the edges of the great people's negotiations, pouring, attending, witnessing. In the truth, that is her whole plot — she is there when Snow wounds Sword in their quarrel, there through the conspiracy's machinery, and there at the end, when the school's masters are dead by each other's hands and the pupil remains.

The versions write her larger and worse. In the King's blue reasoning, Moon is given the lovers' epitaph to deliver: after Snow's staged defeat, it is Moon who brings Broken Sword's sword to Nameless, asking that the two swords — Snow's and Sword's — remain united even in death. The King imagines her, that is, as the conspiracy's loyal mourner: the pupil as the keeper of her masters' unity. It is the kindest of the three Moons, and it is still an invention.

In Nameless's red lie, she is spent entirely. The fabrication requires Sword to be degraded, so Moon is written as his despairing seduction — the heartbroken master taking his pupil — and then requires Snow's jealousy to have a second corpse, so Moon is written attacking Snow for revenge and being killed by her. Neither event occurred. The archive files the red Moon where she belongs: inside the lie's authorship, as evidence of what the lie was willing to spend — a young woman's honour and life, neither of which the teller had met for more than a day — to make a throne room believe a story about other people's passion. The truth restores her in a single stroke: she never left the school, and she is alive when the film ends.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Devotion without negotiation

Interpretation: Moon's love for Sword is the film's only uncalculated feeling — she does not scheme, bargain or conceal. Set among assassins who price everything, her openness reads as a different order of courage, and the film protects it in the truth even while its narrators abuse it.

The pupil's loyalty

Her service at the school is total and unambitious: she is there to learn the sword and to attend its master. The archive reads her as the jianghu's apprentice class in person — the generation that inherits the masters' stories and, in her case, survives them.

Witness, not agent

She decides nothing in the plot, which is her significance: Moon is what the great people's decisions look like from the doorway. The film's sympathy for her is its sympathy for everyone history files as staff.

Skilled with twin swords under Broken Sword's teaching (per the documented cast record) — a real martial education, though the truth never requires her to use it in anger.

SERVICE

The school's continuity

She keeps the household that keeps the masters: in a besieged city, the school's daily life — and its survival past its masters — is her work.

SINCERITY

The unedited account

She is the only principal who never narrates, edits or performs a version of events: the film's one unmediated person.

WEAKNESS

Defencelessness before narrators

Her honour and her life are spendable by any storyteller who needs them; she has no version of her own to answer with. The film makes the vulnerability structural, not personal.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The footnote as a person

Interpretation: Moon is the film's argument that collateral is a narrative category, not a human one. Every version files her under someone else's story; the archive reads her page — and the film's own card for her — as the correction: the footnote, read closely, is a life the plots walked through.

The twin swords

Interpretation: her paired blades mirror the film's great pair — Snow's and Sword's two swords, whose unity the King's Moon is made to plead for. The pupil carries the story's central image in miniature, in her own hands, without knowing it.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the honesty index

Moon is the calibration weight for Hero's narrators: what a teller does with her — the character he knows least and needs most cheaply — reveals his story's ethics. Nameless spends her honour and life; the King spends her as a mourner; the truth spends nothing. The analysis the archive records is that the film grades its versions by her file before it grades them by anything else.

Survival as a verdict

That the truth leaves her alive is not a detail but a judgment: the honest account is the one in which the bystander is not required to die for the principals' meanings. The archive treats her survival as the white version's certificate of authenticity — the lie needed corpses; the truth needed none at the school but the two the lovers made themselves.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Zhang Ziyi

Zhang, fresh from Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon, plays the film's youngest principal; the archive notes the production's use of her — a star of the first rank cast as the household's servant, so that the footnote is played by someone the audience cannot help watching.

The name

The documented cast record gives her name as Moon (Ruyue — like the moon); the archive notes the fitness the film makes of it: she shines, in every version, with reflected light — her masters' — and the truth is the only version that lets her keep her own.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Moon's afterlife in the film's criticism is as its test case: discussions of Hero's unreliable narration reach for her file first, because she is where the versions' ethics are priced. Her survival in the truth has become the standard illustration that the film's white account is its honest one.

Within the Galaxy's records, her page is the archive's standing instruction to itself — a character the sources nearly lose, restored by keeping the versions separate and refusing to let the loudest one win.
