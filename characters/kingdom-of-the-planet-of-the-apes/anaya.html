01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Anaya is a young chimpanzee of the Eagle Clan in Kingdom of the Planet of the Apes (2024), played by Travis Jeffery — Noa's best friend, and the third member, with Noa and Soona, of the trio whose Bonding Day egg climb opens the film. The official character materials define him by contrast and by constancy: less bold than Soona, his dedication to the cause — and his humor — unwavering. The plot cashes both halves: Anaya is captured with the clan, labors in Proximus Caesar's camp, and when the resistance forms inside it, he is one of the four who plant the sea-wall explosives and enter the vault. Anaya matters because he is the story's ground level: the ordinary young ape, frightened at the correct times, through whom the audience can measure what the film's heroes are actually being asked to do. Every epic needs the friend who is scared and comes anyway; Anaya is that friend, documented at the same depth as the king he helped to drown.

ROLE

Supporting lead — the best friend

DEFINING TRAIT

Place in the story

Anaya completes the trio's design: Noa's anxiety about worthiness, Soona's steadiness, and Anaya's open fear are the three registers of the clan's next generation, and the film distributes its dangers among them. His place in the vault party is the design's proof — when the conspiracy forms, the frightened friend is inside it, not excused from it. 03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Anaya's story opens on the towers, with Noa and Soona, on the climb for the Bonding Day eggs — the dangerous ascent to the wild eyrie from which each young ape takes one egg, leaving the rest under the clan's law. The climb establishes the trio's internal order in a single sequence: Noa anxious to prove himself, Soona steady, Anaya the most visibly frightened of the three — and climbing anyway. It is the last ordinary hour of their lives. That night the masked raiders burn the village; Anaya is taken with the rest of the clan to Proximus Caesar's coastal kingdom and put to the vault labor.

When Noa infiltrates the camp and the conspiracy forms, Anaya is inside it from the first: with Noa, Mae and Soona he secretly plants the explosives around the sea walls that hold back the ocean from the settlement — the enslaved preparing their enslaver's ruin under his guards' eyes. And he enters the vault with them, one of the four who walk the old world's arsenal and its picture books and choose the flood. The sources give him no single-handed beat in the climax — no duel, no rescue, no line the coverage preserves — and this file records that as it stands: his heroism in the record is collective, a share in the party's work and the clan's escape, and in the song that brings the eagles down on the king.

He survives the flood with his clan and returns home to the rebuilding. The film's closing movements belong to Noa and Soona; Anaya's story ends, as it was lived, inside the community's — a young ape of the Eagle Clan, home again, in a valley that will need his whole generation to raise its towers again.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions and the film's official character description.

Fear, honestly carried

The film's official description gives Anaya the most psychologically interesting brief of the trio: less bold than Soona, dedication and humor unwavering. Read against the plot, that is a precise character: the one whose fear is neither hidden (Noa's anxiety is about worth; Soona's nerve is silent) nor disabling. Anaya is frightened at the eyrie, frightened in the camp, and present in the conspiracy — the film's demonstration that courage in ordinary people is not the absence of the feeling but attendance in spite of it. His humor belongs to the same system: the jokes of the member who knows exactly how dangerous things are.

The friend's psychology

Anaya has no arc of ambition, doctrine or romance in the record; his psychology is affiliative. He goes where his friends go — up the tower, into the vault — because they go. In a film whose subject is competing claims on a young ape's allegiance (father, prophet, king, mission), Anaya represents the claim that precedes all doctrine: the peer bond. The archive reads his steadiness-in-fear as the clan's actual survival trait, the one its law of restraint was designed to protect: ordinary members who stay.

07 / CHARACTER ARC

Character Arc

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
Beginning state — the frightened climber

A Bonding Day youth in a peaceful valley, the least bold of three friends, in a world whose dangers are heights and ceremony. His arc begins with the smallest fear in the film — and the film honors it by never pretending it was small to him.

The catastrophe — captivity

The raid scales his world from heights to history in one night: enslaved by a king he had never heard of, in a labor force of strangers. Endurance is his arc's first movement, shared with his whole generation.

The conspiracy — attendance as courage

He joins the explosives plot and the vault entry: the frightened friend, present at the plot's most dangerous work. The arc's center is not a transformation of his nature but a proof of it — fear, outvoted by loyalty, on schedule.

End state — home again

Freed by the flood and the song, he returns to the rebuilding valley: the ordinary member restored to the ordinary life the entire story was fought to make possible. His arc closes without promotion, which is its point.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The third climber — the community in the hero's frame

The film opens with three apes on a tower, and its whole argument is latent in the composition: a leader is never alone in the frame. Anaya symbolizes the constituency — the ordinary members whose fear, labor and loyalty are what clans, orders and kingdoms are actually made of. Proximus counts such apes as units; Raka's doctrine counts them as the point; Anaya is the ape both claims are about, standing in the film's first shot.

Fear as the honest instrument

In a film of concealed motives — Mae's mission, Proximus's editing, Noa's guilt — Anaya's fear is the one emotion always accurately reported. Symbolically he is the story's calibrated instrument: when Anaya is frightened, things are as dangerous as they seem, and the audience can trust the reading. The archive notes the craft of it: an epic that wants its wonders believed gives its ground-level character an honest face.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Narrative function: the measure of the stakes

Anaya's function is calibration. The leads of the film operate at the level of doctrine and destiny; Anaya operates at the level of the climb, the camp and the charge-planting, and his reactions set the audience's scale for both. Structurally he is also the trio's proof of concept: the conspiracy's membership — heir, companion, ordinary friend — is the Eagle Clan in miniature, and its success is the film's argument that the clan, not its champion, is the story's hero-unit. The song on the cliff is sung by a community; Anaya is one of its voices, and the archive declines to call that a minor function.

The thinnest file — and why it is kept at full length

The investigation this file is obliged to record: Anaya has the least individual documentation of the six principals — no solo beat in the plot accounts, no critical literature of his own, a character defined in the official materials in two sentences. The result is deliberately the batch's demonstration piece: this is what the Character Archive does when the record is small — it says so, and finds the character's real, if modest, significance inside the truth.

Unresolved questions the film leaves about him

Two, both generational: his place in the rebuilt clan's order — the trio's third member in a story that has promoted its first — and whether the sequel will grant the ordinary member an arc of his own. The film leaves both, with his whole cohort, to the continuation in development.

Behind the Character

Casting

Travis Jeffery plays Anaya in performance capture. His casting was reported in October 2022 alongside Lydia Peckham's, with the pair's roles revealed as two new chimpanzee characters in an Entertainment Weekly article. Jeffery's prior screen work includes Angelina Jolie's Unbroken, in which he played Scot, and Gallipoli.

Performance

Reviewers assessed the two companions together: Peckham and Jeffery "step up to the plate when called for, be it emotional resonance or a necessary beat of levity" — the levity being principally Anaya's register, the humor the official description promises and the performance supplies inside a story of raids and floods.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Within the franchise, Anaya is part of its newest founding generation — the Eagle Clan cohort through whom the post-Caesar world will be continued if the planned trilogy proceeds. His significance in this film is the significance of the ordinary member in a story about extraordinary claims: he is the control group of the film's experiment, the ape unchanged by doctrine, whose survival is the experiment's purpose. The sequel inherits him as its most available character — the friend whose arc has not yet been spent.
