01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Mae is the human co-lead of Kingdom of the Planet of the Apes (2024), played by Freya Allan. She enters the film as an "echo" — the Eagle Clan's word for the feral humans who haunt the edges of ape country — shadowing Noa's clan and scavenging at its margins. The film then reverses her twice: first she speaks, revealing an intelligence no ape in the story has ever encountered in a human; then she is revealed as an agent, the sole survivor of a party sent from a hidden, still-intelligent human settlement to retrieve a satellite decryption key from the vault Proximus Caesar is trying to plunder. Mae matters because she is the film's argument about the other side: every ape faction has a doctrine about humans, and Mae is the human who confirms and refutes all of them at once — Raka's faith that humans can be partners, Proximus's warning that they cannot be trusted, and Noa's dawning suspicion that both are true.

ROLE

Human co-lead

The story's second center of gravity: Noa's guide to the vault, Proximus's prize informant, and the plot's concealed engine.

DEFINING TRAIT

Mission over attachment

Again and again Mae chooses the objective over the alliance — the key over candor, the flood over her rescuers' safety — and the film makes her pay for it in trust.

FATE

Survives — mission complete

She delivers the decryption key to her people's satellite base; humanity's scattered remnants make contact with one another for the first time in generations.

Place in the story

Mae is the reboot continuity's first speaking human protagonist since the Caesar trilogy, and the film deliberately withholds her from the audience as it withholds her from Noa: feral, then articulate, then operational. She is also the living refutation of the apes' settled history — proof that the "echoes" are not the whole of humanity — and the ending positions her as the hinge of the franchise's future: the apes have a new leader; the humans, thanks to Mae, have a voice again.

Mae's identity in the film is a sequence of masks, each discarded under pressure: the feral scavenger (a performance of the very "echo" the apes expect), the grateful dependent Nova, the knowledgeable guide, and finally the operative who strangles a man, shoots a lieutenant and floods a vault with her allies inside it. The file treats the sequence itself as the identity: Mae is whoever the mission requires, and the film's drama is the cost of that requirement to the one relationship — Noa's — that asked her to be someone in particular.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Almost nothing of Mae's life before the film is shown, and the file records that limit plainly: she comes from a hidden settlement of humans who kept their intelligence — a community sheltering at a satellite facility — and she was sent out with companions on a mission to the coastal vault. Proximus's masked raiders killed the rest of her party; Mae alone survived, and continued toward the vault on foot, scavenging. That is the whole of her documented past: a home she does not describe, a mission she does not disclose, and a dead party she mentions only in outline.

She is first seen shadowing the Eagle Clan, stealing food at its edges — the "echo" whose scuffle with Noa cracks his Bonding Day egg. After the raid she follows Noa's rescue journey at a distance until Raka, alone among apes, treats her as a person: feeding her, wrapping her in a blanket, naming her Nova. When Sylva's raiders attack the feral human herd, Mae drops the performance and speaks — revealing that she knows exactly where Noa's clan has been taken, because it is where she was already going. At the river crossing she is saved from drowning by Raka, who is swept away in her place; she and Noa are captured together and brought before Proximus, who wants from her the vault's secrets. Pressed by Noa, she trades a partial truth: a hidden entrance, and a "book" inside that can restore speech to humanity — in exchange for his help getting in and destroying what Proximus wants.

The infiltration is where Mae's training shows. She helps plant the explosives on the sea walls; when Trevathan — Proximus's human advisor — catches them and moves to betray the plan, Mae strangles him to death without hesitation. Inside the vault she goes straight to her target while the apes wander among the tanks and picture books: not a book at all, but a satellite decryption key. At the opened vault door, with Proximus's lieutenant Lightning threatening Soona's life, Mae shoots him dead with a handgun and triggers the explosives — flooding the vault with Noa and his clan inside it, a betrayal of timing she does not warn them about. She escapes through the chaos while the apes climb for their lives.

Her ending is double. At the satellite base, she delivers the key; the dishes wake; a human voice answers from another settlement — her mission, and her people's isolation, ended in a single transmission. And at the Eagle Clan's ruined home she comes to say goodbye to Noa with her handgun held behind her back, against the chance he would not forgive the flood. He does not attack; she does not draw. They part with the film's question spoken aloud between them — whether apes and humans can ever share the world — unanswered by either.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited ending explainers), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Discipline as a way of life

Mae's defining psychological fact is that she is never off duty. The feral act is maintained for weeks of shadowing; the dependency act is maintained through Raka's kindness; the partial truth is maintained through Noa's direct demand for honesty. Even her genuine emotions — and the film gives her real ones, notably around Raka's loss and Noa's trust — are processed through the mission's priorities before they are allowed to become actions. She is the only character in the film who treats every relationship as an operational asset, and the film neither excuses nor condemns the habit: it simply prices it, in Noa's final distrust.

The survivor's arithmetic

Mae is the last of her party, and her decisions carry a survivor's calculus: one life — hers, her mission's — weighed against any number of ape lives, and found heavier. The flood is the clearest expression: she does not know Noa's clan can climb out, and she detonates anyway, because the alternative — Proximus armed — is, in her arithmetic, worse. The film is careful to make the arithmetic defensible and the cost real at the same time, which is why the farewell scene needs the hidden gun: Mae herself is not sure the arithmetic acquits her.

Concealed tenderness

Against the discipline, the performance keeps registering attachment she cannot afford: her grief at Raka's loss, her hesitation in the farewell, the gun that stays behind her back and is never raised. The interpretation the archive offers is that Mae's tragedy is not coldness but rationing — she has exactly as much heart as the other characters, and a mission that taxes all of it. Critics who found the character underwritten on the page noted that Allan's performance supplies the dimension the script withholds: vulnerability performed early, pain banked under control later.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
SKILL

Survival & scavenging

Crosses ape country alone for weeks, living off the land and the edges of settlements, undetected by everyone except the apes she chooses to shadow. Her feral performance is itself a survival skill — camouflage by expectation.

SKILL

Old-world knowledge

Knows the vault's hidden entrance, its layout's logic, what a satellite decryption key is and what it is for, and how to handle a handgun and explosives. She is the only character in the film literate in the old world's machinery.

SKILL

Deception & nerve

Maintains cover identities under direct scrutiny from three factions, trades in calibrated partial truths, and executes two killings — Trevathan by hand, Lightning by gun — without hesitation at the decisive moments.

SKILL

Languages of two worlds

Speaks fluent human speech and understands ape speech and sign well enough to operate among them — the film's only fully bilingual agent, which is precisely what makes her dangerous to both sides.

WEAKNESS

Physical limits

She cannot climb like an ape — the vault infiltration needs Noa's body for its route — and she cannot swim the river crossing that costs Raka his fate. Her mission depends, structurally, on the strength of the species she is deceiving.

WEAKNESS

The mission's blind spot

Her arithmetic discounts ape lives and ape trust as expendable, which wins the objective and loses the peace: she leaves the field with the key and without a single ally, in a world where her people will eventually need both.

07 / CHARACTER ARC

Character Arc

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
Beginning state — the echo

A lone scavenger at the edge of an ape village: to every observer, a feral animal. In truth, a trained agent continuing a mission whose entire party is dead.

First turn — named Nova

Raka's kindness gives her cover and a name, and her first experience of an ape's unconditional goodwill — the relationship her mission will spend and her conscience will keep.

The revelation — speech

Cornered by the raiders' attack, she speaks, trading her deepest camouflage for the alliance she needs. From here the film's question about her shifts from what she is to what she wants.

Turning point — the two killings

Trevathan strangled, Lightning shot: the operative fully replaces the dependent. Whatever Noa believed he was helping, the vault sequence shows him — and the audience — the professional underneath.

End state — the voice restored, the trust spent

She delivers the key and her people speak across the world again; she also leaves the apes' country with a hidden gun at a farewell and no ally in it. The arc closes as a trade the film refuses to score: mission accomplished, coexistence postponed.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The name "Nova" — the old world's label

Raka names her for a tradition he cannot explain — "we call them all Nova; I don't know why" — an inherited label from Caesar's era, when another Nova mattered. Symbolically the name is the apes' whole relationship to humanity in one word: a category applied kindly, without knowledge of the individual inside it. Mae's story is the category failing: she is not "a Nova," she is an agent with a name of her own, and the film's tension is the distance between the name given and the person present.

The "book" — speech itself as the treasure

Her cover story is truer than her audience knows: the "book that restores speech" is a decryption key that restores communication — voice at planetary scale rather than in a single throat. Mae symbolizes humanity's actual evolutionary stake in the film: not the guns both sides fight over, but connection. That she must drown an arsenal to carry home a voice is the film's neatest thematic equation.

The gun behind the back — coexistence, armed

The farewell's hidden handgun is the film's closing symbol: peace, conducted with a weapon in reserve. Mae does not draw it and Noa does not strike; the symbol is not treachery but the honest condition of the two species at the film's end — goodwill, verified by nothing, insured against everything.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Narrative function: the double agent as plot engine

Mae is engineered as the story's information valve. Every major turn — the clan's location, the vault's entrance, the key's existence, the flood — passes through what Mae chooses to reveal and when. This makes her the film's most powerful character in narrative terms, more than Proximus: the king has an army, but Mae has the script. The investigation the archive records is that her power is deliberately made to feel like betrayal, because the audience is positioned inside Noa's trust; the same actions, seen from her settlement's briefing room, would read as a flawless deep-cover operation. The film's maturity is that it never supplies the briefing room.

The withheld backstory

The film gives Mae no flashback, no family, no named home — a withholding several critics found frustrating and others found principled. The archive's reading: the withholding is the character. Mae is the only principal whose interior the film refuses to open, because her function is to be the unknown the apes' doctrines are about. To explain her would be to settle the question she exists to pose. The cost, recorded honestly, is that some viewers experience her as two-dimensional on the page — a gap Freya Allan's performance, with its early vulnerability and later banked pain, is widely credited with filling.

Unresolved questions the film leaves about her

Three stand out. First, authority: who sent her, and would her settlement endorse the flood — the film never shows her reporting it. Second, the feral humans: her people's restored network is a network of the intelligent few, and the film leaves untouched what obligation, if any, they feel toward the "echoes" Mae lived among. Third, Noa: her farewell leaves the gun undrawn and the question open — whether Mae herself believes coexistence is possible is the one thing her discipline never lets her say.

10 / KEY MOMENTS

Key Moments

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
The cracked egg

Her scuffle with Noa at the clan's stores breaks his Bonding Day egg — the mission's first, accidental casualty, and the plot's first cause.

The blanket

Raka feeds her and wraps her against the cold, naming her Nova. Mae accepts the kindness of the species she is deceiving — the debt she will never be able to repay, collected at the river.

She speaks

Under attack, the feral act drops: "My name is Mae." The single most consequential line in the film — the apes' history of humanity collapses in four words.

The river

Raka pulls her from the drowning water and is swept away in her place. Mae's mission survives because an ape she was using spent himself for her.

Trevathan

Caught planting the explosives, she strangles Proximus's human advisor — the operative revealed, and the humans' private war made visible.

The key, not the book

Inside the vault she bypasses the arsenal for a small drive: the satellite decryption key. Her whole cover story resolves into a single object.

The shot and the flood

She kills Lightning to save Soona, then detonates the sea walls with her allies inside the vault — rescue and betrayal in the same minute, which is the character in one gesture.

The farewell

Handgun behind her back, she says goodbye to Noa and does not use it. The film's last word on trust between the species is a weapon neither side fires.

The answering voice

At the satellite base, the key wakes the dishes and a stranger's voice answers from another settlement. Mae's mission — and humanity's long silence — ends off her face, in a room full of her own kind.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Casting

Freya Allan — known to audiences from The Witcher — was among the first cast members announced for the film, in September 2022, in a role initially undisclosed. She is one of only two principal performers in the film who appears without performance capture, alongside William H. Macy's Trevathan: the humans are played in the flesh, a production choice that keeps Mae visually and physically of a different order from the apes around her.

Performance

The role is built as a performance within a performance — Mae is acting feral, then acting dependent — and Allan's work was noted by critics for carrying the character's third dimension: the early softness and vulnerability the deception requires, and the lingering pain that surfaces as the control relaxes around Noa and Raka. Reviewers who questioned the character's writing frequently exempted the performance.

The Nova connection

The name Raka gives her is a deliberate franchise echo: "Nova" was the human girl of War for the Planet of the Apes (2017), and Raka's line — that his kind call them all Nova, he doesn't know why — turns the reference into an in-world fossil, a word surviving its meaning. The archive records it as the film's most-discussed piece of connective tissue, and as this character's naming in the official cast listings: Mae / Nova.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Within the franchise, Mae is the hinge between eras: the first speaking human lead since the Caesar trilogy, and the proof that the reboot continuity's humans are not finished as a power. Her delivery of the decryption key is the film's true strategic event — larger than Proximus's fall — because it converts scattered human remnants into a network, setting the terms for the sequel in development. She also inherits and complicates the franchise's Nova lineage: where the earlier Nova embodied innocent coexistence, Mae embodies its armed, doubtful successor.

Within this Galaxy, Mae's file is the Character Archive's study in asymmetry: the character about whom the film tells the audience least, documented at the same depth as its hero — with the gaps left visible, as the archive's rules require.
