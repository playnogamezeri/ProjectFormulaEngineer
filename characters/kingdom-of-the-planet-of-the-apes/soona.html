01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Soona is a young chimpanzee of the Eagle Clan in Kingdom of the Planet of the Apes (2024), played by Lydia Peckham — Noa's closest companion, described in the film's official character materials as brave and loyal, and identified across the coverage as his childhood friend and emerging romantic interest. She is one of the trio — with Noa and Anaya — whose Bonding Day egg climb opens the film, and she shares every stage of his catastrophe: the capture of the clan, the labor camp of Proximus Caesar, the secret planting of the explosives, the entry into the vault, and the moment at the opened door when Proximus's lieutenant holds her life as the price of Noa's obedience. Soona matters because she is the story's measure of what Noa's leadership is for: not an idea, but the particular people beside him — and the film gives its final image to the two of them together, looking at the stars.

ROLE

Supporting lead — the companion

Noa's fellow climber, fellow captive and fellow infiltrator; the clan's future embodied at his side.

DEFINING TRAIT

Brave loyalty

The official descriptions and the plot agree: of the three friends, Soona is the one who steps forward — into the climb, into the captivity's resistance, into the vault.

FATE

Survives — home again

She escapes the flood with the clan, returns to rebuild, and shares the film's closing telescope scene with Noa.

Place in the story

Soona is the Eagle Clan's second face: where Noa carries the plot, Soona carries the stakes. Proximus understands this instinctively — she is the hostage he chooses when he wants to teach Noa the price of trust — and the film's structure agrees with him: her peril at the vault door is the climax's emotional trigger, and her presence in the final frame is its answer. She is also, with Anaya, the proof that Noa's heroism is not solitary: the infiltration is a trio's work, and the clan that sings down a king is a community, not a champion.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Soona's story begins where Noa's does: on the towers above the Eagle Clan's village, on the eve of Bonding Day, climbing with Noa and Anaya to a wild eyrie to take an eagle's egg each — carefully leaving some in the nest, as the clan's law requires. She is the trio's steadiness on the climb, and the ceremony the climb serves is the last normal day of her life. When the masked raiders burn the village that night, Soona is taken with the rest of the clan to Proximus Caesar's coastal kingdom, into the labor force clawing at the vault.

Noa's arrival in the camp reunites the trio, and Soona becomes a principal of the resistance inside it: with Noa, Mae and Anaya she secretly plants the explosives around the sea walls that protect the settlement, and she enters the vault with them — walking the arsenal of the old world, past the tanks and the picture books, as one of the four who decide its contents must drown rather than arm a king. The decision's price is presented to her personally. When the great door opens onto Proximus's army, his lieutenant Lightning seizes Soona and threatens to kill her, to force Noa's choice between her life and Mae's — and it is Mae's handgun, fired past every doctrine in the film, that saves her, an instant before the flood.

She escapes the drowning vault with her clan, climbs to the cliff with them, and joins the bonding song that brings the eagles down on Proximus. Her story closes at home, in the rebuilding — and in the film's final scene, beside Noa at the telescope he found in the ruins, looking up at whatever he has found to show her in the stars. The film gives her no farewell and no epilogue beyond that shared look, which the archive reads as the point: her future is the clan's future, and it is just beginning.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript. Soona's individual beats are fewer in the sources than the leads'; the file does not invent additional ones.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions and the film's official character description.

Bravery as steadiness

The film's official materials call Soona brave and loyal, and the plot cashes the description in a specific way: her bravery is not daring but steadiness. On the climb she is the trio's calm; in the camp she joins the conspiracy without a hesitation scene; in the vault she walks the arsenal at the plot's most dangerous hour. Where Anaya's fear is shown and Noa's anxiety is the film's subject, Soona's nerve is simply present — the emotional baseline the other two are measured against.

The companion's claim

Psychologically, Soona represents the claim of the particular on the leader. Noa's journey keeps offering him abstractions — Caesar's legacy, the humans' question, the king's doctrine — and Soona is the person those abstractions would be spent on. Her presence at the vault door, as the hostage of Proximus's lesson, literalizes it: the king tries to teach Noa that attachment is a lever, and the film's answer is that the lever is also the reason — the thing the leadership exists to protect.

What the film withholds

Honesty requires the note: the film gives Soona no independent want, no scene of decision that is hers alone, and no stated view of Mae, of Caesar, or of the clan's future. Her psychology must be read from conduct rather than confession — which this file does, and flags. Whether that economy is the character's dignity or her underdevelopment is a question the analysis section takes up directly.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
SKILL

Climbing & falconry training

An Eagle Clan youth of Bonding Day age: the eyrie climb that opens the film is her certification as much as Noa's, and the clan's eagle-bonding culture is hers by the same upbringing.

SKILL

Conspiracy under occupation

As a captive laborer she helps plant explosives around the sea walls under the eyes of Proximus's guards — work requiring nerve, timing and silence, performed by the enslaved against the enslaver.

SKILL

Composure under threat

Seized as a hostage at the vault door with a blade at her life, she does not break the party's discipline or beg the choice Proximus is staging — the composure that lets Mae's shot, not her panic, decide the moment.

WEAKNESS

A captive's leverage

Her value to the story's villains is precisely her value to its hero: she is the hostage Proximus chooses. The film gives her no scene of fighting free of that role — her agency operates inside the conspiracy, not in single combat.

WEAKNESS

An untested worldview

Like all the Eagle Clan, she has known nothing beyond the valley until the raid: no Caesar, no speaking humans, no ape powers. The vault's picture books are as much a demolition of her world as of Noa's, with less of the film's time spent on her rebuilding it.

07 / CHARACTER ARC

Character Arc

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
Beginning state — the steadfast friend

A Bonding Day climber in a peaceful valley, defined by the trio and the clan's law of restraint — the future, untested, in its ordinary form.

The catastrophe — captivity

The raid converts her generation's ceremony into enslavement: her arc's first movement is endurance — surviving the labor camp with the clan's cohesion intact.

The conspiracy — agency inside captivity

Planting the sea-wall explosives and entering the vault, she moves from victim to operative: the arc's center is a captive choosing the risks of resistance over the safety of submission.

The hostage moment — the price named

At the vault door her life is made the currency of Noa's obedience. She contributes the one thing the moment allows — composure — and is saved by the alliance's most compromised member.

End state — home, and the stars

Freed by the flood and the song, she returns to the rebuilding and shares the closing telescope scene: the companion as the future's co-witness, her arc closing where the clan's next chapter opens.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The hostage as the thesis's price tag

Proximus's lesson at the vault door — choose the human or the friend — is the film's argument staged as a hostage scene, and Soona is its price tag: the concrete value that abstract doctrines are always finally denominated in. That the choice is refused by a third party's gunshot is the film's own comment on such stagings: the correct answer to a tyrant's dilemma is to decline the currency altogether.

The final telescope — the shared horizon

The film could have ended on Noa alone with the instrument of the old world. It ends on Noa showing it to Soona. Symbolically, the widened world is not the leader's private revelation but the couple's — and through them the clan's: the future arrives as something shared at home, not as a doctrine imported by a king or a prophet. Soona's presence in that frame is the film's last word on what Noa's journey was for.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Narrative function: the stakes, personified

Soona's function is to keep the film's abstractions priced in persons. Caesar's legacy, Mae's mission, Proximus's doctrine — each is finally tested against what it does to her: enslaved by the doctrine, saved by the mission, freed by the legacy's true heir. The investigation the archive records is structural: remove Soona and the vault-door scene loses its hostage, the climax its trigger, and the ending its second figure. She is load-bearing in exact proportion to her quietness.

The underwriting question, honestly stated

The archive's standing rule is to record the critical reservation where it exists: Soona is the least individually documented of the film's six principals, in the coverage and, to a degree, in the film — she is given loyalty and bravery as stated traits, peril as a function, and no independent want or stated position on the story's questions. Whether this is the companion figure's legitimate economy or a debt the sequel must pay is genuinely open. The file's position: the performance and the structure make her presence felt beyond her lines, but the character as written is a promise the franchise has yet to keep — and the telescope scene reads, in that light, as the promise being formally made.

Unresolved questions the film leaves about her

Two: her view of Mae — rescuer and flooder in one minute — which the film never asks her; and her place in the clan's succession, where the heir's closest companion is traditionally something more than a friend. Both are left, with her whole generation, to the sequel in development.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Casting

Lydia Peckham plays Soona in performance capture. Her casting, with Travis Jeffery's, was reported in October 2022, and the pair's roles were revealed as two new chimpanzee characters in an Entertainment Weekly article — the Eagle Clan's younger generation cast as deliberately as its elders. Peckham's other screen work includes Cowboy Bebop.

Performance

Reviewers noted Peckham and Jeffery together: the two companions "step up to the plate when called for, be it emotional resonance or a necessary beat of levity." Soona's register within that pairing is the emotional one — the trio's steadiness — and the performance carries a role whose written beats are fewer than the leads'.

What the record does not contain

In keeping with the archive's rules, this section records its own limit: the sources consulted for this file contain no detailed production account of Soona's design, Peckham's preparation, or deleted material for the character. Where the leads' files can document a craft process, Soona's documents an absence — itself a fair measure of how the production's publicity weighted its principals.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Within the franchise, Soona belongs to its newest founding generation — the first Eagle Clan cohort, whose members will define what the post-Caesar world becomes if the planned trilogy proceeds. Her significance in this film is positional but real: she is the companion through whom the story prices its ideas, and the co-witness of its closing image. The sequel in development inherits her as its most obvious underwritten asset: the character standing closest to its protagonist, with the most yet to be said.

Within this Galaxy, her file is the Character Archive's exercise in honesty at the thin end of the record: a full file, built only from what the sources support, with the gaps named in place rather than papered over.
