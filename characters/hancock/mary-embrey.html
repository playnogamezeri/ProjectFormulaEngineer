01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Mary is the film's concealed second protagonist: a god in witness protection. She has built, in Los Angeles, the exact life her nature forbids — a husband, a stepson, a house, a school run — and she maintains it by the discipline of a woman who knows that the other one of her kind is in the same city, drinking himself through the decades a few miles away. When Ray brings Hancock home to dinner, her coldness is the film's first tell: she alone is not curious about the famous disaster, because she alone knows what he is. Her revelation comes in stages, as such things do — first the strength, thrown through a wall when he kisses her; then a false account, that they are siblings; then the true one, corrected by herself: they have lived three thousand years, the last of a kind that was called gods and angels, built in pairs — and pairs who stay together become mortal. She left him in that Miami hospital because the attack that took his memory had shown her the bill for staying. The film's climax is her physics collecting: shot while weakened by his proximity, dying as he bleeds beside her, saved only by his leap away — the abandonment, re-enacted as a rescue, with the roles of knowledge reversed.

ROLE

The other one

Co-protagonist in function; the concealed pair, the cosmology's keeper, and the rehabilitation's undoing and meaning.

DEFINING TRAIT

The shelter, maintained

Theron plays her as a woman holding a door shut with her whole life — the suburban performance is flawless and total, and its cracks (the coldness at dinner, the fury at the kiss) are the only honest things in it.

FATE

Alive, at distance

Shot and dying in the hospital, she is saved by Hancock's departure — her heart restarting as he bounds away — and resumes her life with Ray and Aaron, the secret now shared and the distance now kept by both of them.

Place in the story

Mary's page is the record of the film's hidden half: the immortal who chose mortality's neighbourhood and pays its rent in vigilance. The Galaxy keeps it as the pair's second file — the one who remembered everything, for both of them, alone.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Mary's biography is the film's withheld centre, and it is given in her own words, in stages, as her account: her kind are ancient — three thousand years of it — and were called, in the eras they passed through, gods and angels. They were built in pairs, and the pairing is the law of their nature: stay together, and the pair becomes mortal — powers ebbing, bodies aging, death admitted. She and Hancock were such a pair; in her corrected account, he was her husband in another life. Eighty years ago, in Miami in 1928, they were attacked leaving a picture show; Hancock took a head wound that cost him his memory entire, and Mary — nursing an amnesiac immortal who no longer knew her, in a world where their closeness would now kill them both at mortal speed — made the decision the film asks its audience to weigh: she left him in the hospital, unnamed and unclaimed, and let him forget. She has carried both memories ever since, alone, which is the film's quietest sentence on her.

Her present life is the shelter built over that decision: Ray Embrey, a good man met on human terms; Aaron, his son by a mother who died in childbirth, raised by Mary as her own; a house, a neighbourhood, a mortal woman's calendar. The shelter's one structural flaw is geography — Hancock is in her city, a famous drunk disaster on her television — and when Ray, in gratitude, brings his project home to dinner, Mary's coldness is the sound of the door she has held shut for eighty years taking a shoulder. The kiss, when Hancock drunkenly attempts it, gets him thrown through the wall: the shelter's keeper, at full strength, ending the negotiation. Her accounts follow — siblings first, the deflection of a woman buying time; then, cornered by his persistence and her own conscience, the truth entire, including the clause that matters: his nearness is already draining them both.

The clause collects at the worst hour. Weakened by proximity, Mary is shot in a street robbery and Hancock, arriving, bleeds from wounds that would once have been weather; both are hospitalised, failing together in adjoining peril, when Red Parker — escaped, and owed a hand — comes with armed men to finish them. Ray's fire axe saves the room; Hancock's leap saves Mary: dying himself, he hurls his body away from the hospital in great bounds, and as the miles open between the pair, her heart restarts. She recovers into the life she built — Ray now knowing what she is, Aaron's faith in Hancock now a family fact — with the pair's law honoured by both parties this time: he paints his gratitude on the Moon, where she can see it from the shelter, at the distance that keeps them both alive.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The keeper of both memories

Interpretation: Mary's coldness is not temperament but workload — she has remembered for two for eighty years, in the same city as the man who forgot, watching him drink on the news. The film's sympathy for her lie about siblings is built on that arithmetic: the truth had already cost her more than the lie.

The shelter as a moral act

Her suburban life is sometimes read as hiding; the film frames it as building — a mortal life, chosen and maintained at full price, with a stepson raised as her own. It is the only successful human life either immortal manages, and it is hers.

The correction

Her arc's hinge is self-correction: the siblings story is her last deflection, and she withdraws it herself, unprompted by any evidence but conscience — the moment the keeper decides the amnesiac has earned his own past back, at whatever it costs her shelter.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
THE FULL PACKAGE

The pair's equal share

Flight, strength and invulnerability at Hancock's scale — demonstrated once, through a wall, and thereafter by the film's restraint rather than its spectacle.

THREE THOUSAND YEARS

The long memory

She is the archive of her kind: its nature, its law and their shared past are carried in her alone, the other copy having been lost in Miami.

THE PERFORMANCE

A mortal life, sustained

Decades of flawless suburban normality maintained under the same city's lights as her pair's public disasters: camouflage as a life's work.

WEAKNESS

Proximity, and the shelter's door

Near Hancock she becomes mortal — and the shelter she built is in his city, with a husband whose gratitude imports him to dinner. Her two vulnerabilities are geography and love, which the film treats as one.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The wall, thrown through

Interpretation: the film's reveal is staged as domestic architecture failing — a kiss that goes through drywall. The shelter's walls were always the metaphor; the scene simply makes them load-bearing and then doesn't.

The two abandonments

Interpretation: she leaves him in 1928 to save him; he leaves her in the hospital to save her. The film rhymes the two departures exactly, with the knowledge reversed — the second time, both of them know why, and the Moon gets the forwarding address.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the hidden half of the premise

The archive records Mary as the film's structural secret: the superhero story turns out to have been a marriage story all along, told from the side of the spouse who remembered. Her account is the record's cosmology and must be filed as hers — the film gives no second source for the pairing law, the three thousand years or the gods and angels, and her one demonstrated falsehood (siblings) is the record's standing caution: even the archive of her kind edits, under pressure, and corrects herself only when she chooses.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Charlize Theron

Theron's casting was the production's guarded secret — her role was marketed as the hero's friend's wife, preserving the reveal — and her performance is built in two registers, the suburban and the ancient, with the second visible only in retrospect. The wall-throwing scene was staged practically, as the film's single demonstration of her strength, on the principle that one exhibit would outweigh a reel of them.
