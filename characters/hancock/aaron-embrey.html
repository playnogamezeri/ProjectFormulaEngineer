01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Aaron is the film's control group of one: the only person in Los Angeles whose opinion of Hancock never needs rehabilitating. Ray's son — his mother died giving birth to him, and Mary, his stepmother, is the mother he knows — Aaron meets the city's disaster with a fan's total faith, and the film uses that faith as its baseline instrument: when Hancock is hated, Aaron believes; when Hancock is applauded, Aaron is vindicated; when the truth about Mary breaks over the house, Aaron's steadiness is the family's. His one episode of his own is the schoolyard: bullied, he is rescued by his hero in the manner of heroes — the bully humiliated before the school — a small, complete Hancock story at playground scale, in which the rescue, for once, costs the city nothing. The record of Aaron is short because the film uses him as a measure rather than a subject; this page keeps the measure honest.

ROLE

The believer

Supporting; the faith by which the rehabilitation is measured, and the family's centre of gravity.

DEFINING TRAIT

Faith, unpolluted

Head plays him without a flicker of the city's ambivalence — Aaron's certainty that Hancock is a hero is the film's one fixed point, and every adult plot is graded against it.

FATE

His family, whole

He ends the film with his father, his stepmother recovered, and his hero painting thank-yous on the Moon: the believer's world, confirmed.

Place in the story

Aaron's page is the record of the film's smallest complete arc — a bully, a rescue, a faith kept — and of the household the gods and the PR man are all, in the end, defending. The Galaxy keeps it as the believer's file, brief because his function is to be believed about.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Aaron's facts are the film's domestic foundation: his mother died in childbirth, his father Ray raised him with Mary — herself, though the boy does not know it, an ancient being playing the mortal mother at full commitment — and his childhood is the ordinary Los Angeles article, school and schoolyard included. The schoolyard is his one arena: a bully makes him its subject, in the way of schoolyards, and Aaron's faith supplies what his fists cannot. His hero is real, is local, and — this is the film's smallest complete rescue — answers: Hancock deals with the bully in the hero's manner, humiliating him before the school and ending the matter at a scale the city, for once, does not have to invoice.

Through the adult plot Aaron is the constant. He is present for the houseguest era — the disaster at his table, the rehabilitation as a family project — and his belief never requires the campaign his father is running for the rest of the city. When the truth about Mary breaks, and the hospital fills with the adults' emergencies, Aaron's place in the record is the family's reason: the boy is what the shelter was built over, what the axe was swung for, and what the distance is kept to preserve. He ends the film in the intact household, his hero on the Moon — the one Angeleno whose opinion of Hancock the events never had to change.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the control measurement

The archive records Aaron as the instrument by which the film grades its adults: the city's hatred, Ray's campaign, Mary's vigilance and Hancock's self-contempt are all measured against a schoolboy's unchanged reading of the evidence. His thinness in the plot is the design's discipline — a gauge that developed its own arc would stop being a gauge — and his one episode is placed early, at playground scale, to calibrate the instrument before the gods start bleeding.

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Head was cast as Aaron from child auditions and plays the role straight, without the precocity the genre usually buys; the bully sequence was staged as the film's one consequence-free rescue, a deliberate calibration scene placed before the costs begin.
