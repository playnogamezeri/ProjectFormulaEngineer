01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Parker is the film's human-scale villain, and the film is careful to keep him at that scale: no powers, no grandeur — a professional robber with a technician's mind, whose bank siege is the best-planned crime in the picture (hostages as circuitry, every exit a negotiation) and whose defeat is a single act of superhero arithmetic: Hancock severs the hand that holds the dead man's switch. Prison gives Parker the two things he needs — time, and information. The rehabilitation of Hancock is a public story, and so is its sequel: the gods are weakening, the newspapers carry the hospital, and Parker walks out of custody with accomplices and a correct understanding of the pairing law's one implication — that immortals, at the right address, can be killed like anyone. His hospital attack is the film's climax precisely because his reading is right: Mary and Hancock are dying on schedule. What his reading omits is the household — Ray Embrey, the mortal in the corridor, and the fire axe behind its glass.

ROLE

The human predator

Principal antagonist; the robber whose siege makes Hancock a hero and whose revenge nearly unmakes two gods.

DEFINING TRAIT

The technician's grudge

Marsan plays him as a working professional of crime — methodical, unshowy, patient — whose villainy is competence plus memory: he keeps the hand's account open for years and collects with interest.

FATE

Killed by Ray Embrey

Shot and beaten in the hospital attack, he is finished by Ray's fire axe in the corridor — the mortal householder closing the gods' account.

Place in the story

Parker's page is the record of the film's scale discipline: the gods are nearly killed not by a bigger god but by a careful man with a newspaper, a grudge and the right address. The Galaxy keeps it as the robber's file — the hand, the patience, and the axe.

Marsan, a British character actor of famously unglamorous intensity, plays Parker without a trace of the comic-book register — the archive notes that the performance's flat professionalism is the point: he is the one character in the film who treats superpowers as an engineering problem, and he very nearly solves it.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Parker is introduced at his work: a bank robbery run as an electrical diagram, hostages fitted with explosive devices on dead man's switches — release the switch and the hostage dies — so that the police's every option is wired to a corpse. It is the crime that summons the rehabilitated Hancock for his public debut, and Hancock's solution is the film's coldest piece of heroism: he takes Parker's switch hand off at the wrist, defusing the circuit by amputating its keeper. Parker goes to prison one-handed, with the account open.

Prison is where Parker does his real work. The years of Hancock's rehabilitation, Mary's revelation and the pair's weakening are public events — a famous god, a hospital, a story — and Parker, a technician, reads them as specifications. He escapes with accomplices, among them the enormous Man Mountain, and times his collection for the one window the pairing law opens: Mary shot and dying, Hancock bleeding beside her, both of them in one hospital, mortal as patients. His men take the floor apart looking for them; the gods, for the first time in three thousand years of shared history, are prey that can be finished with ordinary weapons by ordinary men. What the specifications omit is the third party in the building. Ray Embrey — unpowered, unarmoured, the husband of one target and the manager of the other — comes down the corridor with the hospital's fire axe and kills Parker with it, in the middle of his collection. The hand's account is closed by the least powerful person in the film, which the film clearly regards as the correct signature.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The professional

Interpretation: Parker's crimes are designed, not improvised — hostages as circuitry, revenge as scheduling. The film's respect for his competence is what makes the climax frightening: his plan is sound, and only an unlisted variable stops it.

The memory

He is the one human in the film who keeps a superhero's account the way Hancock's city keeps his: itemised, unpaid, and patient. The grudge is his professionalism turned inward — a debt he services for years at interest.

The reader

His edge is literacy: he learns the pairing law from the public record and applies it correctly. The film's information economy — campaigns, newspapers, reputations — arms him as surely as it rehabilitated Hancock.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
SIEGE ENGINEERING

Hostages as circuitry

The dead man's switch system: a robbery designed so that every rescue is a killing — defeated only by amputation arithmetic.

PATIENCE

The long account

Years in prison spent reading, planning and waiting for the physics to ripen: his timeline is the longest in the film after the gods'.

INTELLIGENCE, APPLIED

The newspaper as oracle

He derives the pairing law's implication from public information and acts on it at the correct hour — the film's best piece of analysis, performed by its villain.

WEAKNESS

The unlisted variable

His specifications cover gods and police; they do not cover a householder with an axe. Every system in the film fails on the person it did not model, and Parker's is no exception.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The severed hand

Interpretation: Hancock's one act of surgical violence is the film's moral Rorschach — a maiming that saves a roomful of hostages — and Parker carries it as the account the whole climax settles. The film never lets either man call it anything but what it was: arithmetic.

The fire axe

Interpretation: Parker is killed by the building's own emergency provision, wielded by its most ordinary defender — the film's closing argument that the systems which save us are the mundane ones, kept behind glass by people who plan for fires.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the audit's auditor

The archive records Parker as the film's second intelligence: Ray rebuilds Hancock by reading the public correctly, and Parker nearly kills him by the same skill, from the same newspapers. The symmetry is the design's warning — publicity is a weapon with two handles — and his death by the unmodelled householder is the film's final thesis about systems: every specification omits somebody's husband.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Eddie Marsan

Marsan was cast against the blockbuster register deliberately — a character actor's flat menace inside a superhero film — and the bank siege was staged around practical hostages-and-switches rigging to keep Parker's competence legible. The character's survival of the siege with a maiming, rather than a death, was the screenplay's long-planned engine for the third act.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Red Parker is the film's most respected villain — the robber who nearly killed two gods with research — and his death by fire axe is its most quoted mortal victory. In the Galaxy's records his page stands as the technician's file: a wired bank, a severed hand, a patient account, and a corridor where the specifications ran out.
