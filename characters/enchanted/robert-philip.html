01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Robert is the real world's designated representative, and the film is fair about his brief: he processes divorces for a living, raises a daughter alone, and has concluded — rationally, from evidence — that romance is a contract with good catering. His five-year relationship with Nancy is proceeding to proposal on a timetable. His parenting withholds fairy tales the way other parents withhold matches. Into this audited life falls Giselle, and the film watches his defences fail in the correct order: first hospitality (his daughter's casting vote), then curiosity, then the dance at the ball, and finally the kiss — which the fairy tale's own machinery certifies as true love's, over the designated prince's. Robert does not become a romantic. He becomes a man whose realism now includes one (1) verified miracle, filed where he files everything: permanently.

ROLE

The host

Co-protagonist; divorce lawyer, single father, Giselle's reluctant shelter and eventual husband.

DEFINING TRAIT

Protective realism

Robert's cynicism is a father's risk management before it is a philosophy — he withholds the stories because he cannot withhold the world.

FATE

The kiss, certified

His kiss revives Giselle where Edward's fails; he marries her, and Nancy — released by the same audit — marries the prince. Everyone's paperwork, finally in order.

Place in the story

Robert is the film's second experiment: if Giselle tests whether the fairy tale survives reality, Robert tests whether reality can survive the fairy tale — his arc is the control group falling in love.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Robert is introduced at his equilibrium: a successful divorce practice, a daughter raised on protective realism (no fairy tales — the world is what it is), and a five-year relationship with Nancy Tremaine approaching its scheduled proposal.

He and Morgan encounter Giselle in Times Square — fallen from a billboard she mistook for a palace — and Morgan's insistence overrules his professional judgment: the woman in the wedding dress comes home with them. Nancy discovers her there in the morning, in Robert's robe, and the scheduled proposal acquires its first continuance.

Robert spends a day shepherding Giselle through the city — her questions dismantling his positions one by one (why five years? why no date before the wedding? why is Nancy sent flowers by Giselle, in his name, with better instincts than his own?) — and finds his practice's clients, his daughter and his own reflection all voting against his brief.

At the King and Queen's Ball, dressed as a prince for the costume theme, he dances with Giselle to "So Close" — the film's adult standard, sung live — and the dance files the motion his mouth has been opposing all week.

When the poisoned apple drops her, Robert watches Edward's kiss fail — and then, at the prince's own urging and the room's, kisses her himself. She wakes. He then helps fight Narissa's dragon, and the film closes on the amended family: Robert and Giselle married, Morgan with a stepmother, and Nancy — freed by the audit — married to Edward in Andalasia.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The auditor

Robert processes experience through professional categories — risk, precedent, paperwork — and the film's comedy is the categories' steady failure against a woman who files by song. Interpretation: his realism is never refuted as method; it is amended as data. One miracle, verified, is entered into evidence.

The protective father

His withholding of fairy tales is love in a crouch: Morgan's mother is gone, the world is unkind, and he has chosen honesty as armour for her. Giselle's arrival is the armour's audit — the child was starving for exactly what he withheld.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The divorce practice

Romance's paperwork as a worldview: Robert begins the film employed by love's failures and ends it as love's certified case — the firm's best closing.

The prince costume

At the ball he is dressed as the thing he is not, and wins the princess anyway: the film's thesis in fancy dress — the role is not the man, and the man is enough.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The control group

Robert's arc gives the film its evidentiary structure: Giselle's claims about love are tested on the least susceptible subject available — a professional in its dissolution. Analysis: Dempsey's restraint is what makes the certification legible; a showier thaw would read as conversion, and the film wants amendment, not conversion.

The mercy toward Nancy

The plot's kindest structural decision is Robert's counterpart ending: Nancy is not discarded but re-cast — into Andalasia, as its queen. The audit that dissolves his engagement simultaneously promotes hers, and the film's fairness to both parties is a large part of its reputation.
