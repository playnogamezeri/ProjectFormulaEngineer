01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Nathaniel is the film's secret soft centre: a henchman whose entire villainy is unrequited devotion. He keeps Edward troll-hunting, frees the troll to kill Giselle, trails the prince to New York with three poisoned apples — all of it for Narissa, who regards him as furniture with errands. New York educates him the way it educates everyone: by contact. Giselle's innocence, Robert's decency and a talk-show's vocabulary for self-worth work on him across two failed poisonings, until at the ball — ordered to stand by while his queen finishes the job — Nathaniel defects: he exposes the plot, defies her to her face, and chooses the people he was sent to poison. The henchman's reward is the film's kindest: he survives, returns to Andalasia a free man, and writes his life — the servant, finally authoring something.

ROLE

The queen's instrument, resigned

Narissa's servant and saboteur; the poison campaign's field agent; the ball's whistleblower.

DEFINING TRAIT

Devotion, misfiled

Everything Nathaniel does wrong is done for love of the one person who values him least; his arc is the devotion's re-filing, from the queen to himself.

FATE

The author

He defects at the ball, survives the dragon, and returns to Andalasia — where the record leaves him a success on his own account at last: his memoirs, written by the man who used to take dictation from a mirror.

Place in the story

Nathaniel is the film's proof that its education works on everyone: prince, princess, lawyer — and henchman. His defection is the plot's hinge at the ball, and Spall plays it as the film's most human-sized heroism.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Nathaniel serves Narissa out of adoration. His standing orders are the reign's immune system: keep Edward hunting trolls, away from brides. When the song defeats the system, Nathaniel escalates — freeing a captured troll to kill Giselle — and fails by rescue.

After the exile, Narissa sends him through the well to trail Edward in New York, armed with three poisoned apples and instructions to sabotage the rescue. He insinuates himself as Edward's faithful servant while reporting to the queen by mirror.

His two poisoning attempts fail — an apple martini and an apple on a pizza among the campaign's improvisations, foiled by chance and Pip — and the failures educate him: the people he is poisoning are kind to him, the city offers him a vocabulary for his condition, and his queen's regard, examined up close, is contempt with errands.

At the ball, Narissa arrives to finish the work herself. When the apple drops Giselle and the queen moves on Robert, Nathaniel defects in public: he reveals the plot, names his part in it, and defies Narissa — the servant's resignation, delivered at sword's length from a dragon.

He survives the finale and returns through the portal to Andalasia — no longer staff. The record leaves him to the happiest of the film's minor endings: his own story, written by himself.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The yearning servant

Nathaniel's devotion is total and totally unreciprocated — the film's saddest equation, played for comedy until it isn't. Interpretation: his arc is the adult version of Giselle's — she learns that love must be chosen by both parties; he learns it from the other side.

Conscience by contact

He is not argued out of villainy; he is treated out of it — fed, thanked and regarded as a person by his targets. The film's moral technology, applied to its henchman, works exactly as advertised.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The apples

Three of them — the canon's poison, issued as a servant's toolkit: Narissa's evil is literally handed to him, and his arc is the slow refusal to keep holding it.

The mirror

He reports to his queen through mirrors — the fairy tale's communications network — and his defection begins when he starts talking to people in the room instead.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The henchman's bildungsroman

Enchanted gives its most complete moral arc to its least important person. Analysis: Nathaniel's education mirrors Giselle's exactly — a fairy-tale functionary (bride, henchman) becomes a person by contact with a world that treats functions as people. Spall's performance, all yearning and gradual posture, is the film's quietest claim that nobody in it is only their role.
