Bridget von Hammersmark

01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Von Hammersmark is the film's professional: a star of the Reich's cinema who has been passing intelligence to the British, and whose access makes Operation Kino possible. She is introduced at her own celebration in the La Louisiane tavern — drinking with soldiers, playing the card game, managing the room — and survives its massacre as the only living witness, shot in the leg. Extracted by Aldo at gunpoint, her story audited with his finger in her wound, she is fitted with a cast disguised under couture and walks into the premiere on the arm of men she knows are doomed, to place the operation's inside half herself. What kills her is not the wound, the cover or the company: it is Landa, fitting her lost shoe onto her foot in a private room — the detective's evidence, and the fairy tale inverted — and strangling her with his hands.

ROLE

The operation's inside woman

Principal supporting; the British asset whose stardom is the plan's key, and whose death removes its last professional.

DEFINING TRAIT

Nerve, professionally maintained

Kruger plays her as the only adult in the operation: she manages soldiers, spies and a bullet wound with the same publicity-trained composure, and lies to Aldo's face at wound-point without flinching off her story.

FATE

Strangled at her own premiere

Identified by her shoe and her signature, she is taken aside by Landa during the premiere, fitted with the evidence, and strangled.

Place in the story

Von Hammersmark is the film's study of celebrity as cover — the star whom no one looks at properly because everyone is looking at her — and of the evidence that glamour cannot manage: a shoe left in a basement, a napkin signed for a baby. Her page is the record of the operation's most capable person, killed by housekeeping.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Von Hammersmark's war is double: by day the Reich's favourite leading lady, by arrangement the British service's eyes inside its film world. Operation Kino is built on her — she will host the Basterds' inside team at the premiere, in her own industry, under her own name. The rendezvous, in the basement tavern La Louisiane, goes wrong in company she did not choose: German soldiers celebrating a comrade's new son, a Gestapo major at the table, and a drinking game with identities on cards. She plays her part beautifully — it is, literally, her profession — and when the room detonates she survives it, shot in the leg, the only living person from either side of the table.

Aldo's extraction is an interrogation: at the farmhouse where the Basterds hold her, her account of the tavern is tested by his finger pressed into the bullet wound — and her story, that the soldiers were celebrating and the major was chance, holds. She is cast, dressed, and briefed for the premiere with the operation proceeding in ruins around her: her escort is now Aldo himself, in an Italian disguise neither of them believes in. She walks the red carpet on a shattered leg in a gown arranged to hide the cast, performing stardom for the newsreels while the building fills with the men she has come to help kill.

What she cannot perform away is the evidence. In the tavern she left a shoe; for the new father, in the evening's warmth, she signed a napkin — her famous signature, on the record, at the scene. Landa, who has read the basement like a script, takes her aside at the premiere, produces the shoe, fits it to her foot, and — the fairy tale completed as an arrest — strangles her with his hands, alone, unhurried, in a side room of her own industry's greatest night. The operation proceeds to its fire without its last professional, exactly as its enemies' detective arranged.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The professional

Interpretation: von Hammersmark is the only principal in the operation who is good at every part of her job — cover, nerve, access, endurance. The film kills her anyway, on evidence, which is its coldest professional judgment: competence does not audit your housekeeping.

Composure as craft

Her survival tools are an actress's: she manages the tavern's soldiers, Aldo's suspicion and the premiere's carpet with one instrument — the performed self — and it fails only where performance leaves objects behind.

The signature

Her one unforced error is an act of kindness — signing for a soldier's baby — and the film files it without comment as the fatal document: the star's warmth, in writing, at a crime scene.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The shoe

Interpretation: the Cinderella fitting inverted — the prince-detective identifies his quarry by her lost shoe and the recognition is an execution. The film stages celebrity's fairy tale as a police procedure, and it is the operation's most elegant and most brutal scene.

The napkin

Interpretation: a signature is a star's smallest public act, given away free at a thousand tables — and the one that hangs her. The archive files it as the film's verdict on fame: the autograph is evidence that you were there, being loved, in writing.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the professional, audited

Von Hammersmark's death is the film's hinge between its two plots: with her dies the last person who could have run the premiere from inside, and the night devolves to Landa's management by default. The archive records the mechanism as the film's most pointed irony — she is not outspied, outfought or outacted; she is out-filed. The detective beats the actress not at performance but at records, which is the war the film has been about all along: who keeps the evidence, and who merely leaves it.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Diane Kruger

Kruger, herself a German leading lady, was cast as the Reich's fictional counterpart — a layering the production used openly. The strangling scene was performed with Kruger's own insistence on realism under careful choreography, and the shoe-fitting that precedes it was staged by Tarantino as the film's deliberate fairy-tale inversion, down to the camera's patience with the fitting itself.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Von Hammersmark is the film's most admired professional and its most quoted death — the actress-spy killed by her own autograph. In the Galaxy's records her page stands as the operation's audit: nerve, access and composure at the highest level, and a shoe in a basement that none of it could answer for.
