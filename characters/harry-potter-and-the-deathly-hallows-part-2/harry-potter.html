01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Harry opens the film burying a friend and planning a bank robbery, and ends it breaking the most powerful wand in the world rather than keep it. Between those poles sits the revelation that reorganises his whole life: when Voldemort's curse failed on him as a baby, a shard of the Dark Lord's soul lodged in him — he is the Horcrux Voldemort never meant to make, and he cannot be free of it, nor Voldemort be killed, until he lets Voldemort kill him. The film's Harry is therefore a study in a heroism the genre rarely attempts: not winning a fight but walking, unarmed and informed, to the place of his own execution — and being right to do it.

ROLE

The protagonist

The saga’s centre; Horcrux hunter, Horcrux, and the Elder Wand’s true master.

DEFINING TRAIT

Surrender as strategy

His decisive acts are submissions — to the heist’s risks, to the memories, to the curse — each one correctly chosen.

FATE

Alive; the war won; the wand refused

He survives his own death, defeats Voldemort by allegiance rather than power, breaks the Elder Wand, and is seen nineteen years later sending his son to Hogwarts.

Place in the story

Every other page in this film's set is a tributary of Harry's: the trio's labour, Snape's double life, Dumbledore's design and Voldemort's unmaking all terminate in his choices. The archive files him as the saga's argument about power — that it belongs to the person who can decline it.

Radcliffe completes a decade in the role with a performance the archive reads as deliberately unadorned: the Gringotts planner, the school's returning ghost and the boy in the Forest are played at the same quiet register, as if Harry's heroism were a duty roster rather than a destiny. The epilogue required the production to age him — a sequence reshot in December 2010 after the first makeup leaked and was judged to age the cast unevenly.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

After burying Dobby, Harry enlists the goblin Griphook to break into Bellatrix Lestrange's Gringotts vault, where Hufflepuff's cup — a Horcrux — is hidden; the price is Gryffindor's sword, and Griphook collects it mid-heist and abandons them. Trapped, the trio free the vault's dragon and ride it out over London. A vision shows Harry that Voldemort knows of the theft, and that a further Horcrux tied to Rowena Ravenclaw waits at Hogwarts. Aberforth Dumbledore shelters them in Hogsmeade — and reveals he sent Dobby to Malfoy Manor — before passing them through the portrait hole into a school under occupation, where Neville Longbottom leads them in.

The endgame assembles itself from other people's sacrifices. McGonagall duels Snape out of the castle and raises its defences; the Grey Lady's ghost points Harry to the diadem in the Room of Requirement, where Draco's ambush and Goyle's Fiendfyre nearly kill them all — Harry saves Draco and Zabini on brooms, and the diadem is destroyed in the fire. Then, in the boathouse, Harry watches Voldemort order Nagini to kill Snape — the Dark Lord believes the Elder Wand will not serve him while its supposed master lives — and the dying Snape gives Harry his memories. In the Pensieve, Harry learns the whole design: Snape's love for Lily, his double agency, Dumbledore's planned death — and that Harry himself is a Horcrux who must die by Voldemort's hand. Harry walks into the Forest, opens the Snitch, and is comforted by the spirits of his parents, Sirius and Lupin before letting Voldemort cast the Killing Curse.

He wakes in a white limbo shaped like King's Cross, where Dumbledore explains that the curse destroyed the soul-shard, not the boy, and that Harry may return or go on. Harry returns — plays dead in Hagrid's arms as Voldemort displays him — then reveals himself alive in the courtyard. In the final duel, his Expelliarmus meets Voldemort's Killing Curse and the curse rebounds: the Elder Wand, won by Draco from Dumbledore and by Harry from Draco, would not kill its true master. Harry declines the wand, breaks it, and throws the pieces away. Nineteen years later he is at King's Cross again, as a father.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The informed surrender

Interpretation: Harry's walk to the Forest is the saga's thesis performed — he is the only principal who acts on complete information, and the information says: die. The film plays it without triumph, which is the point; his courage is administrative, a correct decision executed at walking pace.

Mercy as reflex

He saves Draco Malfoy twice in one sequence — from Fiendfyre and from the battle's accounting — at the moment of maximum justification for letting him fall. Interpretation: the saga's moral physics run through this reflex; the wand that saves Harry is loyal to him because of a disarming, not a killing.

The refusal of instruments

He breaks the Elder Wand and, earlier, declines to keep the Resurrection Stone or chase the Cloak's legend. Harry's relationship to the Hallows is the anti-Voldemort: objects are tools, and the dangerous ones are refuse.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
DEFENCE & DUELLING

The Expelliarmus specialist

A decade of combat schooling distilled into one disarming spell — which, correctly aimed at the wand problem, wins the war.

THE CONNECTION

The scar as intelligence

His visions into Voldemort’s mind supply the heist’s confirmation and the diadem’s location — the enemy’s soul-shard working, fatally, both ways.

LEADERSHIP

The returning banner

His mere arrival re-founds the defence of Hogwarts: the school fights because he is in it.

WEAKNESS

The Horcrux in him

He is himself a piece of the enemy — the vulnerability no training addresses, removable only by the death he walks to.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The Horcrux as original sin

Harry carries the enemy inside him through no act of his own — the saga's darkest inheritance — and is cleansed not by effort but by submission to the death it requires.

King’s Cross

The limbo is a station: a place of departures, choices and trains — the saga's whole machinery of journeys reduced to one platform and one decision.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Victory by allegiance, not power

The final duel is won before it is fought, in a wandlore technicality earned by a mercy (Draco spared, twice) and a schoolboy disarming two films back. The archive reads this as the saga's closing argument about how wars are actually decided: by accumulated decencies whose strategic value no one could have priced at the time.

The hero who is the MacGuffin

Part 2 inverts the quest structure: the object everyone seeks is inside the seeker. Harry's arc across the film is the progressive discovery that he is the last item on his own list — a revelation the film delivers through another man's memories, so that even Harry's self-knowledge arrives as an inheritance.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

A decade in the role

Radcliffe was cast as Harry at eleven and completed the saga at twenty-one; Part 2 was filmed back-to-back with Part 1 from February 2009 to June 2010.

The epilogue reshoot

The nineteen-years-later sequence was reshot in December 2010 after the original ageing makeup leaked online and the production judged the effect uneven — the only major sequence remade after principal photography.

The film’s standing

Part 2 grossed $1.342 billion, the third-highest total in history at the time and the series' highest; it was the only Harry Potter film released in 3D and took three Academy Award nominations.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Harry's completion in Part 2 — the surrender in the Forest and the broken wand — became the saga's defining image of heroism for the generation that grew up with it: not the duel but the walk. Within the Galaxy, his page is the keystone of the 2011 set, the file to which Snape's, Dumbledore's and Voldemort's pages are all cross-referenced by the story itself.
