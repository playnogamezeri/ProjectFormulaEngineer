01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Voldemort has won everything visible by the film's midpoint: the Ministry, the school, the wand of legend in his hand. What he cannot see — and the film is the story of his not seeing it — is that his immortality has been dismantled piece by piece by schoolchildren, and that the Elder Wand he stole from a tomb answers to a chain of disarmings he never witnessed. His arc is a sovereign's audit conducted too late: each Horcrux's loss arrives as a sensation he cannot interpret, each servant's truth is one he has arranged not to hear, and his final curse is fired by a wand whose loyalty he never thought to question.

ROLE

The principal antagonist

The Dark Lord; founder and supreme leader of the Death Eaters; the war’s author.

DEFINING TRAIT

Immortality as architecture

His soul is distributed across objects and one accident — a system whose every node the film watches fail.

FATE

Killed by his own curse

With Nagini — the last Horcrux — destroyed by Neville, his Killing Curse rebounds from the Elder Wand’s true allegiance and kills him in the Great Hall’s aftermath.

Place in the story

Voldemort is the set's dark sun: Snape's double life, Dumbledore's design, Harry's shard and the wand's allegiance all orbit his ignorance. The archive files him as the saga's study of a mind that made itself unable to understand the things that kill it — love, mercy, loyalty, and other people's housekeeping.

Fiennes plays the unmaking as incomprehension rather than rage: the silken triumph of the early movements thins, scene by scene, into a man interrogating a universe that has stopped answering in his language. The archive notes the performance's discipline — Voldemort never learns, because learning is the one spell he cannot cast; Fiennes lets the audience watch the exact size of the gap.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Voldemort opens the endgame holding the Elder Wand, taken from Dumbledore's tomb, and besieging the last resistance from a position of total institutional victory. The Gringotts theft reaches him as fury and as something worse — a sensation in the architecture: a Horcrux gone. His campaign converges on Hogwarts, where the diadem waits. When the battle is joined he fights it as an audit, withdrawing to the boathouse to settle the wand's accounts: believing Severus Snape, Dumbledore's killer, to be the Elder Wand's master, he has Nagini strike Snape down — the film's most consequential misreading, since the wand's allegiance had passed to Draco Malfoy, who disarmed Dumbledore, and then to Harry, who disarmed Draco.

He grants the defenders an hour to surrender Harry, and Harry comes to him in the Forest. The Killing Curse he casts destroys the soul-shard inside the boy — and knocks Voldemort himself down, a feedback he cannot interpret. He displays Harry's 'corpse' before the castle and demands surrender; Neville defies him; Harry rises alive; the Malfoys and half his army desert. In the Great Hall's wreckage Molly Weasley kills Bellatrix, Neville beheads Nagini — the last Horcrux — and Voldemort, mortal at last without knowing the exact moment it happened, duels Harry. The Elder Wand will not kill its true master. His curse rebounds, and the architecture's architect dies by his own spell, in the ruins of the school he could not take.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The auditor of souls

Interpretation: Voldemort experiences his Horcruxes as accounts — assets lodged in vaults, snakes and boys — and the film's horror is bookkeeping: he keeps excellent records of everything except what things mean.

Contempt as blindness

He cannot model love, mercy or loyalty, so he cannot predict anyone who runs on them: Snape's decade of service, Narcissa's lie (the film's record notes the Malfoys' desertion), Neville's defiance all arrive as system errors.

The terror of the ordinary death

Everything he built was a defence against one fact — that he would die like other men. The film's ending grants him precisely that fact, by his own hand, with no witness able to call it anything else.

The age’s greatest dark wizard

The Killing Curse, the Horcrux rite, flight, legilimency: a repertoire no living wizard matches in open force.

THE HORCRUX SYSTEM

Distributed immortality

Soul-shards in the cup, the diadem, Nagini and — accidentally — Harry: redundancy as a theology.

COMMAND

The terror state

Ministry, school and an army of the frightened: institutional victory, total by the film’s midpoint.

WEAKNESS

The unmodelled world

Love, mercy, wandlore and other people’s quiet loyalties — the four forces that dismantle him, none of which he can perceive.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The distributed soul

Voldemort is the saga's essay on a self treated as property — insured, fragmented, stored — against Harry's self treated as a gift to be given away. The film's ending is the two philosophies' final audit.

The tomb-robbed wand

He takes the Elder Wand from a grave and never from a victory: the film's emblem for power seized as an object, which therefore never becomes his.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Death by clerical error

The saga's supreme villain is killed, in the end, by a chain of custody — Draco disarmed Dumbledore; Harry disarmed Draco; therefore the wand is Harry's. The archive reads this as the series' deepest joke and its thesis in one: the Dark Lord, who understood souls, prophecies and terror, is undone by paperwork he considered beneath him.

The uninterpreted sensation

Twice the film shows Voldemort feeling a Horcrux die and failing to act on the knowledge in time. His tragedy — if the word is allowed — is epistemic: he built a system that reported to a mind that had amputated the faculties needed to read the reports.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

The performance

Fiennes completed the role he began in the fourth film; the production’s record and reviews singled out the unmaking — triumph curdling into incomprehension — as the performance’s design.

The boathouse relocation

Snape’s death was staged in the boathouse — the film’s relocation of the novel’s scene, and one of the finale’s signature settings.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Voldemort's Part 2 death — by his own curse, over a wand's chain of custody, in a school he had already won — fixed the character's standing as fantasy's great study of self-defeating evil: the villain whose systems were perfect except for the world. Within the Galaxy, his page is the dark counterpart to Harry's, and the two files are best read as one argument in two bindings.
