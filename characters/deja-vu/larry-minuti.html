01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Minuti is the film's invoice. A working ATF agent and Carlin's partner, he is the investigation's ordinary professional — until the Snow White unit sends its note back to Carlin's past self, and Minuti, not Carlin, is the man who finds it. Reading a time and a place and doing what agents do, he follows it alone at night to Carroll Oerstadt's bayou shack, where the bomber murders him and burns his body. His death is the plot's hinge: it is the proof that intervention is not free, the argument Pryzwarra uses against the crossing, and the guilt Carlin carries through the window. The film gives him few scenes; the archive gives him his page, because the story's ethics are priced in his name.

ROLE

The price

Supporting; Carlin's partner and the intervention's casualty.

DEFINING TRAIT

Professional instinct

He follows the note because following it is the job — alone, at night, without waiting for the man it was addressed to. The film's tragedy of competence.

FATE

Murdered at the shack

Killed by Oerstadt and his body burned; his murder is confessed by the bomber and avenged, in the revised timeline, aboard the ferry.

Place in the story

Minuti is the film's control case for its own premise: the note was information, sent cheaply, and it killed the wrong man. Everything Carlin does afterward is governed by the lesson — interventions must be paid for in person.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Minuti works the Stumpf investigation at Carlin's side in the film's opening movement — the partner as professional furniture, the man who knows how Carlin thinks and covers the angles Carlin doesn't. When Carlin is recruited into Snow White, Minuti remains outside the secret, working the case in the ordinary present.

The unit's intervention — a note sent back through the window, telling Carlin's past self where the suspect can be found — arrives in the past without its addressee. Minuti finds it. Acting on it as any agent would, he goes alone at night to the place it names: Oerstadt's bayou shack. The bomber is there. Minuti is murdered and his body burned.

His death reorganises the film: it is the evidence that the window's use has a body count, the substance of Pryzwarra's argument against the crossing, and the guilt that Carlin names, in effect, as his second reason for going. Oerstadt confesses to the murder in custody. In the revised timeline Carlin kills Oerstadt aboard the ferry; Minuti's own fate is not shown to change, and the archive records it as the film leaves it — the one debt the crossing does not retire.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The misdelivered message

The note is the film's smallest object and its largest lesson: information sent into the past arrives in the wrong hands at the wrong hour, because the past is not a mailbox but a place where people act. Minuti is the man who acted.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

An honest record

Minuti's file is deliberately the thinnest in this batch, because the film's record of him is thin: a partner's scenes, a discovery, a death, a confession's line item. The archive declines to invent him a fuller life. What can be said on the evidence is structural, and it is considerable: he is the only character killed by the protagonists' side of the plot, and the film's ethics — intervention must be personal or not attempted — are deduced from his body.

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Craven's Minuti

Matt Craven, a veteran of procedural ensembles, gives the film its everyman agent; the production uses his familiarity deliberately — the audience has trusted this actor in uniform for decades, and the note's invoice is paid against that trust.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Minuti remains the time-travel genre's standing warning: the first casualty of a well-meant edit, and the reason the genre's heroes, ever since, are expected to go themselves.
