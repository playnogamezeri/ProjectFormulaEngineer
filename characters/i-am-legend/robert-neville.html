Robert Neville

A U.S. Army lieutenant colonel and virologist — immune, brilliant, and three years into a solitude he manages as a military operation: the patrols, the broadcast, the laboratory, the shutters — every routine a sandbag against the knowledge that he put his family on a helicopter and watched it fall.

01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Neville is the last healthy human in New York and the film's entire society: scientist, soldier, broadcaster, zookeeper of one dog and conversationalist to a store full of mannequins. His mission — cure the Krippin virus from his own immune blood — is also his penance, pursued in the city he refused to leave because leaving would mean the evacuation, and the helicopter, won. The film's question is never whether he can save the species but which ending he is in: the one where his cure requires his death, or the one where he finally understands that to the city's new citizens, he is the monster of their legend.

ROLE

The last man

Protagonist; the immune virologist working to cure the virus that ended his world.

DEFINING TRAIT

Routine as fortification

Every ritual — the noon broadcast, the shutters at dusk, the mannequin small talk — is a defence work against grief and madness, maintained at military standard.

FATE

Two endings, recorded separately

Theatrical: he dies by his own grenade, passing the cured blood to Anna — the legend of the title. Alternate: he returns the Alpha Female, apologises, and drives out of New York alive.

Place in the story

Neville is the film's whole cast for its first hour and its whole argument for its length: whether the man who survives by treating the world as a laboratory can learn, in time, that the laboratory has become a society.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

In 2009, Dr. Alice Krippin's re-engineered measles virus — a cancer cure — mutates. It kills 90% of humanity; most of the rest become Darkseekers. Neville, immune, stays in Manhattan through the quarantine and puts Zoe and Marley on a helicopter out; it collides with another aircraft in the chaos, and he watches them die. Three years later he lives by routine: hunting deer through Times Square with Sam, experimenting on infected rats, broadcasting daily at noon from the South Street Seaport for any survivor, sealing the house at dusk.

Sam chases a deer into a Darkseeker hive and survives; Neville sets a snare with his own immune blood and captures a female Darkseeker for his compound trials. The Alpha Male exposes himself to sunlight trying to follow — behaviour Neville, shaken, files as impossible. His treatment of the female appears to fail. The next day he finds a mannequin he knows — Fred — inexplicably moved to a street across town; raving at it, he is caught in a snare trap like his own, strung up and knocked unconscious until dusk.

Freed at sunset, he is attacked by infected dogs; Sam saves him and is bitten. Neville carries her home and, as the infection takes her, strangles his only companion with his own hands while singing to her. That night he goes out in grief and rage to run down Darkseekers in his SUV and is nearly killed — rescued at dawn by Anna and Ethan, survivors who heard his broadcast on their way to the colony at Bethel, Vermont.

Anna's faith irritates his science; her account of being led to him unsettles it. The next night the Darkseekers besiege the house. In the basement laboratory, Neville discovers his test subject is reverting to human — the compound works. What happens next is the film's famous fork, recorded in the archive's ending note below.

THEATRICAL ENDING: Neville draws a vial of the cured blood, gives it to Anna, seals her and Ethan in a coal chute, and charges the Alpha with a grenade, dying to buy their escape. Anna delivers the cure to Bethel; her voice-over names the survivors his legacy — his legend. ALTERNATE ENDING: Neville sees the Alpha trace a butterfly on the glass, understands it matches the female's tattoo — she is not a specimen but a mate — puts down his gun, returns her, and apologises; the Darkseekers leave. Neville, Anna and Ethan drive out of New York toward Vermont.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Discipline as grief management

Neville's routines are not habits but fortifications, each one answering a specific memory. When Sam dies, the fortifications are abandoned in a single night — the film measures his love by the speed of the collapse.

The scientist's blind spot

He reads the Alpha's sunlight exposure as an impossibility rather than as data, because his model of the Darkseekers — mindless, unattached — is the model that licenses his experiments. The alternate ending is his correction of the model; the theatrical ending is his payment under it.

Humour as a pulse check

The mannequin conversations and the Shrek recitations are the film's diagnostics: as long as Neville is performing normality for an audience of fiberglass, some part of him still believes in an audience.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
SCIENCE

Virology at the end of the world

He designs compound trials alone, maintains a laboratory through three years of collapse, and produces a working cure from his own blood.

MILITARY

Soldier's tradecraft

Patrol discipline, fortification, marksmanship and the tactical sense that keeps one man alive in a city of predators.

ENDURANCE

Three years of solitude

His rarest ability is administrative: he has kept a broadcast schedule, a research programme and a self, unassisted, for a thousand days.

LIMIT

He cannot leave

New York is his post and his grave-site both; the immunity that qualifies him to save the species also sentences him to the scene of his loss.

Anna and Ethan in his house; the cure working in his laboratory; the Darkseekers at the glass.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The butterfly

Marley's word, Anna's sign, the Alpha Female's tattoo and the shape the Alpha cracks into the glass: the film's one symbol, assigned to a child, a believer and a monster in turn — whoever reads it correctly survives the ending they are in.

The mannequins

Neville's congregation of fiberglass is the film's most delicate instrument: his sanity is measured by how well he treats the pretend people, and the plot turns when one of them is moved by real ones.

Light and shutter

His house seals at dusk like an eyelid; the film's whole cosmology is in the shutters — what his species calls night is the other species' day.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The title, in both endings

Matheson's novel ends with Neville realising he is the legend — the monster of the new society's folklore. The alternate ending preserves that recognition; the theatrical ending replaces it with a legend of the older kind, a saviour's. The archive records both because the film, uniquely, released both — and its announced sequel follows the alternate one.

The experiment problem

Neville's trials are conducted on a captive person, under a model that defines her as an animal. The film never indicts him in dialogue; it indicts the model, by giving the Alpha a motive — a mate — that the model cannot express. His page is where the archive files the film's real horror: not the siege, but the laboratory.

Performance of one

Smith's solo hour is the film's foundation: cast as the most companionable of stars and then deprived of anyone to be companionable to, he makes Neville's deterioration legible as the withdrawal of an audience.

Neville sings Marley's song to Sam as he kills her — the performance the whole film is remembered by.

The Alpha battering the laboratory glass into a butterfly shape: the other society's sentence, finally read.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Two endings

The film's original ending — the alternate one — was replaced after test audiences disliked it; several scenes were altered before release. The alternate ending was later restored on home video, and the sequel announced in 2022 follows the alternate ending rather than the theatrical one.

A third adaptation

The film is the third screen adaptation of Richard Matheson's 1954 novel, after The Last Man on Earth (1964) and The Omega Man (1971), and diverges from the novel most sharply in its theatrical ending.

Smith and the dogs

Sam was played by German Shepherds Abbey and Kona; Smith adopted Abbey after production, a much-reported footnote the archive includes because the film's central relationship was, evidently, mutual.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Neville became the defining portrait of the companionable apocalypse: the last man not as a misanthrope but as a host whose guests have died. The film's first hour — one actor, one dog, one empty city — remains its benchmark achievement.

The two endings made the film a permanent case study in what test screenings can remove: the version in which the hero understands, rather than defeats, the other side. The franchise's own future has since ruled in the alternate ending's favour.
