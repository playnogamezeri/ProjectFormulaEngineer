01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Scotty Braddock boards the bus already beaten: a senior who watched a freshman, Double D Davis, take his place in the championship game, riding home inside a victory that is not his. When the Creeper singles out six students by scent, Scotty performs the siege's coldest act of reasoning — he accepts the creature's premise. If it wants specific people, he argues, give it the marked ones and let the rest live: selection, embraced as policy. The bus refuses him, and the refusal is the film's moral centre. Scotty is taken in the field flight anyway — the creature collecting its marked man without the courtesy of the bargain he offered — and his page records the siege's darkest lesson: the triage he proposed would have purchased nothing, because the predator never negotiated.

ROLE

The internal antagonist

Principal supporting; the siege's fifth column — the argument for surrender, made from inside the fortress.

DEFINING TRAIT

Resentment as logic

His triage proposal wears the costume of reason; its engine is the bench — a senior who has already been selected against, extending the principle.

FATE

Taken in the field

Seized by the Creeper during the flight across the field; his death is not shown, and the record keeps the limit.

Place in the story

Scotty's page is the archive's record of the siege's moral front: the bus is threatened from the windows by appetite and from the aisle by arithmetic, and the film is honest that the arithmetic is the harder enemy. He is the ensemble's necessary darkness.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Scotty's siege begins before the creature arrives: he is the senior who lost his starting place to the freshman Double D in the championship game, and he rides home separated from the celebration, his girlfriend Rhonda unable to pull him back into it. The bus's disablement and the adults' abduction strand him inside a second selection — and when Minxie's briefing and the Creeper's window rite mark six students by scent, Scotty among them, he draws the conclusion his resentment has prepared: the creature's list should be honoured. Give it the ones it wants, he argues, and the rest go home.

The bus refuses — Rhonda among the refusers — and the siege proceeds without the bargain. When the students abandon the bus and run for help across the field, the Creeper takes its marked in its own order: Jake is killed, and Scotty is taken in the flight. The record shows the taking, not a death; this page keeps the distinction the film keeps.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Selection, internalised

Interpretation: Scotty has just lived through a selection that priced him as surplus, and his proposal is that experience universalised — if the world runs on lists, the rational move is to be on the right side of one. The film's counter-argument is the bus itself: a team, refusing to price its own.

The sore tooth

His taunting, sulking presence in the ensemble's early scenes establishes the siege's pre-existing fracture: the creature does not create the bus's divisions, it scents them.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The list

Interpretation: Scotty is the film's argument that the true horror of a selection is not the selector but the selected's temptation to administer it for him. The Creeper scents six; only a human proposes to deliver them.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the aisle antagonist

The analysis the archive records: a siege narrative needs pressure inside the walls, and Scotty is engineered for it — his grievance is real, his logic is clean, and his proposal is the film's one unforgivable sentence. The ensemble's refusal, not the harpoon, is the story's moral climax; the harpoon merely ends the night the refusal made endurable.
