01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Clark is the film's still centre: a man whose defining activity for thirty-three years has been not doing what he can do. Born Kal-El — the first natural birth on Krypton in centuries, his cells infused with the Codex carrying his species' entire genetic future — he is raised in Kansas by Jonathan and Martha Kent under a doctrine of concealment: the world is not ready, and his father's risk assessment rules his childhood so completely that Jonathan dies in a tornado rather than let his son be seen saving him. The adult Clark wanders under aliases, performing rescues — an oil rig, a bus in his boyhood — and vanishing. The scout ship in the Arctic gives him his father's recorded ghost and his purpose; Zod's arrival takes the choice out of his hands. He surrenders to humanity, then to Zod; escapes; fights for Smallville and Metropolis; destroys the World Engine; and at the end, in a train station, does the thing the character's tradition forbids — he kills Zod, the last other Kryptonian, to save a trapped family, and screams at what it costs. He ends the film at the Daily Planet, in glasses, smiling: the double life, begun on his own terms at last.

ROLE

Protagonist; Superman

Krypton's last natural son and Earth's adopted one — the Codex's vessel, the scout ship's heir, and humanity's self-appointed protector.

DEFINING TRAIT

Restraint as a biography

His heroism before the suit is a pattern of intervention and flight; the film is the story of the restraint ending — publicly, and at a price.

FATE

Revealed and recommitted

Zod killed by his hand, the Kryptonian revival ended with him; installed at the Daily Planet, in the open secret of a double life.

Place in the story

Clark is the film's argument about power and permission: raised by a father who feared the world's answer, he becomes the answer himself. His page records the man of steel as the Galaxy's film page frames him — restraint's child, learning in one week what thirty-three years of hiding could not teach.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Kal-El is born as his world ends: Krypton, destabilised by the mining of its core, ignores Jor-El's warnings; Jor-El steals the Codex — the genetics registry of his entire race — and infuses it into his newborn son, the first natural birth in centuries, and he and Lara launch the child toward Earth. Zod kills Jor-El during his coup; the ship escapes. In Kansas, Jonathan and Martha Kent find the child and name him Clark. The yellow sun makes him a god in instalments — senses that overwhelm him as a boy (Martha teaches him to make the world small, focusing on her voice as on an island), strength that must never be shown. Jonathan's doctrine governs everything: hide, wait, the world is not ready for what you are. When teenage Clark saves a school bus, the doctrine holds; when a tornado takes Jonathan — the father waving his son back, refusing rescue that would expose him — the doctrine collects its price, and Clark's guilt becomes his second inheritance.

The adult Clark drifts: oil rigs, bars, aliases — rescuing an oil-rig crew from a fire, saving a waitress's dignity at the cost of a trucker's rig, always moving on. In the Arctic he infiltrates the military's excavation of an ancient Kryptonian scout ship and activates it with his father's key: the recorded consciousness of Jor-El tells him his name, his people's extinction, and his purpose — to be a bridge and a hope. A reporter, Lois Lane, has tracked the same mystery to the same ice; Clark saves her life there, and she follows the trail to Smallville and to Martha's door — and, hearing what Jonathan's sacrifice meant, kills her own story to protect him.

Zod's ultimatum ends the hiding. Clark surrenders to the military — in chains he permits, making the terms gentle — and is handed to the Black Zero with Lois. Aboard, Jax-Ur draws his blood: the Codex is in his cells, and Zod no longer needs him alive or willing. He escapes with his father's ghost's help, rescues Lois from her falling escape pod, and fights Zod's soldiers through Smallville — Faora and Nam-Ek, professionals against his brawling power, with his mother threatened at the farm. Then the World Engine descends on Metropolis, its counterpart working in the Indian Ocean: Clark destroys the ocean engine while the military flies his father's ship — the phantom-drive vessel — against the Black Zero, sending Zod's crew back into the Phantom Zone at the cost of Colonel Hardy and Dr. Hamilton's lives. Zod, his people's revival gone, fights Clark across a collapsing Metropolis out of pure revenge, and corners a family in the train station with his heat vision. Clark, holding him, begs — and breaks his neck. The scream that follows is the film's verdict on its own climax. Afterwards: the military tolerates him on his terms, Martha visits a rebuilt farm, and Clark Kent, in glasses, starts work at the Daily Planet — Lois Lane welcoming him with a smile that knows everything.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Restraint as character

Clark's thirty-three years of hiding are not cowardice but obedience — a son's fidelity to a frightened father's doctrine. Interpretation: the film's Clark is defined by the tension between two fathers' instructions, Jonathan's hide and Jor-El's shine, and his arc is the week the second father's voice finally outweighs the first's — after the first has died enforcing his own.

Guilt as fuel

He carries Jonathan's death as a debt and spends the film servicing it in secret rescues. His surrender to Zod is the debt going public: the boy who let his father die rather than be seen will let himself be chained and handed over rather than let strangers die for his secret.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
KRYPTONIAN PHYSIOLOGY

The yellow sun's catalogue

Flight, strength on a civil-engineering scale, invulnerability, heat vision, super-senses — the full suite, still being discovered by its owner as the film proceeds; he flies for the first time in the Arctic, joyfully, in private.

THE CODEX

A species in his cells

His body carries Krypton's genetic future — the film's true contested territory, extractable by Jax-Ur's science whether Clark consents or not.

WEAKNESS

Krypton's air and Krypton's people

Aboard the Black Zero, Kryptonian atmosphere strips his powers and sickens him; and his only physical equals are Zod's soldiers, whose professional training initially outclasses his power.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The bridge

Jor-El's commission makes Clark a symbol by design — the "S" is a Kryptonian glyph for hope, and the film builds him as the meeting point of two worlds: Krypton's future in his cells, Earth's values in his upbringing.

The raised hand

Jonathan's wave-back in the tornado is the film's defining image of the old order: love as concealment. Clark's whole arc is the journey from that hand to the open sky over Metropolis.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The killing of Zod

The climax broke the character's screen tradition — Superman does not kill — and the film stages it as violation rather than triumph: no time, a family in the beam's path, a neck broken by the hero's hands and a scream after. The analysis the archive files is that the scene is the film's thesis about its own realism: a Superman introduced as a frightened secret cannot be kept innocent by genre rules, and the film makes him — and the audience — carry the act instead of excusing it. It remains the most debated scene in the modern Superman canon.

Two fathers, one son

The film's structure is a custody dispute between ghosts: Jonathan's risk assessment against Jor-El's commission, with Clark's thirty-three years as the contested childhood. Zod is the third claimant — Krypton's state, come to repossess the child and the Codex together — and the climax resolves all three claims at once: Clark kills Krypton's claim, having already buried Kansas's, and keeps only the commission he chose himself.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Snyder and Goyer

The film — directed by Zack Snyder, written by David S. Goyer, produced by Christopher Nolan among others — rebooted the Superman series as the first instalment of DC's shared universe, earning $670 million worldwide on a $225 million budget and finishing as the ninth-highest-grossing film of 2013, to mixed reviews.

Cavill's casting

Cavill, who had famously missed out on the role in an earlier era of the franchise, was cast as the first non-American Superman — the production betting the reboot on a performance of restraint rather than on the character's established screen charm.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This Clark Kent founded the DC Extended Universe and reset the screen Superman for a generation: graver, guiltier, and mortal in the one way that matters — he can be made to kill.
