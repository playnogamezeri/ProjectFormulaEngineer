01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Harry begins the film as the wizarding world's most famous infant and its most ignorant citizen: a boy raised in a cupboard by the Dursleys, who learns at eleven that he is a wizard, that his parents were murdered by Lord Voldemort, and that the killing curse aimed at him rebounded and destroyed its caster — leaving a lightning scar and a legend he never asked for. His first year at Hogwarts is a double education: in magic, and in the story everyone else already knows. He finds the friends who become his life's structure, refuses a rival's offered hand, misidentifies his enemy for a full school year, and descends beneath the school to protect the Philosopher's Stone — where he discovers the year's real curriculum: that the Stone comes only to the one who wants it without using it, and that his mother's sacrifice, carried in his very skin, is a weapon no dark lord can touch.

ROLE

The Boy Who Lived

The saga's centre and this film's: a famous orphan learning his own biography in public, one revelation per term.

DEFINING TRAIT

Loyalty past reason

He chooses his people instantly and entirely — Ron on the train, Hermione at the troll, the school at the trapdoor — and the choices organise the whole plot.

FATE

The Stone kept, the year survived

Quirrell dead by the protection in Harry's skin, Voldemort fled, the Stone destroyed by agreement; Harry ends the year with a House Cup and a home he chose.

Place in the story

Harry's page is the archive's record of the saga's foundation stone: the cupboard, the scar, the train, the Mirror and the trapdoor, filed at the scale the film gives them — a boy's first year, on which everything later is built.

Archive scope

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Harry's biography opens with a delivery: on the night after Voldemort's fall, Dumbledore, McGonagall and Hagrid leave the orphaned infant on the Dursleys' doorstep at Privet Drive, with a letter. Ten years of the cupboard under the stairs follow — a household servant's childhood, his cousin Dudley's bullying, and a family policy of total silence about his parents, his world and himself. The silence breaks as his eleventh birthday approaches: letters from Hogwarts flood the house despite Vernon's escalating war against them — boarded slots, burned post, finally exile to a hut on an island — and on his birthday itself Hagrid comes through the door with the truth entire: Harry is a wizard; his parents were murdered by the dark wizard Lord Voldemort; and Voldemort's killing curse, aimed at the infant Harry, rebounded and destroyed its caster, leaving only the scar. In Diagon Alley the fame lands on him — a bank vault of inherited gold, a wand whose phoenix-feather core makes it brother to Voldemort's own — and at the Leaky Cauldron, strangers shake the hand of the Boy Who Lived.

The train to Hogwarts is where his life actually starts. He meets Ron Weasley and shares his sweets; meets Hermione Granger, brisk and encyclopaedic; and refuses Draco Malfoy's offered friendship in the school's entrance hall, choosing his company by instinct — the archive files the refusal among the year's most consequential gestures. The Sorting Hat offers him Slytherin and he begs it for anywhere else; Gryffindor takes him. The year then runs on his curiosity and his nerve: a flying lesson turns into a Seeker's appointment; a midnight escapade finds Fluffy, the three-headed dog guarding a trapdoor on the forbidden third floor; the Halloween troll makes the trio a unit, with Hermione lying to the teachers to protect the boys who saved her. At his first Quidditch match his broom is jinxed — and the trio fix on Snape as the jinxer, the year's great misidentification, which Hagrid's loose tongue feeds by naming Nicolas Flamel, the Stone's maker. Christmas gives him his father's invisibility cloak, sent anonymously, and the cloak gives him the Mirror of Erised, where he sees his parents alive and whole — and sits before it night after night until Dumbledore gently closes the door: it does not do to dwell on dreams.

The spring assembles the plot. Hagrid wins a dragon egg from a hooded stranger who asked, too carefully, how one gets past a three-headed dog — the trio read the trap being set in real time. In detention in the Forbidden Forest, Harry is rescued by the centaur Firenze from a hooded figure drinking a unicorn's blood: Voldemort, sustaining a half-life, waiting on the Stone to give him a body. Convinced Snape is about to steal it for his master, the trio go through the trapdoor themselves — Devil's Snare, flying keys, a chess game that costs Ron his consciousness, a logic of potions — and Harry goes on alone into the last chamber, where the year's account is corrected in full. It is not Snape. It is Quirrell — the stammering professor, who let the troll in, who jinxed the broom while Snape counter-cursed it, who wears, beneath the turban, Voldemort's face on the back of his head. Before the Mirror, Harry — who wants the Stone only to keep it from Voldemort — finds it in his pocket, Dumbledore's enchantment answering the one desire it was built for. Quirrell seizes him, and Lily Potter's sacrifice does the rest: the protection in Harry's skin burns the servant's hands, and Quirrell crumbles to dust while Voldemort's spirit flees. Harry wakes in the hospital wing to the year's settlements — the Stone destroyed by Dumbledore and Flamel's agreement, his friends safe — and at the feast, Dumbledore's last-minute points, sixty of them Harry's for pure nerve, hand Gryffindor the House Cup. He boards the train home having acquired, in one year, a world, a family of friends, and an enemy who will be back.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Instinct over instruction

Interpretation: Harry's great decisions are all instantaneous — the refused handshake, the troll rescue, the descent — and the film ratifies the instinct every time. The archive reads his intuition as the trait the plot is built to reward: he is wrong about Snape all year and right about everything that matters, which the film treats as the correct ratio for an eleven-year-old hero.

Fame, declined

He never once trades on the legend, and his Mirror-image is not glory but family: the archive files the Erised scene as the character's thesis — what the most famous boy in the world wants is the one thing fame cannot return.

Rule-bent, not rule-free

He breaks rules constantly and lies rarely; his transgressions are all in service of someone. The archive notes the film's care here: Harry's disobedience is a form of loyalty, priced accordingly at the feast — sixty points, awarded for exactly the qualities the rules exist to restrain.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
FLYING

The Seeker's gift

A natural on a broom at first touch — the youngest Seeker in a century, appointed on a single unauthorized flight: the film's purest statement of his inheritance from his father.

NERVE

The trapdoor, descended

He goes down knowing the school's protections are already beaten and his suspect is ahead of him: the feast's citation — pure nerve and outstanding courage — is the archive's as well.

THE PROTECTION

His mother's sacrifice

Lily's self-sacrifice lives in his blood and skin: it destroyed Voldemort's body in 1981 and burns Quirrell's hands in the last chamber. It is not a skill; it is a fact about him, and the film's whole theology in one fact.

INSTINCT FOR PEOPLE

The train's choices

Ron chosen, Draco refused, Hermione defended: his personnel judgments are the plot's foundation and never once wrong.

WEAKNESS

The Mirror

Erised nearly keeps him — night after night before an image of the family he lost. His longing is the film's one successful trap for him, and Dumbledore's gentlest rescue.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The scar

Interpretation: the lightning bolt is the saga's whole premise worn on the skin — the mark of the curse that failed, of the mother who interposed herself, and of the enemy who made him famous by failing to kill him. The archive reads it as the film's thesis about survival: Harry's fame is a wound, and the film never lets the audience forget the order of those facts.

The Mirror of Erised

Interpretation: desire, shown backwards — the Mirror gives Harry his parents and gives Quirrell nothing, because the Stone answers only the desire that does not want to use it. The archive files the Mirror as the film's moral instrument: it sorts the cast by what they want, more exactly than the Hat.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the legend as a boy

The film's structural problem is that its hero arrives pre-famous, and its solution is Harry's ordinariness: every set-piece is arranged so that the legend's heir wins by being a loyal, brave, ignorant eleven-year-old rather than by being the Boy Who Lived. The analysis the archive records is that the saga's entire later weight rests on this first film's insistence — the scar explains nothing about him; the train explains everything.

The misidentification as curriculum

Harry is wrong about the year's villain for its whole length, and the film treats the error as education rather than failure: he acts on the best reading available to a child, protects the Stone anyway, and learns the adult lesson — that dislike is not evidence — at the moment it matters least to the outcome and most to him.

Key Moments

"You're a wizard, Harry"

The hut on the island, the birthday, the truth: the cupboard's decade ended in one sentence.

The refused handshake

Draco's hand extended in the entrance hall, and Harry's quiet no: the saga's allegiances, founded.

The troll

Two boys, a levitated club, and a friendship sealed by a lie told to teachers: the trio, founded in a bathroom.

The Mirror

His parents smiling out of the glass, night after night: the film's still centre.

Neville's ten points

The feast's final award, for standing up to his friends: the Cup decided by the boy Harry stepped over — the archive records Harry's applause as the year's last lesson, learned.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Daniel Radcliffe

Radcliffe was cast after Columbus saw him in the BBC's David Copperfield; the archive notes the production's gamble — the saga's centre carried by an eleven-year-old with almost no film experience — and its total vindication in this first instalment's reception.

The title

The film is Philosopher's Stone in most of the world and Sorcerer's Stone in the United States, India and the Philippines — the archive files the Galaxy's title under the original, and the character is identical under either stone.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This Harry — the cupboard, the train, the Mirror, the trapdoor — is the foundation every later entry builds on: the saga's eight films are, in the archive's reading, footnotes to the choices made in this one. The Galaxy's records file his first year as the run's exemplar of the foundation instalment: everything introduced, nothing wasted, the hero defined by his friends and his refusal of the Stone's use.

The protection in his skin, established here, is the saga's deepest mechanic — the archive notes that the whole war, past and future, turns on the fact this film states in a hospital wing: love, in this world, is a defensive technology, and Harry carries it in his blood.
