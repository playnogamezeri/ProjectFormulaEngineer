01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Voldemort enters the saga as a rumour with a body count. The most feared dark wizard of the age, he murdered James and Lily Potter in 1981 and aimed his killing curse at their infant son — and the curse rebounded, destroying his body and reducing him to a spirit, less than a ghost, sustained by will and, in this film, by unicorn blood and a servant's skull. The wizarding world believes him gone or powerless; the film knows better. Riding the back of Professor Quirrell's head beneath a turban, he directs the year's whole campaign for the Philosopher's Stone — the one object that can restore him to a body of his own — and comes within one boy's pocket of success. His defeat, when it comes, is the same fact that destroyed him in 1981, encountered again: Lily Potter's sacrifice lives in her son's skin, his servant burns at the touch, and the spirit flees the ruin, bodiless again, to wait for another servant and another year.

ROLE

The wraith in the turban

The campaign's author and its prize-seeker: bodiless, patient, and entirely dependent on a servant he will spend without hesitation.

DEFINING TRAIT

Power as the only good

His philosophy is stated in the last chamber without ornament: there is no good and evil, only power, and those too weak to seek it.

FATE

Fled, bodiless again

His servant dead by the protection in Harry's skin, the Stone lost and destroyed: the spirit escapes the last chamber as it entered the film — a rumour with a body count, waiting.

Place in the story

Voldemort's page is the archive's record of the saga's antagonist in his weakest and most instructive state: the archive files him here not at his power's height but at his nature's clearest — a voice on the back of a servant's head, explaining exactly what he is to the boy he failed to kill.

He is what rides other people.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Voldemort's biography in this film is told backwards, in other people's memories, and the archive assembles it at the film's own depth. At his height he was the age's terror: a dark wizard of genius and absolute ruthlessness, gathering followers, murdering opponents, and moving — for reasons this film deliberately withholds, on Dumbledore's deferral — against the Potter family. In 1981 he killed James and Lily Potter and turned his wand on their one-year-old son. Lily's death was not incidental: she died interposing herself for the child, and her sacrifice left a protection in the boy's blood that the killing curse met and could not pass. The curse rebounded; Voldemort's body was destroyed; and what remained of him — a spirit of undiminished will and no substance — fled into exile, eventually to the forests of Albania, where the wizarding world's rumour lost him.

The film's present finds him reduced and patient. In Albania he was found by Quirrell, a travelling scholar whose ambition made him an offer the spirit accepted: a tenancy. Voldemort rides the back of his servant's head, hidden by the turban, sustained at a half-life by unicorn blood his servant drinks in the Forbidden Forest — the archive records the economy without flinching: the most feared wizard of the age, kept alive by a teacher's throatfuls of stolen blood, in a school, at his enemy's board. From the turban he directs the campaign entire: the Gringotts attempt, the pub interrogation, the troll, the broom, the gauntlet — his servant's hands doing the work his own cannot. His objective is single and total: the Philosopher's Stone, whose elixir can manufacture him a body, and with a body, the resumption of everything.

The last chamber is his closest approach and his second destruction in miniature. The Mirror baffles his servant; Voldemort's voice supplies the method — use the boy — and when Harry stands before the glass with the Stone answering his unselfish desire into his pocket, the turban comes off and the film gives its antagonist his thesis in person: he offers Harry power, and his parents back, and states his creed entire — no good, no evil, only power and those too weak to seek it. Harry refuses. Ordered to seize the Stone, Quirrell lays hands on the boy and meets 1981 again: the protection in Harry's skin burns the servant's flesh, and Quirrell crumbles to dust in his master's service while Voldemort watches from the back of the dying head. The spirit rises, passes through Harry in a rush of cold, and flees the chamber — bodiless, Stone-less, and unbowed, to possess what he can and wait. The archive closes the file where the film does: the war's author is at large, at his weakest, and entirely undefeated in the only faculty he values, which is his will to return.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The creed, unornamented

Interpretation: Voldemort is the rare antagonist who states his philosophy plainly and means it without remainder — power is the only good, weakness the only sin. The archive reads the last chamber's speech as the saga's founding definition of its evil: not cruelty for pleasure, but a metaphysics in which other people are instruments or obstacles, delivered by a voice from the back of an instrument's head.

Patience at the bottom

Reduced to a whisper and a tenancy, he does not rage at his state; he administers it — a campaign run through a servant, a half-life rationed in unicorn blood. The archive files the patience as the truly frightening trait: the will that survived its own body's destruction is not in a hurry, and hurrying is for creatures with lifespans.

Contempt for the instrument

He spends Quirrell — his host, his hands, his only asset — without a syllable of regret at the moment of contact. The archive records the spending as the creed's proof: to Voldemort, the servant was always a vehicle, and vehicles are not mourned.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
SURVIVAL

The will that outlived the body

His supreme feat precedes the film: a spirit persisting after its body's destruction, sustained by will alone for a decade. The archive counts it the saga's darkest miracle and its premise.

POSSESSION

The tenancy of Quirrell

He rides a living host, shares its skull, directs its campaign and speaks through its mouth: possession as administration, maintained for a school year undetected by the age's greatest wizard.

COMMAND

A campaign run from a turban

Every attack on the Stone is his design executed by his servant: strategy, patience and personnel management from inside a piece of headwear.

KNOWLEDGE

The Mirror, read correctly

He alone in the last chamber understands the Mirror's terms at once — that the boy, not the servant, is its key — and his "use the boy" is the campaign's sharpest order. The archive notes that even his defeat there is a defeat of physics, not of reading: he understood everything except the protection, which no one alive understood but Dumbledore.

WEAKNESS

The protection he cannot model

Twice in the record, Lily Potter's sacrifice defeats him — his body in 1981, his servant in the last chamber — and the film gives no sign he understands the mechanism even after the second lesson. Love, as a defensive technology, is outside his metaphysics, and the archive files the blindness as his defining limitation: the one force in the world his creed cannot price.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The face on the back of the head

Interpretation: the saga's founding image of evil is a parasite's — the archive reads the design as the character's whole metaphysics made visible: Voldemort does not confront the world; he rides it, facing backwards, whispering. Power, in his creed, is always someone else's body.

Unicorn blood

Interpretation: the purest creature in the Forest, bled to sustain the foulest — the archive files the image as the film's price-list for his survival: his half-life is purchased in innocence, by the throatful, and the curse on the drinking is billed to his servant. Even his upkeep is outsourced.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the thesis stated by its villain

Voldemort's last-chamber speech is the film's philosophical climax — the creed against which the whole story is the counter-argument. The analysis the archive records is that the first instalment is structured as a debate he loses on evidence: he offers power and parents; the boy refuses; the mother's love, which his creed prices at nothing, burns his servant to dust in front of him. The film's verdict is delivered not in dialogue but in physics.

The weakest state as the clearest portrait

Later entries will give him a body, an army and a ministry; this film gives him a turban and a throatful of stolen blood, and the archive judges the portrait the more instructive: stripped of every resource but will and a servant, he is entirely himself — patient, parasitic and incapable of pricing the one force that keeps beating him.

10 / KEY MOMENTS

Key Moments

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
The rebound, retold

Hagrid's firelit account of 1981: a curse, a scar, a vanished body — the saga's founding event, delivered as a birthday present of truth.

The Forest figure

A hooded shape at a unicorn's throat, rising from the blood as the boy watches: his first appearance to Harry's waking eyes, and the film's coldest image.

"Use the boy"

The whisper from the turban before the Mirror: the campaign's sharpest order, and its author revealed as the chamber's true occupant.

The unwrapping

The face turning on the back of the servant's head: "There is no good and evil — there is only power": the creed, in person at last.

The temptation refused

His parents offered back, power offered with them — and an eleven-year-old's no: the debate, settled by the evidence of the next minute.

The flight through Harry

The spirit rising from its servant's dust and passing through the boy: the war's author, at large in the walls of the school he failed to rob.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Ian Hart and Richard Bremmer

Voldemort in this film is a collaboration the archive keeps straight: Hart voices him from beneath the turban; Bremmer provides the hooded figure's physical form in flashback and Forest; the face itself is Industrial Light & Magic's. Ralph Fiennes's embodiment belongs to the fourth film onward and is filed on those pages.

The withheld motive

The film deliberately does not say why Voldemort hunted the Potters — Dumbledore defers the answer to Harry in the hospital wing — and the archive records the deferral as the production's fidelity to the saga's architecture: the first film's Voldemort is a creed and a consequence, with his reasons held for later entries.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This film's Voldemort — bodiless, patient, riding a servant, beaten twice by the same mother's sacrifice — is the saga's foundation portrait of its antagonist: everything later entries add is scale. The Galaxy's records file him here as the run's exemplar of evil as tenancy: a will that survives by being carried, and a creed refuted, in his first appearance, by the physics of love.

His open file — why the Potters, why the boy — is the saga's longest, opened by Dumbledore's deferral in a hospital wing and carried, deliberately unanswered, out of this film entire.
