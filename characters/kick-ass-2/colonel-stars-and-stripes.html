01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

The Colonel is the sequel's proof that its world contains grace. Sal Bertolinni spent his working life as a hitman for the D'Amico organisation — the empire Chris now inherits — and came out the other side born again, determined to spend the same skills in the opposite direction. He founds Justice Forever, the team Dave's example inspired, and runs it on a penitent's rules: the team serves before it fights — soup kitchens, patrols, a missing child — and it does not kill. He takes Dave in as the movement's founder and gives the amateur something the first film never had: a commander with a code. His decency makes him the war's necessary first atrocity: when Chris's campaign opens, Mother Russia beheads the Colonel in his own home, and his head becomes the villain's opening statement. He is in the film briefly and organises all of it — Justice Forever's survival, its rules and its final stand are his posthumous work.

ROLE

Founder and commander, Justice Forever

The movement's conscience: a reformed professional who gives amateur heroism a structure, a code and a martyr.

DEFINING TRAIT

Penitence, operationalised

His faith is not backstory but method — service first, no killing, the neighbourhood before the war. He runs redemption like a unit.

FATE

Beheaded by Mother Russia

Chris's opening atrocity: the war declared on the best man in it, in his own home.

Place in the story

The Colonel is the sequel's moral centre of gravity — the one character who has been on the other side and priced it. His page records the founder as the Galaxy's film page frames him: the film's most sincere creation, whose rules outlive him as his team's inheritance.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Sal Bertolinni's first career was murder: a hitman for the D'Amicos, the organisation whose heir now declares himself a supervillain. His second began with conversion — born again, he looked at what Dave Lizewski's example had started in the city and gave it what inspiration cannot give itself: organisation. Justice Forever, under his command, is a team with a doctrine. Its members serve the neighbourhood — soup kitchens, street patrols, the search for a missing child named Tommy, whose parents serve in the ranks — before they fight anyone, and the Colonel's standing rule is that the team does not kill. When Kick-Ass himself joins, the Colonel receives him as the movement's founder and puts him to work in order: service first, then the brothel raid, the team's escalation into real enforcement.

His past is the war's irony: the man who once killed for the D'Amicos now commands the team Chris D'Amico must destroy. When Chris's campaign opens, it opens on the Colonel — Mother Russia comes to his home and beheads him, and Chris taunts Justice Forever with the murder publicly. The atrocity does the Colonel's posthumous work: it converts a service organisation into an army, and his rules — the code he built — are what the team carries into the final battle, standing down afterwards into ordinary service exactly as he would have ordered. He is the film's shortest major life and its longest shadow.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The penitent professional

The Colonel's serenity is a working man's: he has done the arithmetic of his first career and organised his second as its answer. Interpretation: he is the only character in either film who has fully processed what violence costs, which is why his rules are practical rather than pious.

Command as care

He leads by feeding people — the team's first missions are soup and patrols. His authority in the ranks is paternal without softness: a commander who has decided that protection is a service industry.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

Grace in a cynical world

The Colonel symbolises the sequel's counter-thesis: in a film of brands, tantrums and performances, he is sincere — a hitman whose redemption is not a costume but a roster of soup kitchens. His flag-name is worn without irony, the film's one unironic patriot.

The martyr the movement needed

His murder is the story's hinge: movements are founded twice, once by inspiration and once by loss. Dave founded Justice Forever by example; the Colonel refounds it by dying for it.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The smallest role, the longest shadow

Carrey's Colonel is on screen briefly and organises the entire film — the team's doctrine, Dave's education and the war's moral stakes are all his. The analysis the archive files is that the film uses him as its control case for redemption: set beside Chris (the heir who learned nothing) and Mindy (the weapon learning personhood), the Colonel is the finished article — proof the film's world permits a man to become his opposite, priced in full.

The Carrey controversy

On 24 June 2013, weeks before release, Carrey withdrew his support for the film, citing its violence in the wake of the Sandy Hook Elementary School shooting; Mark Millar replied publicly that a film called Kick-Ass 2 had to do what it says on the tin, while insisting the film concentrates on the consequences of violence rather than the violence itself. The Colonel — the film's apostle of consequence — became, in his actor, its real-world argument: the page records the dispute as part of the character's history, since no other figure in the film embodies its stakes so exactly.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Cast in September 2012

Carrey — a declared fan of the first film — was cast as the Colonel in September 2012, the production's coup: its biggest name in its most sincere part.

The withdrawal

Carrey filmed the role a month before Sandy Hook, as he noted publicly, and withdrew his support on 24 June 2013 in the shooting's wake; Moretz defended the film against her co-star's stance. The dispute played out over the Colonel's own scenes — the film's violence debated through the man who renounces it.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Colonel Stars and Stripes is the Kick-Ass films' one completed redemption — a hitman's second career, cut short as his old employer's heir declares war. Within the Galaxy his page is the reference for the character: the founding, the doctrine, the atrocity, and the rules that outlived him.
