Ephialtes

A Spartan by birth, deformed, spared the cliff by parents who fled — raised on the story of the city that would have killed him, and desperate, in the film's cruelest irony, to earn its acceptance in its own line; refused on phalanx mechanics, he sells its secret path to the god who offers him a uniform.

01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Ephialtes is the film's moral stress test. Born Spartan and born deformed, he should have been left on the cliff; his parents fled Sparta to save him, and raised him on its stories — so that the grown Ephialtes wants exactly one thing: to stand in the Spartan line at Thermopylae and be, at last, accepted by the city that sentenced him. Leonidas receives him kindly and refuses him precisely: Ephialtes cannot raise his shield high enough to hold the phalanx's wall, and a low shield endangers the man beside him. Offered work as a water-carrier, he refuses the lesser place, goes to Xerxes, and sells the goat path around the pass — for gold, for women, and for the uniform of the empire that will have him. The 300 die of the sale.

ROLE

The refused man

Antagonist by consequence; the pass's betrayer and the film's most argued-over figure.

DEFINING TRAIT

Belonging, at any price

Ephialtes's treason is not greed but application: he sells the pass to the only employer who will issue him a uniform.

FATE

In Persia's service

He survives the film in Xerxes's train, his name become the Greek word's root for nightmare and traitor — a legacy the film's frame assumes.

Place in the story

Ephialtes is where the film's Spartan doctrine meets its own shadow: the archive files his page as the record of what the cliff at Sparta's gate produces, downstream, at the pass at Greece's.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Ephialtes's history is given in the narration: born to Spartan parents with a deformed body, he was spared the exposure cliff when his parents fled Sparta with him. He grows up in exile, raised on the city's stories, and when the 300 march to Thermopylae he comes to Leonidas and begs a place in the line — to redeem, as he frames it, his parents' flight and his own birth in the only currency Sparta honours.

Leonidas tests the request against the phalanx's mechanics: Ephialtes cannot lift his shield high enough to cover himself and the man beside him. The king refuses him a place in the wall — kindly, and absolutely — and offers him honourable service instead: carrying water, tending the wounded. Ephialtes rejects the lesser place and leaves the camp.

He goes to Xerxes and reveals the goat path that bypasses the Hot Gates, in exchange for gold, women and a uniform of the Persian army. Persian troops use the path to surround the 300; the allies are dismissed, Dilios is sent home, and the Spartans die in the last stand his information made necessary.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Longing as identity

Ephialtes does not want wealth or revenge first; he wants a place in the line. The film's cruelty is that the desire is Sparta's own teaching, installed by exile's storytelling.

All-or-nothing

Offered an honourable lesser role, he cannot take it — belonging, for him, is binary, because the cliff made it so.

The empire's ideal recruit

Xerxes's system — which absorbs every nation and issues uniforms to all — is designed for the men Sparta's system discards; Ephialtes is where the two systems' personnel policies collide.

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

KNOWLEDGE

The goat path

Local knowledge of the pass's bypass — the single most valuable intelligence asset in the film.

SURVIVAL

The exile's life

He has lived outside every system that might have fed him, into adulthood, on his parents' flight and his own endurance.

LIMIT

The shield he cannot raise

A physical fact with strategic consequences: the phalanx's standard is absolute, and he cannot meet it. The film is careful that the refusal is mechanical, not contemptuous — which makes it worse, not better.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The uniform

What Ephialtes finally receives from Persia is not the gold but the clothing of belonging — the film's most economical statement that his treason was an application for membership.

The cliff and the pass

Sparta inspects its infants on a cliff and its soldiers in a shield wall; Ephialtes fails both inspections, and the second failure costs Sparta the first's whole graduating class. The film's structure is the argument.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The film's sore spot

Ephialtes is the figure on whom readings of 300 divide: the narration — Sparta's own voice — files him as a monster of deformity and treason, while the plot the narration reports shows a man manufactured by Sparta's exclusions and refused on a technicality. The archive records both, and notes that the film, unusually, gives the audience everything needed to indict its own narrator's framing.

The historical shadow

A historical Ephialtes did betray the path at Thermopylae, per Herodotus; the deformity, the cliff and the refusal are the film's and the comic's inventions, designed to make the betrayal an argument about Sparta rather than a fact about a shepherd.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Tiernan's performance

Andrew Tiernan performed the role under extensive prosthetics; the production designed Ephialtes's body as the agoge's nightmare made flesh — a design choice the archive records as the film's most contested.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Ephialtes — whose name in Greek tradition already meant traitor and nightmare — became, in this film's version, the standing case study for how a society manufactures its own betrayers, argued over wherever the film is taught or criticised.
