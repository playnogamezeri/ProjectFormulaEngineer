01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Dilios is the only reason the film exists, and the film says so: he is its narrator, telling the story of Leonidas to the assembled Greek army a year after Thermopylae, on the morning of Plataea. Inside the story he is a hoplite of the 300 who loses an eye in the fighting and is ordered home by Leonidas — not as a mercy but as a deployment: someone must tell Sparta what happened, and Dilios is the army's memory. His narration is openly a soldier's telling, wry and awestruck and embellishing, and the film builds its whole aesthetic on that licence: everything the audience sees is a story being weaponised.

ROLE

The frame itself

Narrator and supporting protagonist; the 300's designated survivor and historian.

DEFINING TRAIT

The storyteller's licence, confessed

Dilios narrates like a man recruiting — which is what he is doing — and the film never pretends otherwise.

FATE

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Dilios opens the film in 479 BC, a year after Thermopylae, beginning his story for the Greek army: Leonidas's childhood, the agoge, the wolf, the kingship. Within the tale he marches with the 300 as a hoplite of the line — present for the emissary's aftermath, the Arcadians' arrival, and the assaults at the gate.

In the fighting he loses an eye. When the position is turned by Ephialtes's path and Leonidas prepares the last stand, the king singles Dilios out: he is to return to Sparta and tell the story. Dilios's anguish at the order is the film's most soldierly scene — survival as an assignment he experiences as exile from the only honourable place left.

The frame closes the film: Dilios finishes the tale before the assembled army — declaring that the Persian host which barely defeated 300 Spartans now faces 10,000 Spartans commanding 30,000 Greeks — praises Leonidas's sacrifice, and leads the charge into the Battle of Plataea. The story, delivered, becomes the weapon Leonidas bought with the 300.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The soldier as bard

Dilios's gift is the phalanx's other discipline: memory, arranged for effect. He tells the tale the way Sparta taught him to fight — in formation, without flinching.

Honest about the embellishment

His narration winks at its own exaggerations; the film's monsters and god-kings are presented as a teller's instruments, and Dilios is the teller.

Reluctant survivor

His survival is an order obeyed, not a fate accepted; the frame's energy comes from a man still arguing with his own exemption.
