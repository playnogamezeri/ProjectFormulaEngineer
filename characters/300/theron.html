01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Theron is the war's fifth column in a single career: a Spartan councillor on Persia's payroll — his gold, in the film's account, is the same money that bought the Ephors' oracle — who works to keep Sparta's army home while Leonidas dies at the gate. His instrument is procedure and his masterpiece is the bargain he strikes with Gorgo: his support for her motion, priced at her submission. He exacts the price and then denounces her before the Council anyway — the double betrayal being, for Theron, simply the full monetisation of the asset. Gorgo's sword ends him mid-triumph, and the Persian coins pouring from his robe convert his corpse into her closing argument.

ROLE

The enemy within

Antagonist of the Sparta plot; Persia's purchased councillor.

DEFINING TRAIT

Everything priced

Theron experiences loyalty, procedure and other people's marriages as inventory; his death is an audit.

FATE

Killed by Gorgo in open Council

Run through before the assembled chamber, his Persian gold spilling out as his character reference.

Place in the story

Theron is the film's proof that Thermopylae's decisive traitor was not the deformed shepherd but the well-dressed councillor: Ephialtes sold a path, but Theron sold the army that might have marched down it.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Theron operates in Sparta's council as Persia's insurance policy. In the film's account, Persian gold has bought both the Ephors' convenient oracle and Theron's procedural opposition: while Leonidas is at the gate, Theron's work is to keep the army home and the question closed.

Gorgo, petitioning the Council for reinforcements, must pass through him. He receives her and names his price for supporting her motion: her submission to him. She pays it. The following day, before the assembled Council, Theron betrays the bargain's spirit in full — denouncing Gorgo as an adulteress, moving to annihilate her credibility and her motion in a single speech built from the very transaction he exacted.

Gorgo's answer is delivered in the chamber: she draws a sword and runs him through. Theron falls, and Persian coins spill from his robe across the floor before the Council — his allegiance itemised in his own currency. The chamber, shown the price of its own obstruction, rallies to the queen and votes Sparta to war.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Transactional to the marrow

Theron does not have positions, only positions for sale; even his betrayal of Gorgo is a second monetisation of the same asset.

Silken

His weapons are agenda, insinuation and timing — he never raises his voice in a film of shouters, which marks him as its most dangerous speaker.

Contemptuous

He regards Sparta's pieties as other men's inventory; the film's Spartans die for abstractions Theron has already sold.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The fictional traitor

Theron does not exist in the historical record or the source comic — he is the film's invention, created to give Sparta's home front a face. The archive files him as the adaptation's thesis about how stands like Thermopylae are actually lost: not at the pass, but in the chamber that declines to relieve them.
