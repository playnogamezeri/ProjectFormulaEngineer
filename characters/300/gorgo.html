Gorgo

Sparta's queen — the film's second commander, fighting the war's home front with the only weapons the council chamber allows: argument, alliance and, at the last, a sword. Her farewell counsel to Leonidas is the expedition's price list, and her exposure of Theron is the home front's Thermopylae.

01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Gorgo's war is the harder one to film and the film knows it: while Leonidas holds a pass, she holds a council — petitioning Sparta to send reinforcements to a stand the law has forbidden her husband to make. Her antagonist is Theron, Persia's asset in the chamber, who names a sexual price for his support, exacts it, and then denounces her as an adulteress before the Council anyway. Her answer is the film's second-most-famous act of violence: she runs him through in open session, and the Persian coins spilling from his robe do her arguing for her. The Council rallies; the reinforcements are voted — too late for the 300, in time for Plataea.

ROLE

The second front

Co-protagonist of the Sparta plot; the stand's advocate in the council that refused it.

DEFINING TRAIT

Stillness that out-ranks shouting

Headey plays Gorgo at a lower volume than every man in the film, and the chamber scenes are built around the fact that the room listens anyway.

FATE

Vindicated, too late

She kills Theron, exposes his Persian gold before the Council, and wins the vote — after the 300 are already dead.

Place in the story

Gorgo is the film's proof that Thermopylae was fought twice: once with spears at the gate, once with procedure in Sparta — and that the second battle's traitor did more damage than Xerxes's whole army managed at the first.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Gorgo appears first at the film's hinge of private life: the farewell. Her counsel to Leonidas, as Dilios tells it, is the Spartan formula — come back with your shield, or on it — delivered not as cruelty but as the marriage's shared arithmetic: both of them know what the 300 are for.

With Leonidas gone, she petitions the Council to send Sparta's army after him. The chamber's gatekeeper is Theron, who receives her privately and names his price for supporting her motion: her submission. The film's account — in both its narration and its structure — is that she pays it, and that Theron then rises in Council the next day and denounces her as an adulteress, moving to destroy her credibility and her motion together.

Gorgo answers in the chamber, before the assembled Council: she draws a sword and runs Theron through. As he falls, Persian coins spill from his robe across the council floor — his treason made visible in his own currency. The Council, confronted with the price list of its own corruption, rallies to her cause and votes Sparta to war.

The vote comes too late for the Hot Gates. Its fruit is the army Dilios addresses a year later — 10,000 Spartans commanding 30,000 Greeks — before Plataea, which is where her war and her husband's finally join.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

A Spartan of the same manufacture

Gorgo is the agoge's product in its civilian form: pain unshown, mercy unbudgeted, the objective pursued at personal prices no one else in the chamber would pay.

Procedural until procedure fails

She petitions, bargains and endures the chamber's rules until the rules are revealed as purchased — at which point her remedy is the sword, administered in session.

Dry

Her wit is the film's driest; the queen's lines are delivered like filings, and land like verdicts.

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

ORATORY

The petition

She argues Sparta's grandees toward a war their priests and their paymasters both oppose.

ENDURANCE

The price paid without flinching

Theron's bargain is exacted and betrayed; she absorbs both and keeps the motion alive.

VIOLENCE

The sword in session

When exposure is required, she performs it herself, in public, with a blade — the council chamber's Thermopylae, held alone.

LIMIT

The chamber's timetable

Her victory is real and useless to the men it was for: procedure moves at the speed of scandal, and the gate fell faster.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The spilled coins

Theron is undone by his own currency, poured out of his robe in public: the film's home-front thesis in one image — corruption is only invisible until the body falls.

Two Thermopylaes

Her chamber fight mirrors the pass: a narrow place, a bought enemy, a stand made by the outnumbered — the film cross-cuts the two fronts as one war.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The adaptation's addition

Gorgo's council plot is the film's largest expansion of its source, where she appears only at the beginning; the archive reads the expansion as the adaptation's conscience — the stand at the gate is given a political price, a traitor and a second protagonist to pay it.

The record of Theron's bargain

The film presents the transaction as coercion wearing negotiation's clothes: a price named by the powerful, exacted, and then betrayed. The archive records it as the film presents it — the chamber's crime, not the queen's shame — and notes that her sword, not her suffering, is the scene the film ends the subplot on.
