01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Xerxes is the anti-Sparta: where Leonidas is small, scarred and first in the shield wall, Xerxes is enormous, pierced with gold, and carried above his army on a throne that is itself a building. His empire's method is absorption — every nation in his train keeps its strangeness as a tribute act — and his offer to Leonidas is the empire's whole doctrine in one audience: kneel, and rule Greece as my warlord, wealthy beyond dreams. The refusal, and the spear that opens his cheek in the final stand, are the film's counter-doctrine: the god bleeds. He survives the film, diminished by exactly one wound, which is the wound the story was told to inflict.

ROLE

The god-king

Principal antagonist; emperor of Persia and commander of the invasion.

DEFINING TRAIT

Divinity as production design

Xerxes's godhood is staged — height, gold, attendants, a voice deepened past nature — and the film's point is that staging is what Leonidas's spear audits.

FATE

Wounded and enduring

His cheek is grazed in the last stand; a year later his army faces the Greeks at Plataea, per the frame.

Place in the story

Xerxes is the film's measure of scale: the largest army, the largest throne, the largest claim — set against 300 men and one thrown spear, and the film's arithmetic favours the spear.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Xerxes enters the film by proxy: his emissary demands earth and water from Sparta and is kicked into the pit. His answer is the invasion — a host the narration numbers beyond counting, moving into Greece with its menagerie of conquered nations: the Immortals in their masks, war elephants, a rhinoceros, the executioners and monsters of an empire that collects peoples.

At Thermopylae his assaults break in sequence on the phalanx. He grants Leonidas an audience and makes the empire's offer in person: kneel, and be my warlord over Greece, richer than any king. Leonidas refuses. Ephialtes, refused by Sparta, comes to Xerxes with the goat path, and is bought with gold, women and — the price he names himself — a uniform: to be, at last, included.

With the path betrayed, the 300 are surrounded. Leonidas feigns submission, kneels — and hurls his spear as Stelios leaps: the blade grazes Xerxes's cheek and draws the god-king's blood before the assembled armies. The Spartans die under the arrow storm that follows. Xerxes's war proceeds — to Plataea, where Dilios's finished story is waiting for it.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Absolute, and genuinely puzzled by refusal

Xerxes's offers are, within his system, generous to the point of condescension; Leonidas's refusal registers on him as a category error, which is the film's definition of empire.

A collector

His court is a museum of absorbed nations; inclusion is his mercy and his threat — Ephialtes is bought with exactly the belonging Sparta refused him.

Unblooded, until the cheek

His divinity depends on never having been touched; the film's whole third act is arranged to touch him once, publicly, at spear range.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
EMPIRE

The host

The largest army in the film's world, with its logistics, levies and siege train.

WEALTH

The universal solvent

The Ephors are bought, Ephialtes is bought, Leonidas is offered Greece: gold is his first weapon in every theatre.

PRESENCE

The staged god

Height, gold and voice composed into divinity — effective on everyone except the 300.

LIMIT

He cannot fight his own battles

His system requires other men's spears; at the decisive moment he is a spectator on a throne, and the film's smallest army reaches him anyway.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The mobile throne

Xerxes never touches the ground of the country he is conquering — his divinity is altitude — set against Leonidas, who dies standing in the pass at ground level with his men.

The grazing spear

The wound is trivial and total: the film's arithmetic is that a god who can be scratched can be beaten, and Plataea is fought on that arithmetic.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Dilios's Xerxes

This figure is the narration's freest invention — nine feet tall, gold-chained, inhuman — and the archive files him as such: he is what a Spartan storyteller makes of an emperor, which is itself the film's subject. The historical Xerxes is not this page's business; the story's god is.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Santoro's transformation

Rodrigo Santoro, a Brazilian star, was made nearly unrecognisable — shaved, pierced with gold, heightened by platform and camera, and voiced a register below his own — the production's commitment to Dilios's licence made visible.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This Xerxes — the pierced god on the travelling throne — became the popular image of the Persian king for a generation, and the standing example in debates about the film's method: a narrator's monster, worn by an empire.
