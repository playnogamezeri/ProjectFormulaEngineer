01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Sir Te is the film's establishment made kind: a senior official in Beijing, benefactor and old friend to Li Mu Bai and Yu Shu Lien, who accepts the Green Destiny as Mu Bai's retirement gift and undertakes to keep it. The sword is stolen from his estate on its first night under his roof — the event that starts the plot — and the investigation that follows runs through his household, his servant Master Bo, and his guest Shu Lien. Sir Te's function is structural and quietly central: he is the point where the jianghu's world of swords, codes and revenge is deposited, for safekeeping, into the civil world of houses, servants and hospitality — and the film measures both worlds by how the deposit fares. At the end, after Mu Bai's death, the Green Destiny is returned to Sir Te: the gift completes its circle, minus its giver.

ROLE

The benefactor and host

Supporting principal; the sword's custodian, Shu Lien's host in Beijing, and the investigation's home base.

DEFINING TRAIT

Worried gravitas

A kind man in an armed world, played by Sihung Lung with the warm authority the Galaxy page notes; his house is where the sword was safest and where it was stolen.

FATE

The sword returned

He survives the film; the Green Destiny comes back to his keeping after Mu Bai's death, its retirement finally accomplished by loss.

Place in the story

Sir Te's page is deliberately the smallest of this batch, kept at his role's true size: he has no duel, no secret and no arc of transformation. What he has is position — the film's thesis needs a respectable house for the sword to be stolen from, and a decent man to receive it back. The archive records him at that size rather than inflating him.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Sir Te receives Yu Shu Lien at his Beijing estate, where she has brought the Green Destiny at Mu Bai's request — the great swordsman's retirement gift to his benefactor. Sir Te accepts the custodianship; the film's documented account has Shu Lien deliver the sword to him as the token of Mu Bai's withdrawal from the jianghu.

That night, a masked thief enters the estate and steals the sword. Shu Lien pursues the thief across Sir Te's own grounds and rooftops and loses her to an unseen ally's dart. The theft from his house sets the whole plot in motion, and Sir Te's household becomes the investigation's base: his servant Master Bo works with Shu Lien, traces the theft toward Governor Yu's compound, and makes the acquaintance of Inspector Tsai and his daughter May, newly arrived in pursuit of Jade Fox.

Sir Te himself remains where the film keeps him — at the centre of the civil world the investigation keeps leaving and returning to. After the cavern, Mu Bai's death and Jen's forgiveness, the Green Destiny is returned to Sir Te. The archive records his story at the documented level: the gift, the theft, the household's part in the hunt, and the return.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The decent establishment

Sir Te is the film's proof that the ordered world is not merely hypocrisy: he is generous, loyal to his warrior friends, and serious about a trust he did not seek. Interpretation: the film needs him decent so that the theft reads as the jianghu's failure, not his.

Hospitality as governance

His instruments are rooms, servants and dinners — the civil world's entire arsenal — and the film shows them working exactly as designed and mattering hardly at all against people who can run across his roof.

Worry without illusion

He knows what the sword is and what its presence invites. His acceptance of it is friendship priced correctly: he takes the risk because Mu Bai asked.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The house

Interpretation: Sir Te's estate is the film's diagram of order — walls, gates, servants, guests — drawn so the audience can watch the jianghu pass over its roof without touching a tile. Everything the film says about the two worlds is in that crossing.

The gift that circulates

The Green Destiny's itinerary runs through his house twice: given, stolen, fought over, and returned. Sir Te is the fixed point that lets the audience measure the sword's travels — and Mu Bai's death is priced by the gift coming home without him.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Why the host matters

A theft needs an owner wronged, and the film chooses to wrong its kindest man. The archive's reading: Sir Te's decency is the control condition of the film's experiment — if the sword cannot be safe in his house, it cannot be safe anywhere in the ordered world, which is precisely the conclusion Mu Bai's retirement was trying to avoid.

An honest-record note

Sir Te's documentation is genuinely thin: the sources give his function, his household, the gift and the return, and little interiority. His page is kept at that size deliberately — the archive does not invent a backstory, a family, or a fate beyond the documented record.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Sir Te stands in the archive as the wuxia film's civil pole: the proof that the genre's world contains not only swordsmen and officials-as-villains, but a decent administrator whose house the story can be stolen from and returned to.

The Galaxy film page files him as the decade's most consequential host in film: a gift received, guarded, and gone by morning. His page keeps him at that measure — the fixed point of the film's geography of trust.
