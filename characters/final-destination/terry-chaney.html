01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Terry Chaney gets off Flight 180 as Carter Horton's girlfriend, one of the seven swept out in the fight. She has the group's shortest fuse and its most quoted scene. At the café where the survivors gather after Tod Waggner's death — with Alex's claims dividing the table and Bludworth's rules hanging over them — the argument becomes too much: Terry delivers the film's thesis speech against living in fear, the refusal to spend her survival looking over her shoulder, and storms out into the street, where a speeding bus runs her down mid-sentence, in front of them all. The documented accounts fix the sequence exactly: the heated argument, the frustrated exit, the bus. Terry is the design's second collection and its most theatrical — the one who stops watching, on principle, at the moment the design is watching her.

ROLE

The protest

The survivor who refuses the premise aloud; her death is the film's thesis demonstrated on its own spokeswoman.

DEFINING TRAIT

Bright, fed-up directness

Amanda Detmer plays her, per the Galaxy page, with a directness that makes the cut to silence the film's most quoted shock.

FATE

Run over by a bus

Killed stepping into the street outside the café, mid-argument's aftermath, in view of the other survivors.

Place in the story

Terry is the film's argument with itself, given a face: everything she says at the café is reasonable, humane and what the audience wishes for the characters — and the film answers it with a bus. Her page carries the refusal's full text and its full price, and the naming record's neatest honour: she is named for Lon Chaney.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Terry boards Flight 180 with her boyfriend Carter Horton. In the confusion of Alex's panic and the aisle fight, she is one of the seven removed from the plane, and she watches it explode with the rest. She passes through the FBI interrogation and the memorial period as the group's least patient survivor: the one for whom the aftermath's fear is itself the injury.

After Tod's death, the survivors meet at a café, where Alex and Clear bring what they learned at the funeral home and a heated argument breaks out over what is happening. Terry's frustration peaks: she refuses the survivors' paralysis — refuses, in the film's thesis terms, to live in fear of an invisible list — and leaves. Stepping into the street, she is suddenly run over by a speeding bus, in front of Carter and the others. The documented accounts give the death its exact staging: the argument, the exit, the bus, the witnesses.

Her death is the design's second collection and its first public one: Tod died deniably, at home, filed as suicide; Terry dies in the open, before the whole group, in a manner no ruling can soften. It is after her death that Alex turns to the news report on the explosion and reconstructs the order — her name second in it.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Refusal as vitality

Terry's speech is not denial; it is a philosophy — survival spent in fear is a second death administered slowly. Interpretation: the film honours the position by making its refutation instantaneous rather than reasoned; the bus is not an argument, which is the horror of it.

The group's shortest patience

Where Alex audits and Carter rages, Terry simply declines the whole proceeding. Her directness is the table's only exit from the argument — and the design is waiting at the exit.

Loyalty to the living

Her fury is partly on Carter's behalf and the group's: she is protesting what the fear is doing to the people at the table, not only to herself.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The interrupted sentence

Terry dies the way the film believes people die inside its premise: not at the end of their arguments but in the middle of them. The cut from speech to silence is the franchise's founding punctuation mark.

The bus

Public transport as the design's herald — the most municipal of vehicles, arriving on schedule. Interpretation: the film chooses the least gothic instrument available to prove that the design's fleet is everything that moves.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

The thesis and its price

The Galaxy page's formulation is the archive's: Terry's function is the portrait of the refusal as the design's easiest collection — she stops watching, on principle, and the film files the principle's price without blinking. The archive adds only this: the film does not make her wrong. Her speech stands unanswered by any character; the bus is not a rebuttal but a demonstration that the design does not grade arguments either.

Second in the order

Her placement matters to the film's pedagogy: the design takes the best friend quietly, then the protester publicly. After Terry, no survivor at the table can maintain that nothing is happening — the argument she died refusing is settled by her death, which is the design's debating style.
