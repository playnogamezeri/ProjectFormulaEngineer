01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Memo is Charlie Watson’s neighbor in Bumblebee (2018), played by Jorge Lendeborg Jr. — described by his own card as the boy next door whose crush drafts him into the secret. Brought inside the Bumblebee secret by accident and affection, Memo becomes the conspiracy’s second civilian: witness, driver, accomplice and cover story, present from the garage’s first shocks to the harbor’s end, and rewarded by the film with exactly what he wanted — to be beside her, usefully, when it mattered.

ROLE

The second civilian

Memo doubles the film’s human stake: Charlie does not carry the secret alone, and the war’s logistics gain a driver, a lookout and a friend.

DEFINING TRAIT

Uninvoiced loyalty

His help is constant and his price is zero — the film is careful that his crush never becomes a claim on her.

ARC IN ONE LINE

From the fence to the harbor

He begins peering over the Watsons’ fence and ends the film inside the perimeter of the war’s last night, by invitation.

Place in the story

Memo is the film’s measure of ordinary decency under extraordinary circumstances: he has no skills the plot strictly needs except willingness, and the plot keeps needing it. Structurally he also carries the film’s lightest register — the comedy of a teenager processing a giant robot — which keeps Charlie’s grief story breathable.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Memo is the Watsons’ neighbor in Brighton Falls, carrying an unreciprocated-but-hopeful crush on Charlie when the Beetle arrives in her garage. Drawn in by curiosity and by her, he witnesses what the car becomes — and, to his credit in the film’s economy, does not run, sell or tell: he joins the secret as its second keeper.

His service is logistical and continuous: company in the garage as the scout’s memory returns, a second pair of hands and eyes as the hunt closes in, and presence through the crisis — when Charlie refuses Bumblebee’s capture as the ending, Memo goes with her toward the outpost and the harbor, a civilian teenager walking into a military-alien battle because she asked and because he was asked by his own heart first.

He survives the harbor night inside the secret — one of the handful of humans who know what visited Brighton Falls in 1987 — and the film closes his account where it opened it: beside Charlie, his loyalty having earned the only wage it ever sought, which was to be there.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Bravery by attachment

Memo is not brave in the abstract; he is brave in Charlie’s direction. The film treats this as a real and sufficient kind of courage — most of history’s logistics ran on it.

The comic register

His reactions — awe, terror, delight — are the audience’s own, played honestly; the film uses him to let its wonder breathe between grief beats.

No invoice

The script’s care with his crush is the character’s distinction: he helps without bargaining, hopes without pressuring, and the film rewards the restraint with trust rather than romance — a quietly modern note for 1987.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Narrative function: the witness who stays

Memo exists to prove Charlie’s experience to the film’s own world — a second witness makes the secret a society — and to give her arc a human alternative to the robot: someone her own species, age and street who chooses her without being repaired by her first. Investigation of the structure shows his scenes consistently placed after her loneliest beats, the film quietly insisting that she was never as alone as her grief reported.

Memo’s legacy is small and fond: the saga’s model of the decent bystander, proof that in this film the most important military asset on Earth was a neighbor who could keep a secret and show up. His file is the archive’s reminder that the harbor was held by civilians.
