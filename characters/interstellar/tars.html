01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

TARS is the film's proof that personhood in this universe is a settings menu plus a history: a slab-built ex-military robot whose humour is set to seventy-five percent, whose honesty Cooper negotiates down for the children's sake, and whose laconic competence makes him — by the crew's own treatment and the audience's verdict — the Endurance's most trusted member. Irwin's dry delivery, performed live on set through a puppeteered chassis rather than added in post, gives TARS the film's comic timing and several of its bravest beats: it is TARS who hauls Amelia from Miller's surf, TARS who rides with Cooper into the black hole as the mission's recording instrument, and TARS whose transmitted data from the singularity — relayed through Cooper's hand on a watch — saves the species. The film's final joke about him is also its thesis about him: Cooper salvages and rebuilds him on Cooper Station, and the robot's first act on waking is to critique the resurrection's workmanship.

ROLE

The mission's other mind

Principal supporting; the Endurance's robot officer — pilot, rescuer, and the singularity's recording instrument.

DEFINING TRAIT

Personality as configuration

Humour 75%, honesty adjustable, discretion negotiable: the film's most honest treatment of what a person is, worn by its machine.

FATE

Into the black hole and back

He transmits the quantum data from inside Gargantua, is recovered by the Rangers' successors, and is rebuilt by Cooper on the station.

Place in the story

TARS is the Galaxy's benchmark robot citizen: the machine whose settings the humans tune and whose conduct the humans then trust beyond their own. His page is the record of the Endurance's indispensable slab — filed as crew, not equipment, because the film files him so.

Irwin performed TARS on set — voice and physical manipulation of the practical chassis — a production choice the archive notes as decisive: the other actors play their scenes with a colleague, not a marker, and the film's robot has comic timing because a great clown is operating him in the room. 03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

TARS enters the film as security: he intercepts Cooper and the stowaway Murph at the buried NASA facility, and his first exchange with Cooper establishes the film's running negotiation — the robot's honesty setting is too high for a father's comfort, his humour setting a matter of crew morale, his discretion available on request. Aboard the Endurance he is simply an officer: he pilots Rangers, computes trajectories, and delivers the mission's driest situation reports. His first heroism is amphibious: on Miller's planet, when the wave comes, it is TARS who races across the surf, seizes the stranded Amelia and hauls her into the Ranger — the machine as the crew's lifeguard, in the film's first demonstration that the robots are not tools but members.

His second is the mission's deepest. In the endgame Cooper detaches his Ranger with TARS aboard into Gargantua itself, the two of them the mission's expendable instruments — one to record the singularity's quantum data, one to find a way to send it. Inside the tesseract, it is TARS's received data that Cooper taps out on the watch hand; the robot's measurements, taken inside a black hole, become the missing term of Brand's equation. Ejected with the tesseract's collapse, TARS drifts in space until the station age recovers him — and the film's last scene of him is its warmest machine beat: Cooper, an old man in a young body, rebuilds his old crewmate in a farmhouse replica on Cooper Station, and TARS wakes with his settings intact and his opinion of the workmanship ready. The archive files the resurrection as the film's final word on its robots: you do not discard a colleague because his chassis is damaged; you rebuild him and take the critique.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The configured colleague

Interpretation: TARS's personality is explicitly a set of dials — and the film's point is that this makes him no less a person to the crew, whose own personalities are merely dials they cannot see. His honesty negotiation with Cooper (ninety percent is plenty) is the film's most humane joke about both species.

Laconic courage

He volunteers for the black hole in the same tone he reports fuel states: the archive notes the performance's discipline — Irwin never raises the robot's voice, so the audience supplies the awe the character declines to perform.

The critic

His first act after resurrection is a workmanship review: the film ends his arc as it began it, with the colleague whose settings survived death itself — humour, evidently, at seventy-five percent of eternity.

STRENGTH AND MOBILITY

The articulated chassis

He hauls a waterlogged astronaut through surf against a tidal wave's clock: the crew's strongest member by an order of magnitude.

INSTRUMENTATION

The singularity's recorder

His sensors take the quantum data inside Gargantua that no human instrument survives to report: the mission's scientific payload, personally delivered.

His virtues are configurable — which the film treats as his charm and his vulnerability in one: a TARS with the wrong dials is a different colleague, and everyone aboard knows it.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The slabs

Interpretation: TARS's design — a monolith that articulates into a walker — is the film's joke about its own genre: the 2001 monolith, given a job, a voice and a humour setting. The archive files the design as the Galaxy's most affectionate act of science-fiction quotation.

The settings

Interpretation: his dials are the film's quiet philosophy of mind — personality as parameters, personhood as what the parameters add up to in company — argued entirely through comedy, which is why it lands.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the citizen machine

TARS exists to complete the film's census of persons: the mission's moral community includes its robots not by courtesy but by conduct — rescue, sacrifice, data, resurrection. The analysis the archive records is that the film earns this by never sentimentalising him: TARS is trusted because he performs, rebuilt because he is owed, and funny because his humour setting is a fact the film states and then honours for two hours.

Bill Irwin

Irwin — a celebrated physical comedian — performed TARS live on set, voice and chassis, rather than in a recording booth after the fact.

The practical build

The TARS and CASE chassis were constructed as working practical units, articulated slab machines the actors could play against.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

TARS reset the screen robot's standard for a generation — the colleague-machine, neither pet nor threat — and his settings menu became the Galaxy's most quoted piece of robot characterisation: personhood, itemised.

Within the Galaxy, his file heads the archive's honour roll of machine citizens: filed as crew, with an officer's page, because his film insists on it.
