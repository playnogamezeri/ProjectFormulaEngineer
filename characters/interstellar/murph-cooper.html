01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Murph is the film's true protagonist on Earth and, by its ending, the saviour of the species — the only character whose arc spans the whole story at ground level, from the ten-year-old decoding her bookcase's ghost to the physicist who cracks the gravity equation with data tapped out on a watch hand, to the ancient matriarch on Cooper Station whose name the world itself now bears. Three performers share her (Foy's fierce child, Chastain's driven physicist, Burstyn's serene elder), and the film's boldest structural wager is that the audience will follow her across all three faces while her father — the nominal hero — barely ages a day. Her story is the ghost story inverted: the haunting was love, the haunted house was a laboratory, and the exorcism is an equation.

ROLE

The saviour on Earth

Co-protagonist; Brand's protégée and successor, solver of the gravity equation, namesake of Cooper Station.

DEFINING TRAIT

The ghost, believed scientifically

She insists on the anomaly and on method at once — the film's model of faith: observe, record, refuse to dismiss.

FATE

Humanity's deliverer

She completes the equation with her father's transmitted data, evacuates the species, and dies ancient and surrounded by the family her saved world produced.

Place in the story

Murph is the film's ground truth: everything Cooper does in the sky is for her, and everything that saves humanity is done by her. Her page is the record of the Galaxy's most complete scientist-daughter — the haunted child who grew up to read the haunting as data.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Murph at ten is already the film's scientist: when books fall in her room and dust patterns itself on her floor, she does not merely believe in a ghost — she logs it, charts the intervals, and brings her father data. The coordinates hidden in the dust take them both to NASA; the ghost's last message, decoded from the falling books as STAY, becomes her desperate case against his leaving, and his departure without her forgiveness is the wound the whole film exists to heal. She grows up inside the wound's other half: taken on by Professor Brand as his protégée, she spends her twenties and thirties on the gravity equation that is supposed to lift humanity off the dying Earth — while her brother Tom farms, marries, buries a dust-killed child, and refuses to leave the farm; while the messages from the Endurance arrive across decades; while her own message to her father, sent at the age he was when he left, asks the question she has carried since childhood: did he know he was leaving them to die?

Brand's deathbed gives her the film's buried truth, from his own mouth: he solved the equation years ago. It cannot be completed without quantum data from inside a black hole, which he judged unreachable — so Plan A was always a hope he manufactured to launch the mission, and Plan B, the embryo colony, was the real plan all along. Her fury at the deception — and at her father, who she believes knew — curdles into the film's darkest Earth scenes: Tom's refusal to evacuate his family from the poisoned farm, her desperate arson of his corn to draw him away while she rescues his wife and children, the siblings' estrangement complete. And then, in the farmhouse she grew up in, memory and method meet: the watch her father left her, its second hand stuttering in a pattern she suddenly recognises as Morse — data, quantum data, arriving now, from inside the black hole, from her ghost. Her Eureka is the film's hinge: she runs from the burning fields with the equation completing itself in her head.

The rest is history the film leaves mostly to implication and one closing movement: the equation solved, the stations lifted, humanity evacuated into the rotating worlds by Saturn — the largest of them named Cooper Station, for her. In her extreme old age she is brought, in cryosleep, to see the father who has fallen out of the black hole barely older than when he left: the film's final family scene, the ancient daughter dismissing the young father from her deathbed with the doctrine she has had a century to formulate — no parent should have to watch their child die, and his place is with Amelia Brand, out where the future she built is waiting. She dies as she lived in the film: the person who understood the ghost best, sending the living on their way.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Faith, with a notebook

Interpretation: Murph's belief in the ghost is the film's model epistemology — she neither dismisses the anomaly nor worships it; she measures it. Her childhood method (log the intervals, chart the falls) is her adult method unchanged, which is why the watch's message finds the one mind on Earth prepared to read it.

The wound, worked

Her anger at Cooper is never cured by information — Brand's lie explains the leaving without healing it — and the film is honest that her life's work is partly the wound's sublimation: she finishes the equation her father's absence demanded. The healing, when it comes, is the work itself, and one conversation at the end of a century.

The matriarch's serenity

Burstyn's Murph has metabolised everything the younger Murphs raged against: her deathbed dismissal of Cooper is the film's calmest scene, a century of grief converted into a blessing with instructions attached.

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
PHYSICS

Brand's successor

She completes the gravity equation — the film's central intellectual act — by uniting its theory with the singularity's data.

PATTERN RECOGNITION

The ghost-reader

From dust binary to watch-hand Morse, the film's every coded message is read by Murph: she is humanity's designated receiver.

WILL

The farmhouse stand

She burns her brother's crop to save his family against his will: the film's Earth-bound heroism, as stubborn as anything flown in space.

WEAKNESS

The carried question

For decades she believes her father abandoned them knowingly — a false belief Brand's lie installed and only the watch dispels: her story is the price of other people's deceptions, paid in years.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

Murphy's law, read hopefully

Interpretation: her name is the film's epistemology in a joke her father makes at her birth — whatever can happen, will happen — and her life is its proof in the hopeful direction: the ghost can be her father, the data can arrive, the species can be saved, because everything that can happen includes the good.

The bookshelf

Interpretation: her childhood bookcase is the film's sacred site — a library that is also a telegraph office, a haunting that is also a correspondence — and the archive files it as the Galaxy's definitive image of love as a communications technology.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the true protagonist of the ground

The film's structure is a relay: Cooper carries the first act, Murph the Earth, and the ending reveals the relay was a circuit — his transmissions are her inputs, her equation is his mission's completion. The analysis the archive records is that Murph is the rare blockbuster saviour whose weapon is literacy: she saves the species by reading — dust, books, a watch — and the film's deepest claim is that receiving is as heroic as flying.

11 / BEHIND THE CHARACTER

Behind the Character

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Three performers, one role

Foy, Chastain and Burstyn share the part across a century; Chastain's casting linked the film to her concurrent run of scientist roles, and Burstyn's scenes were shot as the production's emotional summit.

The Eureka's staging

Murph's recognition of the watch data — papers thrown in joy in Brand's old office — was staged as the Earth story's mirror of the docking scene: the same exhilaration, at a desk.

12 / LEGACY & SIGNIFICANCE

Legacy & Significance

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Murph became the archetype of the scientist-daughter in modern science fiction — the ground-bound saviour whose literacy out-weighs the pilots — and Cooper Station's name keeps her, uniquely in the Galaxy, as a character a civilisation is named after.

Within the Galaxy, her file heads the line of receivers: the characters whose heroism is attention — logging, reading, believing methodically — filed as the collection's proof that the haunting was a correspondence.
