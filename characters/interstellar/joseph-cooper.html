01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Cooper is the film's whole engine: a pilot beached by history, raising two children in the dust of an Earth that has decided exploration was a luxury it can no longer afford. McConaughey plays him as appetite disciplined into fatherhood — the man who once flew the edge of the possible, now measuring his life in corn rows — and the film's first act is his slow re-ignition: a ghost in his daughter's bookcase, coordinates in the dust, and a hidden NASA offering him the sky again at the only price he cannot bear to pay, which is time with his children. His arc is the film's argument conducted in one life: he leaves to save them, loses twenty-three years to a single afternoon on Miller's planet, watches his children grow old in a screen's worth of messages, and finally falls into a black hole to send his daughter the data that saves the species — discovering, inside the tesseract, that the ghost she grew up fearing and trusting was him, all along, reaching back through gravity and love.

ROLE

The pilot

Protagonist; the Endurance's commander and the mission's indispensable pair of hands.

DEFINING TRAIT

The explorer beached

His century told him to stay home and farm; every cell of him was built for the sky — and the film prices both callings in his children's years.

FATE

Returned, across a century

He transmits the quantum data from inside Gargantua, wakes aboard Cooper Station an old man in a young body, and departs at the end for Amelia Brand's planet.

Place in the story

Cooper is the Galaxy's fullest study of the explorer as father: the man whose gift and whose love point in opposite directions, and whose film refuses to let him choose between them cheaply. His page is the record of a pilot who saved humanity by the route no plan allowed for — through a bookcase, a watch, and his daughter.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Cooper's Earth is a managed retreat: blight is killing the world's crops one species at a time, dust storms scour the Midwest, and the official philosophy of his age is caretaking — schools teach the Moon landings as propaganda, and a former test pilot farms corn and is told, at a parent-teacher meeting, to be grateful. His daughter Murph's "ghost" — books knocked from her shelf, patterns in the dust — gives him back his real self: he decodes the dust's binary as coordinates, drives into the storm with Murph stowed away, and arrives at the hidden remnant of NASA, where his old mentor Professor Brand lays out the century's secret. A wormhole stands by Saturn, placed — Brand believes — by an advanced intelligence; twelve Lazarus scouts have gone through to survey twelve worlds; and the Endurance will follow up the three promising signals, while Brand completes the gravity equation that will let humanity lift itself off the dying Earth in stations (Plan A), or, failing that, seed a colony from embryos (Plan B). Cooper accepts the pilot's seat knowing the arithmetic: relativity will tax his children's years for his hours. Murph, who has decoded the ghost's message as STAY, will not say goodbye; he leaves her his watch, and a promise, and drives away from the farmhouse without looking back, counting down like a launch.

The mission taxes him exactly as promised, and worse. On Miller's planet — an ocean world in Gargantua's time-well, where an hour costs seven Earth years — a tidal wave kills Doyle and strands them long enough to burn twenty-three years; Cooper returns to the Endurance to find Romilly aged and a generation of messages waiting, including his children grown, his son a father, Donald dead, and Murph, now his own age, asking whether he knew he might never return. The fuel arithmetic forces the mission's narrowing: Mann's planet over Edmunds', against Amelia's argued plea that love might itself be data. On Mann's frozen world the film's second betrayal detonates: the Lazarus legend has falsified his data to summon a rescue, kills Romilly with a booby trap, leaves Cooper for dead in the cold, and dies himself in a botched docking that nearly destroys the Endurance — which Cooper saves in the film's signature feat of flying, matching the station's spin and docking by hand, an impossible manoeuvre flown on nerve and mathematics.

The endgame is Cooper's gift converted into sacrifice twice over. With fuel for one planet only, he slingshots the Endurance around Gargantua — fifty-one more years of Earth time spent in hours — and detaches first Amelia toward Edmunds' planet, then himself and TARS into the black hole, lightening the ship to let her escape: the pilot's final use is ballast. Inside, past the event horizon, he finds the tesseract — a five-dimensional space built, he understands, by humanity's own far descendants, who placed the wormhole and built this room at the one place his love points: behind Murph's bookcase, across all her years at once. He was her ghost: the falling books, the STAY he now rages against his own leaving, and then — understanding arriving with TARS's transmitted quantum data from the singularity — the watch: he moves its second hand in Morse with the data Murph needs, and the girl who believed in her ghost grows into the physicist who reads it. Ejected as the tesseract closes, he wakes in a hospital aboard Cooper Station, a rotating world by Saturn named — the nurse must explain — not for him, but for his daughter. His reunion with Murph is the film's gentlest scene: she is ancient, surrounded by the family her saved world produced, and she dismisses him from her deathbed with the film's whole doctrine — a parent should not watch their child die, and his work is elsewhere. He takes a Ranger and flies, as the film ends, toward Amelia.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

The beached pilot

Interpretation: Cooper's restlessness is not wanderlust but amputation — his century removed the sky from the list of human addresses, and his farming is a prosthesis he never stops resenting. The film's sympathy is total: it treats his need to fly as a fact of his construction, like TARS's honesty setting.

Promise-keeping as navigation

His vow to Murph — I will come back — functions in the film as an instrument: it survives the twenty-three years, the fifty-one years, the event horizon, and is finally kept by the only route physics left open, through the bookcase. The film's sentiment is engineered like its orbital mechanics.

The engineer's grief

He processes catastrophe as problems — docking spins, slingshot masses, Morse on a watch hand — and the film's pathos is that the method works for everything except the messages screen, where the pilot simply sits and weeps at arithmetic no skill can repeal.

05 / ABILITIES & SKILLS

Abilities, Skills & Weaknesses

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
PILOTING

The best pair of hands alive

The spin-matching docking with the damaged Endurance is the film's certification: a manoeuvre the computers call impossible, flown manually.

ENGINEERING

The test pilot's literacy

He reads machines, fields and anomalies natively — the dust's binary is decoded at his kitchen table.

ENDURANCE

The mission's ballast, literally

He spends himself — years, then his ship, then his body's place in the lifeboat — in descending denominations, and the film keeps the accounts exact.

WEAKNESS

The promise's price

His need to return distorts the mission's early arithmetic — the Miller's planet descent is partly his hurry — and the film prices his love as both the mission's engine and its most expensive consumable.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The watch

Interpretation: Cooper's gift to Murph is the film's whole mechanism in an object — a timepiece that becomes a telegraph, carrying data across a century on the back of a promise. Time, the film's antagonist, is defeated by being used as the medium.

The bookcase

Interpretation: the tesseract's location is the film's thesis given architecture — behind the wall of the room where a father read to his daughter, across every moment she spent there: love as the one address five-dimensional beings can build to.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the argument in one life

Cooper is the film's proof that its two vocabularies — relativity and love — describe the same force. The analysis the archive records is that Nolan prices the sentiment with an accountant's cruelty: every hour of Cooper's heroism is invoiced to his children in years, on screen, until the final transaction reverses the flow — the father's fall into the black hole pays the species' debt and the daughter's equation in the same data. Sentiment that survives this film's bookkeeping has earned its place in the physics.

Legacy & Significance
