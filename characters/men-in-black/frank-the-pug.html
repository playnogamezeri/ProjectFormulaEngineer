01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Frank the Pug is an alien informant in the hidden city — a smart-talking, pug-like alien, in the documented cast record's words. When K and J need the Galaxy crisis priced, Frank is who they visit, and his documented contribution is the film's exposition at its most efficient: he explains that Rosenberg, the Arquillian prince who died in Laurel Weaver's morgue, was protecting a miniature galaxy; that the galaxy is a powerful energy source the Bug wants to use to destroy the Arquillians; and the stakes are then confirmed from orbit, as an Arquillian warship arrives demanding the galaxy's return on threat of destroying Earth. Frank's record in the sources consulted is a single, decisive function — the informant's scene — and this page builds on it honestly: the dog who knows, in a city of secrets, is a power, and Frank is drawn entirely at that scale.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Frank's documented biography is his scene. During the Galaxy hunt, K and J visit Frank the Pug, an alien informant, and Frank explains the architecture of the crisis: Rosenberg was protecting a miniature galaxy; the galaxy is a powerful energy source; the Bug wants to use it to destroy the Arquillians. An Arquillian warship soon arrives in Earth's orbit and demands the MIB return the galaxy, or the Arquillians will destroy the Earth.

That briefing re-aims the entire film. The morgue riddle — "the galaxy is on Orion's Belt" — becomes a hunt for an object with a guardian (dead), a thief (the Bug), an owner (the Arquillians) and a price (the planet). Frank supplies the guardian, the purpose and the price in one conversation; the warship supplies the deadline immediately afterwards, as if the film itself were confirming his reliability.

The sources consulted document no further beats for Frank — no role in the Pavilion climax, no fate at the ending. His story, honestly recorded, is complete at the moment the agents walk away knowing what the audience needed them to know.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

Personality & Psychology

The professional gossip

Interpretation: Frank's register is the point — he is never awed by his own information. Read as interpretation, that casualness is his credential: in the hidden city, the informant who treats a galaxy like neighbourhood news is the one who actually lives there.

Loyalty, priced and real

Interpretation: Frank informs for the agency that polices his kind, and the film plays it as a working relationship rather than a betrayal — the refugee who has decided which side keeps the city standing, and sells it his knowledge at a fair rate.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The dog who knows

Interpretation: Frank is the hidden city's perfect citizen-symbol — unremarkable at a glance, fluent in everything, belonging entirely. Read as interpretation, his pug form is the film's thesis about its aliens in miniature: the extraordinary is already here, on a lead, waiting for someone to ask it a question.

Exposition as character

Interpretation: most films hide their exposition; this one casts it as a pug and lets it steal its scene. Frank symbolises the film's confidence that information, delivered with personality, is entertainment.

Function: the exposition engine

Frank carries the plot's single largest information transfer, and the analysis the archive records is how the film protects that transfer: it gives the facts to the character whose form makes the audience lean in. Remove Frank and the stakes must arrive by computer screen or chief's briefing; with him, they arrive as gossip from a dog — the hidden city explaining itself in its own voice.
