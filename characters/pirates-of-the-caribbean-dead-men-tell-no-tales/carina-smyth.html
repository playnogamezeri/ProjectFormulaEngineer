01 / CHARACTER FILE

Overview

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

Carina Smyth is the quest's mind: an astronomer and horologist in a century that calls her a witch for it, sentenced to hang at Saint Martin for the crime of mathematics. She escapes with a diary left her by the father she never knew — Galileo's diary, its cover set with a ruby, its clues written in stars — and she is the only person alive who can read the map to the Trident of Poseidon. The film's double revelation waits at the voyage's end: the diary's author is Hector Barbossa, and Carina — who took her very name from a constellation — is his daughter.

ROLE

The navigator

Co-protagonist of the new generation; the quest is unreadable without her.

DEFINING TRAIT

Science as selfhood

In a world of curses she insists on astronomy — and the stars, it turns out, are exactly where the magic hid its map.

FATE

Her father found and lost in one day

Barbossa reveals himself by dying for her; she sails on with the Pearl's company, his name now hers to carry.

Place in the story

Carina is the film's argument about knowledge: the men chase a magical artefact with superstition and sea-lore, and the route to it is written in the one discipline they mock. Her diary plot — an inheritance misread as a stranger's leavings — gives the quest its emotional rhyme with Henry's: both young people are sailing toward a father.

03 / BIOGRAPHY

Biography: Their Story

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋
REVEAL FULL CHARACTER STORY

Carina was left at an orphanage as an infant by her father — with a diary: Galileo's journal, a ruby set into its cover, the only inheritance and the only clue to who she came from. She grew up studying the diary and the sciences it opened to her, becoming an astronomer in an age and a colony that prosecutes learned women as witches. She named herself, in part, for the stars: Carina, the constellation.

Sentenced to hang at Saint Martin for witchcraft, she escapes her cell, crosses paths with Jack Sparrow's collapsing bank robbery, and — learning that Henry Turner seeks the Trident — offers him the diary's knowledge: the stars themselves form the map, and only she can read it. Arrested again and rescued from the gallows by Henry and Jack's crew, she sails on the Dying Gull, deciphering the diary's clues across the voyage while the company's men debate ghosts.

On the restored Black Pearl, the truth assembles: Jack and Barbossa, studying the diary and its ruby, realise what Carina has not — the diarist is Barbossa himself. He confirms it to Jack alone: he left his daughter at the orphanage with the diary so that she might have a better life than a pirate's, and he has watched for her ever since without claiming her. At the Trident's tomb, with the sea parted and the curses broken, Salazar attacks the anchor chain; Barbossa descends, reveals himself to Carina in the act of saving her — she sees the star tattoo that matches her diary's map — and lets go, killing Salazar and himself in the fall. Carina sails out with Henry and the Pearl: an astronomer who found the Trident by the stars, and her father in the same hour she lost him.

The biography above is an editorial paraphrase of the documented plot accounts (Wikipedia and the cited sources), not a scene-by-scene transcript.

04 / PERSONALITY & PSYCHOLOGY

Personality & Psychology

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation, grounded in the character's on-screen actions.

Reason as armour

Interpretation: Carina's insistence on astronomy is not a hobby but a defence — in a world that hangs women for learning, her science is the one property no one can confiscate, and she wields its vocabulary like a duelling style. The film's joke is that she is right about everything except the conclusion: the stars are a map, and the map is magic's.

The orphan's arithmetic

She has priced her abandonment precisely: a diary, a ruby, no name. Interpretation: her search for the Trident is openly doubled — she tells the company she seeks the artefact; the diary says she is seeking its author. The film lets her be honest about the first and silent about the second until Barbossa speaks.

Grief, instantly converted

She has perhaps a minute of father before the fall. The film gives her no collapse — she climbs, she sails, she continues: her mother's namesake stubbornness and her father's seamanship arriving in the same gesture.

08 / SYMBOLISM & THEMES

Symbolism & Themes

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial interpretation.

The stars as a father's handwriting

Interpretation: Barbossa could not raise his daughter, so he left her the sky, annotated. The star-map is the film's tenderest device — a pirate's love expressed as homework, legible only to a daughter who became a scientist to read it.

Science and magic, reconciled

Carina's arc refutes the film's own binary: the map to the greatest magic is written in the most rigorous discipline she owns. The saga, which began with curses, ends this chapter by filing its miracle under astronomy.

09 / ANALYSIS & INVESTIGATION

Analysis & Investigation

EXPAND SECTION DETAILS ＋

This section is editorial analysis. Documented facts are cited to the sources in Section 13; readings of the character's function are the archive's own.

Function: the saga's new instrument

Each Pirates film hands its quest an instrument — a compass, a chart, a song. Carina is the first instrument who is a person: the map lives in her education. The analysis the archive records is that this makes her the new generation's true captain in waiting — Henry has the errand, Jack has the ship, but Carina has the only irreplaceable skill aboard.

The Smyth question

The film is precise that her father is Barbossa and her surname her mother's; fan accounts sometimes blur the two. The archive records the film's version exactly: the diary, the orphanage and the tattoo are Barbossa's; the name she carries is Margaret Smyth's — both inheritances honoured in her page.
